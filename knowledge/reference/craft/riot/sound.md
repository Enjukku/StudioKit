# Sound

Game sound: what it must do, how cues are designed, layered, processed, placed and reviewed, adapted to our pipeline (sounds are specified as cue cards, generated by AI tools, mixed and wired in Roblox).
SD-01 to SD-06 are the jobs and the design brief (jobs, mood, ambience, gameplay verb, stages, events). SD-07 to SD-10 are the basics of shape, frequency and distance. SD-11 to SD-20 are layering and processing craft. SD-21 to SD-23 are identity and material callouts. SD-24 to SD-28 are editing, mixing and review. SD-29 to SD-33 are sources, library and workflow.
Related: timing in `weight-timing.md` (WT-12, WT-23), the timing contract with animation and VFX in `cross-craft.md`, loudness by rank in `visual-hierarchy.md` (VH-02).

### SD-01 Sound has three jobs and tells the story with every layer
- principle: Game sound gives gameplay cues (what is happening to or around you), feedback on the player's decisions (good or bad), and drives the core emotion. Every audio layer (music, dialogue, effects, ambience, foley, UI) tells part of the story; mixed together they carry the moment.
- why: Sound reaches the player when the eye is busy; a great effect that fights the music or buries the ambience weakens the moment.
- apply: Each cue card names its job and the story point first ("the shot freezes the target"), then which layer carries which part (effect: impact and freeze; ambience: cold world; music: tension; UI: confirmation). List every gameplay state change in a feature (ability ready, taking damage, spotted, resource needed); each gets a cue or a written reason for none. Good and bad decisions get distinct feedback sounds.
- check: Play-test with the screen covered: key events are still identifiable. Listen to a capture one category at a time, then all together: each category has a job and the whole says the story point [inference].
- mistake: Sound as separate assets made in isolation; "music can only tell you so much" (R07:33-34).
- examples:
  - R00c: ability-ready and damage cues, "construct additional pylons", bleak atmosphere, being followed (R00c:4059-4105).
  - R07: the Arcane supervisors put the director's vision in place with all aural elements (R07:30-35).
- roblox_fit: Many phone players play with sound off [unverified], so key cues need a visual twin. Categories map to SoundGroups (music, effects, ambience, UI) so they can be balanced together (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/sound/dynamic-effects.md:15, 59).
- roles: sound-designer, game-designer, film-director, ui-ux-designer, feature-builder, vfx-designer, orchestrator, reviewer, qa-lead
- also: game-design-fun, ui-ux, cross-craft
- sources: R00c-F18 (R00c:4059-4105); R07-F01 (R07:30-35)

### SD-02 Every cue names its emotion, not only its object
- principle: Even a simple sound sets the mood of a scene: the same door opening can be happy, sad or terrifying.
- why: Sound frames how the player feels about what they see.
- apply: Every cue card states the intended emotion as well as the object; the same object in areas of different mood gets different variants.
- check: Listening test: a fresh listener names the emotion without the picture and it matches the cue card.
- mistake: Treating sound as an afterthought ("easy to take for granted", R00c:4028).
- examples:
  - R00c: a door opening in three moods (R00c:4026-4042).
- roblox_fit: No change.
- roles: sound-designer, environment-designer, film-director, game-designer
- also: style-identity, world-level-readability
- sources: R00c-F17 (R00c:4026-4042)

### SD-03 Ambience, foley and one signature element put the player in the world
- principle: Put the character in the space: an ambience bed (wind, snow), foley for the character (clothing, footsteps on the real surface) and one signature environmental element (crows) make the audience feel "in the world".
- why: Ambience sets place and mood before anything happens; foley grounds the character physically.
- apply: Every environment brief includes an ambience cue card: base bed, detail layer, one signature element, and the footstep set per surface; characters get clothing foley. Prefer natural-sounding sources for natural phenomena.
- check: Mute and unmute ambience in a capture: the place feels empty without it; footsteps match the surface the player stands on.
- mistake: Their own critique: the wind "would've benefited from some actual natural recordings of wind" instead of whistles (R07:601).
- examples:
  - R07: whistles and voice became wind on Mount Targon; a rain stick as snow; a coat plus palm fronds for walking on ice; crows on the outro (R07:38-40, 97-98, 108-109, 545-562, 586-595).
- roblox_fit: Ambience as looped Sounds on regions or volumetric parts (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/sound/objects.md:56); footsteps per surface from Humanoid.FloorMaterial (SD-23).
- roles: sound-designer, environment-designer, level-design, animator
- also: world-level-readability
- sources: R07-F23 (R07:38-40, 97-98, 108-109, 545-562, 586-595)

### SD-04 The sound theme comes from what the thing does in play
- principle: When a theme (ice) meets a thing (a weapon), tie the sound to the mechanic: a weapon that slows enemies gets a spreading, slowing ice sound; a slow crumble sells the slow.
- why: A sound that moves like the mechanic teaches it; players hear "slow" and expect slow.
- apply: Cue cards start with the mechanic verb (slows, stuns, heals, knocks back) and pick textures that move like it (slowing = decelerating, spreading textures with longer tails; fast = short, bright). The VFX and animation of the same ability use the same verb.
- check: Play the sound blind to a fresh listener with the cue card hidden: they guess the verb; a "slow" impact has a longer tail than a normal one in the waveform [inference].
- mistake: Picking a theme only by look ("it's ice, add ice sounds") without the behaviour (R07:19-21).
- examples:
  - R07: ice fits the Zapper because Zap slows; "icy spreading, slowing"; foil gives a "slow crumble" (R07:18-21, 26, 46-47, 99-101).
- roblox_fit: Status effects on phones are often only a small icon, so sound carries more of the read [inference].
- roles: sound-designer, vfx-designer, game-designer, art-director, animator
- also: game-design-fun, cross-craft
- sources: R07-F02 (R07:18-21, 26, 46-47, 99-101)

### SD-05 Stage the action: one sound per named stage
- principle: Before making anything, break the action into named stages and design a sound for each: load, charge, second charge, shot, travel, impact, tail.
- why: Each stage is a beat the audience reads: anticipation (load, charge), release (shot) and payoff (impact).
- apply: The cue card for any ability lists the stages as rows with frame numbers from the animation and VFX (30 fps). Animator, VFX designer and feature builder use the same stage names, so markers and sound triggers line up (the timing contract, `cross-craft.md`).
- check: Count stages in the clip and in the sound list: every visible stage has a sound; trigger frames match the clip's marker frames (`clip_check.py events`).
- mistake: One single "shoot" sound for a multi-stage action [inference].
- examples:
  - R07: markers named load, charge, charge two, shot, OML (On Missile Launch), impact, then sounds placed on them (R07:39-40, 219-222, 273-275, 500-503).
- roblox_fit: Animation markers fire stage sounds through AnimationTrack:GetMarkerReachedSignal (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/animation/events.md:12, 47-72); the travel sound is a looped Sound parented to the projectile so it moves with it [inference].
- roles: sound-designer, vfx-designer, animator, feature-builder, game-designer, film-director
- also: weight-timing, vfx-reads-timing, cross-craft
- sources: R07-F04 (R07:39-40, 219-222, 273-275, 500-503)

### SD-06 Attach sounds to game events, listed and tested
- principle: Part of sound design is attaching sounds to events: a line when entering water, a music change when combat starts, a custom reaction to an unusual event.
- why: Reactive sound makes the world feel aware of the player and marks state changes.
- apply: The sound spec lists each event (trigger, cue, priority); the feature builder exposes those events; music states switch on combat and exploration [inference].
- check: QA event list: trigger each event in a play-test and confirm the cue plays once and on time.
- mistake: Events with no sound, or sounds firing twice [inference].
- examples:
  - R00c: a line on entering water, combat music, a sleeping enemy lifted by a balloon gets its own sound (R00c:4327-4349).
- roblox_fit: Server events may arrive late on the client, so play feedback sounds on the client [inference].
- roles: sound-designer, feature-builder, game-designer, architect, qa-lead
- also: game-design-fun
- sources: R00c-F27 (R00c:4327-4349)

### SD-07 Shape the envelope: the attack and transient make the hit land
- principle: A sound's envelope (attack, decay, sustain, release) is its dynamics and decides whether it feels sharp, soft, sustained or lingering. For impacts, boost the very start (the transient) so the hit has punch; add a separate transient layer when the main layer is soft.
- why: The ear judges impact from the attack; a sharp onset reads as contact and weight.
- apply: Cue cards give a target envelope in milliseconds [inference: the sources give no numbers]; a punch: very short attack, quick decay, little sustain, short release. Every hit and shot cue has a defined transient layer or boost, and the transient frame is marked for sync with the VFX flash and the animation contact.
- check: Waveform of the generated file: peak position and length match the card; the onset peaks within the first few milliseconds [inference]; the audio transient frame equals the visual contact frame.
- mistake: Soft, slow onsets on impacts [inference].
- examples:
  - R00c: a punch broken into attack, decay, sustain, release (R00c:4164-4201).
  - R07: a transient shaper to "squeeze a little bit more life out of the beginning"; a rattly mech layer kept for the gunshot transient (R07:52, 81, 241-242, 405, 413).
- roblox_fit: No live transient shaper in Roblox's audio effects (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/audio/effects.md:33-228), so bake it into the file. The same envelope idea maps to animation (anticipation, impact, settle) and VFX (build, peak, fade) [inference].
- roles: sound-designer, animator, vfx-designer
- also: weight-timing, exaggeration
- sources: R00c-F21 (R00c:4164-4201); R07-F13 (R07:52, 81, 241-242, 405, 413)

### SD-08 Think in frequency bands; pitch sets size
- principle: Frequency works like a colour spectrum: each band has its own character, and real sounds span many bands. Pitch maps to size: higher reads smaller, lighter, magical (bright, clean sources pitched up become shimmer); lower reads bigger, heavier, less sharp.
- why: Thinking in bands separates layers as artists separate colours; pitch is the fastest size control.
- apply: Cue cards state each layer's main band (low body, mid, high detail) so layers do not fight; magic and ice layers start from bright, clean sources pitched up; heavy layers are pitched down; a harsh generated sound is pitched down before EQ.
- check: Spectrum per layer: bands do not overlap needlessly; compare two pitch versions in context and pick by the size read; listen on phone speakers and on headphones: the needed band is audible on both.
- mistake: All layers in the same band, masking each other [inference].
- examples:
  - R00c: an explosion split into frequency bands (R00c:4132-4160).
  - R07: ultrasonic sai recordings pitched up became the "magic ice" tone; a spur sound pitched down (R07:68-73, 99, 103, 165-166).
- roblox_fit: Sound.PlaybackSpeed 2 is one octave up and 0.5 one octave down (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/sound/objects.md:170-172); AudioPitchShifter changes pitch without speed and can "make small sounds feel huge by pitching them down" (audio/effects.md:182-184). Phone speakers reproduce little low end [unverified], so important cues need mid and high content.
- roles: sound-designer, vfx-designer
- also: exaggeration
- sources: R00c-F20 (R00c:4132-4160); R07-F08 (R07:68-73, 99, 103, 165-166)

### SD-09 Set how far each sound carries by its importance
- principle: Every sound has attenuation: important or threatening events (gunshot, roar) carry far; small sounds (footsteps, breathing) only up close.
- why: Distance tells where things are and what matters; uniform attenuation hides far events and floods the mix with near clutter.
- apply: Cue cards state the hearing distance in studs; set RollOffMinDistance, RollOffMaxDistance and RollOffMode on Sounds, or the AudioEmitter distance attenuation curve. For stealth or threat games consider a UI indicator for important sounds.
- check: Play-test: walk away from each source and note where it becomes inaudible; compare with the card.
- mistake: Every sound with the same attenuation [inference].
- examples:
  - R00c: gunshot and roar heard far; footsteps and breathing only close (R00c:4229-4259).
- roblox_fit: RollOff: knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/sound/objects.md:123-161; DistanceAttenuation: audio/objects.md:116. With many players, short attenuation on small sounds keeps the mix clean.
- roles: sound-designer, game-designer, level-design, environment-designer, ui-ux-designer
- also: world-level-readability
- sources: R00c-F23 (R00c:4229-4259)

### SD-10 Far sounds lose their highs first
- principle: One sound can attenuate differently per band: highs fade fast, lows carry, so up close it is sharp and far away it is a low boom.
- why: That is how distance sounds; the player hears distance, not only volume.
- apply: Build it as two layers: a high-detail layer with a short RollOffMaxDistance and a low-body layer with a long one [inference]; or a distance-driven AudioEqualizer [inference].
- check: Listen near and far in a play-test: the far version has lost its high detail but kept its low body.
- mistake: A far sound that is just a quieter copy of the near one [inference].
- examples:
  - R00c: a gunshot near versus far (R00c:4261-4277).
- roblox_fit: Per-band distance attenuation is not a single documented setting [unverified]; layering is the route (AudioEqualizer: knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/audio/effects.md:37).
- roles: sound-designer, environment-designer
- also: world-level-readability
- sources: R00c-F24 (R00c:4261-4277)

### SD-11 EQ: cut what is not wanted before boosting
- principle: Use EQ first to remove what is not wanted (rumble, hiss, hum) and focus the sound, rather than boosting; think of sound as sculpture.
- why: Unneeded lows and noise make mixes muddy and mask other sounds; cutting makes room for each layer.
- apply: Clean every AI-generated sound of noise before import. On bright layers (magic, ticks, UI) apply a low cut below their useful range (PROPOSAL default: below about 200-300 Hz [inference]); only one layer per cue owns the low end. Use live EQ only for context changes (underwater muffle).
- check: Spectrum of each layer: no energy below the cut on bright layers, one low-end owner per cue; no audible hiss or hum in the generated file.
- mistake: "A lot of folks will use EQ to boost frequencies" (R07:178).
- examples:
  - R00c: a line read made "more intense" by EQ (R00c:4279-4295).
  - R07: low rumble in the sai recording hacked off to leave "cool, pure, clean tone" (R07:169-179).
- roblox_fit: AudioEqualizer controls frequency ranges live (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/audio/effects.md:35-37); bake fixed cuts into the file (SD-32).
- roles: sound-designer, reviewer
- also: none
- sources: R00c-F25 (R00c:4279-4295); R07-F11 (R07:169-179)

### SD-12 Compression lifts the tail; compress again after layering
- principle: Compression narrows the dynamic range; used hard it lifts the quiet shimmery tail after the attack. After adding echoes, overtones and delays, compress again so the new material sits with the original.
- why: The tail is where the "magic" lives; new layers change the dynamics, so the first pass no longer fits.
- apply: Chain order: clean, compress, add space and texture layers, compress again.
- check: Waveform: the tail is visibly sustained after the attack, not falling to near-silence; peaks are controlled across layers [inference].
- mistake: One compression pass before the layers were added (R07:293-294).
- examples:
  - R07: "incredibly aggressive" compression on the sai to bring out the shimmer; a second pass for "more mileage out of the new tails" (R07:224-231, 292-296, 305).
- roblox_fit: AudioCompressor narrows range and can duck one stream under another (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/audio/effects.md:56-58; SoundGroup ducking: sound/dynamic-effects.md:59); bake heavy compression, keep live compression for ducking [inference].
- roles: sound-designer
- also: none
- sources: R07-F12 (R07:224-231, 292-296, 305)

### SD-13 Reverb and delay place the sound and glue its layers
- principle: Reverb (reflections) tells the space and glues separate layers into one event in one place; delay and echo build size. A dry sound feels pasted on; secondary layers sit further back in the room.
- why: Space tells where and how big; shared space makes layers one event.
- apply: The ambience map names a reverb type per area (hallway, cave, open field); layered cues share one reverb; distant or secondary layers get more reverb and lower level; delay can push magic "into a different universe".
- check: Walk between areas in a play-test: reverb changes match the space size; a dry layer next to wet ones fails [inference].
- mistake: "That feels a little dry" (R07:515); dry sounds in a cave; layers that sound like separate recordings [inference].
- examples:
  - R00c: voices in a hallway, bathroom, elevator and canyon (R00c:4298-4326).
  - R07: reverb and delays on the sai; the charge rendered with reverb so "it doesn't sound so dry"; the end note pushed back (R07:249-254, 315, 515-518, 548, 580-583).
- roblox_fit: SoundService.AmbientReverb (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/_api-reference/classes/SoundService.yaml:61), AudioReverb and AudioEcho (audio/effects.md:77-79, 140-142); per-area reverb needs scripted switching for AmbientReverb [inference].
- roles: sound-designer, environment-designer, level-design
- also: world-level-readability
- sources: R00c-F26 (R00c:4298-4326); R07-F14 (R07:249-254, 315, 515-518, 548, 580-583)

### SD-14 Process in a fixed order, a little at each step
- principle: Build a sound through an ordered chain, each step adding a little character, "not too too much": pitch, low cut, compression, overtones, transient boost, reverb, delay, compression again, air, saturation. Compare raw and processed; the core must still be there.
- why: Order matters (cut before boost, compress after layering); small steps keep the core recognisable.
- apply: The default chain for a tonal or magic layer follows this order, and each step's purpose is written on the cue card. Generated sounds get fixes in the same order.
- check: A/B raw versus processed: the processed version is bigger but the core tone is still recognisable; each step on the card has a stated purpose.
- mistake: Overprocessing ("not too too much", R07:240).
- examples:
  - R07: pitch up, EQ cut, compression, crystalliser, transient shaper, reverb, delay, compression, exciter, saturation on the sai; "plain Jane" versus "big, beefy" chimes (R07:165-177, 237-254, 304-321).
- roblox_fit: The order of live audio effects changes the result (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/audio/effects.md:27).
- roles: sound-designer, reviewer
- also: none
- sources: R07-F15 (R07:165-177, 237-254, 304-321)

### SD-15 Tonal layers: stacked sources, kept clean and separate
- principle: Make tonal building blocks by stacking two or three tonal sources in one key, and keep clean tonal parts as their own layer, separate from mechanical and noisy layers. A clean tone cuts through a dense mix and carries the magic.
- why: Stacking gives a richer, unique tone; mixed noisy and tonal content cannot be balanced.
- apply: For magic tones generate 2-3 tonal sources on the same note and layer them; deliver every ability cue as stems (tonal, mechanical, impact or noise, tail), balanced, then rendered to one file per stage for phones [inference].
- check: The cue's stem list has a separate tonal stem; solo it: clean, no noise floor; tonal layers share a key (no unintended beating) [inference].
- mistake: Noisy and tonal content baked together so neither can be adjusted [inference].
- examples:
  - R07: processed sai chimes are "one tonal component" of the final design; synth chimes are "not noisy, just nice, pure stuff" (R07:162, 168, 322-325, 334-335, 345-347).
  - R07: a subtractive "fairy chimes" synth plus a granular "crystal bell" on one C, "a technique that I use almost daily" (R07:327-352).
- roblox_fit: Separate Sounds per layer cost instances and triggers; render to one file per stage once balanced [inference, unverified].
- roles: sound-designer, vfx-designer
- also: style-identity
- sources: R07-F16 (R07:162, 168, 322-325, 334-335, 345-347); R07-F17 (R07:327-352)

### SD-16 A shot is built from five layer jobs
- principle: A weapon shot, even a fantasy one, needs something that feels like a real shot, built from layers with one job each: low-end body (weight), a high transient tick (contact), a concussive crunch (violence), a report or echo off the environment (space), and a small identity "ting" left unprocessed.
- why: Each layer carries one perception; together they read as one powerful event.
- apply: Shot and impact cue cards list these five slots and the source that fills each; generate each layer separately and combine; only the shot and impact keep the low body.
- check: Solo each layer: it does its one job; remove one at a time: the shot loses weight, contact, violence, space or identity accordingly [inference].
- mistake: Their own critique: with more time they would use a "better gunshot, actual transient" (R07:599).
- examples:
  - R07: a kid's drum, buckyballs on a bottle, palm fronds and pompoms gave "a loose approximation of a gunshot" (R07:377-432, 505-507).
- roblox_fit: Phones lose sub-bass, so the tick and crunch carry the shot on small speakers [inference, unverified]; for distant shooters lower the report and tail through RollOff (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/sound/objects.md:123-125).
- roles: sound-designer, vfx-designer, feature-builder, game-designer
- also: weight-timing, exaggeration
- sources: R07-F18 (R07:377-432, 505-507)

### SD-17 Put movement inside the sound
- principle: A sound with movement in it (a spinning object passing between two mics) has modulation that gives it character; static tones sound synthetic.
- why: Modulation reads as alive.
- apply: Ask the generator for motion ("spinning", "swirling", "passing") on magic and travel layers, or add slow movement in processing (chorus, flanger, Doppler).
- check: Spectrogram shows slow variation across the sound's length, not a flat band; it sounds alive when looped [inference].
- mistake: Flat, static tones for magic [inference].
- examples:
  - R07: sais spun in front of a stereo pair; the movement "gives a cool tonality" (R07:73-74, 232-235).
- roblox_fit: Positional 3D sound collapses most stereo width, so motion must be inside the file or come from the emitter moving [inference] (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/sound/objects.md:56).
- roles: sound-designer, vfx-designer
- also: none
- sources: R07-F09 (R07:73-74, 232-235)

### SD-18 Pass-bys make travel; let the engine move real projectiles
- principle: A Doppler pass-by (louder, then quieter, with an optional pitch bend) makes a texture feel like it moves past; the same trick turns textures into wind. Add the pitch bend only if the material suits it.
- why: Motion past the listener reads as speed and travel.
- apply: Whooshes and travel sounds get a pass-by volume shape; decide the pitch bend per material.
- check: The volume envelope rises then falls; pitch bend is present only where the card says so [inference].
- mistake: A baked pass-by inside a loop that the engine already moves (double Doppler) [inference].
- examples:
  - R07: cardboard, foil and palm frond through a whoosh plug-in; turned into "creepy" wind (R07:469-492).
- roblox_fit: SoundService.DopplerScale applies engine Doppler to moving 3D Sounds (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/_api-reference/classes/SoundService.yaml:178-190); a projectile's Sound gets real pass-by from movement, so do not bake a second one into its loop [inference].
- roles: sound-designer, vfx-designer, environment-designer
- also: vfx-reads-timing
- sources: R07-F22 (R07:469-492)

### SD-19 Process for variety, but keep a natural sound when it works
- principle: Pitch, flanger, phaser and chorus turn one recording into new textures; but processing is optional: if the natural sound works, use it ("No processing, people").
- why: Gives variety from few sources; knowing when to stop keeps sounds natural.
- apply: Make variants of a cue (an upgraded weapon tier) by phase or pitch processing of the base so the family stays recognisable [inference]; always try the unprocessed version first.
- check: Each variant is recognisably the same family as the base; the cue card notes that the unprocessed version was tried.
- mistake: Processing everything by default ("You don't always have to process things", R07:454).
- examples:
  - R07: spliced ice samples through pitch, flange, phaser and chorus became "ethereal"; the casing ting used with no processing (R07:446-455, 466-467, 507).
- roblox_fit: AudioChorus and AudioFlanger exist live (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/audio/effects.md:98-100, 161-163).
- roles: sound-designer
- also: none
- sources: R07-F21 (R07:446-455, 466-467, 507)

### SD-20 Build long, varied beds from spliced takes
- principle: Splice the best takes into each other and crossfade layers over time to make long, varied beds; every cut gets a short fade.
- why: Long varied material avoids obvious repeats; fades prevent clicks.
- apply: Ambient and texture loops are built from several spliced takes with crossfades; each splice has a short fade; spacing selects a few frames apart before consolidating.
- check: Loop the result for 60 s: no click at the loop point or splices, no recognisable repeat inside the first loop [inference].
- mistake: A short loop that repeats audibly [inference].
- examples:
  - R07: selects spaced 5 frames with fades, consolidated; two long files ridden on faders (R07:286-290, 438-466).
- roblox_fit: Looped Sounds repeat exactly; longer loops or randomly timed layers avoid fatigue [inference].
- roles: sound-designer, environment-designer
- also: rhythm
- sources: R07-F20 (R07:286-290, 438-466)

### SD-21 Every weapon and the whole game get a signature sound
- principle: Each weapon or ability has one recognisable signature element (usually tonal) that belongs to it only and appears in every stage; the game itself has a sound identity (signature sounds, homages, forbidden directions) kept across products and updates.
- why: Players identify who fired what, off-screen and in crowds, by a recognisable tone; consistency builds recognition.
- apply: Each weapon or ability cue card names its signature element and keeps it in load, shot and impact. The art bible gets a sound section listing signature sounds and forbidden directions; updates reuse them.
- check: Play all weapons' clips in sequence: each signature is distinct in pitch and timbre; it is heard through the mix with 4 or more other effects playing [inference]; new cues are checked against the sound section.
- mistake: Generic gun or magic sounds that could belong to anything (R07:41).
- examples:
  - R07: the ice Zapper gets a mechanism, a "magical power source" and ice that "rings" while charging (R07:41-44, 322-325).
  - R07: original Jayce and Vi kit sounds snuck into Arcane (R07:134-137).
- roblox_fit: Many players fire at once; a signature in the high mids carries better on phone speakers than a low boom [inference, unverified].

### SD-22 Characters sound like who they are, and sound telegraphs their state
- principle: A character's sound sets them apart from a similar character, rooted in who they are (size, age, material); sound also telegraphs state, like a creak of rising tension before a shield breaks.
- why: Distinct sound lets players tell similar characters apart; rising tension warns before a change.
- apply: The character sound brief states traits and the nearest similar character to differ from; abilities with a timer or a breaking point get a cue that builds as it nears its end.
- check: Blind listening test: a fresh listener tells the two similar characters apart; the warning cue's intensity rises before the break.
- mistake: Similar characters sounding the same.
- examples:
  - R00c: Ivern (a smaller, older tree) versus Maokai; a creaking prop of wood, hinges and rope for his shield (R00c:4366-4382).
- roblox_fit: No change.
- roles: sound-designer, game-designer, vfx-designer, animator, character-rigger
- also: character-design, game-design-fun
- sources: R00c-F29 (R00c:4366-4382)

### SD-23 Every floor material is a footstep callout
- principle: Every walkable material has a footstep sound, and players locate enemies by it; dress floors so the look matches the sound (only a little decorative grass on a floor that does not sound like grass). New materials need sound's agreement.
- why: A floor that looks like grass but sounds like concrete misleads.
- apply: A material list shared with sound maps each floor material to a footstep set; new materials get one or switch to an existing one; decorative material stays small enough not to read as a different floor.
- check: Walk capture with audio: each floor's look matches its footstep sound; every floor material in the place has a footstep cue.
- mistake: "Grass and dirt is a separate sound callout ... I can only do enough grass just to add in some visual variety" (R09:850-853).
- examples:
  - R02: a broken-glass material request may get "make it something different" from sound (R02:470-480).
  - R09: linoleum in Corrode sounds like a lighter wood (R09:409-420, 848-856).
- roblox_fit: Humanoid.FloorMaterial reports the material under a character (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/_api-reference/classes/Humanoid.yaml:365), so footsteps can be driven by material.
- roles: sound-designer, environment-designer, modeler, level-design, feature-builder, game-designer, orchestrator

### SD-24 Design and editing are two separate passes
- principle: Sound design creates new sounds; editing makes them work in the scene. Do them as separate passes.
- why: A sound that is great alone may not fit the picture; fitting needs its own judgement (timing, level, which layers stay).
- apply: Pass 1: make building blocks and candidate sounds. Pass 2: cut them to the markers in context of the capture and the other sounds, dropping what does not serve the moment.
- check: Pass 2 is reviewed against the capture with other sounds present, never the isolated file only.
- mistake: Judging sounds only in isolation [inference].
- examples:
  - R07: editing is "the second part of this job", a "totally different craft" (R07:215-218).
- roblox_fit: Pass 2 happens in Studio play-tests at the game camera with other players' and ambient sounds present [inference].
- roles: sound-designer, film-director, reviewer

### SD-25 Mix as you go; music and effects are designed together
- principle: Mix as you build and pre-mix before the final mix, so notes come early; music and sound effects are designed together and hit the beats and cuts together.
- why: Late mixing finds conflicts too late; music and effects fighting for the same moment weaken both.
- apply: Each feature's sound enters the game's mix (SoundGroup levels) when built; the sound designer and the music owner agree which leads at key beats; music ducks under big effects where needed.
- check: At each feature review, play it with the current music and other systems' sounds: note any masking.
- mistake: Leaving all mixing to a final stage (R07:199-203).
- examples:
  - R07: scenes built before the final mix; the sound and music relationship is "one of the secret sauces" of Arcane (R07:198-204).
- roblox_fit: Duck music under big effects with a compressor and SoundGroup (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/sound/dynamic-effects.md:59).
- roles: sound-designer, film-director, orchestrator, feature-builder
- also: process-iteration, cross-craft
- sources: R07-F25 (R07:198-204)

### SD-26 Review the key event solo, then in the full mix
- principle: First mute the backgrounds and listen to the key event alone ("just take in the shot"), then listen to the whole experience.
- why: The event must work on its own and then survive in the full mix.
- apply: Every sound review has two listens of the capture: effects only, then the full mix; findings are logged for each. The same two-step works for VFX (effect alone, then the busy scene) [inference].
- check: Two listens are logged and the event passes both.
- mistake: Judging only the full mix, where ambience hides a weak effect [inference].
- examples:
  - R07: the backgrounds and crows muted to hear the shot, then "the entire experience of being in the ice world" (R07:585-590).
- roblox_fit: Mute SoundGroups in a Studio play-test for the solo listen [inference].
- roles: sound-designer, reviewer, qa-lead, film-director

### SD-27 Give sound notes in technical terms with a direction
- principle: Sound is hard to give feedback on ("more whooshiness"); shared technical terms make notes precise.

- check: Every sound review note names a term and a direction (for example "attack shorter", "cut highs above X", "longer tail").
- mistake: Notes like "needs more whoosh".
- examples:

### SD-28 Judge against real reference and name every stand-in
- principle: Know what good sounds like by listening to professional reference sounds, and be honest where a result falls short: an improvised or generated stand-in for a real sound is named as such, with the better source proposed.
- why: A benchmark makes judging one's own work possible; fakes approximate, real material carries detail.
- apply: Keep a small set of reference sounds per category; A/B generated sounds against them. Hand-backs list stand-ins (for example "wind from whistles, not natural wind") and the proposed better source.
- check: A/B listen against the category reference is logged; the hand-back lists stand-ins.
- mistake: Judging against weak sources; calling a quick approximation final.
- examples:
  - R00c: professional libraries as the industry standard to learn from (R00c:4431-4447).
  - R07: "maybe not the highest of fidelity"; whistles instead of wind; the deep library unused (R07:597-604).

### SD-29 Sources by texture, not by name: many takes, some odd ones
- principle: Sources need not be literal: ice was made from palm fronds, foil, seashells and bottles. Gather many candidates, judge by listening, and always try some unexpected sources and deliberate misuse of tools; re-sounding a scene with odd sources forces new ideas.
- why: Literal sources are rarely the most expressive; texture, not origin, is what is heard; unique sounds rarely come from the obvious route.
- apply: In our pipeline "recording" is generating: for each cue prompt the AI sound tool with several texture descriptions, not only the literal noun ("dry palm leaves crunching", "foil crumpling slowly", "glass bottle tink"), several takes each (PROPOSAL default: 4-6 per texture [inference]); include at least one unexpected-source or deliberately odd variant (reversed, extreme pitch, unrelated texture) [inference]. Keep good takes for reuse.
- check: Count candidates per layer before choosing (at least 3 [inference]); the option set contains an unexpected or odd variant; the chosen take is picked by listening, not by label.
- mistake: Searching only for the literal thing ("I don't think we're gonna get ice. Let's create ice", R07:55).
- examples:
  - R07: palm frond "sounds the most like ice"; seashells gave a rocky icy texture; an hour of recording per prop (R07:54-63, 77, 88-90, 96, 276-277, 607-608).
  - R00c: a robot film scene re-sounded with cats and dogs (R00c:4449-4477).
  - R07: weird pitch tries and Doppler as "a very fun, happy accident way" to make building blocks (R07:244-248, 374-376, 485, 576).
- roblox_fit: Generated sounds are uploaded as audio assets; keep a reusable library so they are not regenerated per feature [inference].
- roles: sound-designer, vfx-designer, film-director, marketing, reference-images, orchestrator
- also: references-ideation
- sources: R07-F07 (R07:54-63, 77, 88-90, 96, 276-277, 607-608); R00c-F31 (R00c:4449-4477); R07-F28 (R07:244-248, 374-376, 485, 576)

### SD-30 Keep a library of building blocks; cues are data
- principle: Turn raw material into building blocks: clean and cut raw takes, process them into usable parts, combine parts into bigger designs, and file all three tiers in a shared library others reuse. Use library components first and make custom sounds when the library lacks what you need.
- why: Reuse saves time and gives consistency; parts can be recombined for variants; custom work goes where it is unique.
- apply: The game's sound library has three tiers: raw generated takes, processed parts (tonal, mechanical, crunch, low body, tail), finished cues, with tags (texture, pitch, use). New cues start by browsing it. Cue names map to asset IDs and parts in a data table, so code never hard-codes IDs.
- check: Each finished cue lists its parts and the source of each layer; the library index has tags so a search finds parts; no audio asset ID is hard-coded in logic.
- mistake: Rebuilding from scratch every time; not using the deep library (R07:602-604).
- examples:
  - R07: recordings chopped "into bite-sized chunks" and uploaded to the shared library; sounds "building on top" (R07:113-118, 143-155, 436-437, 602-606).
  - R00c: a foley room for what libraries lack (R00c:4350-4364).
- roblox_fit: Roblox audio is asset IDs (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/audio/assets.md); keep them as data (AGENTS.md §2, content as data).
- roles: sound-designer, orchestrator, architect, live-ops-designer
- also: pipeline-production
- sources: R07-F10 (R07:113-118, 143-155, 436-437, 602-606); R00c-F28 (R00c:4350-4364)
