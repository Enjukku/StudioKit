# VFX reference 03: scene placement rulebook (Part 3)

Scope and use: Output 3, merged at claim level from both research runs (Claude: p3-scene, gap-clutter-budgets, gap-telegraphs-danger, p1-composition-camera-attention, gap-riot-guide-talks, with spot rows from gap-phase-timing, gap-hitstop-shake-flash, p9-p10-p11, p2-p12; ChatGPT: 03_scene_placement_rulebook, claim_ledger, contradictions, supporting_reference). Roblox facts re-checked in the local docs mirror.
The VFX agent uses it to decide where an effect goes, how loud it may be next to everything else on screen, and when it may peak. Each rule is a pass/fail check. Effect-level timing and layer recipes live in the other reference files.
Every number carries an R-id (`00-sources.md`) and an evidence level. `PROPOSAL (inference)` values are starting points, not evidence; design numbers stay proposals until the owner confirms. Frames become ms only where the source states its fps.

Keys:
- Evidence level: study | preprint | industry practice (who, year) | spec | inference. "abs" = only an abstract or listing was read.
- "1 pub, both runs" = the same publication was read by both runs: one source, not two. Corroboration counts distinct publications.
- "single tier C" = the claim rests on one tier C source. Tier D = lead only, never proof.
- Rating bands in V-15 and later rows refer to the per-viewer importance rating of V-13 (1-10 scale, reported Riot practice).

## 1. Focal point and eye path

| id | rule | test (pass/fail) | numbers | evidence level | sources |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| V-01 | One Area of Focus per effect: the element that carries the gameplay message (projectile head, contact point, blast origin, danger edge). Trail, debris and smoke are secondary. | Freeze the punch frame: a reviewer names and points at one element; the effect card field `focus` is filled. Fail if two elements compete or the field is empty. | 1 focus per effect [R-367, R-120; industry practice]; maximum count of secondary elements: no number found | industry practice (Beardilocks/Riot 2020; Riot VFX style guide 2017) | R-367, R-120, R-360 |
| V-02 | The focus takes the value extremes (highest and lowest value) and the highest saturation of the effect. The rest stays mid value and mid saturation. | Greyscale view of the punch frame: focus is the highest-contrast patch. Saturation-only view: focus is the most saturated patch. Fail otherwise. | Avoid 0% and 100% value and saturation in general [R-120, industry practice; R-360 is a summary of the same guide]; focus holds the extremes [R-367, industry practice]; numeric ratio: no number found | industry practice (Riot VFX style guide 2017; Beardilocks 2020); the "focus takes the extremes" half rests on R-367 alone, single tier C | R-120, R-360, R-367 |
| V-03 | Reserve pure white (100% value) for the top importance band (ultimate-class moments). | Render every effect: only top-band effects produce clipped white pixels; overlapping copies of a lower-band additive effect must not clip to white. Fail otherwise. | 100% white kept for ultimates [R-372, industry practice]; PROPOSAL (inference): overlap test with 3 copies; basis: additive blend adds light (LightEmission 1 = additive [R-211 spec]); tune by: the largest overlap count seen in playtests | industry practice (Beardilocks/Riot 2020), single tier C | R-372, R-211 |
| V-04 | Steer the eye with value first, then colour, then shape (practitioner order). Size, colour, motion and orientation are the proven guides in vision science, so confirm the order with a timed find test. | Squint/blur test: the value pattern alone shows the eye path. Timed find test (V-05 b) passes. Fail if the path disappears in greyscale. | Guiding attributes: colour, motion, orientation, size "undoubted"; luminance onset "probable"; looming "possible"; colour change "probably not" [R-117 study]; no number for the practitioner order | industry practice (VFX Apprentice/Keyser 2023; Beardilocks 2020; the value-first order rests on tier C sources only) + study (Wolfe & Horowitz 2017) | R-358, R-367, R-117 |
| V-05 | At windup, punch and aftermath, the brightest and most saturated pixels sit on the current top-priority gameplay element (threat, hit, pickup), never on ambient dust, embers or decoration. Brightest is necessary, not sufficient: players with a goal partly ignore salient cues. | (a) Screenshot at windup, punch and aftermath: the max-luminance and max-saturation pixels fall on the intended element. (b) Timed recognition: testers name what to react to; log wrong-target reactions. Fail on either. | no number found | inference (from Riot 2021, Beardilocks 2020, Nguyen 2022) + study (Wolfe 2021 GS6: goals, history, reward and scene meaning guide attention; Nair et al. 2026) | R-080, R-367, R-071, R-084, R-002 |
| V-06 | Plan areas of rest (negative space): calm, low-detail zones around the focus. | Edge-density map of the punch frame: most high-frequency detail sits inside the focus region. Fail if the busiest region is not the focus. | no number found | industry practice (Beardilocks 2020; Coelho-Kostolny 2017 critique; Quitevis GDC 2026, abs, names negative space) | R-367, R-311, R-003, R-142 |
| V-07 | Show travel direction without motion: taper or blur the tail, brightest at the head. | Pause at 3 points on the path; testers name the direction. Fail on any head/tail confusion. | no number found | industry practice (Riot "Clarity in League" 2021; Riot VFX style guide 2017) | R-080, R-120, R-360 |
| V-08 | Put the windup cue on the same screen spot as the later punch, so attention arrives before the payoff. | Overlay windup and punch frames: the windup cue lies inside the punch footprint. Fail if it sits elsewhere. | no number found | industry practice (An-Tim Nguyen, GDC 2022: Titanfall titan drop, Destiny super) | R-071 |
| V-09 | A must-notice layer starts with an abrupt onset (full strength on its first visible frame), not a fade-in. Ambient layers fade in so they do not grab. Do not count on onset alone when the player hunts for a known shape. | Frame-step the first visible frames: must-notice layer at peak opacity on frame 1; ambient layers ramp. Fail otherwise. | Onset capture cost in singleton search: 1057 vs 998 ms, d=.41; none in prescribed-shape search: 560 vs 557 ms, p=.436 (n=24 each) [R-002 study]; design number: no number found | study (Luck et al. 2021 consensus: abrupt onsets give a priority signal unless control settings block it; Abrams & Christ 2003; Nair et al. 2026) | R-087, R-146, R-002 |
| V-10 | Motion toward the camera (looming) pulls attention; receding motion does not. Use toward-camera motion for top-band punches only; routine debris moves across or away. Pair looming with a brightness onset. | Classify each layer's main motion (toward / across / away): toward-camera only in the top band. Fail otherwise. | Looming rated only "possible" as a guide [R-117 study]; looming reaction-time main effect not significant, p=.087, n=71 [R-065 study] | study (Franconeri & Simons 2003; Wolfe & Horowitz 2017; Fernández-Folgueiras et al. 2022) | R-147, R-117, R-065 |
| V-11 | Separate the focus from look-alike neighbours: make it differ in shape, size, colour or motion, or lead them in time. Similar neighbours at the same moment crowd it out. Keep supporting particles uniform among themselves. | Punch frame: the hit spark differs from nearby debris in at least one feature, or its onset leads the debris layer. Fail if same look and same onset. | Crowding drops when target and flankers are separated in time by about 150 ms (before or after); critical spacing ≈0.4-0.5 × eccentricity, "widely reported", varies across studies [R-138 study, review]; spacing varies by observer and position, 2-parameter model 82% cross-validated variance, n=50, letter recognition under fixation [R-054 study, abs]; PROPOSAL (inference): lead the debris layer by ≥150 ms when the focus must be read; basis: R-138; tune by: timed spot-the-spark test at phone size | study (Whitney & Levi 2011; Kurzawski et al. 2023; Itti & Koch 2001) + inference | R-138, R-054, R-149 |
| V-12 | Warnings must be caught in peripheral vision while the player looks elsewhere, at real phone size and camera distance. | Central tracking task plus a peripheral warning on a phone: detection rate and response time vs a no-effects baseline. Fail if detection drops. | no number found | inference (from Wolfe 2021 GS6; Kurzawski et al. 2023) | R-084, R-054 |

## 2. Hierarchy across all on-screen effects

| id | rule | test (pass/fail) | numbers | evidence level | sources |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| V-13 | Rate each effect's importance before building, once per viewer: local player, other players/allies, enemies/targets. | Effect card holds 3 ratings and the decision each visible cue supports. Fail if any is blank. | Scale 1-10, 3 viewer groups, over 750 abilities rated (League) [R-368, industry practice]; no source maps the rating to size, brightness, duration or count | industry practice (Beardilocks/Riot 2020), single tier C for the exact scale (1 pub, both runs); hierarchy itself also in Riot 2021 and the Riot guide 2017 | R-368, R-080, R-120 |
| V-14 | The attention an effect takes ranks in the same order as its importance: size, brightness, saturation, motion and duration follow the rating. Heavier damage = bigger, brighter, sharper. Control effects (stun, root) and decisive defences read stronger than plain damage. | Measure each effect at the punch: peak luminance × screen area, and lifetime. Ranked order matches rating order. Fail on any inversion. | no number found (Riot raised a shield that blocks 99% of damage for this reason [R-374, industry practice]) | industry practice (Riot "Clarity in League" 2021; Beardilocks 2020; Riot PBE post 2020) | R-080, R-368, R-374, R-120 |
| V-15 | Starting map from rating band to channels, to replace once tests give data. | Each new effect lands in one band; V-14 rank test passes. Fail if an effect uses a channel above its band. | PROPOSAL (inference): ratings 8-10 (the top of band 6-8 and all of band 9-10) may use a 100% white core, additive core and the screen-level channels FOV kick, post-effect flash, slow motion and camera moves; band 9-10 adds the largest area; trauma shake is allowed from rating 3 because t³ keeps it near zero (+0.2 -> 0.8% of max); band 6-8 additive core, peak below pure white except rating 8, Highlight allowed (start value: PD-06); band 3-5 normal blend with small additive accents, mid value; band 1-2 (ambient) low saturation, value at or below stage diffuse white, no onset flash; basis: R-372 (white for top band), R-300 (strong colour reserved for weapons and abilities), R-355 (value band), R-211 (LightEmission 0 normal / 1 additive); tune by: V-14 rank test plus playtest "which one mattered more" | inference | R-372, R-300, R-355, R-211 |
| V-16 | When goals conflict: (1) correct gameplay information (hitbox/indicator, threat, pickup, path), (2) readability: noise and brightness, (3) theme. | A theme layer that hides a hitbox edge, pickup or path in any frame is cut or changed. Fail if it ships. | 3 ranked tiers [R-060, industry practice] | industry practice (Riot, Sirhaian & Riru 2022; Beardilocks 2020; Riot guide 2017) | R-060, R-367, R-120 |
| V-17 | Between viewer categories at similar ratings: threat to the local player > local player's own action feedback > pickups and objectives > other players' effects > environment and ambient. UI stays above all effects in value. | Busy-scene screenshot: measured prominence follows this order. Fail on inversion. | no number found | inference (from Nguyen 2022: threat over muzzle flash; Blizzard WoW 2025: own and enemy visuals full, other party members reduced; Hall 2023: effects below UI) | R-071, R-028, R-355 |
| V-18 | Own-weapon feedback stays below threat cues. If testers can describe the muzzle flash after a fight, it is too strong. | Post-fight question "what did your muzzle flash look like?": most testers cannot say. Fail if most can. | no number found | industry practice (An-Tim Nguyen, GDC 2022, citing Doom) | R-071 |
| V-19 | Value band by layer: UI > effect focus > characters > world. | Sample luminance in normal play: median UI > effect focus > character > world. Fail on inversion. | Band order only: effects above characters and below UI [R-355, industry practice, single tier C]; "world last" is our inference; ratio between bands: no number found; floor for critical markers: V-47 | industry practice (David Hall, Sparkadia 2023; Riot VALORANT maps 2020) + preprint (Zhou & Forbes 2022) | R-355, R-100, R-063 |
| V-20 | For must-find actors (pickups, targets, threats), win with outline and silhouette (Roblox Highlight) before adding brighter particles. | Count active Highlights on the busiest client; retired ones are deleted, not disabled. Fail if the count passes the budget. | Cap 255 shown, extras silently ignored, a disabled one keeps its slot; first Highlight up to 1 ms GPU on mobile; mobile cost grows with screen coverage [R-196 spec]; PROPOSAL (inference): ≤20 active per client (start value: PD-09); basis: R-196 cap and coverage cost; tune by: Stats.RenderGPUFrameTime on a low-end phone at 10/20/50 Highlights [R-461] | industry practice (Nguyen 2022: Halo Infinite red enemy / blue ally outlines) + spec | R-071, R-195, R-196, R-461 |
| V-21 | Pulse rate carries meaning: danger cues pulse faster than a resting heartbeat; safe or healing cues pulse slower. | Measure the frequency of every looping cue: danger > safe, no cue above the flash cap. Fail otherwise. | Heals below 60 BPM (= 1 Hz, our arithmetic) [R-346, industry practice, single tier C, no second source]; danger "faster than a heartbeat", no bpm given [R-071, relaying Julian Love GDC 2013]; flash cap 3 per s [R-165 spec]; PROPOSAL (inference): lasting danger zones steady or ≤1 Hz; countdowns 1 -> 2 -> 3 Hz; low health 1 Hz below the flash threshold; safe and healing cues ≤1 Hz; never above 3 Hz at full contrast; "faster than a heartbeat" is met by the imminent countdown cues only (start value: PD-12); basis: R-346, R-071, cap with margin; tune by: danger-or-safe classification test | industry practice (Nguyen 2022 relaying Love 2013; VFX Apprentice 2025); 2 publications, possibly one origin | R-071, R-346, R-165 |

## 3. Clutter: how many, how much screen, overdraw, brightness

| id | rule | test (pass/fail) | numbers | evidence level | sources |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| V-22 | Cut fine texture detail that carries no gameplay. | At gameplay camera distance remove one detail layer; if testers read the effect the same, delete it. Fail if a removable layer ships. | no number found | industry practice (Riot, Sirhaian & Riru 2022) | R-060 |
| V-23 | One dominant colour plus one complement per effect; competing colours desaturated. | Count distinct hues in the effect (ignore value steps). Fail at 3 or more dominant hues. | 2 colours (Nocturne cut) [R-374, industry practice]; 1 dominant + desaturated competitors [R-120, industry practice] | industry practice (Riot guide 2017; Riot PBE post 2020; Hall 2023) | R-120, R-374, R-360, R-355 |
| V-24 | Keep effects short and cut outros first: the main action ends or starts to dissipate early in the lifetime. | Timeline: main action done by the set fraction of lifetime. Fail if it runs past. | "First quarter of lifetime" [R-355, industry practice]; no second source found; PROPOSAL (inference): main action done by 25% of lifetime as the default; basis: R-355; tune by: V-31 and "which hit was it" tests | industry practice (David Hall, Sparkadia 2023), single tier C; Riot guide 2017 demotes outros | R-355, R-120 |
| V-25 | Dissipate as soon as the job is done; the aftermath ranks below any new actionable cue. | After the punch, live-particle count falls fast; a new threat placed in the aftermath area is still found first. Fail if residue wins attention. | no number found | industry practice (VFX Apprentice 2023; Jevremovic, 80 Level 2018) + inference | R-358, R-310 |
| V-26 | Retire hazard language when the hazard ends: no border, danger hue or danger motion after the last damage tick. An expiring trap may blink out on purpose. | Stop on the final damage tick: residue implies no further hit. Fail if a border or danger hue remains. | no number found | inference (from Riot 2022 indicator accuracy) + industry practice (Hall 2023: expiring stun trap blinks out) | R-060, R-355 |
| V-27 | Strip decorative noise from cues that players check often, even at a cost in impact. | Before/after with the information cue circled: cue visible in every frame. Fail if any frame hides it. | no number found | industry practice (Riot VALORANT patch 11.07, 2025; Avalanche Rage 2, 2019) | R-033, R-300 |
| V-28 | Reserve strong colour and saturation for weapons, abilities, hazards and pickups; keep ambient and world effects grounded (low saturation). | Saturation histogram: ambient effects lower than combat and pickup effects. Fail if equal or higher. | no number found | industry practice (Oscar Bernelind, Avalanche 2019; Riot VALORANT maps 2020) | R-300, R-100 |
| V-29 | Prefer a cheap non-particle cue (bloom pulse, rumble, camera shake) over a bigger burst when it carries the message. | Swap the burst for the cue; if testers still get the message, ship the cue. Fail if the burst stays without a reason. | no number found | industry practice (An-Tim Nguyen, GDC 2022) | R-071 |
| V-30 | The noise budget follows the rating: only the top band may be noisy, and only to a degree. | Count events per minute above the routine brightness × area ceiling: all are top band. Fail otherwise. | no number found | industry practice (Riot 2021; Beardilocks 2020) | R-080, R-372 |
| V-31 | Keep the number of effects that need attention at one moment small; everything beyond that is ambient and low in salience. | Set-size sweep (2, 4, 8, 16 concurrent effects plus 1 pickup): find time and misses. Budget = largest count before the knee. Fail above budget. | Working memory 3-5 items [R-139 study]; people track "four or more" targets [R-145 study, abs]; the weak clutter effect at set size 4 came from an easy task, not a safe count [R-029 study]; PROPOSAL (inference): ≤4 attention-needing effects per viewer at once (own action, nearest threat, pickup, one ambient); basis: R-139, R-145; tune by: the sweep | study (capacity limits, not effect counts) + inference | R-139, R-145, R-029, R-034 |
| V-32 | Clutter costs search time; a pickup or threat that shares a hue with nearby effects loses its pop-out. | Hue check: pickup and threat hues differ from every effect near them on screen; timed find test passes. Fail on a shared hue. | Cluttered vs blank background: find time 0.61 -> 0.78 s (+28%, target present), 0.81 -> 1.86 s (+130%, target absent, symbol task), r 0.55-0.66, error up to 0.25 in the worst cell [R-029 study, static video-call images]; clutter rating 3.98 -> 7.05 from low to high item count [R-014 study] | study (Semizer & Rosenholtz 2025; Semizer et al. 2026) | R-029, R-014 |
| V-33 | Score clutter automatically with a Feature Congestion style measure on Studio screenshots; track it per build. | FC score of the busiest scripted view vs the same view with effects off. Fail above budget. | FC tracks human clutter ratings, rho 0.60 [R-014 study]; FC blank 1.92 / mock 2.45 / varied 3.24 with present-target find time 0.61 / 0.66 / 0.78 s [R-029 study]; PROPOSAL (inference): worst-case FC ≤ +30% over the effects-off view; basis: in R-029 a +28% FC rise cost about +8% find time, a +69% rise cost +21-28% (our arithmetic); tune by: correlate FC with the V-31 sweep | study (Rosenholtz et al. 2007; Semizer 2025, 2026) + inference | R-142, R-014, R-029 |
| V-34 | Design inside Roblox engine limits; caps are ceilings, not budgets. | Scene Analysis logs Transparent and Particles counts for the busiest view; every emitter Rate within cap. Fail otherwise. | 400 particles/s per emitter, 100/s on mobile; Lifetime capped at 20 s; particle size costs fill-rate; overlapping transparent layers cost overdraw [R-209 spec]; Scene Analysis breaks out Transparent and Particles triangle/draw-call counts [R-225 spec] | spec | R-209, R-211, R-225 |
| V-35 | Fewer, larger particles; cut the per-effect count as the number of simultaneous emitters rises. | Busiest server moment: live particles per routine impact within budget. Fail above. | Shipped mobile cuts: impact 30 -> 2; 10 simultaneous impacts 300 -> 20; smoke plus sparks 200 -> 50; limits "as low as 3-10" particles per effect in a mobile strategy game where many units fire effects; budget example: one 100-particle effect fired by 200 units [R-248, industry practice]; "reproduce that with, say 10 particles" is one forum poster's mobile example (2017), not a shipped figure [R-385, industry practice]; PROPOSAL (inference): routine brick impact ≤10 live particles when 8 or more players blast at once; basis: R-248, R-385; tune by: GPU frame time on a low-end phone | industry practice (Stanislav Stabrov, Azur Games 2026; realtimevfx mobile thread 2017) | R-248, R-385 |
| V-36 | Keep stacked transparent layers low; halve the worst emitters before shrinking the meaningful silhouette. | Overdraw view: give every particle a flat dark-grey texture (0.1, 0.1, 0.1) with LightEmission 1, so pixel value ≈ 0.1 × layers up to 10 (our arithmetic). Also stack k = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 copies on a low-end phone and read GPU frame time. Fail if the frame average or a hotspot passes the budget. | Arm debugger saturates at 10 layers; 2.44-2.48 fragments/pixel called "quite high" and 10x "definitely too much" for one analysed scene [R-132, industry practice 2015]; halving 3 emitters removed most 7+ layer overdraw [R-204 spec, Unreal]; dark-grey additive view [R-332, industry practice]; PROPOSAL (inference): frame average ≤2.5 layers, hotspots ≤7 layers outside the focus; basis: R-132, R-204; tune by: the k-stack test | industry practice (Lorenzo Dal Col, Arm 2015; Snap Lens Studio docs) + spec (Epic Niagara) | R-132, R-204, R-332, R-211, R-461 |
| V-37 | Thin by screen coverage, not by fixed caps: fade opacity and size as a system covers more screen; thin particles near the camera; under GPU pressure thin the most expensive systems first. | Camera inside smoke and close-up blasts: pickup and path still findable, GPU time in budget. Fail otherwise. | no threshold number in the source [R-114, industry practice]; Highlight mobile cost grows with coverage [R-196 spec] | industry practice (Bungie, Whitley & Mayyasi, GDC 2018) + spec | R-114, R-196 |
| V-38 | Screen-coverage budget for translucent effects (no studio published one). | Difference image, effects on vs off, fixed camera: % of pixels changed. Fail above budget. | no number found in any source; flash-area limits (25% of screen) apply to flashing only and are not a coverage budget [R-168 spec, R-396]; PROPOSAL (inference): routine play ≤20% of pixels changed by effects, top-band peak ≤50% for ≤1 s; basis: none direct, values sit inside our 5-50% test grid; tune by: find-the-pickup test during a 5/10/20/35/50% coverage sweep | inference | R-168, R-396, R-029 |
| V-39 | Few pixels may be brighter than the stage's diffuse white outside top-band moments. | Luminance histogram of gameplay frames: % of pixels above stage diffuse white. Fail above budget. | In 107 graded HDR images, the ITU 99% range was 5.6-14% of pixels above reference white (203 cd/m²) [R-166 spec; broadcast stills, not effects]; PROPOSAL (inference): ≤14% of pixels above stage diffuse white in routine play; basis: R-166; tune by: V-31 sweep with brightness instead of count | spec (ITU-R BT.2408-8, 2024) + inference | R-166 |
| V-40 | Stress-test the full composite: every player's effects, muzzle flashes, lights, post effects, UI and camera motion together, at the game's maximum cadence and real player counts. Per-asset passes do not count. | Scripted worst-case replay on target phones: detection errors, response time, occlusion and GPU frame time measured together; tails do not pile up; the next contact is not masked. Fail on any. | no number found for a transferable count, coverage or overdraw limit | inference (from Keller, Blizzard 2024; Riot 2021; WCAG composite flashing) | R-041, R-080, R-167 |
| V-41 | Record the measurement context with every coverage, brightness or timing number. | Each measurement lists device, resolution, quality level, camera FOV and distance, capture fps and display fps. Fail if any is missing. | ms = 1000 × frames / fps (arithmetic); Roblox FOV default 70, clamp 1-120 [R-182 spec] | inference + spec | R-182 |
| V-42 | When decorative effects are capped or toned down, keep a cheap confirmation of the player's own accepted action. | At maximum cap a tester can always tell an accepted action from a dropped input. Fail otherwise. | no number found | inference (success-dependent feedback raised motives in Kao et al. 2024) | R-043 |

## 4. Gameplay clarity: hitboxes, threats, pickups, path, UI, many players

| id | rule | test (pass/fail) | numbers | evidence level | sources |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| V-43 | The readable danger or indicator edge equals the real gameplay geometry at cast, impact and expiry. Decorative overflow is reviewed separately. Destruction marks (scorch, crack) do not overstate the broken area. | Overlay the server blast sphere or box at the 3 moments: the readable edge sits on the debug edge. Fail on mismatch. | Tolerance: no number found; PROPOSAL (inference): readable edge within ±5% of the true radius; basis: Riot requires a match and states no tolerance [R-060, R-080, industry practice]; tune by: stand-or-move error rate in a dodge/collect test | industry practice (Riot 2022, 2021; Riot PBE post 2020; Embark THE FINALS 7.3.0, 2025: damage shown for accurate judgment) | R-060, R-080, R-374, R-032 |
| V-44 | Never draw a danger edge inside the true hitbox; a big ground circle must not promise more room to dodge than exists. | Overlay test (V-43): no part of the hitbox lies outside the drawn edge. Fail otherwise. | no number found; some games draw enemy indicators "slightly" larger and own indicators "slightly" smaller, described, not recommended [R-336, industry practice, tier C] | industry practice (Riot 2021: Arcane Comet over-promise); the sizing note is single tier C | R-080, R-336 |
| V-45 | Ground danger zones get a clear outline plus fill, follow slopes and stairs, and add a vertical or camera-facing edge element, because circles squash to ovals in third person. | View every zone from the gameplay camera on flat ground, slope and stairs: edge visible and closed in each view. Fail otherwise. | no number found | industry practice (VFX Apprentice AoE guide 2026), single tier C; FFXIV orange default AoE is a lead only (tier D) | R-336, R-427 |
| V-46 | Effects never cover HUD-critical UI (counts, buttons, prompts); text that sits over moving gameplay gets a backing. | Worst composite (V-40): every critical UI element legible. Fail otherwise. | Contrast floor: V-47 | industry practice (Microsoft XAG 117: opaque text backgrounds behind motion; Hall 2023: UI above effects) | R-074, R-355, R-059 |
| V-47 | Contrast floor for gameplay-critical markers (pickup outline, hazard edge, key UI) against their local background. It is a floor for markers, not a reason to brighten every effect. | Measure contrast against the lowest-contrast local background on each stage. Fail below the floor. | ≥3:1 for graphics needed to understand content [R-238 spec]; 4.5:1 standard important elements, 3:1 large, 7:1 high-contrast mode [R-059, industry practice] | spec (W3C WCAG 2.2) + industry practice (Microsoft XAG 102) | R-238, R-059 |
| V-48 | Never use colour as the only channel: add shape, icon, pattern or motion. For two sides, orange vs blue beats red vs green. | Colour-blind simulation plus real players: every essential meaning still read. Fail if one is lost. | 8-10% of males have red/green difficulty [R-398, industry practice]; Overwatch offers 9 colour-blind-friendly colours [R-111, industry practice] | industry practice (Microsoft XAG 103; Game Accessibility Guidelines; Nguyen 2022; Blizzard 2018) | R-015, R-398, R-071, R-111 |
| V-49 | Pickups, targets and threats stay findable through debris and effects: use a beacon or a Highlight with DepthMode AlwaysOnTop for must-find items. | Inside a blast cloud, testers point at the nearest pickup and the nearest threat. Fail on a miss. | AlwaysOnTop draws through blockers [R-195 spec]; Highlight budget: V-20 | industry practice (Nguyen 2022: Destiny motes) + spec; transfer to brick pickups = inference | R-071, R-195, R-196 |
| V-50 | Keep decorative contrast out of gameplay sightlines: ornament sits above or away from path and target screen regions; the play space is lit. | Mark path and target screen regions under the real camera: decorative contrast there is lower than outside.
