## Sensor fit: probed angles next to the formula (check C3)

Camera: lens 50 mm, `sensor_width` 36, `sensor_height` 24, camera looks at a 2 m square plane 10 m away. "formula" is computed in the script from the rule below. "projection" is `camera.calc_matrix_camera(depsgraph, x, y)` read as `2 atan(1/P[i][i])`. "pixel" is measured from a Workbench render at 960x540 or 540x960 (one pixel is about 0.4 deg of error or less). Source: `p04_sensor_fit.py`. Angles in degrees.

| Fit | Output | formula V | `camera.data.angle_y` | projection V | pixel V | formula H | projection H | pixel H |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HORIZONTAL | 1920x1080 | 22.8952 | 26.9915 | 22.8952 | 22.87 | 39.5978 | 39.5978 | 39.55 |
| AUTO | 1920x1080 | 22.8952 | 26.9915 | 22.8952 | 22.87 | 39.5978 | 39.5978 | 39.55 |
| VERTICAL | 1920x1080 | 26.9915 | 26.9915 | 26.9915 | 26.88 | 46.2127 | 46.2127 | 46.21 |
| HORIZONTAL | 1080x1920 | 65.2385 | 26.9915 | 65.2385 | 64.89 | 39.5978 | 39.5978 | 39.36 |
| AUTO | 1080x1920 | 39.5978 | 26.9915 | 39.5978 | 39.55 | 22.8952 | 22.8952 | 22.87 |
| VERTICAL | 1080x1920 | 26.9915 | 26.9915 | 26.9915 | 26.93 | 15.3769 | 15.3769 | 15.34 |

Formulas (a = displayed width / displayed height, here 16/9 or 9/16 with square pixels):

| Fit | Rule | V | H |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HORIZONTAL | `sensor_width` governs the horizontal side. | `2 atan(tan(H/2)/a)` | `2 atan(sensor_width/(2f))` |
| VERTICAL | `sensor_height` governs the vertical side. | `2 atan(sensor_height/(2f))` | `2 atan(a tan(V/2))` |
| AUTO | `sensor_width` governs the **larger** output side. Landscape or square: as HORIZONTAL. Portrait: V = `2 atan(sensor_width/(2f))`, H = `2 atan(a tan(V/2))`. | see rule | see rule |

Other probed facts: `sensor_height` changed 24 -> 12 mm leaves V at 22.8952 for HORIZONTAL and AUTO and changes it to 13.6855 for VERTICAL (`p04 sensor_height_effect`). `camera.data.angle` equals the fit-side angle (39.5978 for HORIZONTAL and AUTO, 26.9915 for VERTICAL) at every resolution (`p04 cam.angle[...]`). In Auto the square-pixel rule above is probed; the case of non-square pixels on Auto is not probed.

## Facts the shotlist tool relies on

All values probed on Blender 5.1.2 unless a docs source is named. The 5.2 docs agree where read; no 5.2 binary was run.

| Topic | Fact | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Effective rate | `rate = render.fps / render.fps_base`. `fps` is an integer (1-32767). `fps_base` is a float (1e-5 to 1e6) stored as 32-bit. | `p02 rate[...]`, `p01 fps_prop_range`, `p01 fps_base_prop_range` |
| Settings for 24 | `fps=24`, `fps_base=1.0` -> 24.000000000000 (this is also the factory default). | `p01 fps_default`, `p02 rate[24]` |
| Settings for 30 | `fps=30`, `fps_base=1.0` -> 30.000000000000. | `p02 rate[30]` |
| Settings for 30000/1001 | `fps=30000`, `fps_base=1001.0` -> 29.970029970030, error vs exact 0.000e+00. Do **not** use `fps=30, fps_base=1.001`: the stored base is 1.0010000467300415 and the rate is 29.970028570928, error -1.399e-06 fps. 24000/1001 works the same way: 23.976023976024. | `p02 rate[29.97 via 30000/1001]`, `p02 rate[29.97 via 30/1.001]`, `p02 error_...`, `p02 rate[23.976 ...]` |
| Reading a rate back | `fps_base` that is a whole number (like 1001.0) gives an exact fraction `Fraction(fps, int(fps_base))`. A non-whole base is approximate: warn and show the digits. (My proposal for the tool; the probe shows why.) | `p02 fps_base_set_1.001_readback` |
| Movie rate | A movie rendered at `30000/1001` reads back as 29.970029830932617 fps (float32 of the exact rate) and has 31 frames for frames 1-31. | `p09 movieclip ...`, `p09 strip.fps ...` |
| Samples and time | Frames 1-31 inclusive = 31 samples. At 30 fps that is 31/30 s = 1.033333333333 s. The distance between positions 1 and 31 is 30 intervals = 1 s. One frame = 100/3 ms (30 fps), 50/3 ms (60 fps), 1001/30 ms (30000/1001). Shots as `[start, next_start)`; the last shot ends at `frame_end` inclusive, so `frame_end = last_exclusive - 1`. | `p08 ...`, `p02 render_animation_1_to_31_file_count = 31` |
| Marker frame | `TimelineMarker.frame` is an integer; floats raise `TypeError`; negative values are accepted and a marker outside `frame_start..frame_end` still works. | `p01`, `p02 marker_*`, `p10 marker before frame_start (5), frame_set(12) camera = CamB` |
| Marker camera | `marker.camera` is a camera object. A camera marker is active **from its own frame** until the next camera marker (`p03`: marker at 10 -> frame 10 is camera B). Before the first camera marker, the first marker's camera is used (`p03 frame_set(1) with scene.camera=CamC and first marker at 5 -> CamA`; frames 1-4 render red = camera A). A marker with no camera is ignored (`p10 unbound marker@15: frame 17 camera = CamA`). Two camera markers on one frame: the one earlier in `scene.timeline_markers` wins (`p10 two bound markers ...`). Deleting the camera object leaves `marker.camera = None`. | `p03`, `p10` |
| Marker read-back | `scene.timeline_markers` iterates in creation order, not frame order (`p10 timeline_markers iteration order`); sort by `frame`. Names may repeat (`p10 duplicate names allowed`). Read the active camera with `scene.frame_set(f)` then `scene.camera` (`p02 frame_set[...]`). | `p10`, `p02` |
| Render-time camera | During an animation render the marker camera is the one rendered (pixels, `p03 rendered_centre_pixel`). A `render_pre` handler still sees the previous frame's camera (`p03 render_pre_camera`: frame 10 shows CamA, pixel is camera B). `render_post` sees the right one (`p03 render_post_camera`: frame 10 shows CamB). After the render, `scene.camera` holds the camera of the last frame rendered (`p02 scene.camera_after_render`). | `p02`, `p03` |
| Sensor fit | See the section above: angles per fit, resolution; `angle_y` is not the rendered V. The tool should compute V, H from the formulas or from `calc_matrix_camera`. | `p04` |
| Evaluated pose | Export `evaluated_get(depsgraph).matrix_world` and `.data.lens` after `scene.frame_set(f)`. Raw `location`, `rotation_euler` and a parent's animation are not the pose. | `p07` |
| Fit constants | Defaults: `lens` 50, `sensor_width` 36, `sensor_height` 24, `sensor_fit` `AUTO`. | `p01` |

## Corrections the skill must carry

| # | Correction | Why (evidence) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `Auto` sensor fit: `sensor_width` governs the larger output side. Never say "Auto adapts to output dimensions" without that rule. | FC-03b, `p04 case[AUTO 1080x1920]`; 5.2 manual extract "square sensor size based on the larger" |
