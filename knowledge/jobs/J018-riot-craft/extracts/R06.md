## Findings

### R06-F01 Three pillars: shape language, colour, timing
- principle: An effect is built from shape language (does it protect or hurt you), colour, and timing. If those three are right, the engine and tool do not matter; timing is "really, really important".
- why: Players read intent from shape (sharp = harm, round = help), meaning from colour, and cause/effect from timing; these work in every engine.
- apply: Every VFX brief and cue card states three lines: shape (sharp/aggressive vs round/protective), colour role (from the art bible), timing (stage start times in seconds). The vfx-designer writes these before building ParticleEmitters/Beams.
- check: In the review capture, answer three questions in one line each: does the shape say help or harm; does the colour role match the bible; are stage starts measured and in order (frame captures at 30 fps).
- mistake: Thinking you need strong painting skill first; the artist says you do not, the three basics matter more (R06:191, R06:194).
- example: The whole "happy storm" is planned as shape, colour, timing (R06:24).
- roblox_fit: Same in Roblox; all three are set through ParticleEmitter Texture/Shape, Color, and Lifetime/Rate/timing in code.
- roles: vfx-designer, art-director, animator, sound-designer, ui-ux-designer, reviewer
- topic: vfx-reads-timing (secondary: shape-silhouette, light-colour, weight-timing)
- lines: R06:24, R06:59, R06:191, R06:367-368

### R06-F02 A celebration is short feedback that a goal was met
- principle: A celebration tells the player "you met this condition and got the buff". In the real game it is small and "wouldn't last that long".
- why: Positive feedback at a threshold confirms the player's choice without taking attention from play.
- apply: When a design adds a threshold (set bonus, combo, level up), the game-designer asks for a short celebration effect + sound; the vfx-designer keeps it brief. Sound default [inference]: total under about 2 s for an in-play celebration, longer only on a non-play screen.
- check: Trigger the threshold in a play-test; the celebration is visible and ends before the player's next decision; measure its duration from the capture.
- mistake: Celebrations that run long or big and stack across units (see F05).
- example: Two Invokers on the board proc the Invoker trait celebration, a cloud popping up (R06:30-33).
- roblox_fit: Trigger from the server event on the client; play a short emitter burst (ParticleEmitter:Emit) and a Sound. [unverified for the exact duration default]
- roles: game-designer, vfx-designer, sound-designer, ui-ux-designer, progression-designer

### R06-F03 The prompt or concept is a suggestion; constraints decide
- principle: Interpret a prompt or concept art freely; tech limits, texture memory budget and time decide what is built. Tell the concept artist early that the result may differ.
- why: A concept painting ignores engine cost; the builder must fit budget and still meet the intent.
- apply: The vfx-designer treats the reference image from the reference-images skill as the target intent, not a frame to copy; list the deviations (budget, texture size, time) in the hand-back so the art-director is not surprised.
- check: Hand-back lists each deviation from the reference with its reason (budget, tech, time).
- mistake: Copying the concept exactly and breaking budget; or deviating silently ("don't get mad at me", R06:36-37).
- example: "A happy storm" from the Teemo hat becomes clouds, lightning and raining candy (R06:28-39).
- roblox_fit: Roblox texture and particle budgets are tighter on phones (Rate 100/s per emitter on mobile, particle-emitters.md L212).
- roles: vfx-designer, art-director, orchestrator, reference-images, modeler

### R06-F04 Build from existing assets and combine them
- principle: Under time pressure, pull clouds, lightning, rain and meshes from earlier effects and combine them ("smush 'em into one").
- why: Saves time and keeps the look consistent with the rest of the game.
- apply: Before making a new texture or emitter, search the game's existing effects (Studio MCP search_game_tree, ReplicatedStorage effect folders) and approved asset ids; reuse, then recolour and retime.
- check: Hand-back names which existing assets were reused and which are new.
- mistake: Making every texture from scratch and running out of time.
- example: Clouds from Aurelion Sol's recall, lightning from Storm Dragon Aurelion Sol, rain from his own Gangplank ship effect, candy meshes from Candy King Ivern (R06:44, 127, 275, 286).
- roblox_fit: Reuse in Roblox means cloning emitters/attachments from an effects library; recolour via Color ColorSequence.

### R06-F05 Footprint budget: many effects at once must stay readable
- principle: Keep each celebration inside one hex (one unit's space) and short, to keep the noise level down. Seven gigantic effects at once means the player cannot tell what is happening.
- why: Gameplay clarity must hold "the entire time"; noise distracts and "could cost them a round" while managing items.
- apply: Set a footprint per effect class in the brief: [inference] personal feedback effect radius about 1 character width (about 4-6 studs), duration short; big effects only for rare, non-overlapping events. Test with the worst case count on screen (all players' effects at once).
- check: Capture at the game camera with the maximum count of simultaneous effects; can you still see every character and the thing the player must act on? Count overlapping effects.
- mistake: "If I just have seven different gigantic effects going off at once, the player's not gonna know what's going on" (R06:51).
- example: TFT celebrations stay within one hex; the demo goes 3x on purpose and says it would not ship (R06:49-54).
- roblox_fit: Many players on one server each trigger effects; phone screen is small, so the footprint matters more. Particle Rate also caps (particle-emitters.md L212).
- roles: vfx-designer, game-designer, art-director, reviewer, qa-lead, level-design
- topic: world-level-readability (secondary: visual-hierarchy, vfx-reads-timing)

### R06-F06 Never cover information the player needs; avoid vertical space
- principle: Effects must not block information the player uses: the enemy bench (scouting), gold, the units, places where items are placed. Do not use high vertical space; keep the character unobstructed.
- why: Blocking information is unfair as well as confusing: one player's effect can hide what another needs to see.
- apply: The vfx-designer lists the info areas from the game's camera (other players, HUD zones, targets, shop) and keeps effects below/away from them. Height default from the demo: in-game the cloud sat at 400 units (perfect), 600 too high, 800 far too high (R06:69-75).
- check: Capture at the game camera during the effect; black-fill mask the info areas and confirm no effect pixels over them. Self-critique list from the source: benches, gold, characters, items blocked? (R06:354-355)
- mistake: Placing effects "super high", which blocks the enemy bench and gives an unfair advantage (R06:70, R06:78).
- example: Final candy storm: "way too noisy, it blocks gameplay clarity, the benches would be blocked, your gold mines would be blocked, the characters would be blocked" (R06:354).
- roblox_fit: Third-person and top-down cameras look down at the play area; an effect above the character can hide the character or other players from the camera. Also keep effects clear of the HUD safe areas on phones. [unverified for a specific engine rule]
- roles: vfx-designer, game-designer, ui-ux-designer, environment-designer, reviewer, qa-lead, level-design
- topic: world-level-readability (secondary: composition-focus, ui-ux)

### R06-F07 Test at the game camera, not only in the editor
- principle: An effect that looks good in the editor viewport can be wrong in game (here it rendered on the characters' bodies). Test in game after every change.
- why: Game camera, scale, characters and lighting differ from the editor view.
- apply: After each change, capture from the game camera in a play-test (Studio MCP start_stop_play + screen_capture), not only from the edit camera.
- check: Every review image is a play-mode capture at the game camera; the hand-back labels it MCP-verified.
- mistake: "if you don't test it in game, it might look wonky" (R06:129).
- example: The first cloud is "way too high" and then renders on the units' bodies in game (R06:65-67).
- roblox_fit: Phone aspect ratio and camera distance change size on screen; capture at phone resolution too.
- roles: vfx-designer, animator, environment-designer, modeler, qa-lead, reviewer
- topic: pipeline-tech (secondary: critique-feedback)

### R06-F08 Iterate by bracketing numbers
- principle: Much of the job is trial and error: try a value, look, overshoot, come back. Bracket: too low, too high, then the value between.
- why: Feel is found by comparison; two extremes show where the right value is.
- apply: For each key number (count, height, size, start time) render 2-3 options and compare at the game camera. Source values: spawn count 35 then 200 (R06:56); height 200, 800 (too high), 400 (perfect), 600 (R06:68-75); spread 100, 150 (R06:102-103).
- check: Hand-back shows the tested values and why the chosen one won.
- mistake: Keeping the first value without comparing.
- example: "A big part of my job is just messing with things and seeing if it works" (R06:99).
- roblox_fit: Change properties live through Studio MCP and capture each option.
- roles: vfx-designer, animator, environment-designer, sound-designer, reviewer
- topic: critique-feedback (secondary: pipeline-tech)
- lines: R06:56, R06:68-75, R06:99-103

### R06-F09 A volume must read solid: no gaps between sprites
- principle: When sprites are spread out, you can see through them and the volume breaks; fill in with more overlapping sprites until gaps close.
- why: Gaps reveal that the cloud is flat cards; a closed mass reads as volume.
- apply: For clouds, smoke, mist: increase overlap (more particles or larger Size) and use two layers (front and back) rather than spreading thin.
- check: Capture at game camera; look for background visible through the mass; a value/black-fill check of the effect layer shows holes.
- mistake: Spreading the emitter wide without adding density ("you can see through it", R06:97).
- example: Spread cloud thickened with more A Sol clouds to reduce see-through (R06:97, R06:106).
- roblox_fit: Each emitter is limited to 400/s, 100/s on mobile (particle-emitters.md L212); prefer larger Size over more particles.
- roles: vfx-designer, environment-designer
- topic: vfx-reads-timing (secondary: shape-silhouette)
- lines: R06:97, R06:105-106

### R06-F10 Colour sets the mood
- principle: The same shapes in dark navy blue read as a threatening lightning storm; warmer pinks and oranges make it read happy ("cotton candy").
- why: Viewers read emotion from hue and warmth before shape.
- apply: Brief the mood in colour words from the art bible (e.g. happy = warm pink/orange, menace = dark blue); set ParticleEmitter Color sequences to match; the reference image states the palette.
- check: Squint/thumbnail check of the capture: does the mood read from colour alone? Run look_check.py palette against the brief's colours.
- mistake: Keeping the reused asset's original colour, which keeps its original mood ("it doesn't feel very happy. It feels very like lightning storm", R06:107).
- example: Navy clouds recoloured pink and orange; it starts to feel "cotton candy" (R06:110, R06:125).
- roblox_fit: Color is a ColorSequence per emitter; Lighting colour also tints particles unless LightInfluence is 0 (particle-emitters.md L462-467).
- roles: vfx-designer, art-director, environment-designer, reference-images, ui-ux-designer
- topic: light-colour (secondary: style-identity)

### R06-F11 Avoid popcorning
- principle: "Popcorning" is seeing each individual particle where it does not blend. Fix it by making background layers blend with the front (similar colours), starting smaller, and fading opacity, not only shrinking scale.
- why: Visible pops break the illusion of one mass and look cheap.
- apply: Give every particle a Transparency sequence that fades in and out (e.g. opaque, then fade to 1 from half its lifetime, R06:345-346); match colours across overlapping layers; avoid particles shrinking to 0 as the only exit.
- check: Frame-by-frame capture of birth and death: count particles that appear or vanish in one frame.
- mistake: Changing one layer's colour so it no longer blends (R06:113); fading only by scale (R06:342-344).
- example: Pink cloud on navy back clouds popcorned; candy on the floor faded by shrinking until opacity fade was added (R06:113-116, R06:343-346).
- roblox_fit: Roblox docs: fading near start and end of lifetime "avoids a popping in/out effect" (particle-emitters.md L183).
- roles: vfx-designer, art-director, reviewer
- topic: vfx-reads-timing (secondary: light-colour)
- lines: R06:113-116, R06:122-124, R06:342-346

### R06-F12 A source of power: brightest point where the player must look
- principle: An effect needs a clear source of power. Put the brightest part where you want the player to look (in TFT usually the unit's chest).
- why: The eye goes to the brightest point first; with no bright centre "there's nothing for them to really focus on".
- apply: Every effect brief names its focus point; the brightest layer sits there. For characters, default focus is the chest/torso attachment.
- check: Grayscale capture: the brightest region is at the named focus point; look_check.py value check confirms the top value is there.
- mistake: Effects with even brightness and no focal point (R06:132-135).
- example: He adds a centre glow dot in the middle of the clouds (R06:136).
- roblox_fit: Use an Attachment at the focus for the emitter; LightEmission makes it additive (particle-emitters.md L257).
- roles: vfx-designer, art-director, animator, film-director, ui-ux-designer, marketing
- topic: composition-focus (secondary: visual-hierarchy, value-contrast)
- lines: R06:131-136

### R06-F13 Soft glow texture
- principle: A glow is a single dot, blurred, then blurred more, with some small irregular artifacts; made as an alpha-blended texture so the background is not needed.
- why: A perfect radial gradient looks synthetic; small irregularities read as energy.
- apply: Generate a grayscale or white dot on transparent PNG (reference-images skill or a scripted image), blur, add slight noise; upload as the emitter Texture.
- check: Look at the texture on dark and light backgrounds: soft falloff, no hard edge, no box edge visible.
- mistake: not stated
- example: Simple glow texture made in Photoshop for the centre dot (R06:137-142).
- roblox_fit: Roblox docs: use .png with transparent background; grayscale with no alpha can use LightEmission 1 to hide dark regions (particle-emitters.md L57).
- roles: vfx-designer, reference-images
- topic: vfx-reads-timing (secondary: materials-detail)
- lines: R06:137-142

### R06-F14 Ramp-up energy
- principle: Show power being drawn in: start the core small and dim, then grow it and brighten it over time; increase spawn rate over the lifetime from 0 to high.
- why: Growing size and brightness reads as charging energy and builds anticipation for the hit.
- apply: Size NumberSequence growing over lifetime (source: scale up over time 2, then settled at 1.5; R06:158-160); extend Lifetime so the build is seen (it "fades out way too soon", R06:155); spawn rate ramp from 0 to 100 over the build, then 75 when 100 was too much (R06:258, 265); start the build early (lightning moved from 1.5 s to 0.5 s, R06:255-257).
- check: Frame captures of the build: size and brightness increase monotonically; clip/frame count of the build is long enough to notice (measure seconds).
- mistake: A core that pops in at full size and dies in a second (R06:155).
- example: Centre dot "getting bigger, it's getting brighter"; lightning "zero to like a bajillion" (R06:159, R06:260).
- roblox_fit: Size and Transparency take NumberSequence over lifetime; Rate can be tweened from code. Rate caps at 400/s per emitter, 100/s on mobile (particle-emitters.md L212).
- roles: vfx-designer, animator, sound-designer, film-director
- topic: exaggeration (secondary: weight-timing, rhythm)
- lines: R06:155-161, R06:255-261

### R06-F15 Do not blow out to white; save white and black
- principle: Do not let effects blow out to white. Save whites and blacks to make things pop and use them sparingly; full white everywhere is "like a flash bang".
- why: White is the strongest accent; overused, it erases colour and hurts readability and comfort.
- apply: Limit pure white to the single focus point at the peak; stacked additive layers sum toward white, so check emitter counts at the core.
- check: Capture the peak frame; measure the share of clipped white pixels (look_check.py value); only the focus point should clip.
- mistake: Six additive dots spawned on top of each other caused the blowout (R06:170).
- example: Teal additive glow on top of alpha clouds blew out to white; reduced to one dot (R06:163-170).
- roblox_fit: LightEmission 1 is additive and overlapping additive particles brighten toward white (particle-emitters.md L257); Bloom in post effects adds more.
- roles: vfx-designer, art-director, environment-designer, ui-ux-designer, reviewer
- topic: value-contrast (secondary: light-colour, visual-hierarchy)
- lines: R06:163-170, R06:203, R06:262

### R06-F16 Fake saturation with an alpha halo behind an additive core
- principle: Additive colours wash toward white; put an alpha-blended, more saturated, darker layer behind the bright core so the core reads coloured.
- why: The eye judges colour from the surround; a saturated ring makes the white core feel pink.
- apply: Two emitters at the focus: a core with LightEmission near 1 and a slightly larger halo with LightEmission 0 in the saturated hue.
- check: Zoomed capture of the core: a visible coloured ring around the bright centre, not a white disc on grey.
- mistake: Using only additive layers, which lose saturation (R06:163-166).
- example: Pink alpha texture behind the white dot, "faking the saturation in the additive dot" (R06:171-174).
- roblox_fit: LightEmission 0 is normal blending, 1 is additive (particle-emitters.md L257).
- roles: vfx-designer, art-director, ui-ux-designer
- topic: light-colour (secondary: value-contrast)
- lines: R06:171-174

### R06-F17 Motion toward the focus leads the eye
- principle: To focus attention without relying only on glow, use motion: elements swirl from outside in toward the centre.
- why: The eye follows movement direction; converging motion points at the focus.
- apply: Particles spawn on a ring and move inward (negative Speed toward centre, or attachments orbit and contract); inward spiral during the build-up.
- check: Frame sequence: tracked particles move toward the focus point; no strong motion pointing away during the build.
- mistake: Relying only on brightness for focus (R06:175).
- example: Clouds spin in toward the centre "to make our eyes go towards the center even more" (R06:176, R06:189).
- roblox_fit: Negative Speed values make particles travel in reverse (particle-emitters.md L204); a Sphere Shape with inward emission can converge (particle-emitters.md L273-275) [inference for the inward setup].
- roles: vfx-designer, animator, film-director, environment-designer, ui-ux-designer
- topic: composition-focus (secondary: rhythm)
- lines: R06:175-176, R06:186-189

### R06-F18 Colour carries meaning
- principle: Colour codes gameplay: green healing, red damage. Poison could be green but uses purple to avoid confusion with healing.
- why: Players decide in a split second whether to stay or move; one colour must not mean both help and harm.
- apply: The art bible holds a colour-role table (heal, damage, poison, shield, buff, rare); every effect and UI cue uses it; no two opposite meanings share a hue.
- check: Review list of all effects by colour role; flag any hue used for both a good and a bad effect.
- mistake: Green poison next to green healing (R06:192-193).

### R06-F19 Strobing stays low and behind
- principle: A flashing strobe layer can add energy behind the focus, but at low opacity; the focus dot itself must not flash and strobe.

### R06-F20 Beginning, middle, end
- principle: Every effect tells a story with a beginning, a middle and an end; this lesson "put my effects farther than they could have gone".
- why: A clear sequence (cause, peak, result) lets players understand what happened.
- apply: Write the effect as a 3-beat story in the brief, e.g. "clouds swirl in (build), lightning goes unstable, burst (hit), candy rains and piles (result)". Sound cue cards follow the same three beats.
- check: Capture and label the three beats with start times; each beat is visible and distinct.
- mistake: An effect with no build or no resolve, so it just appears and stops.
- example: Story: cotton candy clouds swirl in, electricity from instability, burst, candy rain covering the floor (R06:292-295).
- roblox_fit: Script the beats as timed Emit calls or Enabled toggles; Enabled false stops new particles but existing ones live on (particle-emitters.md L490).

- topic: rhythm (secondary: vfx-reads-timing, weight-timing)

### R06-F21 The hit shows cause and effect
- principle: A hit is a slash or flash that tells the viewer something was struck and a result follows. Build energy until it becomes unstable, then hit.
- why: The hit is the beat that connects build-up to result; without it the result has no cause.
- apply: Concentrate the build elements (e.g. lightning spawn shape shrunk to the centre, count increased 3 to 6) then place one short flash on a chosen hit frame.
- check: Frame capture: exactly one peak frame (brightest) at the hit; the result starts after it.
- mistake: not stated
- example: Lightning duplicated, more spawned and pulled into the centre; flash on the hit frame (R06:215-216, R06:226-227, R06:253).
- roblox_fit: A one-frame flash is ParticleEmitter:Emit on a short-lifetime emitter; pair with a sound transient.

- topic: vfx-reads-timing (secondary: rhythm, exaggeration)

### R06-F23 After the hit, move the eye on
- principle: After the hit at the top, guide the eye down: the top calms (spreads out, stops moving, darker) and the bottom lights up.
- why: Movement and brightness pull attention; leaving both at the old spot keeps the eye there and makes the result noisy.
- apply: At the hit, stop spawning the build layers (clouds stop exactly at the hit, R06:269) and lower their brightness; raise brightness where the result plays.
- check: Value captures before and after the hit: the brightest region moves to the result area; no busy motion left at the old spot.
- mistake: "things lingering up top" after the hit (R06:234).
- example: Plan: keep it darker up top, light up the bottom area (R06:237).
- roblox_fit: Toggle Enabled at the hit time; existing particles still finish (particle-emitters.md L490) so keep their lifetime short.

### R06-F24 Time stages in tenths; each starts after its cause
- principle: Timing is tuned in 0.1-0.2 s steps. A result must start after its cause, not with it.
- why: Starting together reads as no cause; a small delay reads as consequence.
- apply: Source timeline: hit delayed by 0.1 s to start at 1.6 s (R06:232-233); rain tried at 2.0 s, set to 1.8 s (R06:277-278); floor candy starts at 2.2 s, after the falling candy (R06:325-326); build lightning from 0.5 s (R06:257); clouds spawn 1-2 s and stop at the hit (R06:270). At 30 fps 0.1 s = 3 frames.
- check: Frame-by-frame capture at 30 fps: list the start frame of each stage; each follows its cause by a few frames.
- mistake: "if they spawn at the same time, it's gonna be a little bit too soon" (R06:326).
- example: Rain "off by a small amount", moved 0.2 s earlier (R06:278).
- roblox_fit: Use task.delay or a timeline table in data (content as data); frame rate varies on phones, so time in seconds.
- roles: vfx-designer, animator, sound-designer, film-director
- topic: weight-timing (secondary: rhythm)

### R06-F25 Size changes must reach every emitter

- example: Everything doubled: scale 1.5 to 2, height to 500 (R06:239-247).

### R06-F27 Random rotation and size, no spin
- principle: Repeated objects need random spawn rotation on every axis and a size range, but no constant spin, or they look uniform ("like a bunch of fish").
- why: Identical orientation and size reveal the copy; constant rotation reads as unnatural for resting objects.
- apply: Rotation range full 360 with random sign; size range from 1 to 3x max (R06:340); RotSpeed 0 for objects at rest.
- check: Capture: no two neighbours look identical; count of matching orientations low.
- mistake: Spawned candy rotating and all the same scale (R06:315-316, R06:339).
- example: Spawn at 360 with chance of +1 and -1 on both axes (R06:317-318).
- roblox_fit: Rotation takes a min,max range and RotSpeed sets spin (particle-emitters.md L494-498); 2D particles rotate only in screen plane.

### R06-F28 Building a pile with lifetime, and keeping it inside the play area
