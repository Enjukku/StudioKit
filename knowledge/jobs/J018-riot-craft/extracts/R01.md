### R01-F01 Research before design
- principle: The first step of any concept is research: compile reference and check the design is culturally resonant and culturally sensitive.
- why: Reference anchors the theme in things players recognize; a sensitivity check prevents designs that offend or confuse.
- apply: Before prompting ChatGPT for any concept sheet, the agent writes a short reference list (real-world garments, props, motifs of the theme, e.g. ringmaster: red tailcoat, top hat, whip) and a sensitivity note; images go into the art bible moodboard only after that.
- check: The brief or reference-images request names its reference sources and a one-line sensitivity check; reviewer rejects a concept with no reference basis.
- mistake: Jumping straight to rendering (he notes he skips this only "in the interest of time", R01:74).
- example: Pulling "Lion Tamer Caitlyn" from a mug and widening it to a big-top ringmaster theme (R01:48-60).
- roblox_fit: Same; Roblox audiences are young, so sensitivity also feeds platform-policy (load the platform-policy skill for mature or franchise themes per AGENTS.md §2).

### R01-F02 Ideate on an in-game mannequin
- principle: Use an existing in-game model with the least bulky costume as a base to paint new ideas over, so iteration is fast and on-model.
- why: Proportions, rig and camera are already right; only the new idea changes.
- apply: Make a "mannequin" render of the base character in Blender (plain or least-bulky variant) from the game camera and use it as the ChatGPT paint-over / edit input for variants.
- check: Every variant concept image overlays the mannequin silhouette at the same scale; proportions match.
- mistake: Starting from a bulky variant that hides the body (he avoids Arctic Warfare for this reason, R01:70).
- example: Pool Party Caitlyn's light costume used as the base for the ringmaster idea.
- roblox_fit: Works well: a Roblox character render at the game camera from Studio or Blender is a cheap base. [inference]
- roles: art-director, reference-images, modeler

### R01-F03 Design to the existing rig
- principle: Before designing, list what the rig already supports (extra joint chains: coattails, hip pouch joints, a hair strand) and what it lacks; use the existing joints for new costume parts and avoid parts that need new joints unless the scope allows.
- why: New joints cost animation and tech time; reusing joints gives motion for free and keeps the team happy.
- apply: Modeler and art director read the rig sheet (rig-pipeline) first; concept prompts name the available dangling parts (e.g. "two coattails, one hair strand at the temple") and forbid parts with no joint (e.g. long hanging sleeves).
- check: Every moving costume part in the concept maps to a joint in the rig sheet; count unmapped parts = 0, or each is a logged scope request.
- mistake: Designing long hanging sleeves when "no joints currently exist on Caitlyn's rig for that" (R01:94-99).
- example: Lunar Wraith's two coattail chains become a ringmaster tuxedo's tails (R01:144-150, 240-243).
- roblox_fit: Roblox rigs use Motor6D or skinned Bones (`_api-reference/classes/Bone.yaml`, `art/modeling/skin-a-simple-mesh.md`); extra bones cost import and animation work, same tradeoff.

### R01-F04 Moving prop parts slide without clipping
- principle: A prop with independently moving pieces must be designed so each piece slides into the next and retracts away from the body.
- why: Animations reuse the same moving parts; a design that blocks them causes clipping in every clip.
- apply: Modeler builds a multi-part weapon (body, outer barrel, inner barrel, stock) as separate meshes whose cross-sections nest; the stock can shorten into the body in the firing pose.
- check: Play the fire/reload clip at game camera and frame-step; no part intersects the arms; clip_check.py or a capture per key frame.
- mistake: Decorations that stop the barrel sliding.
- example: Caitlyn's gun: large body, outer barrel, inner barrel all move; the stock shrinks into the gun so it does not clip the arms.
- roblox_fit: Separate MeshParts joined by Motor6D, or bones in one skinned mesh. [inference]
- roles: modeler, character-rigger, animator, reviewer

### R01-F05 Keep the iconic silhouette slot filled
- principle: A signature element (top hat) is part of the silhouette; a variant may replace or remove it, but must fill that silhouette area with something (headgear verticality, hairstyle), and it should not return in a silly, over-sized form when the character is serious.
- why: Players recognize the character by the outline; an empty slot makes the variant feel like someone else.
- apply: In concept prompts and model checks, list the base character's silhouette landmarks; each variant keeps or replaces each landmark.
- check: Black-fill silhouette of base and variant side by side at game camera; landmark areas are occupied in both.
- mistake: Removing the hat without giving the head height elsewhere; or "mad hattery" proportions.
- example: Lunar Wraith Caitlyn has no hat but vertical headgear and hairstyle "fill that element of her silhouette up"; the ringmaster gets a smaller fascinator-style hat.
- roblox_fit: Same; at phone size the head silhouette is one of few readable landmarks. [inference]

### R01-F06 Bold shapes and values over line and detail
- principle: For a far top-down game camera, think in bold shapes and value relationships, not line work or fine detail; form and value are paramount.
- why: At game scale, line and fine detail disappear; value and shape survive.
- apply: Concept and key-art prompts ask for flat value blocks; modelers prioritize big forms and readable value zones in materials before micro-detail.
- check: Grayscale capture at the game camera (look_check.py value); main parts separate by value with no detail.
- mistake: Getting "bogged down in finer detail" which "starts to muddy things" (R01:156, 188-190).
- example: Thumbnails done without line; he merges and paints rather than delineating.
- roblox_fit: Phone screens make the character even smaller than League's; value and shape matter more. [inference]
- roles: art-director, reference-images, modeler, environment-designer, vfx-designer, ui-ux-designer

### R01-F07 Upper body bright, lower body shaded down
- principle: Make the upper body (face, shoulders, chest, gold trim) higher in value than the lower body (pants, boots), which are "shaded down" and usually darker materials.
- why: The upper body then pops against the ground and carries the read; the legs do not compete for attention.
- apply: Material plan for every character: upper-body materials lighter/higher contrast, lower body darker; sound default [inference]: lower-body mean value at least ~15-20% darker than upper body in a grayscale capture.
- check: Grayscale render at game camera over the real ground; measure mean value of upper vs lower body masks with look_check.py.
- mistake: Equal brightness top to bottom, so the lower body draws "too much attention".
- example: Gold epaulets, lapel and face bright; pants and boots darker on the ringmaster.
- roblox_fit: Roblox ground colours vary per game; check against this game's real terrain. [inference]
- roles: art-director, modeler, reference-images, environment-designer, qa-lead, reviewer
- topic: value-contrast (secondary: visual-hierarchy)

### R01-F08 The source of power is the primary read
- principle: The character's source of power (the weapon) is the most visible part of the design; shrinking or thinning it risks players mistaking the character for another.
- why: Players identify allies and enemies by their primary read; it also tells them what the character does.
- apply: Weapons and signature tools keep their size and thickness in every variant; their value contrast is the highest on the character.
- check: Thumbnail-size and game-camera capture: the weapon is the first thing seen; black-fill silhouette shows it clearly; compare its screen area with the base.
- mistake: "If we were to reduce its size, reduce its thickness... they might mistake her for someone else."
- example: Caitlyn's "comically overstated" sniper rifle is her primary read.
- roblox_fit: On phones the held tool is often the only readable cue of class/role. [inference]

### R01-F09 Layer by garment, then merge

- check: The review render shows the combined character, not single parts.

### R01-F10 True to the character's core
- principle: Each design choice should represent who the character is; keep the "kernel" players love; avoid overly tropey or unnecessarily sexualized choices.
- why: Players are invested in the character; out-of-character choices break that investment.
- apply: Each concept brief states the character's core traits in one line; every added element is justified against it ("how would she dress if she took this job").
- check: Reviewer asks for each element: which trait does it express? Elements with no answer are cut.
- mistake: "Design choices that feel sort of out of place."
- example: Imagining how Caitlyn would style herself if she took a ringmaster job.
- roblox_fit: Young audience: keep designs non-sexualized (platform-policy). [inference]

### R01-F11 Avoid visual noise; gameplay first
- principle: Zoom out often and judge at a glance; a lot happens on screen at once (characters plus abilities), so avoid noise. Ask in order: does it look like the character, does it inform the theme, can players engage without extra thinking.
- why: Noise costs players attention needed for gameplay.
- apply: Every art review runs the three questions; detail is budgeted.
- check: Capture at game camera with typical ability VFX on; can a tester name the character in one glance? Count of small high-contrast details stays low.
- mistake: "A lot of visual noise and extra thinking to the experience."
- example: Skins must not get in the way of abilities being flung around.
- roblox_fit: Many players and effects on screen in Roblox; stronger need. [inference]
- roles: art-director, reference-images, modeler, vfx-designer, environment-designer, ui-ux-designer, reviewer, qa-lead

### R01-F12 Before/after: extend and sharpen to read
- principle: A soft feature that does not stick out from the body is weak; extend it, sharpen it and increase its area relative to the body so it is visible at a glance.
- why: At game scale only features that change the outline read.
- apply: Modeler exaggerates key features (shoulder pieces, hat brims) beyond the reference until they break the silhouette.
- check: Black-fill silhouette at game camera before and after; the feature must visibly change the outline.
- mistake: The soft epaulet that "doesn't stick out too far from her body".
- example: Epaulet extended and sharpened becomes much more visible.
- roblox_fit: Phone camera: exaggerate more. [inference]
- roles: modeler, art-director, reference-images, vfx-designer, reviewer

### R01-F13 Shape language from personality
- principle: Choose shapes from the character's personality: a dangerous character gets sharp, severe, bold geometric cuts; softer rounder shapes suit softer characters or lighter themes.
- why: Viewers read sharp shapes as danger and round shapes as friendly.
- apply: Each character brief states a shape language (sharp / round / square) and it applies to costume, prop, VFX and UI frames of that character.
- check: Reviewer counts dominant shape types in the silhouette; they match the stated language.
- mistake: Soft round shapes on a character meant to feel dangerous.
- example: Caitlyn: "not necessarily aggressive but dangerous if crossed"; sharp geometry.
