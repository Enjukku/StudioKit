## Findings

### R10-F01 Reference the overlap, not the parts
- principle: When a brief merges two ideas (samurai + fly fisher + wind), gather reference for where they overlap: an object that feels like both (katana and fishing pole), iconic costume pieces of the new idea (hat with hooks, waders), and poses from both worlds (fishing and sword fighting). If the exact pose does not exist, make your own reference so the result is not the same picture everyone else paints.
- why: The fusion is the design; reference for each idea alone gives a costume swap, not a merged fantasy. Own reference avoids copying a common internet pose.
- apply: In the reference-images brief, list three overlap columns (object, costume, pose) and ask ChatGPT for a moodboard per column before any key art. For a unique pose, pose the rig or a mannequin in Blender from the game camera and render it as the pose reference [inference: our stand-in for "taking your own photos"].
- check: The moodboard has at least one image per column that shows both ideas at once; the chosen pose is not a stock pose found in the first search results.
- mistake: Painting "the exact same thing someone else is painting" (R10:89-91).
- example: Looking for where a fishing pole and a katana "feel" alike, plus fishing and sword-fighting poses (R10:73-83).
- roblox_fit: No change; Blender pose renders replace photos.
- roles: reference-images, art-director, modeler, animator, marketing, vfx-designer

### R10-F02 Compose inside the final frame and its UI
- principle: Start from the delivery template (card UI, crop marks, bleed) and compose around it from the first sketch; you may hide it while working, but keep its effect on the composition in mind. The UI counts as part of the composition.
- why: Elements placed under the UI or in the bleed are lost or crowded when printed; the frame changes where the eye goes.
- apply: Before any key art, icon or thumbnail, load the platform frame as an overlay: Roblox thumbnail 16:9, icon square with corner rounding, plus any title or button overlays; for in-game shots, the HUD at phone size. Keep the face and focal object out of the overlay zones.
- check: Render with the overlay on: no focal element under UI, crop or rounding; repeat with tools/art/thumb_check.py at player size.
- mistake: Composing on a blank canvas and fitting UI later [inference].
- example: A Riftbound Yasuo card template with UI, crop and bleed areas used from the start (R10:97-108).
- roblox_fit: Phone HUD and Roblox top bar take screen space; ScreenGui.ScreenInsets defines the safe area (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/tutorials/use-case-tutorials/ui/create-hud-meters.md:60-70). Store-page frame sizes [unverified].

### R10-F03 Scribble options, present, pick, iterate
- principle: The sketch phase is messy scribbles that find the layout; do several, present them as options, the art director picks one and gives feedback, then go back and forth. The first composition is often "trash" but holds a nugget to learn from. The sketch is what you show the team.
- why: Comparing options is cheaper than fixing a polished wrong idea; the director chooses before the cost is spent.
- apply: Generate 3 rough, clearly different composition options [sound default; source says "a few"/"a number"] with reference-images at low detail; present with one line of intent each and the maker's own pick; polish only the chosen one.
- check: The hand-back has more than one option, they differ in layout (not only colour), and the polish started after a recorded pick.
- mistake: Going straight from the first idea to polish (he does it only "for the sake of time", R10:158-166).
- example: He skips options for the one-hour demo and says he normally shares several (R10:158-166).

### R10-F04 Keep the character's identity reads
- principle: Any new version keeps the character's fantasy (wind samurai), the silhouette features that are always preserved (big bushy ponytail) and the primary reads (sword, ponytail, very wide pants, sometimes the large shoulder pauldron). If the pose hides one read, accept it only if the others are all there.
- why: Players recognise a character by a few big shapes, not details; losing them breaks recognition.
- apply: Each character's art bible entry lists its 3-5 primary reads; every variant brief, model and key pose names which reads are visible. Modeler keeps those shapes oversized; rigger and animator keep them from being hidden in idle and key poses.
- check: Black-fill silhouette at the game camera: list the primary reads you can see; at least all but one present (source drops one).
- mistake: Losing a defining silhouette feature in a variant [inference from "we usually want to preserve", R10:121-123].
- example: Pauldron not visible in the fishing pose, all other notes hit (R10:126-130).
- roblox_fit: At phone size and a third-person camera only the biggest reads survive; prefer fewer, larger reads [inference].

### R10-F05 Motion in a still
- principle: Show wind and movement in a still image with swirling, purposeful lines (wind lines, fishing line) that create flow; pick a few good lines and commit to them.
- why: Curved flow lines imply direction and speed and give the image dynamism; the eye follows them.
- apply: In key art, icons and thumbnails, ask for 2-3 sweeping motion lines (wind trails, cloth, a line or rope) that run through the focal point; in Roblox use Trail or Beam for the same curves.
- check: Trace the main lines on the render: they form one continuous flow through the focal area and do not cross each other chaotically.
- mistake: Too many uncommitted lines [inference from "I'm just gonna try to commit to a couple", R10:545-547].
- example: Swirling wind lines and fishing line around Yasuo (R10:132-134, R10:543-547).
- roblox_fit: Trails and Beams render curves cheaply; count them against the particle budget on phones [unverified numbers].

### R10-F06 Design the premium tier around separable finishes
- principle: For the highest tier, choose elements that can each get a different finish (foil, gloss, matte, texture), e.g. glossy wet fish, foil scales, high-gloss line; mixed finishes make it feel premium.
- why: Distinct material treatments per element signal value and rarity physically.
- apply: For premium cosmetics, cards or reward frames, split the asset into separately treatable parts: separate MeshParts or materials (shiny Neon/Glass vs matte), a UIGradient shimmer on frames, a Highlight or particle sparkle on one element only.
- check: List each element and its finish; at least 2 finishes differ; the premium version reads as different from the standard at thumbnail size.
- mistake: Painting one flat surface that can only get one treatment [inference].
- example: Fish glossy and wet, scales foil, fishing line high gloss (R10:557-568).
- roblox_fit: No custom shaders, so finishes come from materials, SurfaceAppearance, UIGradient, Highlight (max 255 visible per client: knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/effects/highlighting.md:37) and particles.

### R10-F07 Composition before rendering
- principle: Do not jump into detail early; composition is trial and error and can be frustrating, but no amount of drawing or rendering saves a composition that does not work.
- why: Detail is "the part that looks pretty"; it hides layout problems and costs time on the wrong thing.
- apply: Gate: no material, texture or detail pass until a flat or grayscale blockout of the composition (scene, key art, level shot) is approved.
- check: The approved blockout render exists and is dated before the first detail render.
- mistake: "It's very tempting to go into rendering in detail as soon as possible" (R10:168-170).
- example: He sketches and solves layout before painting (R10:168-176).
- roblox_fit: Same for levels: blockout from the game camera first.

### R10-F08 Composition covers everything in the frame
- principle: Composition is how all elements are laid out (character, background, effects) plus the values and colours and how they work together; the UI is part of it too.
- why: The eye reacts to the whole frame; one craft's change shifts every other craft's balance.
- apply: Review composition on a full capture with effects, lighting and HUD on, not per-asset renders.
- check: Integrated capture at the game camera with UI on; judge focal point and balance there.
- mistake: Judging a character or effect alone and missing how it fights the background or UI [inference].
- example: Definition given while sketching Yasuo (R10:178-186).
- roblox_fit: Phones show HUD, many players and effects at once; the integrated capture is the only honest view.

### R10-F09 Cool plus offbeat, justified by lore
- principle: A fresh variant juxtaposes the character's cool core with an offbeat twist by swapping one iconic item (fly-fishing hat instead of the ronin hat, fishing pole for the sword), and the twist is justified by what the character already does (he makes wind walls, so he can fish with wind).
- why: The contrast is fun; the lore link makes it feel right, not random.
- apply: For skins, events and limited items, write the twist as "keep X core, swap item Y for Z because of ability/lore W".
- check: The brief states the swapped item and the lore reason in one line; the core silhouette (F04) is still there.
- mistake: A random theme with no link to the character [inference].

### R10-F10 Cohesion for the set, a purposeful break for the top tier
- principle: Normal cards follow guides that keep the whole product cohesive; the rare artist cards are encouraged to break the mold in style, so side by side they look "wildly different", which gives each one a specialness beyond its rarity number.
- why: When everything is cohesive, nothing stands out; a deliberate break signals rarity.
- apply: The art bible defines the standard look; a rarity tier may have its own documented exception (style, finish, effect) decided by the art director and logged as a decision.
- check: Standard items pass the art bible check; top-tier items are visibly different and the difference is in the bible, not accidental.
- mistake: Every tier looking the same, so rare feels only like a bigger number [inference from R10:259-263].
- example: Riftbound overnumbered alternate-art cards in each artist's own style (R10:30-36, R10:213-225).
- roblox_fit: Owner decides any style exception; paid-random rarity also needs platform-policy review.
- roles: art-director, live-ops-designer, progression-designer, marketing, game-designer, reviewer

### R10-F12 Values first, colour second
- principle: Define form in black and white, then add colour in a way that keeps the values (he uses an overlay layer); a loose value structure makes colour exploration fast.
- why: Value carries form and read; separating the two problems makes each simpler and colour cannot break the read.
- apply: Review grayscale renders or captures first; when choosing colours, compare the grayscale of the colour version with the approved grayscale.
- check: Grayscale of the coloured render matches the approved value version (look_check.py value measure); focal area still has the strongest contrast.
- mistake: Picking colours that silently change the value structure [inference].
- example: Grayscale forms, then overlay colour keeping values (R10:279-285, R10:506-527).

### R10-F13 Chisel planes, avoid soft everywhere
- principle: Soft airbrush gradients are an easy start but make everything look soft; move to sharper, flat strokes that carve planes so the work feels deliberate.
- why: Clear planes describe form and light; uniform softness reads as mushy.
- apply: Modeler: clear plane changes and bevels over blobby smooth surfaces; texture and shading with defined light/shadow edges; ask reference-images for "planar, defined light and shadow shapes".
- check: In the grayscale render, light and shadow shapes have clear edges on main forms; no area is one smooth gradient.
- mistake: "it always makes everything look super soft" (R10:295).
- example: Switching from airbrush to a flat brush (R10:291-301).
- roblox_fit: Phones at distance blur soft gradients further; defined planes survive [inference].

### R10-F17 Plan the read order and lead the eye
- principle: A still image has many goals (fishing, swordsman, costume, hidden face, wind); settle them by choosing the focal points and the primary, secondary and tertiary reads, then use purposeful swooping lines to move the eye around and place small story details along that path.
- why: Without a set order every goal competes and nothing reads.
- apply: Each key art, scene shot or screen brief lists primary, secondary, tertiary reads; flow lines (rope, path, trail, light shaft) point toward the primary.
- check: Blur or shrink the capture to thumbnail size: the first thing seen is the stated primary; reads appear in the stated order.
- mistake: Trying to "say a lot of things" equally in one still (R10:496).
- example: Fishing line and wind lines circling the eye around Yasuo (R10:500-506).
- roblox_fit: Phone screen is small; plan fewer reads [inference].

### R10-F18 Skin changes palette; canon keeps it
- principle: A skin uses a different palette and hair colours to differentiate it from the base; an in-canon scene of the base character keeps the base palette.

### R10-F21 Diagnose by layer; "not happy" may mean "not done"
- principle: Being unhappy mid-work does not mean the image is bad; it may not be done. When it does not work, ask why by layer: composition (which part?), drawing, colours, shape, flow; fix one at a time, knowing a fix can create another problem. Learn from failed images.
- why: Named causes give a fix; a vague "it's off" does not.
- apply: Review notes name the layer (composition, value, colour, shape, flow, detail) and one fix each; re-check others after each fix.
- check: Every review finding names a layer and a fix.
- mistake: Abandoning or polishing without knowing what fails.
- example: Questions he asks himself during the piece (R10:883-901).

### R10-F22 The project's needs first
- principle: Feedback comes from people solving other problems; the artist's job is to fulfil all those needs in the work. Project first, "looking good is the cherry on top". Painful feedback often signals a gap in goals; shared, transparently stated goals reduce friction. Communicate intentions and question feedback.
- why: Art that ignores the project's goals has no use to it.
- apply: Briefs state the job of the asset; hand-backs explain intent and how each piece of feedback was met or why questioned.
- check: Hand-back maps each feedback item to done or questioned.
- mistake: Artists who cannot take feedback openly (R10:660-664).
- example: Team setting vs art for yourself (R10:666-676).
