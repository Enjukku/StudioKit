## Findings

### R12-F01 Brand first, hidden story second
- principle: The theme/brand must read at once; a deeper story (Champions 2024: a line graph of the player's ups and downs, a rose that opens after 24 kills to release a diamond) is a second layer that rewards those who look, and must not compete with the first read. "The brand is number one, the story is number two."
- why: Players read the main identity in a glance; a hidden layer gives depth and discovery without clutter.
- apply: Brief every themed asset with two lines: (1) the one-glance read (theme, rarity, role); (2) an optional hidden story element sized as a small/medium detail. In reference-images prompts, state the main read first and the story element as a secondary detail. Progression reveals (the rose opening after N kills) are a cheap way to deliver the story over time [inference: maps to an upgrade/tier reveal in Roblox].
- check: At thumbnail size (thumb_check.py) and game camera, can a reviewer name the theme without explanation? Is the story element invisible at that size but findable at close view?
- mistake: Making the story so obvious it becomes the main read and crowds the brand.
- example: Champions 2024 Vandal: line-graph shape, centre rose that opens after 24 kills; melee lines from Korean tiger drawings.
- roblox_fit: Phones show small items; hidden layers survive only as close-up or inspect details (shop preview, inventory 3D view). [unverified]

### R12-F02 The concept is a canvas for the next crafts
- principle: The concept artist creates the base idea and leaves room for 3D, animation and VFX to add their craft; motion/VFX sketches in concept only show the feeling to inspire specialists, not to dictate.
- why: Downstream crafts own their decisions; over-specified concepts block better solutions.
- apply: Reference images and concept briefs fix the idea, silhouette and colour roles; they mark animation and VFX opportunities as "intent" notes (what should move, what should glow) and leave timing and technique to the animator and VFX designer.
- check: Does the concept hand-off name where motion/effects go without fixing frame timings or particle settings? Did each downstream craft get an open question rather than a fixed answer?
- mistake: The concept dictating rules for the whole team.
- example: "I'm creating a canvas for the rest of the team"; sketches of a knife opening (Prime), sword sliding forward (Prelude to Chaos) to inspire VFX/animators.
- roblox_fit: Same in our pipeline: reference-images sets target, specialists build in Blender/Studio.

### R12-F03 Four-step concept process
- principle: Every concept goes through research (many references), initial sketches, refinement of the chosen sketch, then a final render.
- why: Separates idea search from polish so polish is not spent on weak ideas.
- apply: reference-images flow: moodboard/reference pull, then 4-6 rough options, owner/art-director pick, then one refined sheet, then key render. Do not polish before the pick.
- check: Count stages in the job record; no refined render exists without a recorded pick of a rough.
- mistake: Jumping to render on the first idea [inference].
- example: Zeus Phantom: research on Zeus symbols, line sketch, cleanup, colour and shading.

### R12-F04 Generate directions by shifting the theme and giving the object a fiction
- principle: Briefs are a few words; turn them into many directions by moving the theme to the future or the past and inventing what the object really is (a gun made by cyclopes; a gun that is a small lab producing thunderbolts; no bullets, a battery of energy).

- apply: For each themed asset, write 3+ direction one-liners (past, present, future; "what is it really?") before prompting reference images; pick one and let the fiction drive parts (e.g. glass window shows the energy core).
- check: Does each rough option have a one-line fiction? Can every major part be explained by it?
- mistake: Only one literal reading of the theme [inference].
- example: Zeus power locked in a gun that is "a small laboratory or like a facility which creates thunderbolts"; magazine as a battery.

### R12-F05 One message, one symbol, before drawing
- principle: Before drawing, plan what the design says: one clean, simple message and its best visual sign. Look at letterforms of the name for shapes (Z in "Zeus" looks like a thunderbolt, so does the S).

- apply: Write the message and the symbol in the brief ("Zeus = thunderbolt"); test the name's letterforms for a logo or motif; use that symbol as the motif for F09.

- example: Thunderbolt chosen as Zeus's sign; Z and S of the name read as thunderbolts.

### R12-F06 Cosmetics never cost gameplay
- principle: In a competitive game, a skin must not distract or take more screen room in first person, and must not give a disadvantage; gameplay comes first.
- why: Players pay for cosmetics; a skin that blocks view or confuses is a paid disadvantage.
- apply: For any cosmetic (weapon skin, trail, aura, pet), set a screen-footprint limit in the brief and keep the base proportions; VFX on cosmetics stays below combat-effect brightness [inference].
- check: Capture at the game camera on a phone-size viewport with and without the cosmetic: compare covered screen area and whether combat cues stay visible.
- mistake: "Radically changing" the base item.
- example: "It shouldn't take a lot of room on your screen on the 1P"; "you shouldn't pay for the disadvantage."
- roblox_fit: Third-person/top cameras show cosmetics on many players at once; keep them from hiding hits and enemies. [unverified]

### R12-F07 Keep the recognition features of the base object
- principle: Each base object has 2-3 features that identify it (Vandal: banana magazine biggest, gas tube second, rear sight third; Phantom: a straight body with a straight centre line, the stock says nothing). Keep those; "the rest might be changed, and no one notice it." Check both the side/third-person view and first-person view (Vandal reads by a diamond centre shape, Phantom by a rectangle). Proportions dictate every other part.
- why: Players identify item type by a few big features; variants stay recognisable.
- apply: For any item family with variants (weapon skins, pet tiers, building upgrades), list the ranked recognition features of the base in the art bible; every variant keeps them and the base proportions; free areas are where the theme goes.
- check: Black-fill silhouette of variant vs base at the game camera (look_check.py silhouette): ranked features still present; a reviewer names the base type from the silhouette.
- mistake: Reskins that change the identifying parts so the type no longer reads.
- example: Vandal: banana mag, gas tube, rear sight; Phantom: straight line, centre carries it.
- roblox_fit: Third-person camera matches the 3P/side view he names; phones need the big features bigger. Roblox tools also have an inventory icon (TextureId, knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/players/tools.md:23) that must read the same way.

### R12-F08 Shoulder line and bottom line
- principle: Like car design's "character line" / "shoulder line", find the one main line along the top-side of the object first, then the bottom character line; together they create most of the character and the right silhouette.

### R12-F09 Repeat one story motif at several scales

### R12-F10 Direction makes dynamics
- principle: A box reads static, a triangle reads dynamic because its slopes show direction; tilt the motif to create direction and then support that direction with other lines.

- mistake: Static, boxy forms for an object meant to feel fast.
- example: Box vs triangle; thunderbolt tilted to create direction.

### R12-F11 Kiki vs Bouba
- principle: People call sharp shapes "Kiki" and soft shapes "Bouba"; sharp, straight lines read aggressive and create velocity, sloped soft lines read gentle. To make it more aggressive, stick shapes out more along the same line direction.
- why: Shape-meaning link is near universal (1930s experiment as he describes it).
- apply: Set shape language per role in the art bible: heroes/hazards/enemies sharp; friendly/safe/cute soft. Sound and UI follow the same mapping (sharp transients vs soft) [inference].
- check: Silhouette review: ratio of sharp corners to curves matches the intended mood; a reviewer labels mood from black fill.
- mistake: Mixed signals: soft shapes on threats or spikes on friendly items [inference].
- example: Added straight lines and protruding parts; "it looks pretty aggressive already."

### R12-F12 Lines must lead somewhere and correlate
- principle: "All lines, they have to go somewhere and they have to create like a visual flow." Pick a point and run several lines toward it; the brain loves correlation between lines because it predicts and feels stable.
- why: Correlated lines are easy to read and make an object feel designed.
- apply: In Blender/reference prompts, align panel lines, cuts and edges to 1-2 shared vanishing points or parallel to the shoulder line; same for environment kits and UI layouts (aligned edges).
- check: Edge-only render: extend main lines; do they meet in 1-2 points or run parallel? Count stray lines that go nowhere.
- mistake: Random cuts and panel lines with no shared direction [inference].
- example: Lines on top of the Phantom converge to one point to find a new part of the silhouette.
- roblox_fit: Applies to level layouts too (leading lines to goals). [inference]

### R12-F13 Detail where the camera distorts least
- principle: Put the most interesting part on the side closest to the camera and least distorted (the centre/rear of a first-person weapon, e.g. a glass visor); the front is most distorted, so no fine detail there: two cutouts at the front read as two lines in first person.
- why: Perspective compresses far parts; detail there is lost or becomes noise.
- apply: Decide the game camera first, then place focal detail in the zone with the largest screen area; keep far or foreshortened areas to big simple shapes.
- check: Capture at the actual game camera; zoom on the far parts: do small features merge into lines or noise? Remove them if so.
- mistake: Two small cutouts on the muzzle that "will look like two lines" in 1P.
- example: Silencer is the hardest part: simple and readable, front is most deformed.
- roblox_fit: For third-person/top cameras the least-distorted, largest area is the top and side facing the camera; tiny details on far sides are lost on phones. [inference]

### R12-F14 Functional elements make it believable
- principle: Keep buttons, dials, mode switches, grip shapes and soft parts (a rubber stock pad so the shoulder does not hurt): the brain wants to know how to use an object; usability brings personality and believability.
- why: Signs of use let viewers imagine holding it.
- apply: Each prop/weapon/machine gets 1-3 functional details (switch, dial, grip, pad) placed in the focal zone; split materials along a clean straight line where function changes (hard vs rubber).
- check: Can a reviewer say how the object is held and operated from one render? Material split lines are straight/aligned?
- mistake: Pure decoration with no functional parts [inference].
- example: Buttons kept; front grip moved for line flow; rubber stock part; thunderbolt trigger with a cut-out kept comfortable for the finger.
- roblox_fit: Functional details must be medium-size to survive phone view. [inference]

### R12-F15 Structural support reads heavy, not fragile
- principle: When a cut or motif removes mass, add structural/technical support elements so the object reads heavy and less fragile.

### R12-F18 The first concept is a warm-up
- principle: The first day on a theme is hardest: one sketch in a full day; on day two about six sketches, because the theme is understood. The first result is "option number one", not the final.
- why: Understanding the theme accelerates later options.
- apply: Plan concept budgets with a slow first option and faster later ones; never judge the theme by option one alone; ask for more options after the first.
- check: Job plan includes at least 2 option rounds; the first option is labelled warm-up.
- mistake: Treating the first concept as the answer [inference].
- example: "First day I can spend a full day only on one sketch... second day, I can make like six sketches."

### R12-F19 Graphic-design fundamentals are universal
- principle: Contrast, balance, alignment, hierarchy, proximity, scale and proportion, movement make any image balanced: object, character or illustration alike.

### R12-F28 Space contrast like letter spacing
