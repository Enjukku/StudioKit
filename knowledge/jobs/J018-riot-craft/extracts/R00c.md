## Findings

### R00c-F01 Real-time effects are built for cost
- principle: Game effects run in real time, so they must be built far more efficiently than pre-rendered film effects; how an effect is built needs as much thought as how it looks.
- why: The effect competes with the rest of the frame for the same budget every frame; an expensive effect costs frame rate for every player who sees it.
- apply: Plan each effect with the fewest emitters, particles and textures that keep the read; state a budget in the effect spec (emitter count, max live particles, texture count) before building. [inference] default: one ParticleEmitter per read layer (F04), no more than three layers for a common ability.
- check: Count emitters, Beams and Trails in the effect; in a Studio play-test with the expected number of simultaneous casts, watch frame time (performance skill tools) versus the same scene without the effect.
- mistake: Building like film (many layers, high counts) and finding it unshippable on phones.
- example: Intro contrast of real-time game VFX with pre-rendered film/cinematic effects.
- roblox_fit: Phones and many players casting at once make this stricter; Roblox has no custom shaders, so effects are made from ParticleEmitter, Beam, Trail, meshes and lights (effects/index.md; effects/particle-emitters.md). Exact particle caps per device: [unverified].
- roles: vfx-designer, architect, feature-builder, qa-lead
- topic: pipeline-tech (secondary: vfx-reads-timing)

### R00c-F02 No standard recipe; the read is what matters
- principle: There is no standard way to build an effect; the same effect can be made with camera-facing cards, meshes, trails or anything else. What matters is that the finished effect works.
- why: The player sees only the result, not the technique.
- apply: In the effect spec, describe the read (what the player must see first, second, third; timing) and then choose the cheapest Roblox piece per layer: ParticleEmitter (cards), MeshPart with a texture, Beam, Trail, light.
- check: Review the capture at game camera against the spec's read, not against a technique checklist.
- mistake: Locking onto one technique because it is familiar when another gives the read cheaper.
- example: "I might make the same effect using camera-facing cards, meshes, trails" (R00c:3482-3486).
- roblox_fit: Roblox's equivalents: ParticleEmitter (cards, flipbooks: effects/particle-emitters.md:397-415), Beam (effects/beams.md), Trail (effects/trails.md), MeshPart.
- roles: vfx-designer, reviewer
- topic: pipeline-tech

### R00c-F03 What a particle emitter is
- principle: Effects are mostly particle systems: an emitter is an invisible point that fires sprites or models; its options range from one still image to a stream that reacts to gravity and collides.
- why: Knowing the range lets an agent use one emitter for a static glow and another for a moving spray instead of building custom code.
- apply: Use one ParticleEmitter with Rate 0 and a single Emit, or a long Lifetime and Speed 0, for a "single image that sits in one spot" [inference]; use Acceleration and Drag for gravity-like motion (effects/particle-emitters.md:206).
- check: Inspect emitter properties via Studio MCP; capture frames to confirm the particle motion matches the spec.
- mistake: not stated.
- example: Water stream with gravity and collision vs a single image held in place.
- roblox_fit: Roblox particles have Acceleration, Drag and wind (effects/particle-emitters.md:206, 267-269); particle collision with world geometry is not documented in the mirror [unverified] - fake splashes with a second emitter at the hit point [inference].
- roles: vfx-designer, feature-builder
- topic: pipeline-tech
- lines: R00c:3516-3542

### R00c-F04 Primary, secondary, tertiary reads
- principle: An effect has a primary, secondary and tertiary read, and the eye follows them in that order. Design each layer for its rank.
- why: The eye goes to the strongest contrast first; a clear ranking means the player gets the gameplay meaning (where it is, what it is) before the flavour.
- apply: Write the three reads into every effect spec and reference-image prompt: primary = gameplay core (location, hit area), secondary = theme (what element), tertiary = sparkle/support. Give the primary the highest brightness and contrast.
- check: Blur or shrink the capture to thumbnail size (look_check.py thumbnail) and grayscale it: the primary must still be the first thing seen; then hide the tertiary layer and confirm the effect still reads.
- mistake: All layers equal in brightness and size, so the eye does not know where to look.
- example: Fireball: head (primary), ribbon tail (secondary), sparks (tertiary).
- roblox_fit: Small phone screen and third-person camera make the primary read even more important; only the primary survives at small size [inference].
- roles: vfx-designer, art-director, reviewer, reference-images, animator, ui-ux-designer
- topic: vfx-reads-timing (secondary: visual-hierarchy)
- lines: R00c:3551-3560

### R00c-F05 Primary read: bright core plus moving shell
- principle: The primary read must catch the eye first: use something bright (a glow texture on a camera-facing card), then wrap it in a shape with a moving texture so it feels in motion and burning.
- why: Brightness draws the eye; scrolling texture adds life so the core does not look like a static sticker.
- apply: Core = ParticleEmitter with a soft glow texture, LightEmission high (docs suggest 1 for grayscale textures, effects/particle-emitters.md:57), locked to the projectile. Shell = half-sphere MeshPart (made in Blender) with a fire texture that animates (flipbook particle or a texture offset driven in script [inference]).
- check: Frame captures at 30 fps: the core is the brightest value in the effect (value check), and the shell's texture visibly moves between frames.
- mistake: A core that is no brighter than its tail, so the head does not lead.
- example: Fireball head = glow card plus half-sphere mesh with scrolling fire texture.
- roblox_fit: No custom shaders, so "scrolling texture on a mesh" needs a flipbook (effects/particle-emitters.md:397-415), a Beam with TextureSpeed (_api-reference/classes/Beam.yaml:500), or scripted texture offset [inference].
- roles: vfx-designer, art-director
- topic: vfx-reads-timing (secondary: value-contrast)
- lines: R00c:3558-3580

### R00c-F06 Secondary read: thematic detail that sells the idea
- principle: The secondary read carries the theme: details that sell "this is fire". A ribbon trailing from the head with one horizontally tiling texture scrolling along it does this.
- why: The core tells where; the secondary tells what kind, without competing with the core.
- apply: Use a Trail attached to the projectile (Lifetime short; effects/trails.md:154) or a Beam, with one tiling texture; TextureLength sets repeat (_api-reference/classes/Trail.yaml:437); Beam TextureSpeed scrolls it.
- check: In captures, the tail is clearly secondary: lower brightness than the head, readable as "fire" when the head is masked.
- mistake: A tail as bright and wide as the head, which splits focus.
- example: Ribbon emitted from the fireball head with a left-to-right tiling scrolling texture.
- roblox_fit: Trail and Beam textures and modes: effects/beams.md:182-189, effects/trails.md:61-65.
- roles: vfx-designer
- topic: vfx-reads-timing
- lines: R00c:3580-3598

### R00c-F07 Tertiary detail: few, low priority
- principle: Tertiary pieces (sparks) are lower-priority shapes that support the theme; add only a few, or they steal focus and muddy the effect's clarity.
- why: Many small bright bits create noise that competes with the primary.
- apply: Keep tertiary emitters at low Rate and small Size; fade them with Transparency over lifetime (avoids popping, effects/particle-emitters.md:183). [inference] default: tertiary particles never brighter or larger than the secondary.
- check: Count tertiary particles in a frame; compare the read with and without them at thumbnail size: if clarity drops with them on, cut the count.
- mistake: "make sure not to add too many or they'll steal the focus and muddy up the clarity" (R00c:3606-3611).
- example: Sparks coming off the fireball.
- roblox_fit: Fewer particles also helps phone performance; many players casting at once multiplies the count.
- roles: vfx-designer, art-director, environment-designer, reviewer
- topic: vfx-reads-timing (secondary: visual-hierarchy)

### R00c-F08 Restraint: if everything is loud, nothing is
- principle: The VFX artist's role is to communicate gameplay, not to make every effect big and flashy. If every effect is epic and loud, none stands out; it all comes down to clear contrast.
- why: Importance is perceived by contrast with what is around it; a uniform level of spectacle flattens hierarchy.
- apply: Rank all effects in the game (basic attack, ability, ultimate, reward) and assign size, brightness, duration and sound loudness by rank in the effect spec; only the top tier may fill the screen. Same rule for sound loudness and UI celebration [inference].
- check: Put several effects of different ranks on screen together in one Studio capture; the top-ranked one must clearly dominate, and low-rank ones must not compete.
- mistake: "It can be tempting to make every effect big and flashy" (R00c:3630-3632).
- example: "if you make every effect epic and loud, nothing will be epic and loud" (R00c:3650-3654).
- roblox_fit: Many players and effects on a small phone screen make this critical; restraint also saves performance.
- roles: vfx-designer, art-director, game-designer, sound-designer, ui-ux-designer, reviewer, orchestrator
- topic: visual-hierarchy (secondary: vfx-reads-timing, value-contrast)
- lines: R00c:3626-3654

### R00c-F09 Game-wide shape language for effects
- principle: Standardize certain shapes for certain meanings across the whole game, so players understand what an effect does; shape matters most when effects share similar colours.
- why: Players learn the code once and read every new effect by it; shape survives where colour fails.
- apply: Write an effect shape table in the art bible (e.g. plus signs = healing, circles = shields/protection, spiky angular shapes = damage, per the source) and require every effect spec and VFX reference image to follow it.
- check: Grayscale capture: identify effect type by shape alone; black-fill silhouette of key frames should still say heal / shield / damage.
- mistake: Effects with similar colours and no shape distinction, which players cannot tell apart.
- example: League: plus signs for health, protective circles for shields, spiky shapes for damage.
- roblox_fit: Small screens favour bold simple shapes; particle textures carry the shape (effects/particle-emitters.md:57).
- roles: vfx-designer, art-director, ui-ux-designer, game-designer, modeler, reference-images
- topic: shape-silhouette (secondary: vfx-reads-timing, style-identity)

### R00c-F10 Choose colour for what it must say
- principle: Effect colour is chosen by what the effect communicates: which team, what damage type, or what emotional tone.
- why: Colour is a fast code once players learn it, and saturation and hue carry mood.
- apply: In the art bible, give each colour a role (team colours, element colours, mood); effect specs cite the role, not a free choice.
- check: Hue check on captures (look_check.py palette): effects of the same role share the role hue; team colours stay distinct from each other.
- mistake: Picking colours because they look nice, so the colour carries no meaning.
- example: Red vs green lasers (team), element spells (damage type), oversaturated violent fire (tone).
- roblox_fit: Team colour must stay readable on small screens and under game Lighting; check under the real Lighting/Atmosphere [inference].
- roles: vfx-designer, art-director, ui-ux-designer, game-designer, environment-designer
- topic: light-colour

### R00c-F11 Do not over-colour effects
- principle: In a game with hundreds of effects, be careful not to put too much colour into a single effect; it is easy to go crazy with colour.
- why: Multi-colour effects lose the colour code and add noise across the screen.
- apply: [inference] default: one main hue plus a lighter/darker version of it per effect; a second hue only for a defined role.
- check: Count distinct hues in an effect capture (look_check.py palette); more than the spec allows is a fail.
- mistake: "It is easy to go crazy with the colors" (R00c:3768-3770).
- example: League's hundreds of effects.
- roblox_fit: Many players' effects overlap on one phone screen, so colour discipline per effect matters more.
- roles: vfx-designer, art-director, reviewer
- topic: light-colour (secondary: visual-hierarchy)

### R00c-F12 Effect colour analogous to the caster
- principle: Choose an effect colour analogous to (next to on the colour wheel) the caster model's colour, so players can easily identify who cast the spell.
- why: Colour links effect to source.
- apply: VFX spec takes the character's main colours from the model/art bible and picks an adjacent hue; the reference-image prompt for the effect includes the character for colour matching.
- check: Capture caster plus effect together: the hue of the effect sits close to a main model hue (palette check).
- mistake: Effects whose colour has no link to the caster, so it is unclear who did it.
- example: Yasuo's wind wall in light blue; pink wind for Star Guardian and Sacred Sword Janna skins.
- roblox_fit: Applies to skins too: a recoloured skin should recolour its effects [inference].
- roles: vfx-designer, art-director, modeler, character-rigger, reference-images

### R00c-F13 Explosion timing: fast start, slow finish
- principle: An explosion is basically a circle that grows to max size; linear growth feels flat. Make it reach size very fast at the start and slow down as it reaches max size.
- why: Fast-out then ease gives energy and impact; linear motion feels mechanical.
- apply: For a growing ring/sphere, use a Size NumberSequence that jumps most of the way in the first part of lifetime and eases at the end (effects/particle-emitters.md:130-167), or a TweenService ease-out on a MeshPart [inference]. [inference] default: about 70-80% of max size within the first 20-25% of the duration.
- check: Sample size per frame at 30 fps: the size curve is steep at the start and flat at the end, not a straight line.
- mistake: Linear growth (the "if it was linear" demo, R00c:3812).
- example: Explosion as an expanding circle, linear vs eased.
- roblox_fit: Same ease curve for UI pop-ins and reward bursts [inference].
- roles: vfx-designer, animator, ui-ux-designer, sound-designer
- topic: weight-timing (secondary: vfx-reads-timing)

### R00c-F14 Time the build-up too
- principle: The build-up before an explosion can be timed for interest as well; it is also a good sound moment.
- why: Anticipation makes the release feel bigger, and sound and picture hitting the same beat strengthens it.
- apply: Give large effects a wind-up phase (charge, gather, pause) in the spec, with matching sound cue timing; list the frame of the release so VFX, animation and sound align.
- check: Frame-by-frame capture plus audio: the release frame of VFX, animation and sound match within 1-2 frames at 30 fps [inference].
- mistake: An explosion with no lead-in, which feels sudden and weak.
- example: Build-up timing before the explosion, "a good sound example" (R00c:3834).
- roblox_fit: Network latency can shift client-side visuals vs sounds; trigger both from the same client event [inference].
- roles: vfx-designer, sound-designer, animator, film-director

### R00c-F15 The rock-in-the-lake beat

### R00c-F18 The three jobs of game sound
- principle: Sound design has three goals: gameplay cues (what is happening to or around you), feedback on the player's decisions (good or bad), and driving the core emotion.
- why: Sound reaches the player when the eye is busy, and emotion pulls players into the experience.
- apply: Each cue card names its job. Required cues [per source examples]: ability ready, taking damage, spotted by enemy, resource needed. Good/bad decisions get distinct feedback sounds.
- check: List every gameplay state change in the feature; each has a cue or a stated reason for none; play-test with the screen covered to see if key events are still identifiable.
- mistake: not stated.
- example: Ability-ready and damage cues; "construct additional pylons"; bleak atmosphere (Papers, Please), being followed (Outlast 2), engine detail (Project CARS 2).
- roblox_fit: Many phone players play with sound off [unverified]; key cues need a visual twin too [inference].

### R00c-F19 Shared vocabulary for sound feedback
- principle: Sound is hard to give feedback on ("more whooshiness"); learning the technical terms helps notes be precise.
- why: Vague notes cause guesses and rework.
- apply: Sound reviews use the terms frequency, ADSR, attenuation, EQ, reverb, with a direction and an amount; briefs to the AI sound tool use the same terms.
- check: Every sound review note names a term and a direction (e.g. "attack longer", "cut highs").
- mistake: Notes like "needs more whoosh".

### R00c-F20 Frequency like a colour spectrum
- principle: Frequency is the rate of vibration: slower = lower pitch, faster = higher pitch. Like the light spectrum, each band has its own tone, and real sounds occupy a wide range of frequencies.
- why: Thinking in bands lets designers shape and separate sounds as artists separate colours.
- apply: Cue cards state the main band (low body, mid, high detail) of each sound so layers do not fight; e.g. an explosion has low body and high debris [inference].
- check: Listening on phone speakers and headphones: the needed band is audible (phone speakers lose low end [inference]).
- mistake: not stated.
- example: Explosion sound split into frequency bands.
- roblox_fit: Phone speakers reproduce little low frequency [unverified]; important cues need mid/high content.
- roles: sound-designer
- topic: sound
- lines: R00c:4132-4160

### R00c-F21 ADSR envelope
- principle: A sound's envelope is attack (silence to max), decay (drop after peak to sustain level), sustain (level for most of its life) and release (fade to silence). The ADSR shape is the sound's dynamics.
- why: Envelope shape is what makes a sound feel sharp, soft, sustained or lingering.
- apply: Cue cards give target ADSR in milliseconds [inference: the source gives no numbers]; a punch: very short attack, quick decay, little sustain, short release.
