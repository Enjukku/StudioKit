## Findings

### R08-F01 Character starts from the roster gap
- principle: A new character's design is driven first by what the game needs to fill (e.g. "we need a mage or a front line"); that need dictates many visual decisions.
- why: A character that fills a clear role reads as that role and has a place on the board; taste-only designs may duplicate an existing role. [inference]
- apply: Before the reference-images brief for a character, unit, pet or tower, the game-designer states its role (e.g. caster, tank, support) and the brief lists it; the art bible maps role to shape/colour cues. Default: one role per character, written in the brief header [inference].
- check: Reviewer reads the brief and the sheet: can a tester name the role from the image alone? Count how many existing roster entries share that role and look.
- mistake: Designing only from favourite characters; the speaker notes this demo is the exception ("Alune's great, and we're just going to kind of imagine her here", R08:55-56).
- example: TFT gameplay asks for "a mage" or "a front line" and that dictates the character.
- roblox_fit: Same for Roblox unit/pet/tower rosters; role must read from the third-person or top camera at phone size [inference].
- roles: game-designer, art-director, modeler, character-rigger, orchestrator, reference-images
- topic: game-design-fun (secondary: character-design)

### R08-F02 One palette per group so the roster reads as a team
- principle: When many characters from different lines share one board, give each group a dictated palette and fit every member to it, so all characters "feel like a team".
- why: Shared colour ties units into one universe and lets players group them by trait at a glance.
- apply: Art bible defines a palette per faction/trait (named swatches); every character sheet prompt cites that palette by hex. Example from source: Anima Squad = light blues and pinks.
- check: tools/art/look_check.py palette on the new sheet vs the faction swatches; place the new character next to 2-3 group members in one capture and confirm the group reads as one set.
- mistake: Each skin selling its own separate fantasy palette (fine for League's out-of-game skins, wrong for one shared board).
- example: Alune re-coloured to the Set 14 Anima Squad palette of light blues and pinks.
- roblox_fit: Works with Roblox Color3 on parts, SurfaceAppearance tints and UI colours; keep group colour readable under the game's Lighting [inference].
- roles: art-director, modeler, vfx-designer, ui-ux-designer, marketing, environment-designer, reference-images

### R08-F03 Keep the identity anchors through every version
- principle: A reimagined character keeps the features fans know it by (here face paint, collar, giant weapon) and keeps its stature (elegance) while the theme changes.
- why: The anchors carry recognition; change them and players no longer know who it is.
- apply: Each character's anchor list (2-4 items) lives in the art bible; every variant/skin brief and ChatGPT prompt repeats them; modeler keeps them in the mesh; animator keeps the stature in idle poses [inference].
- check: Side-by-side render of base and variant at game camera: every anchor present and visible; black-fill silhouette still shows the weapon and headpiece.
- mistake: A skin so different the character is lost (implied by R08:758: players must not think it is another champion).
- example: Battle Butterfly Alune keeps face paint, collar, giant weapon, elegance.
- roblox_fit: Skins/variants of avatars, pets and towers; anchors must survive the triangle budget, so model them as big forms, not texture detail [inference].
- roles: art-director, modeler, character-rigger, animator, marketing, reference-images, reviewer

### R08-F04 Theme nods in props
- principle: Carry the variant's theme into its gear with small, readable nods (weapon shaped like a chrysalis, gardener gloves because butterflies belong in a garden).
- why: Props tell the story of the fantasy without words; fans enjoy discovering them.
- apply: Variant briefs list 1-3 theme nods on props or accessories; modeler builds them as shape changes on existing anchors, not added clutter [inference].
- check: Reviewer names the theme from the props alone in a close render; the nods do not change the black-fill silhouette of the anchors.
- mistake: not stated
- example: Weapon looks like a cocoon/chrysalis; garden gloves.
- roblox_fit: Small nods only read in close views (shop, inspect); at gameplay camera they may vanish; do not spend triangles on them beyond the budget [inference].

### R08-F05 Design for the crop players see most
- principle: In-game, the character is sold through a very small space seen very often (the crop: face and a bit of the weapon); that crop is the main focus, but the full image is still finished because players enjoy it outside the game.
- why: Most player contact happens in the small view, many times per match; the full image serves out-of-game viewing.
- apply: For every character/item image define its in-game crop first (shop card, icon, pass/product icon) and compose the reference image so the crop works; render the full image as well for the page/marketing.
- check: Crop the render to the in-game frame and view it at player size with tools/art/thumb_check.py; it must still say who it is.
- mistake: Composing only for the full image so the crop shows nothing useful.
- example: TFT crop is "basically just their face and a little bit of their weapon".
- roblox_fit: Roblox icons: experience icon template 512×512, also shown at 150×150 (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/production/publishing/experience-icons.md:34); pass and developer-product icons max 512×512 with no key detail outside the circle (production/monetization/passes.md:28, developer-products.md:30); badges cropped to a circle (production/publishing/badges.md:32).

### R08-F06 Reference: same line first, then the real world, then mix
- principle: Start reference with the best existing illustrations of the same line (for colour, composition, posture, gesture), but never stop there: add real-world reference and combine both into something new.
- why: Existing art is "derivative of derivative of derivative"; only real sources add new information. The brain's visual library is too limited to invent from.
- apply: reference-images agent builds a moodboard in two parts: (a) the game's approved anchors/art-bible images, (b) real-world photos or descriptions of pose, material, place; ChatGPT prompts cite both. Note per reference what it is for (pose, colour, composition).
- check: Moodboard has both parts; each board entry has a stated purpose; the result is not a near-copy of one existing image.
- mistake: Ending reference with "what is already created"; going "off our brain" with no reference.
- example: Anima Squad splashes (Riven's giant sword as crop focus) plus Pinterest "girl sitting reference" for elegant, queenly poses.
- roblox_fit: Same; add Roblox-specific reference (games on the same camera) for scale and read [inference].

### R08-F07 Enough reference; make the missing one
- principle: Collect a breadth of reference (the speaker usually spends a day or two); if no reference shows the needed pose, take it yourself; "there's literally no reason not to have enough reference".
- why: Breadth gives options to "mishmash" into something new; a made-to-order reference removes guessing.
- apply: When no reference exists, generate it: a ChatGPT pose image, a posed Blender mannequin render, or a keyed test pose in Studio; budget reference time before production [inference for tools].
- check: The brief cites at least one reference for every key pose/material; none marked "from memory".
- mistake: Drawing a pose with no reference because none was found.
- example: Speaker photographs her own pose reference.
- roblox_fit: Blender mannequin with the Roblox rig proportions gives pose reference true to the avatar [inference].

### R08-F08 The first output is the weakest
- principle: The first drawing of a day and the first thumbnail of a task are usually the worst; they default to the easy, expected idea. Get them out of the way and make more.
- why: First attempts sit "in the range of expectation"; judgment only arrives when you look again later.
- apply: Reference-image and concept generation never stop at the first result; treat the first batch as warm-up and generate further variants with changed prompts [inference].
- check: Record how many options were made before the pick; a pick from a set of one fails review.
- mistake: "a mistake that I see a lot of people do... we'll just draw one thing".
- example: Warm-up figures before the real sketch; "our first thumbnail is never going to be the best one".

### R08-F09 Register in 0.5 seconds, distinct from the roster
- principle: Players meet the character mostly in the shop while clicking fast; it must register in about 0.5 seconds through colour, shape and palette, and must look unique enough not to blend with similar roster members.
- why: Players memorise colour and shape; two look-alikes cause wrong buys.
- apply: Brief states the 0.5 s target; compare the new character against the nearest look-alikes (same palette or role) and change shape or colour where they overlap.
- check: Place the new icon among the roster icons at player size (thumb_check.py); flash for 0.5 s or blur; a tester or the agent must pick it out. Compare silhouettes and palettes against neighbours with look_check.py.
- mistake: A new character that blends with an existing one (Alune vs Aurora).
- example: "How do we completely register the character to the person in literally, like 0.5 seconds?"
- roblox_fit: Shop/inventory grids on phones are small; 150×150 icon size is a real display size (production/publishing/experience-icons.md:34); touch players tap quickly.
- roles: art-director, modeler, ui-ux-designer, marketing, game-designer, reviewer, qa-lead, reference-images

### R08-F10 Gesture before detail
- principle: Nail the overall motion of the body (gesture) first, loosely, and leave details for later; gesture is what sells the character's believability.
- why: Big problems change everything below them; detail done early is wasted when the pose moves.
- apply: Animator blocks key poses with the full body line before fingers/face; reference-images asks for loose pose options before detailed sheets; modeler builds poses from a blocked mannequin first [inference].
- check: In a black-fill or low-detail render, the pose alone shows the attitude; in clip_check.py sampled frames, the line of action is clear before detail passes.
- mistake: Getting into "nitty-gritty detail issues" before overarching ones.
- example: Loose figure sketches of Alune solving body motion only.
- roblox_fit: Roblox avatars are blocky/R15; gesture must come from the whole-body pose since faces are small at game camera [inference].
- roles: animator, art-director, modeler, character-rigger, reference-images, film-director

### R08-F11 Write the sell list first
- principle: Before drawing, ask: what am I selling, what excites players, what does it say about the character, is it recognisable. Turn that into a short numbered list and solve the thumbnail for it plus the silhouette read.
- why: Players should project themselves into a story slightly beyond reality; a stated fantasy keeps every choice serving it.
- apply: Every visual brief has a "sells" list of 2-4 items (e.g. 1 faction, 2 role, 3 personality) and each candidate is scored against it.
- check: Reviewer reads each item and points to where the image shows it; missing item = fail.
- mistake: Not stated directly; R08:576-579 warns against not knowing the goal.
- example: Alune sells (1) Anima Squad, (2) caster, (3) elegant, queenly figure.
- roblox_fit: Same; for Roblox the "sells" list also drives the game icon and thumbnail promise [inference].

### R08-F12 Several near-finished options, pick the clearest
- principle: Make at least four or five thumbnails that feel "really, really finished", compare them, use elimination, and pick the clearest direction; more ideas may follow from seeing them.

- mistake: Settling on one option without comparison.

### R08-F13 Work small to solve the whole image
- principle: Early stages are done at a small canvas size; small size stops you from rendering detail you do not need yet and keeps focus on solving the overall image.
- why: At large size you see pixels and want them to look good; at small size you can only judge big shapes.
- apply: Early option renders/images at low resolution (Blender preview renders, small ChatGPT images); review them at small size; full resolution only after the direction is fixed [inference for sizes].
- check: Early-stage files are small; the reviewer judges them at thumbnail size, not zoomed in.
- mistake: Working big early and polishing pixels before the image works.
- example: "it literally disallows you from being able to do more than you need to"; hair rendering impossible at that size.
- roblox_fit: Small viewing also matches phone display size [inference].

### R08-F14 One named problem per stage
- principle: Art is problem-solving: name the problem you are solving now and solve problems in order (pose, then how the pose supports the story, then lighting).
- why: One problem at a time is less intimidating and each solved step constrains the next.
- apply: Each work stage in a brief names its single problem and its check; the agent does not open the next problem until the current one passes [inference].
- check: Hand-back lists the problem solved per stage with its check result.
- mistake: Mixing all problems at once (implied).
- example: "the pose is good—what's her story? How does her pose support the story?"

### R08-F15 Fit the character's icons into the frame

### R08-F16 Camera angle states status
- principle: Choose the view angle from the character's personality: Alune looks down at the viewer because she is "above us", never on the same level as anyone; ask how a player would feel meeting the character.
- why: Viewing from below makes a character feel powerful and distant; the feeling of meeting her (fear, "she is going to kill me") is the target.
- apply: Briefs for key art, icons, cinematics and showcase renders state the intended feeling and the camera height to match (low camera looking up for dominant characters; eye-level for friendly) [inference for defaults].
- check: In the render, compare camera height to the character's eyes; ask whether the image gives the stated feeling.
- mistake: not stated
- example: Thumbnails of Alune "somewhat looking down at us".
- roblox_fit: Gameplay camera is fixed (third-person/top), so this applies to icons, thumbnails, cutscenes and shop previews, where the camera is free [inference].

### R08-F17 Make what that character's fans want
- principle: Work to the need, not the artist's preference; learn what players who love the character want to see, and ask fans of it; the artist's opinion is not more valid than anyone else's.
- why: Their excitement is the measure of success; understanding it also makes repeat work worthwhile.
- apply: Before a character or update, collect what fans of that character/game ask for (live-ops feedback, Discord) and write the one-line "fans want" into the brief.
- check: The output shows the fan-expected trait at its strongest (e.g. extreme joy for a party character).
- mistake: Making what you like instead of what the audience wants.
- example: Kobuko fans want someone "joyful to almost an insane amount".
- roblox_fit: Roblox feedback sources: game Discord, comments, analytics [inference].

### R08-F18 Lighting and contrast tests on the rough
- principle: Once the pose is set, refine value, then test colour and several lighting scenarios and contrast ratios on the rough before painting further.
- why: Light decides mood and focus; changing it later is expensive.
- apply: Make 2-3 lighting variants of the blockout (Blender fixed camera or Studio Lighting presets) and compare; record the chosen key/fill direction [inference for count].
- check: Grayscale capture of each variant; look_check.py value range; the focal area has the highest contrast.
- mistake: not stated
- example: Sketch moved to Photoshop for "a lighting test" and "different contrast ratios".
- roblox_fit: Studio Lighting, Atmosphere and post effects set the scene light; test at game camera on phone-like resolution [inference].
- roles: art-director, environment-designer, vfx-designer, reference-images, marketing
- topic: light-colour (secondary: value-contrast)

### R08-F19 Feedback early, from partner crafts
- principle: Ask others at the thumbnail stage ("does this arm look off?") and decide rationally from what you hear; early notes are worth far more because anything can still change. Concept and splash artists review each other's stages.
- why: Fixes cost least before work is committed; being "precious" and alone wastes drawing time.
- apply: Orchestrator schedules a reviewer pass right after options exist, before render; partner crafts (modeler on concept, art director on renders) review each other.
- check: Job record shows feedback taken at the option stage, not only at the final.
- mistake: Staying "in your head" and "precious" without other opinions.
- example: Two concept artists and one splash artist give each other feedback at concept and splash stages.
- roblox_fit: not stated (process).
