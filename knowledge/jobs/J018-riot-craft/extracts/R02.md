## Findings

### R02-F01 Big to small
- principle: Block in the primary colours and big shapes over the whole asset first; go to medium shapes, then details, only after the big read works.
- why: The big read is what players see first and from far away; small detail on a wrong base is wasted.
- apply: Modeler and environment agents: first pass = flat colour zones per material in Blender (one material per zone), render; second pass = medium forms; third = details. Reference-images: ask ChatGPT for a flat-colour blockout option before a detailed one.
- check: Render pass 1 at game camera: do the colour zones alone say "grass, stone pedestal, fox"? If not, do not start details.
- mistake: Rendering details early ("A lot of times, I want to get into those details because it's kind of fun") (R02:154-156).
- example: Big green blotches for the grass and a basic grey for the statue and pedestal before any detail (R02:66-84).
- roblox_fit: Same for Parts/MeshParts: material and colour per part first, then decals or SurfaceAppearance.
- roles: modeler, environment-designer, art-director, reference-images, character-rigger, vfx-designer, ui-ux-designer
- topic: references-ideation (secondary: pipeline-tech)
- lines: R02:56-58, 82-84, 154

### R02-F02 Hue and value variation, not a flat fill
- principle: Do not fill a material with one colour. Shift the hue and value slightly in random patches, because nature is not uniform.
- why: Slight shifts make forms "pop" and give "happy accidents"; flat fills read sterile and fake.
- apply: In Blender materials, mix 2-4 near hues (hue shift within a few degrees, value ±5-10% [inference, sound default]) by noise texture; for Roblox Parts, vary BrickColor/Color slightly across neighbouring parts of the same material.
- check: Palette measure with tools/art/look_check.py on the render: a material region should show more than one hue cluster but stay inside its colour family; a single flat swatch = fail.
- mistake: "they are pretty monochromatic... people don't tend to shift their color wheel... ever so slightly to a new hue, to a new value" (R02:86-94).
- example: Adding a little red into the grey stonework (R02:84-86).
- roblox_fit: Roblox built-in materials are uniform per part; variation comes from Color per part, MaterialVariant or SurfaceAppearance ColorMap (docs: art/modeling/surface-appearance.md). Keep variations subtle on phones where small patches merge.
- roles: modeler, environment-designer, art-director, reference-images
- topic: light-colour (secondary: materials-detail)
- lines: R02:68-74, 86-96, 152-154, 230

### R02-F03 Lighter values lead the eye
- principle: From the first pass, put lighter values toward the focal subject so the eye goes there.
- why: The eye goes to the lightest, highest-contrast area first.
- apply: Environment: ground and surroundings slightly darker toward the edges, lighter near the landmark or objective. Marketing thumbnails: lightest area on the hero.
- check: Grayscale render at game camera; blur it; the lightest blob should sit on the subject.
- mistake: not stated.
- example: Lighter greens in the grass toward the centre of the piece to focus on the fox (R02:74-80).
- roblox_fit: Use local lights, light-coloured ground materials or decals near objectives; check on a phone-size capture.
- roles: environment-designer, art-director, modeler, level-design, marketing
- topic: composition-focus (secondary: value-contrast)
- lines: R02:74-80

### R02-F04 Start from baked AO and curvature
- principle: Bake ambient occlusion and curvature first and paint on top; respect the shadows they place, keep the contact shadow under the object defined.
- why: AO puts shadows "where we want them" and does the heavy lifting; curvature finds every edge so the texture stays tight.
- apply: Blender: bake AO and a curvature/pointiness mask, multiply AO into the colour map at reduced strength, use curvature to drive edge highlights. Lower the curvature layer's strength (he lowers its opacity) and clean triangle artifacts.
- check: Render with flat (unlit) shading: forms should still read from the texture alone; contact shadow under the statue visible.
- mistake: Painting over and losing the lighting detail the AO already gave (R02:174-176); curvature artefacts that highlight triangles (R02:146-148).
- example: Calculated occlusion and curvature in 3D Coat; "There's no actual lighting in here. This is all just... flat texture" (R02:98-108).
- roblox_fit: Roblox lighting gives no baked AO on meshes; AO must be baked into the SurfaceAppearance ColorMap or texture [inference].
- roles: modeler, environment-designer
- topic: materials-detail (secondary: pipeline-tech)
- lines: R02:98-118, 174-180, 366

### R02-F05 Edges set hardness; edge pass, but not every edge
- principle: Sharp, tight edges make stone feel hard; an edge pass (reinforcing edges) stops the mesh looking mushy. Then remove or vary some highlights, because every edge lit is not believable.
- why: Edge contrast tells the eye about material hardness and form.
- apply: Curvature-driven edge highlight on hard materials; vary its strength with a noise mask so 20-40% of edges are dimmer [inference, sound default]. Soft materials (grass, fur) get soft edges.
- check: Zoom render at game camera: edges visible on the stone silhouette and key planes; check not all edges are the same brightness.
- mistake: "if every edge is highlighted, it makes it not believable" (R02:578-580); "Without the edges, the mesh can look pretty mushy" (R02:566-568).
- example: "Stonework is really sharp, and it gives it that hard feel to it" (R02:116-118).
- roblox_fit: On phone screens edge highlights are often the only material cue at distance; keep them.
- roles: modeler, environment-designer, art-director, reviewer
- topic: materials-detail (secondary: value-contrast)
- lines: R02:114-118, 564-580

### R02-F06 No rendering every grass blade
- principle: Grass and other mass surfaces get form and fluffy clumps, not every blade. Put the time into the focal subject.
- why: Blade-by-blade detail is noisy and steals attention and time.
- apply: Ground cover = soft clumps with flat rest areas; a few blades pulled out in chosen spots (see F22). Spend most effort budget on the landmark/hero asset.
- check: Count: detailed grass areas should be a minority of the ground area in the render; grayscale noise check on ground vs subject.
- mistake: "everybody, when they texture grass, they try and do every blade of grass, and it's just so noisy" (R02:424-426); "fall into the trap of just rendering every grass blade" (R02:126).
- example: Fluffy grass with areas of rest, as on Summoner's Rift (R02:422-430).
- roblox_fit: Roblox Terrain Grass decoration is uniform; ground clumping comes from material patches and few placed meshes [unverified].
- roles: environment-designer, modeler, level-design, art-director
- topic: world-level-readability (secondary: visual-hierarchy)
- lines: R02:124-128, 420-430

### R02-F07 One fixed light direction
- principle: Paint as if lit from one direction (top-down on Summoner's Rift) and never move the light during the work; Rift baked one light over the whole map as the "North Star".
- why: Top-down light "gives the player a good sense of physicality and a good sense of orientation" (R02:134-136); a shifting light means repainting everything.
- apply: Set one key light direction per game in the art bible; bake highlights and AO for that direction; match Roblox Lighting sun direction (ClockTime, GeographicLatitude) to it.
- check: Render all assets in one scene: highlights on every object face the same way.
- mistake: Shifting the light source mid-work: "you're gonna just repaint the whole thing" (R02:750-752).
- example: Highlights from the top; he considered top-left and kept top-down for simplicity (R02:752-756).
- roblox_fit: Roblox has dynamic sun; baked highlights must agree with the game's Lighting setting, and with a day/night cycle baked light can conflict [inference].
- roles: modeler, environment-designer, art-director, vfx-designer
- topic: light-colour (secondary: materials-detail)
- lines: R02:130-136, 248-250, 748-760

### R02-F08 Mirrored vs unique UVs
- principle: Mirrored UVs halve texturing time but anything painted appears on both sides; for asymmetric story detail (cracks, damage) use unique UVs.
- why: Symmetry reads artificial; story wear is asymmetric.
- apply: Blender: mirror UVs for base passes on symmetric props to save texture space; for hero props with cracks/wear, unwrap unique (or at least the visible front) before detail.
- check: Look for repeated identical marks on both sides in the render.
- mistake: Painting cracks on mirrored UVs (R02:412-416); the projection "big X" from symmetrical UVs (R02:304-306).
- example: Re-UV'd the fox unique to paint a single crack along its face (R02:668-676, 722-728).
- roblox_fit: Unique UVs on a 1024 map lower texel density; choose per asset (docs: art/modeling/texture-specifications.md:131).
- roles: modeler, environment-designer, character-rigger
- topic: pipeline-tech (secondary: materials-detail)
- lines: R02:136-146, 412-418, 686-712

### R02-F09 Time-box and review questions
- principle: Respect the time box; do not finish the head while the rest is blank. Review asks: is it looking right in the play space, reading right, telling the right story?
- why: Rabbit holes leave whole assets unfinished; play-space review catches problems close-up work hides.
- apply: Plan passes; a polished prop is about "a day's worth" for a human (R02:160-162) — agents: set a pass budget per asset. Review captures at the game camera, not close-up.
- check: Hand-back includes the play-space capture and answers the three questions.
- mistake: "go down a rabbit hole, and then be left with a whole model to re-texture, and you've only gotten the head done" (R02:158-160).
- example: Fox statue review: play space, reading, story (R02:162-164).
- roblox_fit: Capture in Studio at the real camera (third-person/top) via Studio MCP.
- roles: modeler, environment-designer, reviewer, orchestrator, qa-lead

### R02-F11 Colour-blind safety and reserved red
- principle: Gameplay-critical colours come from design (red = enemy); use red with caution elsewhere. Check saturation numerically, not by eye, and get a colour second opinion; advocate colour-blind settings.
- why: Players who cannot see a threat lose unfairly; an artist's own perception can oversaturate.
- apply: Art bible lists reserved gameplay colours (enemy, danger, team); environment avoids them. Check palette saturation numbers against the bible; reviewer checks under colour-blind simulation [inference].
- check: look_check.py palette: no reserved hue in environment above a set saturation; test in deuteranopia/protanopia simulation [inference].
- mistake: "when I get shot by someone I can't see, that can be rough" (R02:212-214); oversaturating reds (R02:208).
- example: He measures how far out his greens are in saturation and tones back (R02:438-444).
- roblox_fit: Roblox has no global colour-blind filter we confirmed [unverified]; build pair cues (shape + colour). ColorCorrectionEffect can shift scene colour (docs: environment/post-processing-effects.md:76-85).
- roles: art-director, environment-designer, ui-ux-designer, vfx-designer, game-designer, reviewer, qa-lead

### R02-F12 Stylise, do not copy

- mistake: not stated.
- example: Old Indonesian statues that only hint at the animal (R02:222-228).

### R02-F13 Hand-made imperfection
- principle: Small visible inconsistencies make art feel hand-crafted and less sterile.
- why: Perfect procedural uniformity reads as machine-made.
- apply: For AI/procedural output add controlled irregularity: vary stroke/edge strength, break symmetry slightly. Our risk of "AI look" is high, so this matters.
- check: Look for exact repeats, perfectly even edges or tiling in the render.
- mistake: Sterile output (R02:238-240).
- example: One-layer messy blending for a "handcrafted feel" (R02:232-240).
- roblox_fit: Avoid obvious tiling of Roblox materials over big areas (Texture StudsPerTile, docs: parts/textures-decals.md:116).

### R02-F14 Turn the forms

### R02-F15 Rule-set texturing

- check: Two assets of the same material side by side in one render match.
- mistake: not stated.

### R02-F16 Bring everything to the same level
- principle: Take the whole asset (and scene) to one level of finish, then the next; hit everything at the same quality bar.
- why: Gives a "holistic, consistent texture"; one over-finished part looks out of place.
- apply: Pass-based work in every craft; review compares parts side by side.
- check: In the render, no part is clearly more detailed than its neighbours of equal importance.
- mistake: Leaving grass "a blur" while the statue is finished (R02:420-422).
- example: Moving to the pedestal before detailing the fox further (R02:260-268).

### R02-F20 Budget detail by player speed and camera distance
- principle: Put detail only where the player will see it at their speed and camera distance; step back often.
- why: Close-up detail is wasted when the camera is far or the player runs past "at Mach 10".
- apply: Detail tiers per area: paths players run = macro only; stop points and landmarks = more. Work at the game camera distance.
- check: Capture at game camera and speed; detail that vanishes there is waste.
