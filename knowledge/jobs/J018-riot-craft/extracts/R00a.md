## Findings

### R00a-F01 Three jobs of game art
- principle: Game art is everything the player sees and hears (characters, effects, enemies, environment, UI, animation, sound). It has three jobs: clarity, satisfaction, and a cohesive style that sets an emotional tone. It is "way more than just a pretty wrapping".
- why: Art is the player's only window into game state and the main carrier of feeling; a pretty asset that hides state or gives no feedback fails its job.
- apply: Every brief and review names which of the three jobs the asset serves and how. Reviewers score clarity first, satisfaction second, style third [inference: order from the video's own sequence, R00a:92-94].
- check: For each asset in a capture at the game camera, answer three questions: can the player read what it is/does? Does it respond to the player? Does it look like the rest of the game (compare to the art-bible anchors)?
- mistake: Treating art as decoration ("are artists just making a pretty wrapping?", R00a:86).
- example: The host lists characters, effects, enemies, environment, UI, animation and sound as all art.
- roblox_fit: Same on Roblox; sound and UI count as art and need the same three checks.
- roles: art-director, game-designer, orchestrator, reviewer, vfx-designer, sound-designer, ui-ux-designer, animator, modeler, environment-designer

### R00a-F02 Clarity of critical information
- principle: Clarity means the player instantly knows which way to go, where they are, who is ally and who is enemy, and how much health they have. It is hard because games show a lot at once and the player has very little time.
- why: The player decides in fractions of a second; information that needs study is information they miss (the video points to a League team fight).
- apply: List the critical facts for each screen/scene (route, location, ally/enemy, health, threat). Give each a dedicated read: team colour or Highlight outline for allies/enemies, landmark for location, path contrast for route, HUD bar for health.
- check: Capture the busiest expected moment (many players + effects) at the game camera on a phone-size viewport; for each critical fact, time how long it takes to find it (target: at a glance [inference]). Run look_check.py thumbnail size to see if the fact survives shrinking.
- mistake: Testing readability in an empty scene; the hard case is the crowded fight.
- example: "Have you ever been in a team fight in League of Legends?"
- roblox_fit: Phones shrink everything and many players/effects share the screen, so the busy case is the default. Highlight can mark allies/enemies but only 255 display at once (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/effects/highlighting.md:37).
- roles: art-director, ui-ux-designer, vfx-designer, environment-designer, game-designer, qa-lead, reviewer, level-design
- topic: world-level-readability (secondary: visual-hierarchy, ui-ux)

### R00a-F03 Satisfaction: responsive feedback
- principle: Every player action gets clear, responsive feedback: an immediate change on button press, a good feeling on success, a bad feeling on failure. The form varies (meaty hit pause, big ceremony, flowing momentum, simple run and jump) but every action must be responsive and satisfying.
- why: Immediate feedback confirms the input was received and makes the result feel caused by the player; matching the size of feedback to the event teaches what matters.
- apply: For every input, the feature-builder and crafts list the feedback stack (animation, VFX, sound, UI change) and when it starts. Default [inference]: first visible/audible response on the press frame or next frame; big wins get a ceremony, failures get a distinct negative cue, not silence.
- check: Frame-by-frame capture at 30 fps from press to first visible change; count frames. Listen/look for a distinct cue for both success and failure.
- mistake: Feedback only for success; or delayed/absent response on press.
- example: Hit pause, big ceremony, momentum, run and jump shown as types of satisfaction.
- roblox_fit: Network latency means client-side immediate feedback (local VFX/sound) while the server confirms the result [inference; see networking-security skill]. Hit-stop on Roblox is done by pausing/slowing animation tracks and effects [unverified].
- roles: vfx-designer, sound-designer, animator, ui-ux-designer, game-designer, feature-builder, qa-lead
- topic: game-design-fun (secondary: vfx-reads-timing, sound, weight-timing)

### R00a-F04 Style sets the emotional tone
- principle: Every art style evokes a different emotion. Its levers are colour, character design, environments and how far proportions are pushed. The same gameplay in a different style feels different.
- why: Players read tone from colour and proportion before they read any rule; more colour and pushed proportions read light-hearted, less colour and real-world forms read serious.
- apply: The art bible names the target emotion and sets each lever explicitly (palette saturation, proportion push, realism of props/environment). Reference-image prompts state these levers, not just "stylized".
- check: Put a capture next to the art-bible anchors; check palette with look_check.py palette; compare proportion ratios (head-to-body) against the anchor character.
- mistake: Mixing levers (pushed cartoony characters in a desaturated realistic world) so the tone is unclear [inference].
- example: PUBG vs Fortnite: similar gameplay; Fortnite has more colour and pushed proportions (light-hearted), PUBG less colour, real guns and environments (serious).
- roblox_fit: Roblox audiences and the blocky avatar push many games toward bright, pushed styles; the choice still must be deliberate and consistent.
- roles: art-director, marketing, modeler, environment-designer, ui-ux-designer, vfx-designer, reference-images
- topic: style-identity (secondary: light-colour, character-design)
- lines: R00a:175-219

### R00a-F05 Visual hierarchy
- principle: Visual hierarchy means the most important information is the easiest to see. Games have thousands of art pieces, so a hierarchy is critical to stop them clashing. Artists make it by manipulating contrast to create areas of focus.
- why: Attention is limited; the eye goes to the strongest contrast first.
- apply: The art director ranks what must be seen in each scene (e.g. 1 threat, 2 player, 3 objective, 4 background) and assigns contrast budget to match. Background assets get the least contrast.
- check: Grayscale capture at the game camera: the highest-ranked item must be the first thing noticed; look_check.py value to confirm the ranked item has the strongest local contrast.
- mistake: Every asset made "cool" at full contrast so the screen clashes.
- example: Thousands of pieces in one game must not clash.
- roblox_fit: User-made or imported assets of mixed styles are common on Roblox, so the hierarchy must be enforced at review.
- roles: art-director, environment-designer, vfx-designer, ui-ux-designer, modeler, reviewer, level-design
- topic: visual-hierarchy (secondary: value-contrast)
- lines: R00a:221-234

### R00a-F06 Everything is contrast
- principle: Everything an artist does can be framed as contrast, for focus and for the overall feeling: shape, colour, saturation, size, layout, position. Sound, music and narrative use contrast too.
- why: Perception notices difference, not absolute values.
- apply: When something does not read, ask which contrast type is missing and add one (shape, value, hue, saturation, size, position) rather than adding more detail. Sound designers use contrast the same way (quiet before loud, a unique timbre for the important cue).
- check: For the focal item, list which contrasts it wins against its surroundings; at least one should be strong [inference].
- mistake: Trying to fix readability by adding detail instead of contrast.
- example: The host lists shape, colour, saturation, size, layout, position, and says sound, music and narrative use contrast too.
- roblox_fit: No custom shaders, so contrast comes from colour, material, Lighting, Highlight and size choices.
- roles: art-director, vfx-designer, sound-designer, ui-ux-designer, film-director, environment-designer, modeler
- topic: value-contrast (secondary: visual-hierarchy, sound)
- lines: R00a:236-252

### R00a-F07 Focus by lowering contrast elsewhere (checkerboard)
- principle: To make one spot the focus, reduce the contrast of everything else so the viewer knows where to look.
- why: In a field of equal high contrast nothing stands out; lowering the surroundings makes the focus win without changing it.
- apply: Before brightening/boosting the hero item, lower value range and saturation of its surroundings (environment, secondary props, background UI). In reference-image prompts, ask for a quiet background and one high-contrast focal point.
- check: Grayscale capture: the focal item is the only area with full black-to-white contrast; look_check.py value on focus vs surround.
- mistake: Raising contrast on everything (a full checkerboard).
- example: A black-and-white checkerboard where every other square loses contrast so the centre square becomes the focus.
- roblox_fit: Atmosphere Density and Offset can lower distant contrast globally (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/environment/atmosphere.md:29, 46).
- roles: art-director, environment-designer, vfx-designer, ui-ux-designer, marketing, film-director, reference-images
- topic: value-contrast (secondary: composition-focus)
- lines: R00a:261-285

### R00a-F08 Shapes as silhouettes and pointers
- principle: Shapes create distinct points of contrast and clear silhouettes, and act as arrows and pointers that show where to look, what is dangerous and what is safe.
- why: Shape is read before detail and colour; pointed/directional shapes direct the eye and carry meaning (danger vs safety).
- apply: Give dangerous things and safe things different shape families (agreed in the art bible) [inference: the video shows the idea, not the families]. Use directional shapes in environment and UI to point at the route or the objective.
- check: Black-fill silhouette capture: each object type is identifiable by outline alone; danger vs safe shapes are distinguishable without colour.
- mistake: Similar silhouettes for threats and harmless props.
- example: Shapes shown pointing to where to look, what is dangerous and what is safe.
- roblox_fit: Third-person and top-down cameras see silhouettes from above too; check the top silhouette, not only the side.
- roles: modeler, vfx-designer, environment-designer, ui-ux-designer, game-designer, art-director, level-design
- topic: shape-silhouette (secondary: visual-hierarchy)

### R00a-F09 Size signals importance
- principle: Changing only size can establish which enemy is the most important to be aware of.
- why: Bigger reads as more powerful and more threatening at a glance.
- apply: Scale the most dangerous/important enemy noticeably larger than common ones; keep sizes consistent with threat across the roster.
- check: Capture the enemy lineup at the game camera; rank by apparent size and compare to the design's threat ranking.
- mistake: Size that contradicts threat (a tiny boss, a huge harmless mob) [inference].
- example: Altering size establishes which enemy matters most.
- roblox_fit: On a phone, small enemies may shrink to a few pixels; size gaps must be bigger than on PC [inference].
- roles: modeler, game-designer, art-director, character-rigger, level-design
- topic: visual-hierarchy (secondary: shape-silhouette)

### R00a-F10 Stark colour for must-see things
- principle: A stark colour contrast clearly marks things that are really important to see.
- why: A hue no other object uses becomes a code the player learns instantly.
- apply: Reserve one or two colours in the palette for gameplay meaning (enemy, interactable, route) and keep them off decoration.
- check: look_check.py palette: the reserved hue appears only on the meaningful objects; scan captures for decoration using it.
- mistake: Using the signal colour on decoration so it loses meaning [inference].
- example: Red enemies in Superhot; red jump spots in Mirror's Edge.
- roblox_fit: Colour-blind players and small phone screens: pair the colour with shape or Highlight [inference].
- roles: art-director, environment-designer, vfx-designer, ui-ux-designer, game-designer, level-design
- topic: light-colour (secondary: visual-hierarchy, world-level-readability)

### R00a-F11 Detail placement: rest vs high detail
- principle: Where detail goes, and how much, is a hierarchy tool. Make areas of rest and areas of high detail; that difference is contrast.
- why: Uniform detail is noise; rest areas let the detailed focal areas read.
- apply: Put detail where the player looks (face, weapon, interactable, landmark); keep large surfaces plain. Brief modelers and image prompts with where the detail lives.
- check: Thumbnail-size capture: detailed areas match the focal points; large quiet areas exist. Squint/blur test [inference].
- mistake: "It is tempting, especially in a world of copy and paste, to cram as much detail into your game objects as possible."
- example: Journey and INSIDE use large areas of rest.
- roblox_fit: Less detail also saves triangles and texture memory (per-mesh 20,000 triangle cap, knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/art/modeling/specifications.md:34; 1024x1024 max texture, art/modeling/texture-specifications.md:131).
- roles: modeler, environment-designer, art-director, ui-ux-designer, vfx-designer, reference-images
- topic: materials-detail (secondary: visual-hierarchy)

### R00a-F12 Atmospheric perspective / implied detail
- principle: Realistic games also imply detail: they reduce detail in the distance (atmospheric perspective). This leads the eye and creates depth.
- why: Distant low-contrast, low-detail areas read as far away and push attention to the nearer, sharper area.
- apply: Use Atmosphere and fog to drop distant contrast; use simpler distant models/LOD. Keep the high-detail work in the near and mid ground the player uses.
- check: Capture from the play camera: value contrast falls with distance (look_check.py value on near vs far bands).
- mistake: Full-contrast, full-detail backgrounds that compete with the play space [inference].
- example: Uncharted and God of War reduce detail in the distance.
- roblox_fit: Atmosphere Density and Offset blend distant objects into the sky (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/environment/atmosphere.md:29, 46); also lowers draw cost on phones [inference].
- roles: environment-designer, art-director, film-director, marketing, level-design
- topic: world-level-readability (secondary: composition-focus, light-colour)

### R00a-F13 Concept art is problem solving
- principle: Concept art is not about beautiful detailed drawings. It helps the team solve problems by designing visual solutions; drawing and painting are only tools to communicate ideas.
- why: The team needs decisions, not finished pictures; a picture that answers the question is enough.
- apply: Each reference-images request states the problem it answers. Judge the image by whether it answers the question, not by finish.
- check: The image's caption names the problem and how the image answers it; reviewers can say yes/no to that.
- mistake: Making a beautiful image that solves nothing.
- example: Host interrupted: "Concept art isn't about making beautiful, detailed drawings."
- roblox_fit: Same; ChatGPT images are targets, never final assets.
- roles: art-director, reference-images, orchestrator, modeler, vfx-designer, environment-designer, ui-ux-designer
- topic: references-ideation

### R00a-F14 Know the problem and constraints first
- principle: Before drawing, find out what you are solving and set constraints. Constraints narrow focus and prevent endless spinning on ideas.
- why: Exploration without a target does not push the team toward its goals.
- apply: Every visual brief opens with: problem, constraints (theme, camera, mechanics, numbers), success test. No image generation before these are written.
- check: Brief has the three items; each delivered option can be checked against each constraint.
- mistake: "Just drawing something cool doesn't necessarily push the team toward its goals." The foil: "I'm not really sure where I was going with this drawing."
- example: The "ABCs" joke: always be solving.
- roblox_fit: Roblox constraints include phone camera, triangle/texture budgets and no custom shaders; list them in the brief.
- roles: art-director, reference-images, orchestrator, game-designer, modeler, vfx-designer, environment-designer, ui-ux-designer
- topic: references-ideation (secondary: critique-feedback)

### R00a-F16 Design for the camera
- principle: How the game is experienced changes the concept: first-person, third-person and top-down each bring focus to different parts of a design and have different limits.
- why: The camera decides which parts of an object are ever seen and how big.
- apply: Every model, effect and environment brief names the game camera (angle, distance). Put design emphasis on parts visible from it (e.g. top-down: head, shoulders, weapon top, ground decals).
- check: Render/capture at the game camera, not a beauty angle; confirm the key features are visible from it.
- mistake: Designing in a front view that the game camera never shows [inference].
- example: First-person vs third-person vs top-down perspectives.
- roblox_fit: Roblox games are mostly third-person or top-down on phones; this is the default review camera.
- roles: modeler, art-director, animator, vfx-designer, environment-designer, ui-ux-designer, reference-images, level-design

### R00a-F17 Mechanics and numbers are constraints
- principle: Game rules and mechanics set huge constraints; the concept artist usually figures out what a mechanic looks like (e.g. pulling enemies close). Even a number like health points shapes the design.
- why: The look must tell the player what the mechanic does and how tough something is.
- apply: Game-designer briefs give the mechanic and its numbers (HP, range, speed) to art, VFX and animation; the visual must express them (e.g. a tanky high-HP unit looks heavy).
- check: Show the asset without text; ask what it does and how tough it is; compare to the spec.
- mistake: Art made before mechanics, which then contradicts them [inference].
- example: An ability that pulls enemies close: the concept artist finds what it looks like.
- roblox_fit: no change.
- roles: game-designer, vfx-designer, animator, modeler, progression-designer, art-director
- topic: game-design-fun (secondary: references-ideation, vfx-reads-timing)

### R00a-F18 Research and a narrow moodboard with pillars
- principle: Research first (play similar games, collect images of the setting, visit it if possible), then build a moodboard. It gets team feedback with little work and is a gut-check on direction. An effective moodboard is clear, can include short descriptors (pillars), and represents a fairly narrow direction. Studying real reference makes creations more compelling.
- why: Early cheap alignment avoids late expensive pivots; a narrow board keeps everyone on one target.

### R00a-F36 Material inputs and maps
- principle: A shader/material has inputs (colour, transparency, metallic); an image plugged into an input is a texture map, and the same image means different things in different channels. Colour, normal (how light hits bumps) and height maps together sell a surface (cracked rock). Pushing a value too far looks wrong.
- why: Light response, not only colour, tells material.
- apply: Build materials from colour + normal + roughness + metalness maps in Blender for SurfaceAppearance; tune strengths at the game camera and back off when it looks overdone.
- check: Capture under the game's Lighting: the surface reads as its material at game distance; no overdone bumps.
- mistake: Cranking a map up "too much".
- example: Cracked-rock material from colour, normal and height maps; height "too much".
- roblox_fit: SurfaceAppearance supports ColorMap, NormalMap, RoughnessMap, MetalnessMap (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/art/modeling/surface-appearance.md:63-114); no height map in this list and no custom shaders, so height detail goes in geometry or normal map.
