## Findings

### R09-F01 The map is the chess board
- principle: The map is the board on which the real game happens; players should not notice it except when they really need to. Bespoke map mechanics stay in the background and come to the foreground only in key moments the designers planned.
- why: Players' attention belongs on the core activity (here, gunfights); a mechanic that constantly demands attention takes "a lot of mind share" (R09:40) and crowds out the core game.
- apply: For every map mechanic (teleporter, moving door, hazard, jump pad), the game-designer writes in the spec: (a) the core activity it must not compete with, (b) the 1-3 planned moments where it comes forward. Environment and VFX treat the mechanic's look as quiet by default and loud only at those moments [inference].
- check: Watch a capture of a normal round at the game camera: count how many seconds the mechanic draws the eye (motion, glow) outside its planned moments; target near zero [inference].
- mistake: A mechanic so "gamey" it dominates the map ("lava is a little bit too gamey for VAL", R09:38).
- example: VALORANT maps have teleporters, fall-offs and rotating doors, but they stay background to the shooter.
- roblox_fit: Applies directly to competitive or objective Roblox modes; in obbies or simulators the "mechanic" may be the core activity, so the rule is: decide which layer is the board and which is the game [inference].
- roles: game-designer, level-design, environment-designer, vfx-designer, art-director

### R09-F02 Prototype with crude stand-ins, then dress
- principle: Test a mechanic as fast as possible with a stand-in ("just say something is red and it'll deal damage", R09:312-313) and a quick visual pass, to learn how it feels and whether art can serve the gameplay, before investing.
- why: A hook normally takes "months of iteration" to lock (R09:70); cheap tests show early whether the idea plays and reads.
- apply: Feature-builder makes the hazard volume as a plain coloured Part with a damage script; level-design places it in the gray box; art and VFX only start after a playtest says the hook works. Keep the stand-in colour different from final art so nobody mistakes it for final [inference].
- check: Playtest log exists before the art brief; the art brief cites the playtest result [inference].
- mistake: Polishing the look of a mechanic nobody has played.
- example: A red crate texture stood in for lava; Michelle later made it look like lava.
- roblox_fit: Studio play-test via the Studio MCP is the "press play" equivalent; a Touched/region damage Part is the cheap stand-in [unverified for any specific API].
- roles: game-designer, level-design, environment-designer, feature-builder, orchestrator

### R09-F03 Keep red and fast motion out of the environment
- principle: Reserve red for gameplay meaning (enemy outlines, abilities that are dangerous or shootable) and avoid fast movement in the environment; keep the environment neutral so it does not clash with small gameplay pieces.
- why: The default enemy outline colour is red, and red is the shared language for "danger / you can shoot this"; red or moving set dressing competes with those signals. Fast motion pulls the eye from the fight.
- apply: Art-director writes a colour-role table in the art bible: which hues are reserved for gameplay signals (enemies, hazards, hit markers, damage UI) and banned or limited in set dressing. Environment and modeling keep large surfaces neutral; reference-images prompts state "neutral environment, no red accents" when red is reserved [inference on how to apply].
- check: tools/art/look_check.py palette on a game-camera capture: share of pixels in the reserved hue band outside gameplay objects (target close to 0, owner sets the number). Watch a 10 s capture for moving environment elements near sightlines [inference].
- mistake: Bright red, fast-moving set pieces ("red is normally a color we try to avoid, and then fast movement is also something we wanna avoid to not distract the player", R09:60-62).
- example: Lava is "bright red and it's on fire which is really fast", so it is the hardest possible element to fit into a VALORANT site.
- roblox_fit: Roblox Highlight can outline players/objects and is limited to 255 simultaneous instances on a client (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/effects/highlighting.md:37); whatever colour the game uses for enemy or danger highlights is the reserved hue. On phones small reserved-colour signals are fewer pixels, so environmental clashes cost more [inference].
- roles: art-director, environment-designer, vfx-designer, ui-ux-designer, modeler, reference-images, reviewer
- topic: light-colour (secondary: value-contrast, world-level-readability)

### R09-F04 Art that looks great is not always great for gameplay
- principle: There is a standing tension between art that looks great and art that plays great; always bridge it, and ask "how is my art enhancing gameplay?" rather than chasing a perfect piece of art.
- why: Players judge the place by how it plays; beautiful noise costs readability where play is most intense.
- apply: Every environment, prop and VFX brief names the gameplay read it must support (cover, sightline, route, hazard) and every review checks that read before beauty [inference].
- check: Reviewer's first question on a capture: "what is the gameplay read here, and does any art element weaken it?" before style questions.
- mistake: "You don't wanna get too lost in creating the perfect piece of art" (R09:1018-1020).
- example: Bombsites, where the most intense play happens, get the strictest readability priority (R09:56-58).
- roblox_fit: Same on Roblox; phone screens make the readability side heavier [inference].
- roles: art-director, environment-designer, modeler, vfx-designer, game-designer, reviewer
- topic: critique-feedback (secondary: world-level-readability, style-identity)

### R09-F05 A level is a puzzle with ability-shaped holes
- principle: Level design creates "glorified puzzles"; the player's abilities are the pieces that fit the holes. The line to walk is intriguing but not too frustrating.
- why: Spaces that require the player's tools to solve make those tools meaningful; too hard reads as unfair.
- apply: For each space, level-design lists which player tools or abilities solve it (smoke a sightline, jump a gap, break a line) and at least one non-obvious solution [inference].
- check: Playtest: can each listed tool actually solve the space? Is there a space no tool solves (frustration) or one solved by just walking (no puzzle)? [inference]
- mistake: not stated.
- example: In VALORANT, smokes, flashes and movement abilities are the puzzle pieces for each site.
- roblox_fit: Fits any Roblox game with player tools or abilities; for obbies, the "pieces" are jump, sprint and timing [inference].

### R09-F07 Separate working files per discipline, clear submission names
- principle: Split one map into sublevels per discipline (design blockout, lighting, art per area) so people do not overwrite each other, coordinate when overlap happens, and always give each submission a clear name so a broken build can be traced.
- why: Many people work on the same map in parallel; separation prevents collisions and clear names speed recovery.
- apply: Our studio: each role owns its own folder/model in the place (e.g. Blockout, Lighting, Art_SiteB) and its own files; commits say what changed; a crossing edit goes through the job's sideways-exchange note [inference mapping].
- check: No two roles edit the same Studio container in the same job without a logged hand-over; commit messages name the area and change.
- mistake: "Toe stepping" on shared files; breaking the build with an unclear submission (R09:126, 524-526).
- example: VALORANT maps use many sublevels ("usually there's way more than just three"); a good CL name lets people know what broke.
- roblox_fit: Rojo/file mode keeps code separate; place content separation is by folders/models in the data model [inference].

### R09-F08 One orientation convention across all maps
- principle: Use one fixed spatial convention across every map so players can orient in a new map at once (attackers: B site always on the left, A always on the right; defenders mirrored).
- why: Players transfer knowledge from old maps to new ones; "where's B? just look to your left".
- apply: game-designer and level-design define per game any cross-map conventions (shop always to the right of spawn, exit always ahead) and record them in the game profile; every new map keeps them [inference].
- check: New map review: list conventions, confirm each holds from the spawn view.
- mistake: not stated.
- example: Corrode, a new map: attackers find B by looking left.
- roblox_fit: Fits hubs and multi-map Roblox games with a fixed spawn camera direction [inference].
- roles: level-design, game-designer, environment-designer

### R09-F09 In-world wayfinding: subtle to obvious
- principle: Environment art adds in-world pointers to objectives, from subtle (a sign where all letters except A are worn out) to obvious (a big letter). Put site markers outside the site where players approach; put the biggest one on the landmark everyone sees coming in.
- why: Markers guide the approach; inside the site a marker is redundant but still reinforces.
- apply: Environment-designer places objective markers on approach routes and on the main landmark facing the approaching side; modeler builds them as decals or SurfaceGui-free texture details [inference].
- check: From each spawn route capture at the game camera: is an objective marker visible before entry? Is the main landmark's marker readable at thumbnail size (tools/art/thumb_check.py) [inference].
- mistake: Markers only inside the objective (redundant) instead of on the approach.
- example: Sunset's parking sign with only the A legible; a huge B on the tower facing attackers.
- roblox_fit: On phones, signs must be large to read; a Decal on the landmark is cheap [inference].

### R09-F10 Conflict at the objective makes the game; tie it to lore
- principle: A clear conflict built into the objective (attack to plant, defend to stop) generates the gameplay questions (how do attackers take space, how do defenders hold it). Tying the objective to the world's story gives art more to work with.
- why: Players need something to overcome; lore turns the objective into a place art can express.
- apply: game-designer states the conflict per mode in one sentence; environment-designer gets the lore reason for the objective so the place can show it [inference].
- check: Spec has the conflict sentence and the lore reason before blockout.
- mistake: not stated.
- example: The spike steals radianite between Alpha and Omega Earths; each map shows radianite differently.
- roblox_fit: Applies to any objective mode.

### R09-F11 Forward-facing paths, no doubling back
- principle: From spawn, paths should face forward so players feel they always progress; avoid turning around or looping back in multiplayer maps. Keep the layout simple so players navigate easily and reach fights faster.
- why: Doubling back "feels weird" in multiplayer; simple layouts reduce time-to-fun. A player who never gets lost is the sign of good level design.
- apply: level-design draws the route graph from each spawn; every edge from spawn to objective should not reverse direction more than [inference] 90 degrees; side loops only where intended. Exploration games (Metroidvania) may do the opposite deliberately.
- check: Top-down capture with routes drawn; walk-through capture: does the camera ever turn back to reach the objective? Time from spawn to first contact [inference].
- mistake: Dead-end-and-return layouts copied from exploration games into multiplayer maps.
- example: VALORANT spawns where everything faces forward vs Metroidvania backtracking.
- roblox_fit: Mobile players navigate with a thumbstick; reversals cost more [inference].
- roles: level-design, game-designer, environment-designer

### R09-F12 Playtest at any moment with the real tools
- principle: Make the blockout in the engine so you can press play at any moment and test an angle; test with the actual player tools (e.g. play a smoke character to test chokes and cover).
- why: Something that "looks really good" may not play correctly; only playing shows it.
- apply: level-design and feature-builder run Studio play-tests through the Studio MCP during blockout, not only after; each test names the tool tested (sprint, ability, weapon) [inference].
- check: Blockout hand-back lists play-tests run with what was found (angles closed, dead space found).
- mistake: Building the blockout in an external modeller and only testing late (other studios use Maya; VALORANT blocks out in engine for this reason, R09:869-878).
- example: Diego plays Omen to smoke chokes, then closes an angle and spots dead space.

### R09-F13 Plan around round-start positions
- principle: Know where players start each round (barrier drop points); do not build the map around them, but plan what the first seconds look like, because a fast early fight in one area cascades into decisions everywhere else. Sometimes a fast fight is wanted, sometimes slow space-taking.
- why: Opening timings set the pace of a round; cause and effect cascade across the map.
- apply: level-design computes spawn-to-contact times per route (walk speed x distance) and marks where opposing teams first meet; game-designer decides which meetings should be fast [inference on the timing method].
- check: Table of spawn-to-contact times per route; play-test confirms the intended first fights.
- mistake: Ignoring start positions so opening fights happen by accident.
- example: Ascent: a fast fight across mid off the barrier drop dictates how both teams play mid.
- roblox_fit: Round-based Roblox games with team spawns and start gates; WalkSpeed default 16 studs/s [unverified].
- roles: level-design, game-designer, architect
- topic: game-design-fun (secondary: rhythm)

### R09-F14 Flat play spaces, smooth head level, intentional verticality
- principle: Keep maps mostly flat; put elevation only in specific spots and keep ramps as short as possible. Keep things smooth at head level so players do not have to make huge flicks; verticality is allowed but must be intentional. A target a few pixels off the expected line can decide a fight.
- why: Crosshair placement matters; friction from terrain makes players feel the map killed them.
- apply: level-design sets a standard eye-height plane per area and lists every deliberate elevation spot with its reason; modeler keeps floors flat (no bumpy rocks in walk areas) [inference].
- check: Capture at the game camera along each route: count positions where a likely enemy sits far above or below the expected aim line; each must be in the intentional list [inference].
- mistake: Long ramps and random height changes; mossy rocks under feet that ruin crosshair placement (R09:1013-1015).
- example: Boxes someone can teleport onto force "a huge flick" — avoided unless intentional.
- roblox_fit: For third-person or top-down phone games the aim line matters less; the principle becomes "no surprise height that hides threats from the camera" [inference].
- roles: level-design, environment-designer, game-designer, modeler, qa-lead
- topic: world-level-readability (secondary: game-design-fun)

### R09-F15 Turn a boundary into a risk/reward hazard

### R09-F48 Characters must not blend into backgrounds
- principle: As the character roster grows, new characters can blend into older backgrounds, and some backgrounds become impossible. Too much environment detail lets abilities "crank that up to 11" and small ability objects get lost. Players stare at one angle for a long time, so it must be clean enough to take the fight.
- why: If a player blends into the background you "can't even track this person"; detail plus effects overloads the eye.
- apply: Art-director keeps a register of character and environment value/colour ranges; every new character is checked against every environment and vice versa [inference]. Character-rigger and modeler deliver character renders for this check.
- check: Capture each character against each environment at the game camera; look_check.py value contrast character vs background; an ability object on the busiest wall is visible.
- mistake: "If you're trying to find a sova dart on a super realistic brick wall, you're gonna get so lost in there" (R09:1003-1005).
- example: Characters blending in make you "go cross-eyed"; Sova dart on realistic brick.
- roblox_fit: Players wear any avatar colours, so environments should avoid matching common avatar ranges at player height [inference].

### R09-F49 Environment VFX with motion: tiny, slow, partly hidden
