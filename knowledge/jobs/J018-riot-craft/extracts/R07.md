## Findings

### R07-F01 Sound is storytelling across all audio layers
- principle: The job is to tell the story with every audio element: music, dialogue, sound design, backgrounds (ambience) and foley. Each one alone tells only part; edited and mixed to work together they carry the director's vision.
- why: Players and viewers read a moment from the combined result; a great SFX that fights the music or buries the ambience weakens the moment.
- apply: For every feature or scene, the sound-designer's cue card names the story point first ("the shot freezes the target"), then which layer carries which part (SFX: impact and freeze; ambience: cold world; music: tension; UI: confirmation). The orchestrator's brief names the moment's story, not just "add sound".
- check: Listen to the capture with only one category at a time, then all together: does each category have a job, and does the whole say the story point without the picture? [inference]
- mistake: Treating sound as separate assets made in isolation (implied by "music can only tell you so much", R07:33-34).
- example: The two supervisors describe their role on Arcane as storytellers putting the director's vision in place with all aural elements.
- roblox_fit: Same in game; the categories map to SoundGroups (music, SFX, ambience, UI) so they can be balanced together (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/sound/dynamic-effects.md:15, :59).

### R07-F02 The sound theme comes from the gameplay attribute
- principle: When applying a theme (ice) to a thing (the Zapper), tie it to what the thing does in play: the Zapper slows enemies, so the ice sound spreads and slows; a slow crumble sells the slowing.
- why: A sound that matches the mechanic teaches the mechanic; players hear "slow" and expect slow.
- apply: Cue cards start with the mechanic verb (slows, stuns, heals, knocks back) and pick sound textures that move like it: slowing = decelerating, spreading, crumbling textures with longer tails; fast = short, bright. The VFX and animation for the same ability get the same verb, so all three agree.
- check: Play the sound blind to someone (or a listening agent with the cue card hidden): can the effect verb be guessed? Compare tail length of a "slow" impact vs a normal one in the waveform [inference].
- mistake: Picking a theme only by look ("it's ice, add ice sounds") without the behaviour (not stated directly; inferred from R07:19-21).
- example: Ice drawn from a hat fits the Zapper because Zap slows; they plan "icy spreading, slowing"; foil gives a "slow crumble" for "ice spreading".
- roblox_fit: No change; status effects in Roblox games are often only shown by a small icon on a phone, so sound carries more of the read [inference].

### R07-F03 Signature tone for every weapon
- principle: One of the most important pieces of a weapon sound is a signature tone: one recognisable, usually tonal element that belongs to that weapon only.
- why: Players identify who fired what, off-screen and in crowds, by a recognisable tone; it also builds the weapon's identity.
- apply: Each weapon/ability cue card names its signature element (a ringing tone, a note, a specific "ting") and keeps it in every variant (load, shot, impact). Clean tonal parts are the best carriers (see F16).
- check: Line up the clips of all weapons in the game and listen in sequence: is each signature element distinct in pitch/timbre from the others? Can it be heard through the mix at the game camera with 4+ other effects playing [inference]?
- mistake: Generic gun or magic sounds that could belong to anything (inference from R07:41).
- example: For the ice Zapper they plan a mechanism, a "magical power source" and ice that "rings" while charging.
- roblox_fit: Many players fire at once in Roblox; a signature in the high mids carries better on phone speakers than a low boom [inference, unverified].
- roles: sound-designer, vfx-designer, art-director, game-designer, marketing

### R07-F04 Stage the action, one sound per stage
- principle: Before making anything, break the action into named stages and design a sound for each: load, charge, second charge, shot, OML ("On Missile Launch", the projectile travel), impact.
- why: Each stage is a beat the audience reads; staged sound gives anticipation (load, charge), release (shot), and payoff (impact).
- apply: The cue card for any ability lists the stages as rows with frame numbers from the animation/VFX (at 30 fps): e.g. load, charge, release, travel loop, impact, tail. The same stage names are used by animator, vfx-designer and feature-builder so markers, AnimationEvents and sound triggers line up.
- check: Count stages in the clip and in the sound list: every visible stage has a sound and none is unvoiced; trigger frames match the clip's marker frames (tools/anim/clip_check.py sample numbers vs the cue frame list).
- mistake: One single "shoot" sound for a multi-stage action [inference].
- example: Eliot names markers load, charge, charge two, shot, OML, impact, then places sounds on them.
- roblox_fit: In game the stages are triggered by code: animation markers (KeyframeMarker / GetMarkerReachedSignal) fire the load and charge sounds; the travel sound is a looped Sound parented to the projectile so it moves with it [inference; marker API not grepped here, unverified].

### R07-F05 Frame-accurate sync markers first
- principle: Start by going through the scene and placing frame-accurate markers at every synced event (an impact) and at every scene change (in and out points). The markers are the visual guide for a precise edit.
- why: Sound that lands a frame off reads as loose or late; a hit only feels like a hit when the sound's transient sits on the contact frame.
- apply: Animator or film-director exports the contact frames (30 fps) with the clip; sound-designer places each sound's transient on that frame, not the file start (trim any lead-in silence). For cutscenes, mark every cut.
- check: Frame-step the capture: transient peak in the waveform on the same frame as the visual contact (0 frames late is the target; 1 frame early is safer than late [inference]). Check every Sound file starts at its transient (no leading silence).
- mistake: Placing sounds by ear across a whole scene without sync points (inferred from R07:211-213).
- example: For the gauntlet fight scene, a frame-accurate marker on each gauntlet hit gives the sync point for that impact.
- roblox_fit: Network and play-start latency move sounds later on clients; trigger hit sounds on the client that sees the hit, and keep files trimmed [inference, unverified].
- roles: sound-designer, animator, film-director, vfx-designer, feature-builder, qa-lead
- topic: weight-timing (secondary: sound, rhythm)

### R07-F06 Design and editing are two crafts
- principle: Sound design creates something never heard before; editing makes those sounds work in the scene. Do them as separate passes.
- why: A sound that is great alone may not fit the picture; fitting needs its own judgement (timing, level, which layers stay).
- apply: Sound-designer works in two passes: (1) make building blocks and candidate sounds, (2) cut them to the markers in context of the capture and the other sounds, dropping what does not serve the moment.
- check: Review pass 2 against the capture, never the isolated file only.
- mistake: Judging sounds only in isolation [inference].
- example: Eliot calls editing "the second part of this job" and the two "totally different crafts".
- roblox_fit: Pass 2 happens in Studio play-tests at the game camera, with other players' and ambient sounds present [inference].
- roles: sound-designer, film-director, reviewer

### R07-F07 Any object can be a source; gather a lot
- principle: Sources need not be literal: ice was made from palm fronds, foil, seashells, bottles, a rain stick, a whistle and voice. Collect many candidates, record generously (normally an hour on one prop), and judge after. Always record interesting sounds when you meet them.
- why: Literal sources are rarely the most expressive; texture, not origin, is what is heard.
- apply: In our pipeline, "recording" is generating: for each cue, prompt the AI sound tool with several texture descriptions, not only the literal noun (e.g. "dry palm leaves crunching", "foil crumpling slowly", "seashells rattled in a hand", "glass bottle tink"), and generate several takes each (default 4-6 per texture [inference]); keep a library of good takes for reuse.
- check: Count candidates per layer before choosing (at least 3 [inference]); the chosen take is picked by listening, not by its label.
- mistake: Only searching for the literal thing ("I don't think we're gonna get ice. Let's create ice", R07:55).
- example: Palm frond "sounds the most like ice" and became the core ice texture; seashells gave a rocky "could-be-icy" texture.
- roblox_fit: Generated sounds must be uploaded as audio assets; keep a reusable library so they are not re-generated per feature [inference].
- roles: sound-designer, reference-images, orchestrator

### R07-F08 High frequencies for magic; pitch changes size
- principle: Higher-frequency material manipulates well into magic and gives it character. Pitching a sound changes its perceived size and reveals hidden overtones: ultrasonic recordings pitched down reveal detail; pitching up a pure tone gives a "high shimmery magical" feel; pitching down makes a sound less sharp ("spur-like").
- why: Pitch maps to size and weight in perception: higher = smaller, lighter, magical; lower = bigger, heavier.
- apply: Magic and ice layers start from bright, clean sources and are pitched up; heavy/big layers are pitched down. When a generated sound is too harsh, pitch it down before EQ.
- check: Look at the spectrum: magic layer energy mostly above the low-cut (see F11); compare two pitch versions in context and pick by size read.
- mistake: Not stated.
- example: Ninja sai recorded with the 100 kHz Sanken mic; sai pitched way up became the "magic ice" tone; spur sound to be pitched down.
- roblox_fit: Sound.PlaybackSpeed 2 = one octave up (twice as fast), 0.5 = one octave down (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/sound/objects.md:170-172); AudioPitchShifter changes pitch without speed and "make small sounds feel huge by pitching them down" (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/audio/effects.md:182-184). Small random PlaybackSpeed variation per play also avoids repetition [inference].
- roles: sound-designer, vfx-designer

### R07-F09 Movement inside the sound
- principle: A sound with movement in it (a spinning object moving between two stereo mics) has modulation that gives it character and a more interesting flavour than a static one.
- why: Static tones sound synthetic and flat; modulation reads as alive.
- apply: Ask the generator for motion ("spinning", "swirling", "passing") on magic and travel layers, or add slow movement in processing (chorus/flanger, F21; Doppler, F22).
- check: Listen for change across the sound's length; a waveform/spectrogram with visible slow variation, not a flat band [inference].
- mistake: Not stated.
- example: Sais spun in front of an X/Y stereo pair; the movement between mics gives "a cool tonality".
- roblox_fit: Positional 3D sound collapses most stereo width in the world; motion must be inside the file (modulation) or come from the emitter moving [inference; volumetric/point behaviour: knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/sound/objects.md:56].

### R07-F10 Building blocks and a shared library
- principle: Turn raw recordings into "building blocks" (sound Legos): clean and cut raw material, process it into usable parts, combine parts into bigger, crazier designs, and file everything (raw, processed, final) in a shared library others reuse.
- why: Reuse saves time and gives consistency; layered parts can be recombined for variants.
- apply: Sound-designer keeps a game sound library folder with three tiers: raw generated takes, processed parts (tonal, mech, crunch, low body, tail), finished cues. New cues start by browsing it. Cut out silence and junk before filing.
- check: Each finished cue lists the parts it uses; the library index has tags (texture, pitch, use) so a search finds them [inference].
- mistake: Rebuilding from scratch every time; also the self-critique that they did not lean on the deep library in the challenge (R07:602-604).
- example: Recordings chopped "into bite-sized chunks", filler removed, uploaded to the Riot library; sounds built "on top, building on top".
- roblox_fit: Roblox audio is asset IDs; keep a data table mapping cue names to asset IDs and parts so code never hard-codes IDs (AGENTS.md §2 content as data).
- roles: sound-designer, orchestrator, architect, live-ops-designer

### R07-F11 Subtractive EQ: cut first
- principle: Use EQ first to remove what is not wanted (low-end room rumble, air conditioner) and focus the sound, rather than boosting; accentuate the wanted part in other ways. Think of sound as sculpture.
- why: Unneeded lows make mixes muddy and mask other sounds; removing them makes room for each layer.
- apply: On every bright layer (magic, tick, UI), apply a high-pass/low-cut below its useful range (default: cut below ~200-300 Hz for shimmer/tick layers, no number in source [inference]); only the low-body layer keeps the lows.
- check: Spectrum of each layer: no energy below the cut on bright layers; only one layer per cue owns the low end.
- mistake: "A lot of folks will use EQ to boost frequencies" (R07:178).
- example: Low rumble in the sai recording hacked off below the EQ line to leave the "cool, pure, clean tone".
- roblox_fit: AudioEqualizer controls volume of frequency ranges (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/audio/effects.md:35-37); better to bake the cut into the file (F27). Phone speakers reproduce little low end anyway [inference, unverified].

### R07-F12 Compression to lift tails, and again after layering
- principle: Compression reduces dynamic range (loudest vs quietest); used aggressively it lifts the quiet shimmery tail after the attack. After adding echoes, overtones and delays, compress again so the new material sits at the level of the original.
- why: The tail is where the "magic" lives; uncompressed it disappears under the attack. New layers change dynamics, so the first pass no longer fits.
- apply: Process order: compress after cleaning, then again after adding reverb/delay/granular layers.
- check: Waveform: tail visibly sustained, not decaying to near-silence right after the attack; peaks controlled across layers [inference].
- mistake: Single compression pass before adding layers (implied by R07:293-294).
- example: "Incredibly aggressive" compression on the sai to bring out the shimmer; a second compression to get "more mileage out of the new tails".
- roblox_fit: AudioCompressor reduces dynamic range and can duck one stream under another (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/audio/effects.md:56-58; ducking via SoundGroup: sound/dynamic-effects.md:59). Heavy compression is better baked in; live compression is for mix ducking [inference].

### R07-F13 Shape the transient so the hit lands
- principle: Boost the very start (transient/attack) of an impact so it has more life and punch; add a separate transient element when the main layer is soft.
- why: The ear judges impact from the attack; a sharp onset reads as contact and weight.
- apply: Every hit/shot cue has a defined transient layer or transient boost; sound-designer marks the transient frame for sync (F05). VFX flash and animation contact frame use the same frame.
- check: Waveform: steep onset (peak within the first few ms [inference]); visual contact frame = audio transient frame.
- mistake: Not stated.
- example: TransMod used to "squeeze a little bit more life out of the beginning"; a rattly mech sound kept "for just helping out the transient on the gunshot"; boosted attack on the drum.
- roblox_fit: No live transient shaper in the audio effects list (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/audio/effects.md:33-228 lists equalizer, compressor, reverb, chorus, distortion, echo, flanger, pitch shift, tremolo, fader, analyzer), so bake it in the file.

### R07-F14 Reverb and delay give size and place
- principle: Reverb adds a tail so the sound "lives in the space"; delay/echo builds size; a sound without them feels dry. Place each element in depth (pull it "to the back of the room") so layers do not all sit at the front.
- why: Space tells the listener where and how big; consistent space across layers glues them into one event.
- apply: Cues get a space treatment matching the environment (open ice field = long, sparse echo; interior = short room). Distant or secondary layers get more reverb and lower level. Delay can push a sound "into a different universe" for magic.
- check: Listen in context: does the cue sound like it happened in the scene's space? Dry layer next to wet layers = fail [inference].
- mistake: "That feels a little dry" (R07:515) before adding a tail.
- example: Cinematic Rooms reverb and Slapper delays on the sai; the charge sound rendered with reverb so "it doesn't sound so dry"; end note delayed out and reverb added to push it back.
- roblox_fit: AudioReverb simulates reflections; AudioEcho repeats with diminishing volume (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/audio/effects.md:77-79, :140-142); SoundGroup/zone reverb can give one space per area so per-file reverb can stay short [inference].
- roles: sound-designer, environment-designer, level-design
- topic: sound (secondary: world-level-readability)
- lines: R07:249-254, R07:315, R07:515-518, R07:548, R07:580-583

### R07-F15 Processing chain order, small amounts
- principle: Build a sound through an ordered chain, each step adding a little character, "not too too much": pitch, low cut EQ, heavy compression, granular echo (overtones), transient boost, reverb, delay, compression again, exciter (air/brightness), saturation. Re-check by recording the result and looking at the waveform.
- why: Order matters (compressing after layering, cutting before boosting); small steps keep the core sound recognisable.
- apply: Sound-designer's default chain for a magic/tonal layer follows this order; for each step record what it was for. Generated sounds get the same order of fixes.
- check: Compare raw vs processed side by side ("plain Jane" vs "big, beefy" chimes); the core tone must still be there.
- mistake: Overprocessing (implied by "not too too much", R07:240).
- example: Brad's chain on the sai: pitch up, EQ cut, compression, Crystallizer, TransMod, Cinematic Rooms, Slapper, compression, Fresh Air, Saturn.
- roblox_fit: Order of live audio effects changes the result (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/audio/effects.md:27).
- roles: sound-designer, reviewer
- topic: sound
- lines: R07:165-177, R07:237-254, R07:304-317, R07:318-321

### R07-F16 Keep clean tonal layers separate
- principle: Keep clean, pure tonal components as their own layer, separate from mechanical and explosive layers, so each can be manipulated alone. A clean tone cuts through and carries the magic essence.
- why: Noisy and tonal content mixed in one file cannot be balanced; pure tones cut through a dense mix.
- apply: Every magic/ability cue is delivered as separate stems (tonal, mech, impact/noise, tail), combined in Roblox or rendered from a session where each is adjustable.
- check: Cue file list has the tonal stem separate; solo it: clean, no noise floor [inference].
- mistake: Not stated.
- example: Processed sai chimes are "one element and one tonal component of what that final design will be"; the synth chimes are "not noisy, just nice, pure stuff".
- roblox_fit: Separate Sounds per layer cost more instances and triggers; for phones, render to one file per stage once balanced [inference, unverified].

### R07-F17 Stacked synths for tonal parts
- principle: Generate tonal building blocks by stacking several synthesizers/virtual instruments (here a subtractive synth "fairy chimes" and a granular "crystal bell"), played together or one note at a time, recorded and used as ingredients.
- why: Stacking gives a richer, more unique tone than one source; one clear note stays clean.
- apply: For magic tones, generate 2-3 tonal sources in one key (e.g. same note C) and layer them; keep the result as a building block.

### R07-F18 The layered shot recipe
- principle: A weapon shot, even a fantasy one, usually needs something that feels like a real gunshot, built from layers: low-end body (a small drum with bass boost, distortion, heavy compression, transient boost), a high-frequency transient tick (metal ball on glass), a concussive crunch (palm frond) for the HF part of the transient, a report = the echo of the shot off the environment (pompom, filtered, distorted, limited), and a firing-pin "ting" left unprocessed.
- why: Each layer carries one perception: weight (low), contact (tick), violence (crunch), space (report), identity (ting).
- apply: Shot and impact cue cards list these five layer slots and which source fills each. Generate each layer separately and combine; the low body stays only in the shot/impact.
- check: Solo each layer: does it do its one job? Remove one at a time: does the shot lose weight/contact/space? [inference]
- mistake: Their own critique: with more time they would use "better gunshot, actual transient" (R07:599).
- example: A kid's drum, buckyballs on a bottle, palm fronds and pompoms combined in a sampler gave "a loose approximation of a gunshot".
- roblox_fit: Phones lose sub-bass; the tick and crunch carry the shot on small speakers, so check on phone-like output [inference, unverified]. Many simultaneous shots: lower the report/tail layers for distant shooters via RollOff (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/sound/objects.md:123-125).

### R07-F19 Charge-ups rise

### R07-F21 Phase effects make new sounds from one recording
- principle: Pitch, flanger, phaser and chorus all manipulate phase/pitch copies of one signal; running a source through them yields new textures. But processing is optional: if the natural sound works, use it.

- apply: Variants of a cue (e.g. upgraded weapon tier) can be made by phase/pitch processing of the base, keeping the family recognisable [inference].
