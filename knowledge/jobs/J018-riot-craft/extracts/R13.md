## Findings

### R13-F01 Recognisable in the first second beats unique
- principle: Among concept options, choose the one players identify correctly within the first second, even if it looks "basic, safe" and another option has a more unique shape. An ambiguous shape that could be another character's item is a big problem.
- why: Artists are "visual communicators"; viewers must understand what they look at "within the first second". A shape that could be "Senna's gun, or Galio's wing" fails that.
- apply: When choosing among reference-image options (the reference-images skill makes several), score each for "does a player name the subject/owner/function in 1 s"; pick the highest, then add uniqueness inside that read. Write the one-second read as the first acceptance line in the brief.
- check: Show the option at thumbnail size (thumb_check.py / look_check.py thumbnail size) and in black-fill silhouette; ask a fresh agent "what is this and whose is it?" with 1-second-glance framing; fail if the answer is wrong or names another subject.
- mistake: Picking the most interesting shape that "doesn't register immediately" as the intended subject (R13:56-58).
- example: Option 2 had the best shape but could read as Senna's gun or Galio's wing; option 3 was chosen because it reads instantly as Zilean.
- roblox_fit: Phone screens and a distant third-person or top camera shrink props to a few dozen pixels, so the one-second read must survive thumbnail size [inference].

### R13-F04 Light and shadow blocking is step one
- principle: The first step is to set one light direction and block in light, shadow and cast shadows quickly, without caring about clean edges; lighting on special materials (golden plates, bloom) comes second.
- why: The overall lighting scheme decides form and readability; edges can be cleaned later.
- apply: In Blender/Studio set the key light direction before any detail work (one sun/key direction, cast shadows on); in reference prompts state the light direction. Polish edges later.
- check: First review capture at game camera: is there one clear light direction and readable cast shadows? Fail a detail pass that starts before that.
- mistake: Trying "to get to the perfect edge, the perfect line" at this stage (R13:89-93).
- example: Light set from upper side, cast shadows blocked on the shrine, then airbrush bloom on the golden plates.
- roblox_fit: Studio Lighting has one sun/moon direction set by time of day and GeographicLatitude; choose it per scene early (environment/lighting.md) [unverified detail].
- roles: art-director, environment-designer, modeler, reference-images, level-design

### R13-F05 Decisive marks: viewers see hesitation
- principle: Work with tools that force a decision per mark (opaque 100% hard round brush, no blending) and do not rework the same area repeatedly; "they can see your fear if you show it in your brush."
- why: Repeated, indecisive strokes read as unsure and weak; decisive shapes read as confident and design the gesture fast.
- apply: For agents: prefer few, bold shape decisions over many small adjustments. In Blender, change a shape with one deliberate edit and re-render, not dozens of micro tweaks; in reference prompts ask for "bold, confident, clean shapes, hard edges" and avoid blurred, overworked paint-overs. Accept a mistake and fix it later rather than averaging options together.
- check: In the render or image, look for muddy, overworked zones (many tiny value steps, blurry edges); fail where a shape boundary is unclear.
- mistake: "If you repeat your brush too much, they'll know that you're not sure what you're doing" (R13:105-107).
- example: Default Photoshop hard round brush at 100% opacity used for all shape design.
- roblox_fit: No change.

### R13-F06 Hue-shifted shadows, not darker local colour
- principle: Shadow colours are not just a darker version of the local colour; shift the hue so shading is not monochrome.
- why: Darker-only shading is "very boring"; playful colour in shadows is part of the game's charm.
- apply: In Blender materials/vertex paint and in Studio, set shadow tints with a hue shift (e.g. warm or purple-ish shadows under cool light, see F29); in Roblox, tint Lighting.Ambient/OutdoorAmbient rather than leaving neutral grey [inference]. Ask reference images for "coloured shadows".
- check: Sample shadow and lit pixels of the same material in a capture (look_check.py palette): shadow hue should differ from lit hue, not only value.
- mistake: "picking the local color and going for a darker version of it" (R13:111-113).
- example: Shrine shadows painted with different hues, "not for TFT" monochrome.
- roblox_fit: Lighting.Ambient and OutdoorAmbient colour the unlit areas (environment/lighting.md) [unverified exact property behaviour].

### R13-F07 Screen priority: UI and VFX, then characters, then environment
- principle: The most important things in the game composition are UI and VFX; characters come second; environment art should always be the backdrop.

- apply: Write this order into the art bible; environment and props get lower contrast and saturation than characters; characters lower than VFX/UI (see F31).

- mistake: Environment that grabs attention.
- example: TFT board: UI and spell VFX over units over arena.
- roblox_fit: Roblox scenes often have many players and effects at once on a small phone screen, so this order matters more [inference].

### R13-F08 Solve problems in 2D before 3D; paint over screenshots for feedback
- principle: Always have a concept before 3D; when feedback arrives on a 3D asset, screenshot it, paint over it in 2D, then apply the changes to 3D.
- why: In 3D a change means model, UVs and more; in 2D it is "a couple of strokes", so iteration is cheap and you do not get "lost in the forest".
- apply: Before Blender work, have an approved reference image. On feedback, take a Studio/Blender capture at the game camera and ask the reference-images skill for a paint-over of that capture showing the fix; get it approved; then edit the model.
- check: Every 3D change after review traces to an approved paint-over or reference; no unreviewed exploratory 3D rebuilds.
- mistake: Making exploratory changes directly in 3D.
- example: Feedback screenshot cropped and painted over in Photoshop with three notes (R13:826-848).

### R13-F09 Big, medium, small; kill "half and half" shapes
- principle: Never make shapes the same size; make one huge or big, one medium, one small to create rhythm. Equal-size "half and half" shapes compete and the viewer cannot tell which to look at first. It is the first thing he fixes in other people's paint-overs and is hard to get rid of because humans default to it (like a child's drawing where house, sun and tree are the same size).
- why: Size contrast creates hierarchy and rhythm; equal sizes compete and look dull.
- apply: For any group (rocks, plants, crystals, debris, UI elements, particles), set size ratios before placing; a sound default is roughly 3 : 2 : 1 or larger steps [inference]. Use few instances with clear size steps; vary plant heights (low and tall). Same applies to VFX particle sizes and UI element sizes.
- check: In a capture or black-fill render, measure the bounding sizes of sibling elements; fail when two or more neighbours are within about 15% of each other in size [inference threshold].
- mistake: "half and half shapes always tend to compete against each other and make the painting look bad" (R13:223-225); "three of them are looking very similar in sizes? That is not good" (R13:947-949).
- example: Three similar rocks fixed to one big, one medium, one small; feedback "one plant is boring" fixed with big, medium, small, low and tall plants.
- roblox_fit: Also lowers instance count: a few varied pieces beat many equal ones on phones [inference].

### R13-F10 From line work to value
- principle: Line work is useful early to know the shapes, but later it hurts, especially when translating to 3D; collapse it into the base and describe how form turns with value. "You are working on a 2D image, but you are trying to tell the story in 3D."
- why: Value, light, perspective and shape flow create the volumetric illusion a modeler can read; lines do not describe form.
- apply: Reference images for modelers should be rendered with light and shadow (form described by value), not line art; ask for "painted, no outlines" once the design is chosen. Modelers read planes from the value changes.
- check: View the reference in grayscale: can each main plane change be seen? If form is only in outlines, request a value pass before modeling.
- mistake: Keeping heavy line work that hurts the final result.
- example: A line on a rock suggests a face turning in 3D; it is painted as a value change.

### R13-F11 Shape flow guides the eye to the focal point
- principle: Use lines and secondary shapes (bushes, cracks, edges) that frame and point at the one main focal point, so the eye is led there.
- why: Viewers' eyes follow directional shapes; the focal point gets read first.
- apply: Choose the focal point (here the golden sand disk) first; orient plants, rock edges, debris and lighting gradients toward it; in levels, point props and paths at the objective.
- check: At game camera, draw or imagine the main directions of secondary shapes; most should converge on the focal point; fail if they lead off-screen or to a minor element.
- mistake: Secondary shapes pointing away or at random.
- example: Bushes all point toward the golden disk.
- roblox_fit: Under a moving third-person camera the flow must work from the usual approach angles, not one view [inference].

### R13-F13 Zoom out; details are an illusion
- principle: Work and judge zoomed out; do not spend time on intricate small details. Scribbly marks inside a correct value structure read as detail from a distance. The image must look good with or without small details.
- why: Viewers read the big picture; detail is perceived, not counted (painters with fading vision got better by losing detail).
- apply: Spend effort on big shapes and values first; texture detail only where the game camera can resolve it. Judge every model and texture from the game camera distance, not close-up.
- check: Review captures at game camera and at thumbnail size before any close-up; a piece that only works zoomed in fails.
- mistake: "zooming in and try to paint all those intricate little corners, little highlights. Don't do that." (R13:317-319)
- example: Scribbles on the golden plate read as detail when zoomed out.
- roblox_fit: Phones show assets small, and Roblox loads lower texture quality first on weak devices (art/modeling/texture-specifications.md:24), so fine detail is often lost anyway.
- roles: art-director, modeler, environment-designer, vfx-designer, reference-images, reviewer

### R13-F14 Gather enough references; take ideas, do not copy
- principle: Build a reference board before starting, tied to the theme (an existing skin that shares the set's materials, colour refs, plant shape refs); what he shows is less than 10% of what he normally gathers. Take ideas (shape language, colour and light use), never copy a design.

### R13-F17 A scale reference in every scene
- principle: Always add some small element of known size (plants, or anything players recognise) so viewers can judge scale; it also makes the focal point look more prominent.
- why: Without a known-size object, players cannot tell how big the main object or their character is.
- apply: Place known-size items (plants, doors, steps, fences) next to big focal props; in Roblox, compare to the default avatar (about 5-6 studs tall) [inference].

### R13-F21 Project the concept onto the model as a start
- principle: Project the 2D concept onto the 3D model from a matching view as a rough first texture, then fill unprojected gaps and clean up. It is not one-to-one, but it is "better than nothing."

- mistake: Leaving unprojected gaps or stretched projections.

### R13-F24 Polish = sharp edges, balanced with soft

### R13-F26 Low-poly painted workflow keeps changes cheap
- principle: In the hand-painted workflow you start low-poly directly (no high-poly bake); changing the model (e.g. making a plant bigger) is easy: re-export and replace geometry, and the painted texture updates live.

### R13-F28 Fixed camera and phone budgets

### R13-F31 Value and colour range per layer
