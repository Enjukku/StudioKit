## Findings

### R04-F01 Interpolation is not animation
- principle: Putting in two key poses and pressing play gives a linear blend, which twists the body through itself because controls live in different spaces. The animator must "fight the computer" and place the in-betweens on purpose.
- why: A linear blend has no weight, no arcs and no intent; limbs take the shortest path through the body, so it reads as mechanical ("looks like trash").
- apply: Never ship a clip with only start and end keys. In Blender, after setting key poses, add breakdowns for root, feet and arms and scrub every frame for body parts that pass through each other. For Roblox KeyframeSequence, every Pose gets an explicit EasingStyle; do not rely on Linear between far-apart poses.
- check: Render the clip at 30 fps from the game camera and step frame by frame: no limb passes through the torso, knees do not invert, feet do not drift. clip_check.py: sample the root and foot positions; a straight-line path between two keys over more than ~6 frames [inference] flags a missing breakdown.
- mistake: "If you just press play after you put the poses in, things should look good theoretically" (R04:66-68), and the body "twisting inside of herself" (R04:70).
- example: Akali's kneel and finger-heart poses blended directly twist her body into a mess (R04:64-76); the leg swoop does not happen until the foot is moved by hand (R04:148-156).
- roblox_fit: Roblox KeyframeSequence poses interpolate per Enum.PoseEasingStyle (Linear, Constant, Elastic, CubicV2, ...) (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/_api-reference/enums/PoseEasingStyle.yaml:22-60); there is no automatic arc, so arcs must be keyed.

### R04-F02 Root first, then layers
- principle: Work in layers: first the root (centre of gravity / COG), keeping the whole body keyed on the same frames so timing is easy to change, then add poses at the key moments, then offset parts, then details.
- why: The centre of mass carries the weight story; if the root path and timing are right, the rest follows. Keying the full body on shared frames keeps retiming cheap during blocking.
- apply: In Blender by Python: pass 1 keys only the root/hips control (position and rotation) plus feet; pass 2 keys full-body poses on the same frames; pass 3 offsets limbs; pass 4 secondary (hair, fingers, face). Retime in pass 1-2 by moving whole-body keys.
- check: After pass 1, render the root-only version from the game camera: does the root path alone read as the action (rise, hang, fall, settle)? clip_check.py root Y curve shows one clear rise and fall with holds where intended.
- mistake: Polishing a pretty pose before the root timing works; pose-to-pose people "smooth things out as they go along" (R04:86-88) - she notes her method "looks weird and bad for a long time" first (R04:90).
- example: She builds the whole move with just the root before saying "there's no pose in the air where we've just animated the root", then adds poses (R04:522-526); "I keep everything keyed on like the whole body through these poses ... so that I can easily adjust the timing" (R04:436-437).
- roblox_fit: Same in Blender; the root becomes HumanoidRootPart / LowerTorso motion in the exported clip. Root translation in a Roblox clip moves the visual, not the physics body [unverified].

### R04-F03 Weight off before the foot moves
- principle: A character standing on two feet cannot lift or slide one foot until its weight has shifted onto the other limb. Show at least a little contact and where the weight went, even in a fast, bursty game move.
- why: Viewers know from their own bodies that lifting a loaded foot means falling over; skipping the shift breaks believability of the whole move.
- apply: Before any foot lifts, key the root moving over the planted foot (lean or sink) for at least a few frames. For snappy gameplay moves the shift can be very short (game engine blending may cover the gap), but keep one frame showing contact and weight direction.
- check: Step the clip: on the frame a foot leaves the ground, the root's ground projection must sit over the other support (foot or hand). clip_check.py: horizontal distance from root to the support foot at lift-off frame should be small [inference: compare against stance width].
- mistake: Named as the most frequent demo-reel note: "missing the clear transfer of weight from one foot to another, one limb to another"; characters "standing there on both feet and then they will just move a foot" (R04:456-460).
- example: To swoop Akali's back leg she first has to "pull her weight off of that foot somehow" (R04:98-100); later she shifts weight "a little earlier so that she's starting that weight shift. So it makes sense that she can move this other foot" (R04:662-666).
- roblox_fit: Gameplay clips on Roblox are short and fade into each other with AnimationTrack fadeTime (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/_api-reference/classes/AnimationTrack.yaml:251); the weight shift can live in the first frames of the next clip, but must exist.

### R04-F04 Hold the first pose before moving
- principle: Lock the starting pose for a few frames so the character does not move immediately.
- why: The viewer needs to register the start pose before change begins; an instant move from frame 0 reads as a pop.
- apply: Key the full rig unchanged at frame 0 and about frame 5 (her demo value) before the first motion key. For gameplay actions where response time matters, keep this to the minimum the design allows [inference].
- check: Frames 0 to 5 identical in clip_check.py samples (pose delta near zero) for showcase clips; for gameplay clips check the hold against the game designer's input-response budget.
- mistake: not stated (implied: moving "immediately from this pose", R04:106).
- example: "pop the frame five and just lock her down so she doesn't move there for a little bit" (R04:108).
- roblox_fit: Gameplay responsiveness on Roblox (network plus input) may forbid long starting holds; use this mainly for emotes, idles, showcase and cutscene clips [inference].
- roles: animator, vfx-designer, sound-designer

### R04-F05 Head stable in world space
- principle: Keep the head (and shoulders) controlled in world space so they hold their orientation while the body turns under them; turn the head late and fast, like a ballerina spotting.
- why: A stable head gives balance and "interesting motion ... for free"; dancers keep the head locked because it makes sense for balance and stability.
- apply: In the Blender rig, give head (and optionally shoulder) controls a world-space orient option or key the head to counter body rotation. In a spin, keep the head facing the old direction, then snap it to the new direction in a few frames.
- check: Frame-by-frame: during a body turn the head's world yaw changes less and later than the chest yaw. clip_check.py: compare head vs torso yaw curves; head curve should lag then catch up quickly.
- mistake: The head simply rotating with the body (implied). She warns world space makes spins harder to key (R04:142-144).
- example: Akali's head keeps the initial rotation as her body turns (R04:136-140); she turns her head "quickly, like a ballerina" (R04:946-948).
- roblox_fit: Roblox Motor6D rigs have Neck under UpperTorso; world-space behaviour must be baked into keys in Blender because the exported clip stores local joint transforms [unverified].
- roles: animator, character-rigger
- topic: weight-timing (secondary: character-design)
- lines: R04:134-146, R04:940-948

### R04-F06 Pose for the game camera
- principle: Check poses from the camera the game will use; move or rotate limbs so the silhouette reads, avoiding limbs pointing straight at the camera (foreshortening).
- why: A leg aimed at the lens collapses into a short shape and looks boring; a side-on bent knee reads as a "cool shape".
- apply: Set a fixed Blender camera that matches the Roblox game camera (third-person over-shoulder or top-down) and judge every key pose through it. Rotate the character's feet or torso a few degrees toward profile when a limb would foreshorten.
- check: Black-fill silhouette render from the game camera at each key pose (look_check.py silhouette): both knees and the extended limb should be visible as separate shapes, not overlapping the torso.
- mistake: "The leg looking directly down the camera ends up looking a little bit more boring and a little less dynamic" (R04:766).
- example: She moves the kicked leg back because it ended "a little closer to camera than I think I want" for "a nicer silhouette on the end of that kick" (R04:158-161); turns Akali's feet sideways because "we do see her from the side" (R04:760-764).
- roblox_fit: Roblox cameras are player-controlled in many games, so also check from the two or three most common angles (behind, side, top) on a phone-size render [inference].
- roles: animator, film-director, art-director, reviewer
- topic: shape-silhouette (secondary: composition-focus)

### R04-F07 Animation as the glue between crafts
- principle: Animation sits between tech art and audio, staggered with VFX; the animator agrees frame lengths with design, needs tools from engineering, pairs with effects and audio for impact, and should join early to say what can and cannot be done.
- why: Effects often "elevate animation beyond anything that we could do" and audio adds "oomph"; late involvement means infeasible requests or wasted potential.
- apply: The animator joins at design time (game-designer brief) and returns the frame counts and event markers (contact, release, impact) that VFX and sound key from. Any timing change goes to VFX and sound in the same hand-back.
- check: Each gameplay clip has a written frame length agreed with the game designer and named markers listed in the contract; VFX and sound cue cards reference those markers.
- mistake: Not involving animators early; doing animations without confirming "the right frame lengths" (R04:172-174).
- example: She describes animation as "a bit of a hub" and a "go-between" (R04:180-182).
- roblox_fit: Roblox KeyframeMarker / GetMarkerReachedSignal lets VFX and sound fire on animation markers [unverified].
- roles: animator, game-designer, vfx-designer, sound-designer, orchestrator, feature-builder

### R04-F08 Read the numbers behind the motion
- principle: Look at each control's per-axis curve, not only the moving picture; odd motion shows up as curves "going crazy", and a flat stretch shows where travel stops ("hitting this wall").
- why: The eye misses small pops when everything moves at once; a single-axis curve shows exactly where and when.
- apply: Use clip_check.py (sampled clip numbers) or Blender fcurve dumps per control and axis (translate Y = up/down) to find spikes, flips and flat sections.
- check: For each major control, print sampled values per frame: look for sudden jumps (possible flip/pop), unintended plateaus (stalls), and spikes past the key values (unintended overshoot).
- mistake: Judging only by eye with the whole body moving.
- example: Translate X/Z curves flattening means "she's not having any more move horizontally through space" (R04:606-612).
- roblox_fit: Roblox Animation Editor has its own Curve Editor with tangents (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/animation/curve-editor.md:61-65); our pipeline does the reading in Blender and clip_check.py.
- roles: animator, qa-lead, reviewer
- topic: pipeline-tech (secondary: weight-timing)
- lines: R04:194-211, R04:604-612

### R04-F09 Mass cannot vanish
- principle: When mass is on one side and the character suddenly faces the other, there must be a clear transfer of energy; "a very sudden absence of space where something once was" reads wrong.
- why: Newtonian intuition: energy and carried mass "has to go somewhere"; a pop breaks belief.
- apply: Between two poses with a big mass change, add a breakdown where the mass visibly moves (lean, arm out, step). Frame the question each time: where does this weight go?
- check: Frame-by-frame: the silhouette's mass centre (or root) moves continuously; no single frame where a large body volume disappears from one side. Black-fill silhouette strip of consecutive frames shows a smooth mass shift.
- mistake: The body that "just pops in this other direction" (R04:250).
- example: Akali's tight spin with the body facing screen-left must not pop to the other direction (R04:248-254).
- roblox_fit: Same for VFX bursts and props that change direction [inference].
- roles: animator, vfx-designer, reviewer

### R04-F10 Counterbalance makes fast moves believable
- principle: Other body parts counter a broad move (arm out to catch balance, hand "touching the ground"); that visible counter-effort lets a very fast move feel possible.
- why: The viewer accepts speed when the body clearly braces against it.
- apply: For each large swing, add an opposite reaching limb or a hand support for 2-4 frames [inference on count].
- check: In the key frames of the swing, find the counter limb moving opposite to the swinging limb; it exists and is visible from the game camera.
- mistake: A swoop done "super quick" without counter, "not super believable" (R04:482).
- example: Arm out to counterbalance during the swoop (R04:240-242, R04:256); a hand touching the ground makes the quick swoop believable (R04:482-488).
- roblox_fit: no change [inference].
- roles: animator, reviewer

### R04-F11 The driving limb leads; carry momentum
- principle: Identify what drives the motion (here the leg, powered by the obliques) and let the root lean with it; do not stick hard into each pose but swing out and into the next so momentum carries.
- why: Force has a source; momentum flowing through poses feels alive, hard stops feel robotic.
- apply: Key the driver first and let the root follow a frame or two later [inference]; overshoot slightly past a pose and settle into it instead of arriving dead on.
- check: Driver limb curves lead the root curve; arriving at a pose, motion decelerates over several frames, not on one frame.
- mistake: Sticking "so hard to this last pose" (R04:262).
- example: "the leg is what should really be driving that motion"; "she's carrying the momentum into the squat" (R04:260-264).
- roblox_fit: no change [inference].

### R04-F12 Holds as copied pairs
- principle: Create a hold by duplicating the key a few frames later ("copied pairs") instead of stepping all keys; this keeps the real timing visible while blocking.
- why: Stepped keys hide the in-between timing; copied pairs make the pause clearly a pause while the motion still plays.
- apply: In Blender by Python, copy all channels of a key to a later frame to make a hold. Stepped (constant) interpolation is fine for a quick pose review only.
- check: Holds show as two identical keys with a flat curve between; preview with normal interpolation, not stepped.
- mistake: Relying only on stepped keys and losing the sense of timing (R04:278).
- example: "I will duplicate the key that I previously had to create a hold" (R04:282).
- roblox_fit: Roblox's equivalent of stepped is PoseEasingStyle Constant (knowledge/external/roblox-creator-docs/_api-reference/enums/PoseEasingStyle.yaml:29-33).
- roles: animator
- topic: rhythm (secondary: pipeline-tech)

### R04-F13 Know which joints can break
- principle: Anatomy knowledge tells you which joints can be stretched, held unnaturally long or smeared without looking wrong; when a motion is hard, think about it at a bare anatomical level.
- why: Smears and stretches read as speed when placed on believable joints, and as broken rigs when not.
- apply: Smear or stretch only along limbs in the direction of travel for 1-2 frames [inference]; use real muscle logic (wind-up before a throw) when stuck.
- check: Step the frames of a smear: the stretched part follows the motion path and returns within a frame or two; no reverse bends at elbows/knees.
- mistake: Breaking the character in ways that look weird (R04:296).
- example: Smear frames: "a couple things that are unnaturally stretched, held unnaturally long" (R04:298); figure models holding a pre-throw pose to show flexing muscles (R04:304-306).
- roblox_fit: Roblox Motor6D (R6/R15) rigs cannot scale or stretch limbs in standard clips; smears must come from VFX Trails/Beams or skinned-mesh bone scaling [unverified].
- roles: animator, modeler, character-rigger
- topic: exaggeration (secondary: weight-timing)
- lines: R04:294-300, R04:306-312

### R04-F14 Cartoony hang time
- principle: For a heroic jump, extend the time at the top (a small hold near the apex, "tabletop" arc) and then let gravity take over very quickly so the body is "vacuumed" back down.
- why: Hang time sells strength and style; the fast fall keeps it from looking floaty.
- apply: Real jump: fast rise, gradual slowdown, fall. Toony jump: rise, flattened top with a short hold, then a faster fall than the rise. Agree the hang with the game designer if the jump is gameplay.
- check: Root Y curve: a flat or near-flat section at the apex, then a steep descent shorter than the ascent [inference on ratio]. Watch at 30 fps: no floaty fall.
- mistake: Falling too slowly or too quickly; she corrects "she's falling a little quick" by retiming (R04:354).
- example: Akali leaps "higher than people can normally jump" and hangs before landing (R04:336-352).
- roblox_fit: Gameplay jumps in Roblox are physics-driven by Humanoid; hang time applies to animation layered over it or to scripted moves [unverified].
- roles: animator, vfx-designer, sound-designer, game-designer
- topic: exaggeration (secondary: weight-timing)
- lines: R04:336-352

### R04-F15 Uneven rhythm
- principle: Animation on an even cadence feels predictable; a varied, "jazzy" rhythm of fast and slow beats is exciting.
- why: Viewers feel rhythm like music; sameness lowers attention.
- apply: Vary the gaps between key beats (not all keys N frames apart). Do the same for VFX bursts, UI animation sequences and sound hits.
- check: List the frame gaps between the main beats; they should not be all equal. Play with sound off and tap along: if the beat is metronomic, vary it.
- mistake: "everything is on the exact same cadence becomes predictable" (R04:360-362).
- example: "swoop up and then she needs a little down" retimed by feeling the rhythm (R04:354-356); "ka-du-doom dah" (R04:364).
- roblox_fit: no change [inference].
- roles: animator, vfx-designer, sound-designer, film-director, ui-ux-designer
- topic: rhythm

### R04-F16 Land low and strong
- principle: Landing straight up is not impactful; land lower into a strong pose, hold the bottom a little, then rise.
- why: Absorbing the fall shows weight and power.
- apply: On landing, key the root lower than standing height with bent knees; hold the bottom a few frames; then rise to the end pose.
- check: Root Y minimum at landing is clearly below standing height; silhouette at the landing frame is a strong, readable pose.
