## 1. Who does what in each stage (numbered)

"Hand-run" = the lead's own chat spawns every agent with the `Agent` tool and keeps `crew.md` (CO:95, SK:17, SK:44). "Saved workflow" = the lead calls `/job-<type>` for one stage; a script spawns the agents and returns one result object; there are no agent ids and no scouts inside (CO:79, CO:95, WFR:7-24). Both run the same steps; the workflow fixes the order and caps (WFR:7-24).

1. **Owner message arrives (lead only).** The lead loads the `orchestration` skill at the start of every owner request (AGENTS.md §1; SK:12). It reads the message twice, checks `knowledge/jobs/active.md`, other chats and `git status`, and writes the four lines (SK:13). Belief check (spawns the analyst, Sonnet medium) only when a claim about current players would change the direction, no analyst reading under 21 days exists, and the game has player data (SK:14). The lead sizes S/M/L and picks the recipe (SK:15; CO:21-28). S: the lead does it or hands it to one role, self-review plus one domain check, shows the result, stops (SK:16).
2. **Open the job (lead; M and L).** Check the recipe's prerequisites; re-list `knowledge/jobs/`; copy `templates/jobs/`; write the job card (owner's words verbatim, four lines, size, type, job lead, direction leads, crew, stop floor and bar, budgets, acceptance checks, gates); add to `active.md`; start `crew.md` for hand-run stages; brief the direction leads with the packet line (SK:40-45). Files the lead writes: card, `active.md`, `crew.md`, `decisions.md`, `ledger.md`, inbox drafts (KN:15).
3. **Direction stage (D1-D3).**
   - Saved workflow: one `stage: direction` call. The script runs D1 (the direction leads in parallel, none seeing the others), D2 (each lead reads the others' files, lists only production-changing conflicts), D3 (the job lead writes `direction.md`, other leads sign or name an incompatibility, one resolution round if any signer objects). The cutscene stage is **11 agent runs** (4 + 4 + 1 + 2 signers; +1 for a resolution round), counted from WF:263-340.
   - Hand-run: the lead spawns each direction lead (depth 1). A subagent job lead "directs through `direction.md`, the contract and briefs it drafts for the lead to send" (CO:95).
   - Who writes: each lead its own `direction/proposal-<role>.md`; exchange notes in `exchange/`; the job lead `direction.md`; signers `direction/sign-<role>.md` (WF:270-340). The lead writes none of these.
   - Who reads: the lead reads the stage result and each hand-back's `coverage` against the files, not the summary (SK:116; SK:19).
4. **G1 (lead and owner).** The lead builds the one-screen packet in the SK:78 order (artifact as players meet it, goal question, options at equal finish, recommendation, checks, doors closed, usage). It runs pending image batches with the owner's yes (SK:19). **The lead waits for the owner here**: a workflow cannot pause, so no next stage starts before the gate answer (SK:19; CAP:24). The lead records the owner's words and the approved revision in `decisions.md` and each option not picked as a closed door (SK:79).
5. **Proof stage (L only; M runs the proof inside `build` and shows it at G3: CO:27).**
   - Job lead drafts the contract (stable ids, revision, Consumers table); the architect fills code file owners (SK:55).
   - Needs round: each consumer writes its needs against the contract draft before its producer builds; consumers check producers; the decision owner merges the next revision; at most 2 rounds (SK:56; CO:60).
   - Cheap integrated proof made by only the crafts it needs; the recipe's proof reviewers review it in order (cutscene: art-director, then architect; up to 3 loops, then `needs-decision`: WF:497-532).
   - **G2** (lead and owner wait, as at G1).
6. **Build stage.**
   - Craft planning (cutscene: 7 planning agents plus a lock by the film-director = 8 runs: WF:589-634).
   - Production in parallel along the Consumers table; decision owners check in at named points; "no grand reveals" (SK:62).
   - Integration: one integrator, one Studio driver at a time (CO:64, CO:135; WF:745-755).
   - Review chain on one frozen candidate (CO:98): scripts → maker self-review → next-craft check → domain lead → technical/QA beside the integrated creative order → lead readiness. The cutscene chain is **7 agents per round** (scripts, qa-lead, performance-engineer, reviewer, film, art, sound-sync; +2 for the `event` risk) before any repair (WF:763-835).
   - Repairs go back to the makers in dependency order; the candidate is re-imported and recaptured; 2 rounds (code 3), then `needs-decision` (WF:837-847).
7. **Between stages (lead).** Read every hand-back's `coverage` against the files; record the owner's words and the doors closed; update `active.md` and `crew.md`; write inbox drafts from the hand-backs' `lesson` fields; run `images_pending` batches; start the next stage with the gate answer as input; start a fresh session when this chat is past 500 k context (SK:19; CL:45; J023 decisions D1).
8. **Readiness and G3 (lead).** Every acceptance check has evidence at the candidate's revision; the lead looks at key frames or captures itself (SK:73). G3 packet with the checks block, `BAR` lines, could-not-check list. **Lead waits for the owner.** A G3 change request becomes a restated problem and a `repair` stage (SK:20; GF:66-74).
9. **Close (lead + doc-keeper).** Doc-keeper (Haiku or Sonnet) files decisions, precedents, vocabulary, systems, tried-and-failed, taste log, catalogs, metrics, inbox drafts; the lead checks, runs `python tools/kb_index.py`, removes the job from `active.md` (SK:82-91; KN:105-106).

Hand-back reading: workflow stages return `status, outcome, steps, images_pending, skipped_slots, lessons` plus stage fields (WFR:58); in a hand-run stage the lead reads each hand-back itself. A hook checks hand-back shape only (BH:58).

## 3. How a job lead works and why the kit has independent proposals, one exchange round and sign-offs (for a non-technical reader)

A job lead is the one expert who is in charge of a single job from start to finish, for example the film director for a cutscene. It does not work alone, and it does not talk to you; the lead chat talks to you and gives the job lead the job. First, the job lead and two or three other experts each write their own idea of how the job should go, and they are not allowed to look at each other's idea yet. This is done because people and AI agents copy what they see first: in one study, a wrong early answer pulled correct answers off course in about one case in nine. Second, each expert reads the others' ideas once and lists only the disagreements that would change how the work is made, such as "your effect needs 2 flashes a second but the sound needs 80 milliseconds between hits." Agreement needs no writing, which keeps this round short. Third, the job lead writes one plan that settles each disagreement and shows the options you can choose from, and the other experts sign it or say what breaks. The signature is there so no craft finds out later that the plan cannot be built. You then pick the direction (gate 1). After that the crafts that depend on each other state what they need from each other before anyone builds, so the sound maker knows when the effect lands. Honest status of the evidence: the reasons come from outside studies and from how human game studios work, and our own tests so far prove the machinery runs and that independent reviewers find real mistakes in our code. We have not yet measured that the independent proposals, the exchange round and the sign-offs make a better game than one strong agent would, and the limits of one round each are marked as starting settings, not proven best values.
