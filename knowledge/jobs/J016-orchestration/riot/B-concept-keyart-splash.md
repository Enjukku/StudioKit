## Transcript CA: League skin concept art (Thomas Randby)

### CA-1. Speaker, role, team, coverage
- Thomas Randby, Senior Concept Artist, League PC team, "personalization initiative" = skins team (CA:17-19). Sole concept artist on the Caitlyn Art and Sustainability Update (ASU) (CA:21-23); designed Spirit Blossom Cassiopeia (CA:26).
- Prompt drawn from a mug: Lion Tamer / ringmaster "Big Top" Caitlyn (CA:38-60). He paints over his own Pool Party Caitlyn concept used as a mannequin, in the in-game view (CA:66-72); he says normally concept would not start in game (CA:74).
- Covers: rig opportunities and limits, League value hierarchy, silhouette, weapon design and contact points, gameplay role read, the ASU's goals, how thematics are chosen and pitched, career path.

### CA-3. Steps, in his order
1. Theme selected (data-driven, plus pitches from anyone at Riot) and assigned (CA:484-490, CA:520-530).
2. Research: compile reference, check cultural resonance and sensitivity (CA:52-56).
3. List the rig's opportunity space and limits before drawing: coattail chains, shawl that can become a cape, hip joints for pouches/holster/whip, one hair chain; no sleeve joints so no hanging sleeves; gun pieces that slide and a stock that retracts (CA:76-112, CA:136-138).
4. Thumbnails (largely value/shape, little line), normally done, with the design mostly planned, before going in game (CA:152, CA:267-271, CA:343).
5. Paint in the in-game view; one Photoshop layer per garment like layers of clothing (CA:166-172).
6. Costume first, weapon second: a weapon is easier to design for a known character (CA:425-433).
7. Push value structure: shade down lower body (CA:435-439).
8. Merge layers and paint once the layout works (CA:381-383, CA:904).
9. Check contact points and clipping (grip, barrel hand, stock at shoulder) (CA:656-668, CA:870-880).
10. Consolidate detail: keep what reads, delete noise (CA:938-944).
11. "Design lock" on the character (normally takes weeks) (CA:606-610).
12. Stay present through production to support every discipline (CA:248-256).

### CA-4. References and research
- Research is the first step; early pre-production is mostly compiling reference (CA:52-56).
- Reference used mid-paint to raise interest (gold filigree vs a flat gold line) without adding noise (CA:319-323).
- Prior skins act as a rig and silhouette reference (Lunar Wraith coattails, Officer/Safari hats) (CA:82-90, CA:126-132).
- The ASU drew on the 2020 season cinematic and short stories, so the in-game Caitlyn matches the one players imagine (CA:441-456, CA:472-478).
- A "thematic development" team normally builds visual libraries (shape language, iconography, names, logos) for a new universe (CA:590-598).
- Avoiding copying: not discussed directly. He mentions fantasising real-world elements (CA:904-906) and avoiding overlap with other thematics (CA:799-807). No reference counts given.

### CA-5. Self-review before showing anyone
- Zoom out, look at a glance, check how it reads at scale (CA:184-188).
- Judge at real in-game size ("about this large") (CA:413-415).
- Test on the real game view; a front-facing beauty shot misleads (CA:271-275, CA:916-930).
- The three opening questions (CA:194-200).
- Hand contact points do not clip (CA:870-880); static hair must not reach into the costume where it would deform (CA:549-553).
- Delete details the eye does not need (CA:942-944).

- Thin in this transcript. Named stage: "design lock" (CA:606-610).

### CA-8. Order of work
- Research and rig limits → thumbnails → in-game paint → costume → weapon → detail consolidation → design lock → support through production (CA:52-112, CA:267-271, CA:425-433, CA:606-610, CA:248-256).

## Transcript KA: VALORANT key art (Su Ke and Brandon Meier)

### KA-1. Speakers, roles, coverage
- Su Ke, senior principal illustrator, works with marketing and the character dev team on the illustration art style (KA:24-26).
- Brandon Meier, principal visual designer (graphic design, logotype, layouts), 7.5 years at Riot, on VALORANT since launch (KA:28-30).
- Task: Halloween episodic key art as a fake horror movie poster; prompt drawn from a hat, "zombie apocalypse survivors" (KA:30-42). No real brief: they write it themselves (KA:50-52, KA:61-63). Normal process takes weeks to months (KA:48).
- Covers: brief inputs, keyword research, mood boards, thumbnails, choice points, illustrator-designer collaboration, lighting, VALORANT shape language, layer structure, the asset family built from one key art, when it is finished.

### KA-2. What great work looks like
| Sign | Detail | Cite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Serves the product strategy | The thing being sold is the hero (new map = map is hero of the composition) | KA:162-164 |
| Hypes on first sight | Key art is the first thing players see at login | KA:204-208 |
| Brand-close but unique | Brand fonts modified per episode; episode naming convention ("-tion"); VALORANT red; logo lock-up kept, grit added | KA:63-79, KA:297-301, KA:391-395 |

### KA-3. Steps, in their order
1. Brief intake: normally from creative directors, brand managers and producers, built around the product (new map, new agent, gun skins) (KA:55-61).
2. Name and colour idea within brand conventions (KA:63-79).
3. Story question: what happened? (KA:81).
4. Research in parallel: illustrator searches keywords; designer mood-boards (Pinterest, Behance, ArtStation) and retro horror posters; pick an in-game map that fits (KA:83-89, KA:95-111, KA:101, KA:119-141).
5. Thumbnails: 3 compositional ideas, generic-but-poster-like (KA:91-93, KA:144-156). Designer explores fonts and logotypes at the same time (KA:158-160, KA:293-301).
6. Review together; add story variants (comic vs epic Sage) (KA:209-287).
7. Silhouette and pose with flat shapes, no detail; rough light direction (KA:465-482).
8. Designer drops the illustration into the layout early to check poster scale (KA:483-487).
9. Lighting stage: choose light direction for the story; rim light on foreground (KA:671-683).
10. Designer iterates crops, colourways, layouts; lock general layout and composition first, then add texture, smoke, sparks (KA:698-706).
11. Polish: more reference (crowd), VALORANT shape language in details (KA:710-730).
12. Keep layers in foreground / midground / background groups (KA:777-797).
13. Hand off a layered PSD with switchable layers and a "classic version" (KA:852, KA:868-872).
14. Final layout (16:9), tagline, sparks (KA:882-898).
15. Expand to the asset family: ~20 assets per key art, up to ~50 pieces; end card, logo bumper, social headers, Twitch/YouTube box art, wallpapers, phone wallpapers; regional teams and vendors build live activations; a design kit (KA:732-748, KA:874-876, KA:902-906).

### KA-4. References and research
- First move is keyword search; each keyword gives facts, not images to copy: "apocalypse" → dusty colour key; "survivors" → serious, worn expressions; "zombie" → grey and green; orange as complement (KA:83, KA:95-113).
- Mood board from Pinterest, Behance, ArtStation; genre research (retro horror posters, named films) (KA:85-89, KA:115-141).
- Designer supplies extra reference mid-polish for what a crowd looks like, even though it shows no zombies (KA:712-716).
- Reusing existing palettes/gradients from past projects to test colour quickly (KA:651-655).
- Dev artefact as reference: Sage's power/player fantasy card (KA:261-265).
- No counts given; copying not addressed except that early careers mimic what inspires (KA:916-917).

### KA-5. Self-review
- Silhouette and pose with cheap shapes, so a failed pose costs little (KA:465-482).
- Place the art in the real layout to check scale (KA:483-487).
- Read what the image actually shows and adapt: background zombies read as graves, so make them graves (KA:489-491, KA:525-527).
- Done-check: every shape has a goal, no transparency overlays, no airbrush (KA:817-829).
- Colour check on a calibrated phone; wrong Photoshop colour profile once shipped wrong colours (KA:661-670).

### KA-6. Collaboration
| From / to | What | Cite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creative directors, brand managers, producers → artists | Brief parameters based on what the product does | KA:55-61 |
| Character dev team (agent concept, environment, narrative world building, modeling, animation) → marketing | Marketing starts once dev knows firmly what the agent looks like, then puts the final wrapper on it | KA:188-202 |
| Illustrator ↔ designer | Work in tandem; either can spark the other. Revelation: illustration's circles became the whole episode's shape language. Episode 3: designer's attacker/defender red/green idea changed the illustration palette | KA:395-423 |
| Illustration → designer's type | Logotype "changes drastically" once thumbnails arrive | KA:395-401 |
| Game UI → marketing | Defender icon reused as a graphic shape | KA:768-776 |
| Illustrator → designer hand-off | Layered PSD, switchable layers, variants | KA:868-872 |
| Designer → many teams | Design kit; regional teams, vendors, partners build activations | KA:742-748 |
| Working style | Quick informal "huddles" instead of scheduled meetings; direct feedback without ego | KA:433-457 |

### KA-7. Review and approval
- Two choice points: first, people choose a sketch by its thumbnail silhouette; second, when the key art is done, people choose the designer's treatment (KA:164-170).
- Present 2-3 solid, distinct directions, then vary the chosen one (KA:170-172).
- Always state the artists' own preference, whatever the reviewers then choose (KA:172-176).
- When reviewers leave the choice to the artists, go back to the strategy of the brief (KA:160-164).
- If all options meet the brief, the choice becomes subjective: about 95% objective, 5% the artist's call (KA:834-846).
- Finishing: eventually choose a direction; the artist sometimes sends small changes after "final" (KA:830-834, KA:848-850).

### KA-8. Order of work
- Upstream lock: the agent's look is fixed by the dev team before marketing art (KA:200-202).
- Parallel: illustration thumbnails and designer's type/mood board run together (KA:158-160); designer's layout runs while the illustrator polishes (KA:483-487, KA:503-505).
- Locked order inside the art: silhouette → lighting → detail (KA:465-473); layout/composition locked before texture extras (KA:704-706).
- Going back: illustration can redirect type and episode shapes (KA:395-413); designer's colour idea can change the illustration's palette (KA:415-421).
- Passing remark: "the first idea always goes better", so get it out first (KA:289-291). (Contrast: SP:622-634 says the first thumbnail is rarely best.)

### KA-9. Mistakes named
| Mistake | Avoid by | Cite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Details before pose | Flat shapes first; cheap to change | KA:475-482 |
| Pretty over form | Light and contrast for 3D form | KA:811-816 |
| Random shapes, transparency, airbrush | Purposeful, clean shapes | KA:817-829 |
| Blocky shapes lose interest | Rim light | KA:754-758 |
| Wrong colour profile on export | Calibrated screens, phone check | KA:657-670 |
| Proportion slip (Gekko too tall) | Compare character heights | KA:459 |

### SP-1. Speaker, role, coverage
- Unnamed in the transcript: "Illustrator and Concept Artist on TFT Gameplay" (SP:12), 3 years at Riot (SP:200). Colleagues named: Sonny (original Aphelios/Alune concept artist) (SP:750), KK and Danny (TFT concept artists) (SP:886).

### SP-3. Steps, in her order
1. Gameplay need sets the character (SP:50-56).
2. Character concept (done before; design not covered) (SP:126-132).
3. Reference board in PureRef: existing illustrations first, then real-world photos; usually a day or two (SP:60, SP:88-94, SP:148-186, SP:212-214).
4. Warm-up figures to get the bad first drawing out (SP:220-232).
5. Loose gesture sketch; solve big problems, not details (SP:246-252, SP:262-272).
6. Thumbnails, normally 4-5 fairly finished ones, on a small canvas (SP:292-310).
7. Refine value, then colour and story (SP:444-450).
8. Lighting test with different contrast ratios in Photoshop; ask co-workers at this stage (SP:470-498).
9. Silhouette check: fill in dark value, squint, push and pull the edge (SP:501-508).
10. Add depth (foreground), quiet background, keep the crop square clear (SP:510-534).
11. Choose one thumbnail (A of A-D); refine it (SP:617-642).
12. Merge to one layer, nudge shapes, stay "anti-precious" (SP:644-656).
13. Gates: thumbnail → green light (direction refined; "things aren't going to move anymore") → render (metal looks metal, fabric looks fabric) → polish (small light effects, noise, camera look) (SP:656-692). Reaching green light usually takes 1.5-2 weeks (SP:706-712).
14. Work on the face early (SP:820-838).
15. Weekly likeness check with the character's original concept artist (SP:747-756).
16. Final check that crop and full piece show the same person; deliver the in-game crop (SP:891-894).

### SP-4. References and research
- Reference is the most important step in any art (SP:82-88).
- Start with existing illustrations that inspire (Anima Squad splashes for colour, composition, posture, gesture) (SP:148-164).
- Never end with existing art: it is "derivative of derivative of derivative"; add real-world reference (Pinterest pose search) (SP:166-186).
- Mix real-world and illustration reference into something new; breadth of choice is what makes a splash good; without reference you are limited to your memory library (SP:186-198).
- Volume: her board in the episode is "very small" compared with usual; normally a day or two of gathering (SP:212-214).
- If no pose reference exists, photograph yourself (SP:214-218).

### SP-5. Self-review
- Warm up; first drawing of the day is bad (SP:220-232).
- Ask the overarching questions: what am I selling, what excites players, is the character recognisable? (SP:268-272).
- Ask which problem you are solving now, at each stage (SP:312-316, SP:466-470).
- Work small so pixel detail cannot distract (SP:302-310, SP:714-722).
- Dark-fill silhouette and squint (SP:501-508).
- Know what the end result looks like before showing; if you do not, nobody else will (SP:568-583).
- Face early, so notes are not all about the face (SP:820-838).
- Same-person check between crop and piece (SP:891-892).

### SP-6. Collaboration
| From / to | What | Cite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gameplay → art | Unit role need (mage, frontline) | SP:52-54 |
| Set direction → artist | Trait palette set for the set | SP:108-110 |
| Original character concept artist → splash artist | Weekly likeness checks: is the character represented as first decided? | SP:747-756 |
| Concept artists ↔ splash artist | Small team (2 concept, 1 splash); splash artist gives feedback during concept, concept artists during splash | SP:878-888 |
| Co-workers → artist | Ask at the lighting/thumbnail stage whether an arm or an eye looks off | SP:478-498 |
| Art director → artist | Decides when the piece is finished | SP:814-820 |

### SP-7. Review and approval
- Four gates, each passed on before the next: thumbnail, green light, render, polish (SP:656-692).
- Thumbnail review sells the idea fast and rough, so reviewers latch onto the idea, not a biased preference for one rendered detail (SP:554-566).
- Early outside opinions are worth more than solo progress, because "anything can happen" at that stage (SP:494-500).
- Done when the art director's notes repeat and stop adding to the piece (SP:814-820).
- An off face draws the first note every time and hides other useful notes (SP:820-834).
- Her opinion as artist is not more valid than anyone else's (SP:402-404). No conflict process described.

### SP-8. Order of work
- Gameplay need → concept → reference → thumbnails → pick → green light (composition locked) → render → polish (SP:50-56, SP:656-692).
- Most changes should happen before green light (SP:534-540); after green light parts do not move (SP:672-676).
- Concept and splash overlap through cross-feedback (SP:884-888).

### SP-9. Mistakes named
| Mistake | Avoid by | Cite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ending reference at existing art | Add real-world reference | SP:166-172 |
| No reference | Brain library is too small | SP:194-198 |
| Trusting the first drawing / first thumbnail | Warm up; make several thumbnails; first one is often the expected default | SP:220-232, SP:620-634 |
| Working big too early | Small canvas | SP:302-310, SP:714-722 |
| Being precious | Merge, smudge, move things | SP:534-540, SP:644-656 |
| Rendering details at thumbnail stage | Rough so reviewers judge the idea | SP:560-566 |
| Not knowing the end state | Picture it before presenting | SP:574-585 |
| Face off | Face early | SP:820-834 |
| Expected, typical idea | Explore a spread of ideas | SP:856-864 |
| Making for the algorithm (career remark) | Make what you want to make | SP:778-784 |

### 2. What great work looks like (shared signs)
- Read at the real viewing size first: silhouette and value before detail (CA:152-162, CA:908-916; KA:465-473; SP:318-324, SP:501-508, SP:735-736).
- One identity read is protected above all: Caitlyn's gun (CA:648-656), Alune's collar/weapon/face (SP:334-338, SP:724-741), the product as hero of the key art (KA:162-164).
- Truth to the character and its role: core personality, gameplay role, and consistent face/posture across products (CA:178-184, CA:711-738; SP:743-763; KA:221-225).
- Fit the house style as checkable rules: League value hierarchy (CA:154-162), VALORANT triangles/rectangles, clean, no airbrush (KA:718-730, KA:817-829), TFT set palette (SP:102-112).
- Every element earns its place: delete noise (CA:942-944), every shape has a goal (KA:817-819).
- A story or fantasy, not just a costume: abilities recontextualised (CA:347-358), story from an ultimate (KA:261-276), pose supporting story (SP:456-470).
- Light and value do narrative work: bottom vs top light (KA:673-679), rim light separation (KA:754-758), shaded-down lower body (CA:365-371).

### 3. Steps (merged order)

### 4. References (combined)
- Always first (CA:52-56; SP:82-88), and heavy (a day or two, SP:212-214).

### 6. Collaboration (combined)
- Upstream locks feed downstream art: rig (CA:78-82), dev agent look (KA:200-202), set palette (SP:108-110), original character design (SP:747-756).
- Constraint owners speak before design: rig/animation/tech art limits (CA:94-99, CA:240-244).

### 7. Review and approval (combined)
- Choice points: thumbnail (KA:166; SP:662-668), lock/green light (CA:606-610; SP:668-676), final treatment (KA:168-170).
- Feedback style: direct, without ego (KA:447-457); early outside eyes (SP:494-500).
- Rough at review time so people judge the idea (SP:554-566).
- Conflict / ties: return to the brief's strategy; if all meet it, artist's call on the last 5% (KA:160-164, KA:834-846).
- Done: notes repeat (SP:814-820); at some point a direction must simply be chosen (KA:830-834).

### 8. Order of work (combined)
- Big decisions early, cheap to change; small decisions late (KA:475-482; SP:534-540; CA:154-158).
- Costume before weapon (CA:425-433); silhouette before light before detail (KA:465-473); layout before texture (KA:704-706); green light before render (SP:668-684).
- Parallel tracks with sync points: illustration ↔ type/layout (KA:158-160, KA:483-487).
- Going upstream happens and is welcome when evidence appears: in-game reading (CA:916-930), illustration reshapes branding (KA:401-413).

### 9. Mistakes (combined top list)
1. Detail before read (CA:154-158; KA:475-482; SP:262-268).
2. Judging in the wrong view (beauty shot, large canvas) (CA:271-275; SP:302-310).
3. Being precious / attached (CA:920-926; SP:534-540, SP:644-656).
4. First or expected idea accepted untested (SP:620-634, SP:856-862).
5. Copying from existing art only (SP:166-172).
6. Designs ignoring rig, 3D or physics (CA:94-99, CA:399-407, CA:632-640).
7. Identity read weakened or likeness off (CA:650-656; SP:820-834, SP:755-758).
8. Noise, random shapes, unfinished-looking techniques (CA:942-944; KA:817-829).
9. Colour shipped from an uncalibrated pipeline (KA:668-670).
