## 1. Speaker, role, team, what the episode covers

### T: Jason Chan, Riftbound card art
- Visual development artist on Riot's Long-Form Animation team (Arcane) (T:19). Was on the original Splash team; painted many base champion splashes (T:22-26). Did Arcane season 2 concepts (T:26-29). More than 10-12 years at Riot (T:389-392).
- Made one "overnumbered" alternate-art card for Riftbound: rare cards that Riot artists paint in their own personal style, not the splash style, with premium print finishes (T:30-36).
- Covers: his process for trading-card art from a brief: brief, reference, a few sketch options, polish (T:38-43). Real cards take 1-2 weeks; the demo takes about an hour (T:45-47). Brief drawn at random: "Windfisher Yasuo" (fly-fishing Yasuo) (T:51-55).
- Also covers: why art matters in a card game (T:357-387), feedback and team goals (T:644-750), burnout (T:328-356), portfolio and practice advice (T:785-850).

### K: Knight Zhang, 2XKO champion concepts
- Principal Character Concept Artist on 2XKO (Riot's fighting game); job is to bring League champions into a fighting game (K:14). On the team since 2022; character designer of Vi (costume and overall look) and Warwick (K:16-20); also event illustrations (K:21).
- Covers: translating Katarina from her League design into 2XKO (K:22). Stated pipeline: research, exploration (as many ideas as possible), refinement into the 2XKO style, final design handed to the character art team (K:24-26). Real time: research about a week, concepting 2-3 months (K:29).
- Context: Katarina was in an early 1v1 slice of the game; once 2XKO became a 2v2 tag fighter she proved too strong and is not on the roster (K:32-34). Demo twist: a darker Halloween Katarina that makes "Sinister Blade" obvious (K:36-37).

### Combined
- Both sit at the start of a pipeline, but with different outputs. T makes a finished 2D product (the card picture). K makes a design that other crafts build; his concept is never "final art" (K:302-304).
- Our nearest seats: T maps to marketing (icon, thumbnail, key art) and to the art director's reference images; K maps to the art director's direction work plus the modeler and rigger hand-off.

## 3. The steps they actually follow (in order)

### T (his recap is T:918-932)
1. Get the brief (random in the demo) and find a lore reason for the twist (T:51-61).
2. Gather reference that answers how the brief's ideas merge (T:63-91).
3. Open the card template with UI, crop marks and bleed; hide it while working but keep its effect in mind (T:97-108).
4. List the champion's identity reads to keep (T:117-130).
5. Scribble rough compositions; usually several, mostly not shown (T:109-113, T:474-484).
6. Present a few sketches to the art director; the AD picks one and gives feedback; back and forth (T:158-166, T:198-209).
7. Clean up the chosen sketch; still discovering specifics (T:148-156).
8. Make a silhouette mask layer for the character (T:269-277).
9. Values in grayscale: carve forms, set lighting (T:279-309).
10. Color pass on an overlay layer that keeps the values; find local colors and pop colors (T:506-537, T:926-928).
11. Rebalance the composition for color (T:569-579).
12. Details and rendering for touch and feel (T:863-881).
13. Troubleshoot what is not working until happy or out of time (T:883-901).
14. Final touches (background pattern); remove any that break the composition (T:903-909).
- Pace: normally at least a day on each step, to move on with confidence (T:936-938).

### K (his recap is K:441-448)
1. Get direction from design or narrative (K:241); in the demo, the Halloween "darker" theme (K:36-37).
2. Research canon and gather exploratory reference; mood board around materials, faction and fashion (K:38-54, K:442-443).
3. Exploration: draw the base costume first, then change small and large parts (hair, garments, makeup, mood, body type, pose) (K:57-58, K:240-283). Real time a few weeks, about five times the demo's drawings (K:287).
4. Throughout: flip the canvas often (K:164-179) and push silhouette and shape (K:187-195).
5. Select a couple of designs or the single strongest; others help decide (K:269-275, K:289-290).
6. Iteration: branch from the main idea with small and big changes, plus one last wild bid (K:290-297).
7. Decide: here a mix of options A and B that keeps her poise and charisma (K:298-301).
8. Refine against technical limits (hair off the nape, collars off the neck) (K:304-314).
9. Set the focal area and simplify around it (K:317-324).
10. Color and values; test overlaps (K:352-382).
11. Finish only as far as the modeler needs; hand to the character art team (K:26, K:302-305).

### Combined
- Same skeleton: brief or direction, research, many rough options, choose with others, refine, values and color, finish to what is needed.
- Difference: T polishes one image to the end; K stops when the next craft can build.

## 4. References and research: when, what for

### T
- When: first step after the brief, then reopened during work for details and ideas (T:63-65).
- What for: one question per brief, here how to marry fisherman, samurai and wind (T:71). Looks for objects that overlap (katana and fishing pole) (T:73-77), iconic costume props (hat with hooks, waders) (T:79), and poses from both domains (fishing and sword fighting) (T:81), then merges them (T:83).
- Own photos for a pose the internet lacks, so the result is not the same as other artists' work (T:87-91).
- Broad habit: be a fan of many media; new ideas come from outside the genre's pool (T:831-850). Research is also how he finds what he likes in an unfamiliar subject (T:694-698).

### K
- When: every 2XKO champion concept starts with research, about a week (K:29, K:38).
- What for: to work without bias and to widen where champions could go (K:40-41).
- How he picks reference: do the expected first, then look for the unexpected element (K:42-43).
- Canon inventory: what she has and does not have; her faction's look, so she still reads as a citizen of Noxus (K:44-48).
- Mood board on material reads; fashion research, which he calls underused (K:45-50). Inventory of her current materials: black leathers, metal spikes, spiked bracer, red hair (K:50-53).
- Other people's takes on the character show the consensus of what makes her "her" (K:214).
- Studio precedents serve as reference: Darius cape, Yasuo cowl, Ahri braid, Darius shoulder spikes (K:89-90, K:108-109, K:310, K:285-286).
- Current fashion trend (big pants) tried as one option (K:268).
- Curiosity beyond his own taste (K:326-350).

### Combined
- Both research before drawing, and both aim research at a question (merge three ideas; widen the canon), not at a pile of pictures.
- K adds an inventory of the existing character and studio precedents; T adds making your own reference to avoid a look everyone else shares.

## 5. Self-review before showing anyone

### T
- A fixed list of diagnosis questions when it does not work: composition, and which part of it? drawing? colors? shape? flow? (T:883-891). A fix can create a new problem, so he loops (T:893-897).
- Rebalance after every color change (T:569-579).
- Check the template and card UI against the composition (T:107-108, T:184).
- Ask where the eye goes first and whether he leads it (T:496-502).
- Check the identity notes are hit (T:126-130).
- Try an addition, remove it if it breaks the composition (T:907-909).
- Drop pieces that never find balance; they are never shown (T:589-593).
- Feeling stuck does not mean the image is bad; it may be unfinished (T:581-584).
- Most first scribbles are never shared; the first composition is likely weak but teaches something (T:480-484).
- Personal connection as a gut test of quality (T:682-688); this does not transfer to AI agents.

### K
- Flip the canvas often: fresh eyes, symmetry errors, values, proportion (K:164-179). He prefers flipping to rotating because it is one button (K:178-179).
- Read the silhouette in a key pose (K:185-187).
- Pose the character with overlapping limbs to catch color merges (K:368-374).
- Expect extra noise from motion, light changes and effects, even on simple designs (K:99-105).
- Ask what each addition adds or takes away (K:140).
- Cost check: is the expensive part worth it (K:110-111, K:128-129).
- At the end, sort what is definitely the character and what is not (K:224-226).

### Combined
- Both use a fixed check habit (T's question list, K's flip) to see the work with fresh eyes, and both re-check the whole after changing one part (T:569-579, T:893-897; K:323-324).

**How the 2XKO style is set and kept across many artists and crafts.** K gives shared rules and precedents, not a written style-guide process.
1. A priority order: fighting game first, League second (K:161-163).
2. Shared readability rules: separable body parts, trackable extremities (K:112-117); cel shading needs line work and shadows on what matters, but too much of both plus too many colors makes noise (K:93-97).
3. Shared cost rules: cloth and capes are expensive (K:87-91); nothing hangs in front of the shoulders (K:106-111); parts kept off joints (K:305-312).
4. A camera rule: design for P1 (left side, facing right), so the right side of the body is seen most (K:153-160).
5. A roster-wide proportion shift: everyone is larger than life and beefier than in League (K:196-197, K:210).
6. A material language taken from 2D fighting-game heritage (K:259-260).
7. Roster precedents as teaching cases: the Darius cape made the team swear off capes (K:89-90); the Yasuo cowl worked only with costly animation (K:108-109); the Ahri braid moves as one unit (K:310); Darius shoulder spikes reused as a family motif (K:285-286).
8. Group choice among options (K:269-272).
9. The character art team carries the design through production (K:26).
10. Players read changes as canon changes (Vi's flipped details), so changes need a reason (K:149-152).
- Not stated in K: a style-guide document, a named art director, approval meetings, or how VFX and UI stay in style.

### Combined
- T works inside guides with an AD picking sketches (T:198-213); K works inside shared genre rules with a group picking options (K:269-272).
- Both say feedback comes from people with other problems to solve, and the artist must satisfy those needs (T:644-659; K:122-129).

## 7. Review and approval

### T
- Stage 1: rough sketches. Normally several options; back and forth until the best composition is found (T:158-166).
- The AD usually picks one, gives feedback, then back and forth. He says this holds for all professional art: cards, concept, books, games, film (T:198-209).
- Most first scribbles are not shown at all (T:480).
- Feedback you disagree with still has to be addressed; it comes from people solving other problems (T:644-659). Professional work is iteration on feedback (T:660-665).
- Number of rounds: not stated. Reviews after the sketch stage: not described.

### K
- After exploration (a few weeks, about five times the demo's drawings), the team narrows to a couple of designs or the strongest one (K:287-290).
- He hands the choice to others; they say what does or does not feel like the character, or what feels cooler (K:269-271). He feels he must weigh about 20 people's possible opinions (K:272).
- Iteration on the chosen idea, then a decision (K:290-301).
- Who reviews by role and how many rounds: not stated. Total concept time 2-3 months (K:29).

### Combined
- Both review at the rough-options stage, before polish (T:158-166, K:287-290). Neither states round counts or later approval stages.

## 8. Order of work

### T
- Order: brief, reference, template, sketch options, AD pick, values, color, rebalance, details, final touches (T:918-932).
- Rules: composition before rendering (T:168-176); value before color (T:279-285); about a day per step (T:936-938).
- Locked when: the composition after the AD picks a sketch (implied by T:203). Nothing else is called locked.
- Going back: color forces a rebalance of the value composition (T:569-573); fixes cause new problems, so he loops (T:893-897).
- Parallel work: not stated.

### K
- Order: research (about a week), exploration (a few weeks), selection, iteration, final, then the character art team's production (K:24-26, K:29, K:287-304).
- Upstream first: direction from design or narrative comes before exploration (K:241).
- Concept handles downstream limits before others begin (K:141-145).
- Locked when: nothing is precious "until proven otherwise" (K:233); what is definitely the character is decided at the end of exploration (K:224-226).
- Going back upstream: a gameplay change (1v1 to 2v2 tag) made Katarina too strong, so she left the roster (K:32-34). A player reaction to Vi's flipped details questioned canon (K:149-152).
- Parallel work: not stated.

### Combined
- Both put the cheap, wide stage first and the expensive, narrow stage last. Neither describes parallel crafts.

## 9. Mistakes they name and how they avoid them

| Mistake | Avoidance | Cite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rendering too early because it is fun | Solve composition first | T:168-176 |
| Soft airbrush everywhere, so everything looks soft | Move to sharp, flat brushes and planes | T:291-297 |
| Painting the same thing as everyone from the same web images | Shoot your own reference | T:87-91 |
| Adding an element that breaks the composition | Try it, remove it if it fails | T:903-909 |
| Thinking a stuck image is a bad image | It may be unfinished; keep rebalancing; drop it only if it never balances | T:581-593 |
| Saying yes to everything, burnout | Balance load, say no, take breaks; breaks also solve problems | T:330-356 |
| Team keeps growing scope to make it cooler | Know what you sign up for | T:398-406 |
| Treating feedback as an attack | Take it with curiosity; it solves other problems | T:644-665 |
| Art that looks great but misses the project's goal | Align on shared goals; communicate them openly | T:722-750 |
| Making "looks good" the main goal | Solve the project's need first | T:670-676 |
| Line work plus shadows plus colors piling into noise | Fewer details; count motion, light and effects | K:93-105 |
| Assuming a simple sheet stays simple in motion | Expect noise from motion, light and effects | K:99-105 |
| Capes, heavy cloth, items in front of shoulders | Avoid, or use only when clearly worth the cost | K:87-91, K:106-111 |
| Long hair mass at the nape, collars near the neck | Keep parts off joints; condense hair into one unit | K:305-312 |
| Key identity detail on the side the camera rarely shows (left-eye scar) | Move details to the visible side | K:156-160 |
