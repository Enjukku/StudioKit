| 3 | One-line fantasy | As a tiny thief, I sneak into a sleeping giant's castle and carry its treasure home, while every sound I make can wake it. |
| 4 | Hook | You are the size of a spoon and the treasure is the size of you. The rarest treasure is also the loudest (a golden goose that honks, a harp that sings), so the thing you want most is the thing most likely to wake him. Everyone in the castle shares one giant: when somebody gets greedy, everybody runs |
| 5 | First image | (concept art not made: no images in this task.) Camera at table height, on a giant kitchen table. A tiny player tiptoes past a snoring giant face, hugging a golden egg as big as the player. One huge eyelid is half open. Two other tiny players freeze behind a salt shaker. Readable action: carrying. Scale: player vs face. Stake: the eye |
| 6 | Core loop in one sentence | To fill my shelf with rarer treasure, I climb into the giant's castle and choose what to carry (quiet and small, or loud and rare) and when to move, then the giant stays asleep or wakes and chases the loudest thief, then my treasure earns coins for quieter shoes, a bigger bag and a deeper room. |
| 7 | Signature moment | Setup: the giant snores; a golden goose sits on the table. Decision: a player grabs it. Consequence: the goose honks, the snore stops, the eye opens slowly, everyone freezes; a friend rings a dinner bell across the room and the giant turns toward the bell. Payoff: the goose-carrier dives down the beanstalk slide home, and the goose lays golden eggs on the player's shelf. All with normal inputs (move, grab, ring). Needs: giant face and eye animation (sleep states must read at phone size), snore and wake sounds (the main warning; must also read with sound off), noise-meter UI, chase and "tossed out the window" animation (comic, no injury), slide VFX |
| 8 | Why now | Collect → place at base → earn is the economy of the largest recent Roblox hits: Steal a Brainrot had over 25 million concurrent users in September 2025 (Roblox newsroom 2025-12-16, RC45) and 153,037 playing at our sample (DG2, 2026-10-21 03:56 UTC). "Trigger a danger, survive it carrying your prize home" is in Kick a Lucky Block's own description ("Survive the tsunami that you've triggered", DG2), a game Roblox named among 2026 new games that quickly became top experiences (DG1, filed 2026-07-30). Hazard retrieval alone faded: Escape Tsunami For Brainrots had a tracker peak of 4,996,032 on 2026-01-24, and its highest point in the 7 days before 2026-10-20 was 32,495 (IR1 row 3, S18). That is about 0.65 % of its peak, measured peak against peak. J004 gives no dates for when the rise and the decline started. Alternative explanation: those games may grow from their collectible theme, creator videos and player-vs-player theft, not from the carry-home structure. What may be temporary: the collectible theme they share, and the hazard-retrieval wave. No trend data for stealth or fairy-tale themes: not verified |
| 9 | Familiar foundation and original contribution | Borrowed (function only): collect, carry home through danger, place at base for income, upgrade, reach rarer stock (Escape Tsunami For Brainrots, Kick a Lucky Block, Steal a Brainrot). Changed rule: the danger is one shared, sleeping character; one shared noise meter means any player's greed wakes it for all; the giant chases the loudest player, and only that player drops their loot (dropped loot can be picked up by others). Rarity is tied to noise and weight. Own expression: original giant, castle, treasures, UI, sound. Rights: the giant-and-beanstalk idea comes from an English folk tale that is in the public domain (check before release); no film or book adaptation's characters, names or look; no "brainrot" content, no tsunami, no lucky blocks. The title does not use the "Steal a …" pattern |
| 10 | Smallest buildable version | Keeps the decisive action: carry a treasure past a sleeping giant who can wake. Content: one giant, one room (the kitchen table), 4 readable sleep states (deep sleep, light sleep, stirring, awake and chasing), one shared noise meter, 5 treasures with weight and noise values (TBD, game-designer, tuned in grey-box), a beanstalk climb up and a slide down, a base shelf that pays coins, 3 upgrades (quieter shoes, bigger bag, faster climb), a dinner bell as the one co-op tool. Excluded: more rooms and giants, player-vs-player base theft, trading, pets, rebirth. Dependencies: server-owned giant states and noise (architect; noise comes from server-checked actions), giant model and face animation (art director, animator), sound states (sound designer), noise meter and freeze cue (UI). Target devices: phone first (FY2025 DAU mobile 83 %, RC01); one-thumb controls; every sound cue also has a visual cue |
| 11 | Failure hypothesis | Strongest reason it may fail: the giant becomes predictable. After a few trips players learn the pattern, stop hiding, and the tension dies; without player-vs-player theft there is no social drama to replace it. Second: a stranger can make noise on purpose to wake the giant for everyone (griefing). Cheapest test: a grey-box (one table, one giant with 4 states, 3 treasures, a base) with 4-6 target-age players for one session. Watch trip 1 against trip 10: do players still wait for the snore, choose differently between quiet and loud treasure, react when he wakes, and start the next trip without a prompt? Add one tester told to grief: do the others still finish trips? Result that changes the plan: tension gone by trip 10 → switch the recommendation to Guard the Dragon Egg (strongest loop proof of the set) or Kick the Meteor (cheapest build) |
