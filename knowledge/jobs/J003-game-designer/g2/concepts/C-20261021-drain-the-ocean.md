| 3 | One-line fantasy | As a treasure hunter, I drain the ocean so that the lost seafloor comes out of the water, while the tide keeps coming back for my loot. |
| 4 | Hook | The whole server works on one impossible job. Every pump lowers the same sea, and every drop uncovers a new piece of the seafloor nobody has walked on: reefs, then a shipwreck, then a sunken town, then a giant plug at the very bottom. Everyone wants to know what happens when you pull it |
| 5 | First image | (concept art not made: no images in this task.) Wide shot from a cliff. A half-empty sea, a shipwreck standing upright in wet mud, tiny players with pumps and shovels on the seabed, a wave building on the horizon. Readable action: pumping and digging. Scale: players vs an emptied sea. Stake: the wave |
| 6 | Core loop in one sentence | To reach the deeper, rarer seafloor, I scavenge the zone the server has uncovered and choose how far down to go before the tide, then I sell what I carried home and my bigger pump lowers the water for everyone, then a new zone opens and the plug comes closer. |
| 7 | Signature moment | Setup: the water is one step above the next zone. Decision: keep pumping or go down to scavenge. Consequence: the level drops, the sea pulls back with a roar and a shipwreck rises out of the water; everyone runs down the wet slope to its chests (loot is per player, so nobody takes another player's chest). Payoff: the tide horn sounds; players with heavy chests sprint uphill as the wave comes; those it catches wash up on the beach and drop what they carried (no drowning). Needs: a visible water-level drop, a wave VFX that reads from far away, a tide horn, seabed art per zone, a per-player share readout ("your pump: x %") |
| 8 | Why now | The catch-sell-upgrade-reach-rarer-pools loop holds players: Fish It had 70,628 players at our sample (DG2, 2026-10-21 03:59 UTC, one moment, not daily users) and a tracker peak of 2,716,766 on 2025-12-07 (IR1 S21, sampled maximum, not audited). The J004 trend note says of the fishing family: "Proven 2025 peaks, 2026 residual audiences" (IR1 row 4, `knowledge/reference/trends/01-roblox-data-loops.md:213`). Meaning: the 2025 peaks are proven, and in 2026 an audience remains (residual). No 7-day figure for Fish It is in the note, so the size of the drop is not measured like-for-like: not verified. The whole-server shared goal has no dated evidence in our sources: not verified. Alternative explanation: fishing games may hold players through calm, low-pressure collecting, which scavenging under a tide may not give |
| 9 | Familiar foundation and original contribution | Borrowed (function only): collect, sell, upgrade gear, reach rarer pools (Fish It, Fisch). Changed rule: the rarer pools open for the whole server together, because every player's pump lowers one shared water level; the tide returns on a cycle and takes what you carry if it catches you; at the bottom the server pulls one plug, the sea drains, and a new sea fills in while each player keeps their gear and collection. Own expression: all original; a sunken-city zone may use public-domain myth with original designs. Note: the owner's dam game also has a whole-server goal against water. The fantasy differs (discover, not defend) and its water tech may be reusable (not verified, architect) |
| 10 | Smallest buildable version | Keeps the decisive action: the whole server lowers the sea and uncovers new ground. Content: one bay framed to read as open sea, 4 zones and the plug, a shared water level with 5 steps, a personal pump with 3 tiers, dig spots and chests per player, a dock to sell, one gear line, one tide wave per cycle, a finale where everyone pulls the plug together, then the same bay refills. Excluded: more seas, boats, diving, pets, trading. Dependencies: lowering water in steps on phones (architect probe first), tide wave VFX, seabed terrain art per zone. Promise ledger note: the title says "ocean"; the smallest version is a bay that must read as the sea. Target devices: phone first (RC01) |
| 11 | Failure hypothesis | Strongest reason it may fail: one player's pump is a small share of the whole server's drain, so players do not feel their own effect, and the big drain moments are too rare to carry the time between them. Second: lowering a large water body may cost too much on mid-range phones. Cheapest test: first an architect probe (6 water-level steps in a bay on a mid-range phone: frame time and memory). Then a grey-box with about 8 players: can each player point to what their own pump did, and do they stay through the time between zones? |
| 12 | Evidence | Sources: RC01, IR1 row 4 (S21), DG2 (`../funnel.md` §1). Observed facts: one counter snapshot and one tracker peak for the reference; nothing about retention or revenue.
