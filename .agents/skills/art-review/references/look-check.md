## 1. The capture manifest (one row per image, kept as text next to the images)
Record missing fields as `missing`; never guess them from the picture (04:§4.4).
| Field | Example |
| --- | --- |
| capture id, file, timestamp | `c04`, `captures/c04.png`, 2026-11-02 14:10 |
| game/build, asset revision | `place v41`, `boss_mesh r3` |
| art-bible revision, anchors in force | `rev 3`, `a02, a05` |
| viewport px, orientation, crop, encoding | `1170x2532 portrait, none, PNG` |
| camera transform, FOV or ortho scale, gameplay distance | `third person, 70°, 14 studs` |
| capture state and kind (table below) | `busiest`, `layer cut: effects off` |
| time of day, lighting style, post stack, quality tier | `noon, Soft, Bloom+CC, quality 3` |
| UI scale, HUD and safe-area state, input mode | `1.0, HUD on, CoreUISafeInsets, touch` |
| player pose, encounter state, player count | `idle`, `slam wind-up`, `8 players` |
| capture device or route | `iPhone 12 (Studio MCP)` / `Blender render` / `ChatGPT image (route, model)` |
| derived files | look-check JSON paths, diagnostic images |
| **UI screens instead of camera fields:** canvas size, export scale, fit factor (UIScale), safe-area mode, Figma file + node id or build path, frame/state id, which screen came before it in the flow | `1920x1080 @1x, f 0.347, CoreUISafeInsets, node 1:3 / state 04-commit, after 03-review` |

**Capture kinds** (ask for the ones the question needs; the studio operator or the job's Studio driver takes them, `studio-mcp`):
| Kind | What it is | Proves | Cannot prove |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Busiest, game camera | the worst expected moment (most players, effects, UI) at the game camera and the game FOV | the gameplay read, composition, hierarchy at one moment | timing, sound, motion, other moments |
| Layer cuts | the same camera with UI off, effects off, or characters off (one change each) | which layer competes (K09, CP9); the UI-off cut: extremes and contrast without the HUD (RD5, RD10; §5 line 9) | a layer's own band, saturation field or reserved-hue share: other layers are still in the frame |
| Environment only | the same camera with UI, effects and characters (players and NPCs) off together | every measure of the environment: band, near-black, depth contrast, saturation field, reserved hues (RD3, RD9, CL1, CL9, K19-K20; §5 lines 4-5, 8-9) | the composed read; anything about characters or effects |
| Mask capture | the same camera, a flat `#FF00FF` backdrop behind the subject, shadows off | the subject's mask for value, silhouette, thumb and palette on the in-scene capture | anything about the scene |
| Lowest quality | the same view at the lowest supported graphics quality | that cues survive low settings | phone performance |
| Frame strip | frames at a known rate (anticipation, event, recovery) | timing order, peaks, what covers what over time | feel at normal speed (owner on a playable preview), sound |
| Lighting variants | 2-3 light setups of the same blockout or rough, grey | the light choice before detail | final materials |
| Flat-key render | a Blender render on a flat background (`review_renders.py`) | silhouette, region values, variant footprint | the in-game read |
| Lineup | the roster or tier set side by side at one camera and scale | size rank, look-alikes, family resemblance | play context |

Capture rules: PNG, never JPEG (compression moves the extremes and the reserved hues); keep originals next to derived images; no beauty grade before measuring; before/after pairs share camera, pose, light, seed and competing effects (a lighting test changes only light, and says so); put an approved anchor in the same scene for style comparisons; three evidence channels stay apart — neutral material render (shape and material), silhouette render (outline, negative space), full game capture (does identity survive light, clutter, UI and effects) (04:CP1-CP4). Every coverage, brightness or timing number names device, resolution, quality level, camera distance and frame rate.

## 2. Running the tool and reading the JSON
`python tools/art/look_check.py <check> <image> [options] --out <review folder>/<capture>-<check>.json --out-dir <review folder>/diag` (`contrast` takes no image and no `--out-dir`: `contrast --sample <image> --fg-box ... --bg-box ... --out <json>`, or `contrast --pair FG,BG --rule <r> --out <json>`)
| Check | Use it for | Read | Never conclude |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `value` | value hierarchy; does the subject separate from its own surround; layer bands | `subject`, `ring` (p5-p95, mean), `subject_ring_ratio`, `whole`; the luminance image (the grey view) | a pass for world art without a project tolerance; accessibility; motion readability |
| `contrast --pair FG,BG --rule <r>` | UI text and UI components with a named rule; without a rule, any two sampled regions (`--sample --fg-box --bg-box`) as a diagnostic | unrounded ratio; pass/fail against the named rule (wcag-text 4.5, wcag-large 3.0, wcag-nontext 3.0, xbox-text 4.5, xbox-large 3.0, xbox-hc 7.0) | a UI rule applied to world props or enemies; mixing standards (WCAG exempts inactive text, Xbox asks 3:1 for it); the Xbox 36/72/144 px values define "large" text on mobile, they are not a minimum size (FC-A FA08-FA12) |
| `silhouette` | outline identity, size at the camera, change vs an anchor | `bbox_px`, `bbox_frac`, `area_frac`, `components` (sheets: one per view), `iou` with `--compare` and its alignment | appeal; recognition by players; a universal pixel minimum |
| `palette --roles roles.json` | role colors drifting from the bible | clusters (Lab, hex, share); per role `delta_e2000` to the nearest cluster; the swatch strip | "the right number of colors"; style match from a histogram; that a small accent is absent (default k=8 merges it: §5 line 4 counts pixels); that a material is not flat (k-means splits one hue by value: §5 line 10) |
| `thumb --size WxH` | the real icon slot, thumbnail crop or phone viewport | the small image and sheet; `scale`; `value` and `silhouette` re-run on it | a universal thumbnail size (use the real slot) |
| `thumb_check.py icon` / `thumbnail --size WxH --size-source "<capture id, device>" --bottom-band N` (refuses `--size` without `--size-source`) | the game icon at 150x150 and the thumbnail at the measured tile | master checks (square, 512, 16:9), small-size contrast, components, the subject share in the bottom band | click-through; truthfulness (compare with an in-game capture yourself) |

**Verdicts.** `measured` = numbers only. `pass`/`fail` only when you pass a tolerance (`--rule`, or `--min-ratio` / `--min-iou` / `--max-delta-e` with `--tolerance-source "<D-id or standard>"`). `invalid-mask` = no usable subject mask: fix the mask before reading subject or ring numbers; the `whole` distribution is still measured.

**Masks — rules learned by running the tool on our real inputs (2026-10-22):**
- **In-scene captures have no flat border:** `value`, `thumb` and `silhouette` give `invalid-mask`. Ask for the mask capture, run `silhouette` on it, and pass its black-fill with `--mask` to the in-scene capture (tested: hero subject/ring 2.94 on a 1600x720 frame).
- **Never a `--box` that holds background** on a render or capture: every background pixel counts and the median moves with no error (tested: a dark lower body measured 0.285, the magenta background's luminance, instead of 0.053). `--box-key` fails (`border is not flat`) when the box edge crosses the subject. Cut region masks from the subject's black-fill instead (§5 line 1).
- **A box is safe** only when every pixel in it belongs to what you measure: the play area of the environment-only capture (the environment band), a UI control on a flat panel with `--box-key`, or a sample box fully inside one part. A single layer cut (effects off) still holds characters and HUD: tested, the play-area p95 went from 0.108 to 1.0 with no error.
- **`subject_ring_ratio` is symmetric** ((brighter + 0.05) ÷ (darker + 0.05)): it is never below 1 and says nothing about which side is brighter. A pass line needs a real starting value ("≥ 1.5"), never "> 1"; read the two medians for the direction.
- **The ring is whatever surrounds the mask.** On a flat-key render it is the key colour, so a ring number means "against the backdrop", never "against the ground". Pass `--exclude <mask>` (dark = excluded) to keep the subject's own other parts out of the ring.
- **Flat grey review renders** (`review_renders.py`, sRGB 128): pass `--key-tol 4` to every call that keys the background; the default 40 dropped a lit skin-tone head from the black-fill with no error (tested).
- **`silhouette --compare` aligns by bbox height**, so a taller variant (a new hat) is scaled down before the IoU: compare footprints by `area_frac` and `bbox_px` of both images at the same camera, and read the IoU as change in shape, not size.
- Default `--key auto` keys a flat border color and fills enclosed key-colored areas; `--keep-holes` when an enclosed gap is part of the silhouette; `--component N` for one view of a sheet. Check the silhouette image before trusting any number (04:CP5). Ground shadows count as subject; mask them out when they matter.

**UI screens.** Auto-key rarely finds a subject on a full screen: mask a control with `--box x0,y0,x1,y1` (add `--box-key` to drop its panel color), keep neighboring controls out of the ring with `--exclude-box`, and keep the ring narrow where controls sit close. "Is the primary the brightest control?": `value --compare-subjects` with one named box per control (tested: ranks `buy` over `chances`). Text contrast from a rendered screen: `contrast --sample <image> --fg-box ... --bg-box ...`. Box coordinates come from the spec, the Figma node or a measured mask — never from eyeballing the image.

**Roles file** (from the bible's color roles): `{"danger": "#e8582a", "reward": "#ffd34d", "interactable": "#4fc3f7"}`. Authored hexes only for base-color renders. On a lit capture the roles come from the capture itself: a reference block (or a gameplay object) per role in the stage's plain material, its lit face sampled as rendered under the same light (`contrast --sample` with a box inside it, as `environment-craft/references/measure-captures.md` M12 does), the way the bible's reserved-colours row takes reserved hues as rendered; under a stage light the distance to an authored hex grows with the light's level and tint, not with the material. Under a dim or night look also ask for the same view under a neutral light. Compare authored swatches, base-color renders and lit captures separately (R-06).

## 3. What the tool cannot do
Motion, timing, sound, device performance, player recognition, appeal. Edge density and color-vision simulation are not built (J009-D5; edge density is unreliable for stylized outlines, 02:M11). Screen share of an effect or panel: mask it (or key it on a capture with the effect isolated) and read `silhouette` `area_frac`; record how the mask was made. For color-blind checks, ask for a simulator capture (e.g. Color Oracle, record the mode) and a non-color channel; for recognition, run a cold read and, when the run allows a subagent, a fresh reader with no notes; report uncertainty (02:M6, M9). Measures the tool lacks are §5 command lines (tested) or manual checks with a tool proposal (J018 proposals file): the reserved-hue pixel share, the hue spread inside one material, chroma per horizontal band, the squint (blur) view, the mirror, the contact sheet, the near-black and clipped-white counts, detail density (manual: busy zones in the squint view). Small slots (icons, phone tiles) are looked at on the `thumb` sheet's nearest x4 panel, never at 1:1: below about 200 px the model's own view is unreliable (R-02).

## 5. Command lines (each run on 2026-10-22 on the input type it names)
Run from the repo root. `<d>` = the review's `diag` folder. Before trusting a number, ask what would make it wrong with no error (listed per line).
1. **Region mask from a black-fill** (upper vs lower body, a focal zone, one material). Input: a flat-key render or a mask capture's black-fill. Measure a region with `look_check.py value <image> --mask <black-fill> --mask-box x0,y0,x1,y1` (J019 G3-P8). Write a region mask file only when another run needs it as `--exclude` (the lower-body case below):
   ```
   python -c "import sys;from PIL import Image,ImageChops;m=Image.open(sys.argv[1]).convert('L');x0,y0,x1,y1=map(int,sys.argv[2].split(','));b=Image.new('L',m.size,255);b.paste(0,(x0,y0,x1,y1));ImageChops.lighter(m,b).save(sys.argv[3])" <d>/<name>.silhouette.png x0,y0,x1,y1 <d>/<region>.mask.png
   ```
   Tested: upper body p50 0.428, lower body 0.053. RD13 reads lower p50 ÷ upper p50 against the bible's ratio (`art-bible.md` §7 Value bands): repair round 2, 0.078 on a dark-legged render (pass at about 0.7), 0.987 when the lower body matched the upper (fail). Wrong with no error: box edges guessed by eye; take them from the capture operator's `WorldToViewportPoint` of named parts or the modeler's projection.
   **Lower body against the ground** (RD13): only on the in-scene capture over the real ground, with region masks cut from its mask capture's black-fill (same camera). Cut both regions (upper rows, lower rows), then `look_check.py value <in-scene capture> --mask <d>/lower.mask.png --exclude <d>/upper.mask.png`: the exclusion keeps the upper body out of the ring, so the ring is ground. Pass `--min-ratio <the bible's character target, starting value 1.5: art-bible.md §7 Separation targets> --tolerance-source "<D-id or PROPOSAL starting value>"` for a verdict. Tested on a 1600x720 frame: legs painted the ground's colour gave ratio 1.04 (fail at 1.5), dark legs 2.02 (pass); without `--exclude` the ring took 400 upper-body pixels. Wrong with no error: run on a flat-key render (the ring is the key colour: tested 4.65 against magenta); a "> 1" pass (the ratio is never below 1). A lower region under 0.1 % of the frame gives `invalid-mask`: ask for the same camera angle at a closer distance or a larger viewport, recorded in the manifest.
2. **Squint view** (read order, detail clumps): blur by a share of the frame height (starting value 0.008, PROPOSAL; record it). Input: the busiest capture.
   ```
   python -c "import sys;from PIL import Image,ImageFilter as F;i=Image.open(sys.argv[1]);r=max(1,round(i.height*float(sys.argv[3])));i.filter(F.GaussianBlur(r)).save(sys.argv[2]);print('blur radius px',r)" <capture> <d>/<name>.squint.png 0.008
   ```
   Then one `contrast --sample <d>/<name>.squint.png --fg-box <inside item> --bg-box <its adjacent background>` pair per ranked item: the ratios must fall in the rank order (tested: objective 3.57, hero 2.90, rock 1.13). Wrong with no error: a sample box that crosses the item's edge; keep boxes fully inside the item and fully on its surround.
3. **Mirror** (fresh eyes): `python -c "from PIL import Image,ImageOps;ImageOps.mirror(Image.open('<image>')).save('<d>/<name>.mirror.png')"`.
4. **Reserved-hue share** (CL1). Input: the environment-only capture; `<exclude>` = a black-fill of the gameplay objects that may wear the hue (dark = excluded) or `-` for none; distance = CIEDE2000 to the swatch, the bible's value (starting value: `art-bible.md` §7). The kit mode `look_check.py reserved` (J018 G3-P8) reads `measurements.share` and `per_swatch`, and writes the marked pixels (`images.marked`).
   ```
   python tools/art/look_check.py reserved <capture> --swatch <reserved hex> [--swatch <the same colour as rendered>] --max-de 10 [--exclude <black-fill of gameplay objects>] [--box x0,y0,x1,y1] --out-dir <d>
   ```
   Tested: 0.00072 found where 0.072 % of the frame was the reserved red; 0 on the same frame without it. `palette` at the default k=8 merged the same accent into a brown cluster (ΔE 24) and would have passed it. Repair round 2: environment-only with a red enemy excluded 0 px; the same frame with a red HUD health bar 0.0125 (14 400 px). Wrong with no error: a frame that still holds characters, effects or the HUD (their reserved hues count as decoration); the lit, tone-mapped colour drifts from the authored swatch; sample the reserved colour as it renders in the same capture (`contrast --sample` on a gameplay object, its `hex`) and use that swatch too.
5. **Chroma per band** (CL9: is the player band calmer than above it?). Input: the environment-only capture. Chroma = max minus min of R, G, B, 0-255 (how colourful; HSV saturation would flag tinted near-black); it is not the environment designer's CIELAB chroma, so compare the bands with each other, never with their neutral limit. Bands are boxes `name=x0,y0,x1,y1`.
   ```
   python tools/art/look_check.py bands <environment-only capture> --band above=0,360,1600,420 --band player=0,420,1600,560
   ```
   Tested (repair round 2): environment-only, decoration above head height 90.0 vs player band 12.0 (pass); a saturated player band 100.0 vs 10.0 (fail). Wrong with no error: a frame that still holds characters, effects or the HUD (12 saturated avatars moved the player band from 12.0 to 47.2); an upper box that reaches into the sky (a blue sky lifted it from 10.0 to 67.5, so a saturated player band at 60.0 passed); in a perspective frame a row is not a height: take the rows from head-height and floor markers at the subject's distance (capture operator), or use a side-on capture.
6. **Contact sheet** (one image per option round, colour row over grey row, at phone height). Viewing aid only: the grey row is not the measured luminance (that is `value`'s luminance image).
   ```
   python tools/art/look_check.py sheet <d>/sheet.png <option A> <option B> <option C> --height 360
   ```
7. **Phone size of a whole frame:** `look_check.py thumb <capture> --mask <mask capture black-fill> --size <phone WxH>`; `scale` = phone height ÷ capture height (tested 0.5 for 1600x720 → 800x360). Never `--size` at the subject's own size on a whole frame. Without `--mask` the small image is still written but subject numbers are `invalid-mask`.
8. **Environment band:** `look_check.py value <environment-only capture> --box <play-area rows>`: read `subject` p5 and p95 against the bible band. Tested on the same 1600x720 play area: environment-only p5 0.069, p95 0.108; the effects-off cut with 10 characters and a HUD bar still on gave p95 1.0 with no error. Never on a single layer cut or the busiest capture. The `whole` distribution includes the sky. p5 and p95 cannot see a small black or white patch: run line 9 on the same box. **Depth bands** (RD9): the same command once per band (near rows, far rows, both below the horizon); compare the p5-p95 spreads. Tested: a calm far band 0.048 vs near 0.303 (pass), a busy far band 0.565 vs near 0.048 (fail); 22 characters left in the near rows moved its spread to 0.953 and passed the busy far band with no error; a far box that reached into the sky moved 0.048 to 0.261.
9. **Near-black and clipped counts** (RD3 "no pure black", RD5 "no large clipped area"). Input: near-black, the environment-only capture's play-area box; clipped white, the UI-off cut (effects and characters stay on: they must not blow out either) with the box below the horizon and `<exclude>` = a black-fill of the named accents (dark = excluded; from their mask capture or a line 1 region mask) or `-`. Thresholds: luminance below 0.004 (the environment designer's near-black measure) and at or above 0.98. The kit mode `look_check.py extremes` (J018 G3-P8) writes the marked pixels (blue = near-black, red = clipped) and warns on a JPEG input.
   ```
   python tools/art/look_check.py extremes <capture> --box x0,y0,x1,y1 [--exclude <accent black-fill>] [--near-black 0.004] [--clipped 0.98] --out-dir <d>
   ```
   Tested: a 30x30 pure-black patch in the play area gave 900 px (share 0.0016) while p5 stayed 0.069; a 200x100 white sign gave clipped share 0.017 with the 10x10 accent excluded, and 0 once only the accent was left. Repair round 2: a clean busiest frame with 5 white HUD icons and a sun disk gave clipped share 0.0094 on the whole frame (a false fail); its UI-off cut below the horizon gave 0, and 0.024 with a 200x100 white sign. Wrong with no error: a JPEG capture (compression lifts or crushes the extremes: ask for PNG); the HUD or the sky in the box; characters in the near-black box; an accent mask that also covers the faulty area. Open the marked image only when a count is not zero.
10. **Hue spread inside one material** (CL6, MD4; the modeler's CL5). A screening aid: it finds a flat or one-hue material; it never judges taste. Input: one box fully inside one material on its lit side (no cast shadow, no edge of another material, decal or trim), on a PNG: a flat-key or review render, or a capture (of a place: Atmosphere and fog off). It reads the spread of the CIELAB hue angle over 4 × 4-pixel blocks (the kit mode `look_check.py hue-spread`, the studio's one flat-fill measure, J018 G3-P8): a light gradient, a white highlight and a value texture change it little; only a change of hue raises it. It returns `valid` false and verdict not-measured on a near-neutral or low-chroma box.
   ```
   python tools/art/look_check.py hue-spread <image> --box x0,y0,x1,y1
   ```
   Pass: `hue_sd_deg` above the flat limit (starting value 3°, PROPOSAL); under it the material is flat, or monochrome (a value texture in one hue), and fails. Valid only with `median_chroma` about 15 or more and `blocks_used` near 1: a near-neutral material (grey stone, steel) gives NaN or a hue angle that swings with noise; judge it by eye and say so.
