# Reference images: one picture of the target for every agent

## 1. When this skill starts
Before any visual work is built: a new game or stage, a character, creature, model, prop, animation, effect or screen. Also when work drifts from the style, and when a review note needs a picture (a paint-over of a capture). The game needs `knowledge/design/art-bible.md` (copy `templates/skill/art-bible.md`); without it, the art director (or the lead) writes it first and the owner approves its locks.

**You see stills.** You judge images and measured numbers, never play. "Reads well" means measured and looked at the size players really see the subject (§4 step 1), not at full size. After a compaction, re-read §4, §6, §7 and §8.

## 4. Steps
An image batch runs in rounds. Steps 2-6 repeat for each round; the budgets are starting values (PROPOSAL; the brief's cap wins; owner J005-D8: numbers for every image, eyes only on a few).
| Round | When | Output | Images |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R0 brief and research | always | image brief, research board, keyword table | 0 |
| R1 rough options | every new look (character, prop, place, theme, variant, screen, key moment, a new signature pose) | 6-12 one-line hooks (text), then a labelled warm-up and the 3-5 strongest hooks as roughs; the pick sheet | hooks 0; ≤ 6 with regenerations; highest quality (I-13) |
| R2 iterate | the pick needs a merge or one named change | 1-3 variants | ≤ 3; highest quality (I-13) |
| R3 final | after a recorded pick, or at once when an approved image covers the look | the working reference the next maker asked for | one per view or panel, + ≤ 2 regenerations each; I-13 |

1. **Brief and research (R0).** What the image must show and why (`rules/messages.md`): the problem it answers; the constraints (family, game camera and angle, the size players see it: the slot in px or the subject's height in px on a phone at the game camera, from the game profile or the modeler's game-camera render; budget, quality); the end (the success test); the sell list (2-4 items), what must read first (1-2 parts), and for a character, prop or place its card. Then the research board and the keyword table. Fields and examples: `references/rounds.md` §1-§2. No prompt before these exist.
2. **Manifest:** one row per image in a CSV: `id, family, identity, inputs, anchors`. The id says the round (`<asset>-warm`, `-a`..`-e`, `-v1`, `-final-back`). `identity` describes only the subject (purpose, subject, pose or action, must-keep details, subject-specific "no" items: `references/prompt-patterns.md` §5); the style comes from the locks. In R1 each option carries its own hook and one fiction line; in R2 each variant names the one element it changes; in R3 each row names its view or panel. Keep the manifest with the job (`knowledge/jobs/<job>/` or `content/<area>/<asset>/refs/`).
3. **Check the prompt:** `python tools/art/art_refs.py print <manifest>` from the repo root (input paths relative to the repo root) and read the assembled prompt and the attached anchors. Fix the identity, not the lock. When the identity has to contradict its lock (a back view under a three-quarter lock), stop: the art director proposes a family whose lock allows it and the owner approves the lock (I-04).
   **Who generates.** An agent generates and downloads only when its tools include the built-in browser **and** its brief records the owner's yes for this batch. Otherwise it writes the checked prompts to `image-prompts.md` next to its deliverable (the manifest path, each row's assembled prompt, its input images, the round), lists `images: pending <path>` in its hand-back, and continues; the step does not fail and the item is not an open coverage item. The lead runs pending batches in its own chat between stages (steps 4-6, the owner's yes per batch) and passes the image paths into the next stage. A rough round and its final round are two batches: the pick sits between them. No craft builds visual work before the approved image exists.
4. **Generate (default route: ChatGPT in the agent's built-in browser, owner J009-D16).** The owner is signed in; never type credentials, never sign out. For each manifest row: open a **new normal chat** (`https://chatgpt.com/`; temporary chats cannot make images) in its own tab (several tabs run at once); set the thinking-effort control to the row's quality (I-13); paste "Create an image." plus the prompt assembled by `python tools/art/art_refs.py print <manifest>`; attach the row's input images and say each one's role; wait for "Generated image 1" (about 1-2 minutes). Read and view the result on the page, review it (step 5), then download only with the owner's yes **for this batch** (file list, source chat, destination `content/art/refs/<family>/<id>.png`): recorded in your brief, or asked by the lead in its own chat. Never download without that yes. Save the prompt beside the image (`<id>.json`: prompt, chat URL, date, route, round). **Clean up after every batch, whatever the answer** (owner J009-D18): approved → download, then archive each chat and close its tab; declined → archive each chat and close its tab. Archive, never delete (agents may not delete data; the owner bulk-deletes archived chats). Fallback route: `python tools/art/art_refs.py run <manifest> --limit N` (Codex; older model, lower quality in the 2026-10-21 comparison); it stops at the first failure: report it, do not retry in a loop.
5. **Review** every image with its round's lines (`references/self-review.md` §2) before anyone builds from it: numbers for every image; eyes on one contact sheet per round, every failing number and the pick. Roughs: black-fill and grey at real size, colour only on options that pass. Finals: the full check, big to small. Regenerate with a fixed identity, max 2 tries per image, then report.
6. **Pick and approve.** R1 ends with one pick sheet (`references/rounds.md` §4). The requester (or the owner at a gate) picks; record who and their words. Images the owner approves can become anchors (`art_refs.py approve <family> <id>`), so later batches match them.
7. **Hand over** the image path in the next brief's `refs`, with: take and ignore; the iconic qualities the build must keep; gaps and placeholders (PROPOSAL); what should move or glow, as intent, never as settings; material intent per region; the pick record; the image counts (`references/rounds.md` §5, §7).

**Every agent can get ChatGPT images** (owner J002-D11, 2026-10-21: "ChatGPT has a better image gen model"): it generates them itself or writes the prompts for the lead (step 3, "Who generates"). Routes: the built-in browser (default, step 4, owner J009-D16; the owner always wants the newest model, J009-D9); the fallback `art_refs.py run` (Codex); a prompt pack the owner runs in the ChatGPT app for large batches; Figma `generate_image` only when the owner asks for it. Same locks in every route. No art bible yet (a kit test, a new game): `--bible templates/skill/art-bible.md` runs on the template's starter locks.

| I-02 | **Anchors beat adjectives.** Attach up to 3 approved anchors; the owner's own images first, our newest approved one last. Anchors guide the whole look (shape, color, light, materials, rendering); where an anchor and the lock disagree, the lock wins (owner J009-D11). The model's generic glossy default is a fault | first game anchor library (2026-10) |

| I-04 | **Model references are single, clean views.** One subject, three-quarter view, T- or A-pose, nothing overlapping, plain background. Extra angles in one image make Tripo build extra models. Build views are separate images, one view each, the approved image attached, never a collage; a view the lock forbids needs a family whose lock allows it (art director proposes, owner approves) | first game, owner D-153 (2026-10-11); J018 print run |
| I-05 | **Check before building:** views match, construction possible and attached, no extra or missing parts, the back plausible, scale against a player right, it reads at the size players see it: the final lines FN1-FN15 (`references/self-review.md`) | owner rejected first builds made without approved refs (first game and the dam game, 2026-10) |
| I-06 | **Images are inputs.** A reference is a target and a source of shapes, never the shipped asset; UI stays live layers, models are built, effects are built. Under a three-quarter lock (character, prop, model) the image keeps the lock's view and the game-camera read is judged at R3 (FN13). Light direction and reserved hues come from the lock. ## 6. Rules (final check)
- No craft builds visual work before an approved image exists: one the owner approved at a gate or in a digest, or an approved anchor or reference that covers the work. Gate packets show the images.
- No style text outside the art bible; no franchise characters, logos or real brands in prompts.
- No keys, passwords or account details in manifests or the repo; the tool uses the owner's logged-in Codex app.
- Generated images are saved with their prompt record; never overwrite an approved reference.
- Content stays inside the game's maturity label and `platform-policy` (it wins): no sexualised bodies or poses, blood and gore only as the label allows, a real culture or ethnicity represented accurately, a theme that could read as cruelty carried by a prop motif instead of an act. Reveal, reward and tier images show only tiers the game really gives.
- Images carry no text, numbers, logos or UI unless the family's pattern says so (`references/prompt-patterns.md` §4).

## 8. Core principles for this craft
The studio's shared principles, stated for making reference images; numbers follow the studio's master list. Each has an example from our work and the self-review line that checks it (`references/self-review.md`). Where a §5 practice already holds the rule, the principle points to it.

- **P1 The gameplay read comes first.** An image states the read it must support before its mood: what a player must know from this subject at a glance (it is a threat, it is mine, it is a pickup, it is about to hatch). Between two options, take the one a player names correctly at a glance, even when the other is prettier. Example: two egg designs for a pet game; the ornate one reads "decoration" at its shop-tile size, the plain one with a big glowing crack reads "about to hatch": the plain one is picked. Check: BR2, RO4, PK1.
- **P2 Judge it where players meet it.** Every image is judged at the size players see the subject and from the game camera's angle (at R3 for families whose lock fixes a three-quarter view), never only at full size. Example: a sword sheet looks rich at 1536 px; at 40 px in a hand at the third-person camera only the blade shape and the guard read, so the guard is pushed. Check: BR2, RO4, FN5, FN13.
- **P3 One read first; contrast only there.** The brief names 1-2 read-first parts; the image gives them the strongest value step and keeps the rest quieter. Example: a boss key art where the glowing core is the lightest area and the arena behind stays low in contrast. Check: RO5, FN6.
- **P4 Big before small.** Roughs are judged as black-fills and greys before colour; detail is asked for only after the pick (I-14, I-15). Example: four pet roughs compared as black shapes at 60 px before anyone discusses fur colour. Check: RO4, RO5.
- **P5 Shape carries identity and meaning.** The brief lists the 3-4 identifying elements and asks for them chunky; the shape language comes from the card (sharp for danger, round for friendly, square for stable) and the bible's vocabulary. Example: a friendly slime pet stays round and soft; the hazard mushroom gets a spiked cap. Check: RO4, FN5, FN10.
- **P6 Less is more.** One or two lines of subject and 1-3 must-haves (I-11); detail clumped on the focal part with quiet areas around it; masses indicated (hair in a few big clumps, grass as soft patches). Example: "hair in four big clumps" instead of a list of strands. Check: FN9.
- **P7 One code for meaning.** Reserved gameplay hues never appear on decoration (the lock carries the list); effect shapes follow the bible's shape table (plus for heal, circle for shield, spikes for damage); one hue never means help and harm. Example: red is the enemy colour, so the friendly shop's awning in the hub concept is orange. Check: FN7, FN10.
- **P8 A still implies the moment.** Pose sheets, key frames and panels show the moment at its peak or just before it, in time order (build, peak, result), with motion shown by a pose's line of action or 2-3 flow lines, never by blur. Example: the hammer pose at contact, the head on the anvil and the knees bent. Check: FN12.
- **P9 Push what must read, on a believable base.** Hook shapes and poses are pushed past real (adjectives turned into pose changes, pulled back where weight stops being believable); balance, attachments and held weight stay believable. Example: a "heavy" digger gets a wide, low stance and holds the drill close to the body. Check: FN3, FN5, FN12.
- **P10 The subject's character drives every choice.** The card comes first (personality words, role, tension, how it moves); every element in the image traces to a line of it, and props and places get personality words too. Example: a kiosk card "old, cheerful, cluttered" gives patched paint, a crooked sign and stacked crates. Check: BR3, PK1.
- **P11 One world, one style.** Style comes from the lock and anchors (I-01, I-02); every result is compared with 2-3 anchors at the same size; variants keep every identity anchor. Example: a winter skin of the game's knight keeps the pan-shield, the dented helmet and the orange plume. Check: FN4, FN10.
- **P12 Controlled variation, never exact repetition.** The subject gets use and wear where hands go and one asymmetric element (hue-shifted shadows come from the lock); reject the uniform glossy finish and exact repeats; never vary what carries meaning. Example: a crate stack where each crate has different dents, the reward crate still marked the same way. Check: FN5, FN9.
- **P13 Problem, constraints and end before making.** R0 comes before any prompt (step 1); builders list every deviation from the reference with its reason. Example: the starter-pet brief names the 60 px read, the sell list and the modeler as the next maker before the first prompt. Check: BR1.
- **P14 Research, many distinct options, a reasoned pick.** A board with our anchors and real-world reference, one source from outside games; breadth in cheap text hooks, the strongest few as images, the warm-up set aside, one radical, the pick with reasons (`references/rounds.md` §2-§4). Example: eight hooks for the starter pet, four generated beside the warm-up; the radical flame-pup lost on the pet read. Check: BR4, RO1-RO3, PK1.
- **P15 Cheap stages first; do not be precious.** Roughs are disposable; the pick freezes the big shapes; a closed direction comes back only with new owner words; a weak image is regenerated or dropped, never defended. Example: the candle pup was dropped at the pick, logged as a closed door and not proposed again. Check: PK2, FN4.
- **P16 Critique against the goal, one named problem at a time.** The pick sheet asks a goal question ("which reads as most dangerous at phone size?"); review notes become paint-overs with one change each (I-17); the mirror and the grey refresh your own eye; the hand-back names one honest weakness. Check: PK1, PO1-PO2, HO3.
- **P17 Build for the next craft.** Ask the next maker what views and notes they need; deliver separate clean views, intent notes instead of settings, text-free images (I-18). Example: the rigger gets the back view and a line saying which coattail is rigid. Check: HO1, HO4, FN12.
- **P18 Know when it is done.** Stop criteria and the image budget are set in the brief; a concept stops when shapes, values, colours and the focal point are settled; after 2 regenerations an image is reported, not chased. Example: the pet's final round stopped once the modeler had no shape or colour questions left; the tail flame stayed an intent note for the effect. Check: BR7, HO2.
- **P19 Images show the real outcome.** Reveal, reward and tier images show the tier the player really gets; a common result never wears a rarer frame; marketing images show only what the game has. Example: the common egg's reveal frame keeps the plain trim; the gold trim is drawn only on the legendary frame. Check: FN15.
- **P20 Every player can read it.** Tiers, teams and states differ in shape, trim or icon as well as hue, and still read in grey. Example: rarity frames for pet cards differ in corner shape as well as colour. Check: FN7.
