# The read plan, the ranked work list and the option round

A place is planned around what the player must read in it. Write this sheet before the first reference image; every later choice (layout cue, light, colour, prop, sound) traces back to one of its lines, and the self-review (`review.md` §7) measures against it.

## 1. The read plan sheet (`content/stages/<stage-id>/read-plan.md`)
| Field | What to write | Example (a mining-town hub) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Job and conflict | the game designer's job sentence and conflict sentence, copied with their revision | "Sell ore, upgrade the pick, find the next mine"; no conflict (social hub) |
| Personality | the place as a character, 3-5 words; they drive shape language and materials | "busy, dusty, proud, frontier" |
| Story facts | 3 facts: who lives or works here, what happened, climate and age; each mapped to props, wear or layout | miners live upstairs of the shops (laundry lines, lamps lit at dusk); a cave-in closed the old shaft (boarded entrance, rubble); dry and windy (dust drifts against the north walls) |
| Theme cues | 2-3 signature materials or shapes that make the setting unmistakable | ore veins in the rock, timber shoring, rail tracks |
| Key views | the views the review measures: the first view after spawn, each decision view, the busiest moment, the main return trip | first view, mine-gate fork, shop row at peak, sell trip back |
| Ranked reads per key view | rank 1, 2, 3 for that view, from the game designer's read rank (UI and gameplay effects outrank characters; characters outrank the environment) | first view: 1 the mine gate (goal), 2 the sell stall, 3 the upgrade shop; backdrop last |
| Critical facts | what the player must know at a glance in each key view (where to go, where am I, ally or enemy, danger, objective) | where the gate is; where to sell; which shop is open |
| Focal point per view | one object, with the contrast it must win (value, hue, saturation, size, shape, motion) | the mine gate: lightest area, only warm light, tallest frame |
| Smallest gameplay object | the smallest thing players must spot on a floor, and each floor type it can lie on | dropped ore chunk (1 stud) on cobble, dirt, plank |
| Value band and reserved colours | the art bible's environment band and reserved hues for this stage; until the art director sets them, the starting values in `art-review/references/art-bible.md` §7 marked `PROPOSAL` | band 15-85 % grey; reserved: red (danger), gold (rare ore) |
| Key light | the sun direction and time of day per area, chosen in grey on the greybox (step 5) | ClockTime 15, sun over the shop row onto the gate |
| Detail spots | 1-3 places per area where detail clusters; everything else is rest | the gate, the sell stall, the fountain |
| Ranked work list | agreed with the game designer: play spaces and routes first, landmarks and first view next, filler last; effort, unique assets and triangles follow the rank | 1 shop row and gate path, 2 gate and first view, 3 back alleys (kit only) |
| Stop criteria and pass budget | the conditions that end the work, and how many full passes and option rounds you may spend | every `review.md` §7 line passes at both graphics settings; 2 option rounds; 3 build passes |

Each field has a source (layout, art bible, owner D-id, game profile) or is `PROPOSAL`. A field you cannot fill is an intake question, not a guess.

## 2. The ranked work list
The environment fills most of the screen and there is always more to build than time allows. Rank the work with the game designer before the blueprint: play spaces and the main route first, landmarks and the first view next, side areas and filler last. The rank sets where unique assets, triangles and polish go; the background takes reused kit pieces. The 3-5 most-visited views get every top-ranked item visible and finished in them.

## 3. The option round (step 3)
- **Research first.** Real-world reference for the place type (layouts, odd details, materials, how water or rock really behaves) and the game's own anchors. Note per entry what it is for and one specific detail you will use. Take ideas, never a design.
- **Keyword table.** For each word of the brief: colour key, setting, typical prop, typical sound ("abandoned" → dust greys, boarded doors, swinging sign, creak).
- **Three distinct directions,** each from a different source or reading of the theme (a different layout idea, landmark or light, never only a colour change), one deliberately unexpected, taking an idea from outside games (architecture, film, a real place). The first batch is a warm-up: label it so and keep going. All options at the same low finish and small size, same camera, labelled A, B, C.
- **Pick with reasons.** One recommendation with the reason tied to the read plan ("B frames the gate as the brightest shape from spawn"); one line per rejected option on why it lost; record what is kept from a rejected one. An option that reads as another genre or tone loses, however beautiful. A second round may vary one named element of the pick (the light, the landmark, the ground) and merge the strongest two. Ties go to the owner with your recommendation.
- **Image budget:** at most 3 option images per round and 2 rounds before the owner; refined images only after a recorded pick.

## 4. Constraints dressed as the world
For each gameplay constraint the layout gives you (a boundary, a choke point, cover, a height gate, a hazard), list 2-3 fictional answers (a wall, a building, a cliff over the sea; a rail, a fence, a collapsed bridge) and pick with the art director the one that is most believable and beautiful in this setting. Record one line per constraint: "why is this here in the world?". The collision matches what players see; the layout itself stays the game designer's.
