# Design for motion: what the rig must make possible

Read at S2 (`SKILL.md` §3 steps 2, 4, 6, 8, 10). The modeler builds the fixes (`modeling-design/references/read-and-form.md` §6; its checks MO1-MO7 in `modeling-design/references/review.md` §5); the animator states the poses (S1). This file is the rigger's side: what to screen, which controls the read needs, how pushes reach Roblox, and how the plan is posed before anyone builds.

## 1. The design-for-motion screen (S2 step 4)
Run it on the reference set (front, back, sides, game camera) or the blockout, against the §B stress poses. Every finding gets an owner: the modeler (in the S3 brief), the art director (a look change, through the lead), or the animator (a pose that cannot be reached).
| Item | Look for | Usual fix | Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mass on a joint | static, unjointed mass inside a bend zone or across a planned cut: high collars, scarves, armour rims at the shoulder, static hair reaching the nape, shoulders or chest, a belt over the hip crease | move it off the joint, bundle it (hair into one unit set away from the nape), shorten it, mount it on one side of the cut, or give it its own rigid piece | count on the marked plan renders; each has a route the modeler can build; posed plan (§4) shows no collision |
| Drapes over the arms | capes, cowls, sleeves or tabards hanging in front of the shoulders, arms or body; pauldrons mounted on the shoulder are fine | shorten, split behind the arm, mount on the shoulder, or drop | the arm outline stays separate from the torso in the idle, run and attack black-fills |
| Costly parts | capes, loose cloth, tassels, long hair, coattails, a hanging whip, dangling charms: each needs bones, weights, keyed secondary motion and phone cost | keep only with a written reason (theme, premium value) and a rigid or bundled fallback; reuse existing chains before adding new ones | count against the rig budget (§E); the animator and the lead see the count at K2 |
| Parts the rig moves, not built to move | a mouth modelled shut or as a painted line, eyelids without enough lines to close, teeth fused to lips, a jaw with no cavity | model it open-able (circular loops round the mouth, lines across the lids), swap a graphic face, or choose a simple jaw on purpose | a test pose with the mouth open and eyes closed on the proxy shows no tear [V] `art/characters/creating/modeling-best-practices.md` (mouth and eye edge flow) |
| Moving parts without room | a barrel that cannot slide because of decoration, a lid that hits the hinge, a stock that cannot retract past the arm | separate named parts with pivots and nesting cross-sections; travel cleared of the body | full travel posed: no overlap with the arms or body (§4 numbers) |
| Contact points | grips, stock-to-shoulder, seat, hand positions, feet on a mount | the same positions in every variant, with clearance so hands do not clip | variant sockets equal the base within tolerance (`rigging-craft/references/tests.md` §6 R-11) |
| What holds it on | a costume or prop piece with no visible attachment (it floats) | add a strap, clip or buckle, or ride it rigidly on the bone it rests on | each piece names its parent bone; nothing drifts in the stress poses |
| In the round | a part designed from the front only: a lapel that stops at the neck, a brim that ends at the side, an unknown back | ask for the back and side views; decide the missing view with the art director | the defect map includes the back; the plan renders include the back and the game camera |
| Material intent | a shiny region that could be metal (rigid) or cloth (bends); fur over a joint that could hide a cut | ask the art director; the answer decides the route | one material-and-attachment line per moving region (P-03) |

## 2. The controls the read needs (S2 step 6; offered at K2 by default for characters)
| Control | Why | Plan | Roblox note | Test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clavicles (shoulder shrug and reach) | wind-ups, throws and jumps take their energy from the shoulders lifting and tightening first; without them poses look dead | clavicle bones (raise, drop, forward, back) with ranges from the §B wind-up poses; enough loops or cover at the shoulder for the full range | among R15 bodies only advanced R15 has clavicles (`characters/index.md`); a custom rig may add them; on a standard 15-joint body, give UpperTorso and the upper arm the range instead | wind-up test clip: `clip_check.py` rom inside the manifest range; the shoulder area survives the extremes (cap exposure, overlaps) |
| Spine and torso (line of action) | a key pose reads through one clear line of action from head to feet; the torso must bend and twist along it | enough spine segments, or torso tilt and twist range on a two-piece torso, for the curve and the lean the key poses need; on blocky bodies the line comes from torso tilt and the limbs | a standard R15 body bends only at the waist (UpperTorso to LowerTorso); advanced R15 adds Spine and Chest | the signature pose's black-fill shows one dominant curve or diagonal (G-06) |
| Hips (pelvis tilt and shift) | every weight shift tilts the hips; landings sink through them | pelvis control separate from the root, tilt and shift ranges from the landing and crouch poses | the root still owns travel (in-place clips) | crouch and landing poses: no hip collapse; protected opposite thigh still |
| Head world orient | in a spin or fast body turn the head can hold its world direction, then turn late and fast; keying it by counter-rotation is slow and drifts | a switch on the head (optionally the shoulders) that holds the head's rest world frame; it must not move the head at rest | Roblox stores local joint transforms (the Neck under the UpperTorso): the switch is authoring-only and is baked into the head's local keys with visual keying | spin test clip: head drift ≤ tolerance through a 180° body turn, after the bake and after FBX re-import; the switch toggled at rest moves the head 0° |
| Hands | splay on contact, drag on a fall, a grip, a gesture: hand shapes change the profile even when hands are small | finger chains (curl and spread per finger or per group) or hand-pose controls; where the rig has no fingers, swap hand meshes | among R15 bodies only advanced R15 has finger joints (up to 15 per hand); a custom rig may add them; on phones prefer fewer, bigger hand shapes | each named hand shape posed and seen at the game camera in the key images |
| Hook-shape parts | the ponytail swoop, the cape tip, an ear, a weapon fin must face the camera on held moments, even when that is not physically exact | one control per hook part, rotatable without leaving its attachment | keyed into the clip; never a runtime spring on top of a baked lag | the part turned to its extreme toward the camera: attachment holds, no seam |
| Chains (hair, tail, strap, cape) | a heavy mass moves as one chunk, then the chain offsets bone by bone | a base control that drives the chain as one mass, plus per-bone controls for offsets; enough segments to bend both ways | baked to the export bones | chain swung to both extremes: no collapse, no penetration of the body |
| Feet and hands that hold contact | feet stay planted while the hips move; a hand stays on a ledge or a two-handed grip | IK with a planted target, a pole, an IK/FK pose-match switch, foot roll where the clip list needs it | IK does not export: baked samples only; runtime IKControl is a separate builder feature | contact-hold test clip: `clip_check.py` contact_slide ≤ the rig sheet tolerance |
| Spin-heavy controls | Euler channels flip at the wrap (170° to -170° in one frame) | quaternion rotation mode for root, hips and anything that spins | KeyframeSequences use quaternions; Studio's Curve Editor converts to Euler unless its Default Rotation Type is Quaternions (`animation/curve-editor.md`); a quaternion range is checked in `quat` units or as a swing (`rigging-craft/references/controls-export-import.md` §3) | `clip_check.py` pops with a landmark off the spin axis |
| Left and right | twinning is the animator's choice, never the rig's | every left and right control independent; mirroring is an authoring tool | — | no constraint copies one side to the other |

Offer the whole set at K2; the animator strikes what the clip list does not need. Each kept control goes into the control manifest with its range, its keying policy and the read need it serves.

## 3. Pushes past real, by Roblox-safe routes (S2 step 8)
- **No scale in clips [V]:** a KeyframeSequence pose stores only a CFrame (`_api-reference/classes/Pose.yaml`); a Bone's Transform is a CFrame (`Bone.yaml`). Bone scale on a skinned mesh is unverified: probe in Studio before relying on it.
- **Routes per push:**
| Push | Route | Rig consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squash on contact, stretch on speed | a squashed or stretched swap mesh shown for 1-2 frames; a code-driven scale on a separate part; a VFX ring or dust | swap meshes share the pivot and sockets of the base; volume kept (when one axis stretches, the others shrink); the swap is a named event the builder plays |
| Smear on a fast swing | a smear mesh or a Trail or Beam along the motion | a socket at the limb or weapon tip for the trail; the smear mesh rides the bone |
| Oversized hold or hang | posed range beyond the natural limit on joints that can take it (shoulders, spine twist, hips), held for longer | extend the ROM only there; the cut cover or bend zone is sized for the extended range; elbows and knees never invert |
| A hook shape that must show | the part turned toward the camera at held moments | its own control (§2) |
- **A believable base earns the extreme:** contacts, weight shifts and arcs stay correct around the pushed moment; the rig keeps the contact controls working through it.

## 4. The posed plan at the game camera (S2 step 10; at the character proof it is the proxy pose)
1. **What to pose:** the hardest 2-3 §B stress poses (combined, D-10) and the signature pose; for variants, the same poses on the base and the variant.
2. **On what:** a proxy (one bone per part at the planned pivots) on the blockout, or on the source mesh for a Tripo model, split on a copy at the §C cut planes with the cuts filled and each fill put in its own `cap_<cut id>` group, one per cut, so a blockout box between two cuts counts only its two caps (`rigging-craft/references/blender-mcp.md` §6 `mark_plane_caps`, once per cut); a socket holds only the parent's faces the child's cap covers at rest: a blockout from primitives has closed faces under its children, so mark each socket with `child=`, or record it n/a in §C and count that cut on the child's cap; the game camera from the game profile with its distance and vertical field of view, a player-sized reference block, the frame at phone proportions.
3. **Numbers first, for every pose** (recipes: `rigging-craft/references/blender-mcp.md` §6; they are screening aids with known false readings, so a failing or borderline number is checked on the still before it becomes a finding): new overlap pairs between parts that are not parent and child (a hand in the face, a collar in the jaw); cap exposure counted from every review camera on points over the whole of each cap face (recipe 1: a single centre point misses a cap that rotates about its own centre); the black-fill component count and the figure's box (`look_check.py silhouette`, counted as `rigging-craft/references/tests.md` §2 says: a small figure's detached piece is not listed on a full phone frame): a count above the rest count means a piece detached; a box that touches the frame edge means the figure is cropped and every number is wrong.
4. **Then the images, within the budget:** the posed set as black-fill and shaded from one render pass at the game camera (at most 4 poses) plus one close view of the worst one. Look for: limbs, hands, head and feet separate; identity anchors and hook shapes visible from the side the camera sees; nothing hanging in front of the arms; the source of power clear. A number tells you where; the black-fill tells you whether it still reads.
5. **Outcome:** a finding becomes a design-for-motion note (§1), a route change (`SKILL.md` §3 step 5) or a limit stated at K2.
