# Measuring an effect: the capture request and the commands

Numbers first, images only where a number fails or a key still is due (`review.md` §I budget). You do not drive Studio: you name the capture, the studio-operator records it, you measure it. Outputs go to `output/vfx/<effect-id>/checks/` (git-ignored working output).

**Every command in this file is a screening aid, not a verdict.** Next to each one is its list of known false readings (too low, too high, or a pass it is known to miss), each found in a test. Three rules hold for all of them:
1. **Never send a fix note on a number alone.** A failing reading, or a borderline one (within about 10 % of its threshold, or a count at the cap), is checked by eye on the stills of the frames it names (the flash onsets, the widest row, the masked still) before any fix note goes out; sound by ear. Write "checked on stills `<names>`" on the line.
2. **Never trust a pass the recipe is known to miss.** When the scene holds a case from the command's too-low list, the pass stands only after the workaround written next to it, or the eye check, confirms it; otherwise the line is "not verified".
3. **A new false reading** you find goes into the hand-back's `lesson` with the case and the numbers, so it reaches the list and the test set of the kit tool proposed for it.

## 2. Clip numbers
`python tools/vfx/clip_metrics.py <folder>` writes `<folder>/metrics.json`: per row `ms`, `coverage_pct` (changed pixels, % of the screen), `bright_pct` (changed pixels with luma ≥ 230, % of the screen), `box` (x0, y0, x1, y1 as fractions), `centre`, `mean_rgb`, `peak_luma`; a summary with `onset_ms`, `peak_ms`, `peak_coverage_pct`, `peak_bright_pct`, `visible_ms`, `mean_coverage_pct`, `moving_camera`.

| Line | Read |
| --- | --- |
| BL-02 build rises | the rows from the tell start to `peak_ms`: `coverage_pct` and `peak_luma` rise |
| BL-03 one peak | the row with the highest `bright_pct` (or `peak_luma`) against the marker time; no other row more than 2 frames away close to it; `bright_pct` > 0 only on the flash rows. `peak_ms` is the coverage peak: a shape that grows after the flash moves it later (tested: 733 ms for a flash at 400 ms) |
| BL-04 fast in | segment = the rows after the flash frames to the last row with `coverage_pct` > 0 (the build before the contact is BL-02); read the size as the row `box` width (x1 − x0), never `coverage_pct`: coverage is an area, so for a disc or a ring whose thickness grows with it 70 % of the peak coverage is about 84 % of the size, and on coverage a ring built to the C-21 curve failed (tested). The first row at ≥ 70 % of the segment's widest row falls in its first quarter (one frame of slack for the frame step): the one-liner below. Slow out is read on the curves (S) |
| BL-06 eye moves | `centre` and `box` of the rows after the peak move toward the result area |
| BL-07 converging | `box` width and height shrink across the build rows |
| BL-08 tail | the last row with `coverage_pct` > 0 |
| BL-09 footprint | `peak_coverage_pct` against the tier cap; every row's `box` top (y0) below the maximum-height line (a y fraction measured on the effects-off frame). `box` bounds all changed pixels, so a ring or aura around a character has a box that contains them: the cover test is the information-area overlap (§3), never the box |
| IG-05 attempt cue | the first row with `coverage_pct` > 0, never `onset_ms` |
| IG-07, IG-08 | §6, never these rows |

Tested: a ring with keys 0.38 → 0.72 at t 0.25 → 1.0 passes (first 70 % row 1167 ms, quarter 1192 ms; the coverage reading failed it), the same with a fixed line width passes, `linear grow` fails (1367 ms). **Screening aid; known false readings:** any other layer that widens the box (smoke, debris thrown wide, the target's knock-back) sets the width (tested: a `linear grow` ring with a wide smoke puff passed): read the width on the key stills' masks instead, or take a folder with that layer off; a shape that grows upward (a pillar, a geyser) reads the box height (`box[3] − box[1]`); a start ms on a flash frame makes the flash the first row. A FAIL, or a first 70 % row within one frame of the quarter mark, is checked on the stills of the widest row and the quarter-mark row before a fix note.

**`clip_metrics.py` is a screening aid; its known false readings (tested):**
- The effect is on screen in more than half the frames → the median background holds the effect: the numbers invert (still frames count as the effect). Pad the folder.
- `onset_ms` and `visible_ms` count only rows above the larger of 0.2 % and a tenth of the peak: a small attempt cue or a dim build before a big outcome is missed (a cue at 0-100 ms reported `onset_ms` 700). Read the rows.
- A changed pixel differs from the background by more than 40 levels: dark smoke on a dark floor, faint glows and low-opacity layers barely count or vanish (a dark smoke layer measured 0 %). Judge them on stills (§3) with a lower threshold.
- The top-left 10 % × 7 % of the frame (Roblox menu buttons) is ignored; anything else that moves counts as the effect.
- A change of under 40 levels over the whole screen (a grade, Bloom or ColorCorrection pulse) is not seen: coverage and `bright_pct` stay 0 (tested: three full-screen flashes in one second read 0). Count flashes with §6.
- `bright_pct` counts luma ≥ 230, not white: a saturated light core counts as near-white (tested: a (255, 245, 120) yellow core, luma 234, held 10 rows read 0.125 % on each, the same rows as a held white core), and a white core less than 40 levels from a bright sky or floor drops out of the change mask (tested: a 2-frame white core on the top of a sky gradient read 0 on every row). Before a BL-03 or VS-02 fix note, or a pass on a bright background, open those rows' stills and read the core's colour and where it sits.
- A layer on screen for the whole folder (a lasting zone, a countdown) makes the median its middle state: a strong pulse counts at both its bright and its dark phase (1.5 Hz reads as 3 Hz), a weak one not at all. Pulse rates come from the spec and §6.
- Only folder names with `_follow` are flagged as a moving camera; any camera move breaks the background.
- Frames are ordered by name: past 999 frames `f1000_…` sorts before `f101_…`, so the rows come out of time order (tested: 1100 frames, `onset_ms` 33500 for a flash at 33167 ms, `visible_ms` −333). Pad the index to 4 digits (§1); a negative `visible_ms` or rows whose `ms` does not rise is this trap.
- Frame names without `ms` stop the tool ("no f###_<ms>.jpg frames": the docstring drops the `ms` the code needs). That one fails loudly.
- Before a fix note on a row number (a late onset, a long tail, a footprint over the cap), open the still of that row and its mask (§3) and confirm the changed pixels are the effect.

## 4. Mesh black-fill renders (Blender, VS-08)
Render the mesh shape at the game camera (distance, height and FOV from the game profile) with Workbench, flat lighting, single colour black, world colour `#FF00FF`. Then `python tools/art/look_check.py silhouette <render> --key FF00FF --keep-holes --out-dir <checks>` and `thumb ... --key FF00FF --size <phone viewport>`. ## 5. Curve shape numbers (BL-04, CP-07)
Roblox interpolates a NumberSequence linearly between keypoints. For a growing layer, the value at t = 0.25 divided by the curve's maximum must be about 0.7 or more (PROPOSAL; bracket it). Library size curves (`content/vfx/curves.json`): `ease-out grow` 0.94, `start big, partial shrink` 0.87, `bump (early peak)` 0.83, `start 60%, grow to full` 0.82 pass; `partial grow` 0.69, `grow from 38%` 0.53, `S-curve grow` 0.51, `bump (mid peak)` 0.46, `linear grow` 0.25 fail for a burst (add a key near t = 0.25, or pick another). Slow out (BL-04): the layer leaves more slowly than it arrived: its fade-out (Transparency toward 1, or a shrink for sparks and flares) takes longer than its fade-in. `constant size` and `linear shrink` start at full size: right for an impact that appears big. Fades: Transparency near 1 at t = 0 and t = 1 means it fades in and out (`fade in, hold, fade out`); `opaque hold` is for impact layers and sparks that shrink out, never for masses.
```
python -c "import json;d=json.load(open('content/vfx/curves.json'));f=lambda k,t:next((v0+(v1-v0)*(t-t0)/(t1-t0) if t1>t0 else v0) for (t0,v0,*a),(t1,v1,*b) in zip(k,k[1:]) if t0<=t<=t1);[print(c['name'],round(f(c['keypoints'],0.25)/max(v for _,v,*z in c['keypoints']),2)) for c in d['size']]"
```
For a layer with literal keys in `spec.json`, the same arithmetic on its keys. **Screening aid; known false readings:** it reads the size curves only (a transparency curve can have a maximum of 0, and dividing by it fails, so fades are read by hand from their keys); a value just under 0.7 is borderline: before a fix note, check the BL-04 width reading (§2) or the build stills, where the layer's real size shows.

## 6. Flashes and pulses (IG-07, IG-08)
`clip_metrics.py` cannot count these (§2: the 40-level threshold hides a full-screen grade pulse; a layer on screen the whole folder doubles or hides a pulse). This script is the studio's one flash counter, a screening aid (its known false readings and the eye check before a fix note are in the bullets after it): cinematics (`film-craft`) and marketing videos (`marketing-craft`) run it on their own frame folders, and no book keeps a copy. Save it once as `flash_pulse_vfx.py` in your scratch folder and run `python flash_pulse_vfx.py <folder> <fps> [x0,y0,x1,y1]`. Input: a folder of frames of one image type, `.jpg` or `.png`: a capture folder (`f###_<ms>ms.jpg`), the marketing frame command's `f####_<ms>ms.jpg`, Blender renders (`0001.png`). Frames go in the order of the numbers in their names; times come from the `_<ms>ms` in the names, else from the frame numbers in the names at `<fps>` (a Blender `frame_step` keeps true times). It stops with a message on a folder with fewer than 2 frames or two image types, a name that is not a frame name (for example the `<frame>.luminance.png` that `look_check.py value` writes), names of more than one pattern (per-shot renders copied together: renumber them into one sequence in edit order), names whose times do not rise, a 16-bit or float grey frame, or a box not given in fractions (tested: a `frame_step` 2 render of a 5 Hz flicker reads 5, before these name checks 10; two shots copied together and a folder holding `value` images stop with a message, before 8 for 4 and 5, and 3 for 5; counts unchanged on 12 Blender and `f####_<ms>ms.jpg` folders). It reads the frames themselves (no median background) in relative luminance and prints two flash counts, over 10 % of the frame (large: the cinematic and marketing count) and over 3 %, then the box rate:
```python
import sys, glob, os, re, numpy as n; from PIL import Image as I
STEP, AREAS, G = 0.1, (0.10, 0.03), 3  # PROPOSAL: net luminance rise; share of the frame (large, mid); grid for local flashes
d, fps = sys.argv[1], float(sys.argv[2]); box = [float(v) for v in sys.argv[3].split(',')] if len(sys.argv) > 3 else None
if box and not (len(box) == 4 and 0 <= box[0] < box[2] <= 1 and 0 <= box[1] < box[3] <= 1): sys.exit('box: x0,y0,x1,y1 as fractions of the frame, x0 < x1 and y0 < y1')
nm = lambda p: os.path.basename(p)
fs = sorted((p for p in glob.glob(d + '/*') if p.lower().endswith(('.jpg', '.jpeg', '.png'))), key=lambda p: [int(v) for v in re.findall(r'\d+', nm(p))])
ext = {os.path.splitext(p)[1].lower() for p in fs}
if len(fs) < 2 or len(ext) > 1: sys.exit(f'need 2 or more frames of one image type in {d}: found {len(fs)}, types {sorted(ext)}')
odd = [nm(p) for p in fs if not re.fullmatch(r'[A-Za-z]*_?\d+(_\d+ms)?\.(jpe?g|png)', nm(p), re.I)]
if odd: sys.exit(f'not frame names (number, or f###_<ms>ms): {odd[:3]}: move them out of {d}')
if len({re.sub(r'\d', '', nm(p)) for p in fs}) > 1: sys.exit('more than one name pattern (per-shot renders?): renumber into one sequence in edit order')
ms = [re.search(r'_(\d+)ms\.', nm(p)) for p in fs]; fn = [int(re.findall(r'\d+', nm(p))[0]) for p in fs]
t = [int(m.group(1)) for m in ms] if all(ms) else [round((k - fn[0]) * 1000 / fps) for k in fn]  # frame numbers: a frame step keeps true times
if any(b <= a for a, b in zip(t, t[1:])): sys.exit('frame times do not increase in frame-number order: check the names')
print(len(fs), 'frames,', nm(fs[0]), 'to', nm(fs[-1]), '| times from', 'the names (ms)' if all(ms) else f'{fps:g} fps')
def lum(p):
    im = I.open(p); im.mode[0] in 'IF' and sys.exit(f'{nm(p)} is a 16-bit or float grey image ({im.mode}): it reads as white; save 8-bit RGB')
    c = n.asarray(im.convert('RGB'), float) / 255
    return n.where(c <= 0.04045, c / 12.92, ((c + 0.055) / 1.055) ** 2.4) @ [0.2126, 0.7152, 0.0722]
R = {a: None for a in AREAS}; bm = []
for i, f in enumerate(fs):
    L = lum(f); h, w = L.shape
    if box: bm.append(L[int(box[1]*h):int(box[3]*h), int(box[0]*w):int(box[2]*w)].mean())
    for a in AREAS:
        if R[a] is None:  # region: [slice, share needed, lo, hi, armed, onsets]; the whole frame, then G x G blocks
            R[a] = [[n.s_[:, :], a]] + [[n.s_[h*y//G:h*(y+1)//G, w*x//G:w*(x+1)//G], min(1, a*G*G)] for y in range(G) for x in range(G)]
            for r in R[a]: r += [L[r[0]], L[r[0]], True, []]
            continue
        for r in R[a]:
            x = L[r[0]]; lo, hi = n.minimum(r[2], x), n.maximum(r[3], x)
            net = ((x - lo) >= STEP).mean() - ((hi - x) >= STEP).mean()  # rising share minus falling share
            if r[4] and net >= r[1]: r[5].append(i); lo, hi, r[4] = x, x, False  # a flash: wait for a fall
            elif -net >= r[1]: lo, hi, r[4] = x, x, True  # a net fall over the share, waiting or not: count rises from here (a smaller lasting dim still can: see the list)
            elif not r[4] and ((hi - x) >= STEP).mean() < r[1] / 2: lo = x  # waiting after a flash and almost nothing falls (under half the share): the trough follows the frame (a flicker after an eased rise counts)
            r[2], r[3] = lo, hi
most = lambda on: max((sum(t[s] <= t[o] < t[s] + 1000 for o in on) for s in on), default=0)
for a in AREAS:
    b = max(R[a], key=lambda r: most(r[5]))
    print(f'flashes over {a:.0%} of the frame: onsets', [nm(fs[i]) for i in b[5]], '| most in any 1 s:', most(b[5]))
if box:
    b = n.array(bm); lo, hi = b.min(), b.max(); m, k = (lo + hi) / 2, 0.1 * (hi - lo); st = b[0] > m; r = []
    for i, v in enumerate(b):
        if not st and v > m + k: r.append(i); st = True
        elif st and v < m - k: st = False
    hz = 'no pulse (swing under 0.005)' if hi - lo < 0.005 else round((len(r) - 1) * 1000 / (t[r[-1]] - t[r[0]]), 2) if len(r) > 1 else 'n/a (fewer than 2 rises)'
    print('box luminance', round(lo, 3), '-', round(hi, 3), '| rises', len(r), '| Hz', hz)
```

- **Flash (IG-07):** a flash is a net rise of 0.1 or more in relative luminance from each pixel's lowest value since the region was armed, over the share of the frame (or 9 × that share of one of nine blocks). After a flash the region waits; a net fall of 0.1 from each pixel's highest value since arms it again. While it waits and almost nothing falls (under half the region's share), the trough follows the frame, so the rest of an eased rise or a fade-up cannot hide a flicker that follows, and a camera move past dark shapes is not counted again and again. A net fall over the region's share also re-arms a region that is already armed, from that frame, so a lasting dim, smoke or a shadow over more than the count's share cannot hide later flashes (a smaller one can: the too-low list). PROPOSAL thresholds; record them. So an eased flash or a tweened grade pulse counts once at 24, 30 and 60 fps, a flicker after an eased rise counts at its rate, and a character walking across counts nothing. On the busiest-scene folder each count is at most 2 in any 1 s by design, never more than 3. Tested (coverage table d, repair rounds 3 and 4): three 1-frame flashes in 1 s → 3 and 3; three 2-frame ramps → 3; two eased full-screen pulses 500 ms apart → 2 at 30 and 60 fps; a 4 Hz square pulse → 4; an eased rise, then a 4 Hz flicker → 4 at 24, 30 and 60 fps; a 2 s fade-up, then a 5 Hz flicker → 5 at each rate; a lightning rise, then a 5 Hz flicker → 5 at each rate, also with dark rain over the whole frame; a lasting dim of a bright stage, then three 8 % flashes in 1 s → 0 and 3; a 5 % white flash 3 times a second → 0 at 10 %, 3 at 3 %; a bright figure of 3 % walking across → 0 and 0; pans over stripes and over a textured scene → 0 and 0; a cave-to-exterior pan → 1 and 1; camera pans and tracking moves past dark columns, tree trunks or a forest against a bright sky → 1 and 1 at 24, 30 and 60 fps (before repair round 4: up to 8 and 12). Then add every Bloom, ColorCorrection or other post-effect pulse in the spec, and every pulsing layer, by its rate and screen area (S). A pulsing zone over about 3 % of the screen with a strong swing counts as flashes at its rate (a 4 % zone at 2 Hz → 2 per second). A flash under about 3 % of the screen is below the flash area: BL-03 and VS-02 judge where and when its near-white sits.
- **Flash count: a screening aid; known false readings (tested, every review round).** Before any fix note on a count at or over the cap, open the frame of every listed onset and the frames between them, and confirm each onset is a flash on screen. Before trusting a count under the cap on a scene that holds a too-low case, run the workaround or count the flashes on the frames by eye; without one of them IG-07 (and R-10 in a cinematic) is "not verified". **Too low:** two flashes that overlap in time in one region count once (a large flash during a zone's bright phase: not counted at 3 %; a fire spreading inside the block of a 5 Hz flicker: 4 for 5); lights or areas that flash in turn inside one region (a siren, alarm lights left and right, two-tone warning lights) cancel each other: a 4 Hz pair of 12 % lights reads 1 at 10 % (4 at 3 %), a pair of 3 % lights in one block 0 and 1, at 24, 30 and 60 fps; a flash on the same frame as a larger darkening in its region (smoke arriving, a grade dimming) is cancelled (2 of 3 counted); a lasting darkening smaller than the count's share cancels the first flash after it when the flash covers less than the share plus the darkening, so a short burst reads one too few (a 7.6 % shadow, then four 15 % flashes in 1 s: 3 at 10 % at 24, 30 and 60 fps; a 6 % shadow, then three 15 % flashes: 2 at 10 %; 1.5 % smoke beside three 4.3 % flashes: 2 at 3 %); a camera pan past dark shapes or dark rain over about a tenth of the frame can leave the whole frame waiting after one early onset, so the 10 % count stops (four 15 % flashes in 1 s during a column pan or under dark rain: 1 at 10 %, 4 at 3 %, at 24, 30 and 60 fps); during a camera pan over strong light and dark shapes, a flash that lifts the dark parts by less than 0.1 reads 0 (four +35-level flashes a second over a 40/220-level stripe pan: 0 and 0); a flicker faster than half the capture rate aliases (its rate comes from the spec, as IG-08); a flash on the first frame is no onset, so a folder or an edit that opens on a flash reads one low (a vertical edit at 60 fps with quarter-screen flashes at 0, 250, 500 and 750 ms: 3 for 4): look at the first frame. **Workaround for the too-low cases:** time each light or flashing area with its own tight box (the box reading of this script; one box per run): its rate in Hz (or, for one short burst, its number of rises) is that area's flashes per second, and it counts as flashes at that rate when it covers more than the count's share of the frame (10 % for cinematics and marketing, 3 % for effects). Tested: each light of the alternating pairs 3.96-4.0 Hz at 24, 30 and 60 fps; four 15 % flashes under dark rain or during a column pan 4 rises, 3.91-4.0 Hz; four flashes in 1 s after a lasting shadow 4 rises at each rate. In a cinematic or an edit with rain or a pan past dark shapes, read both counts, never the 10 % count alone. **Too high:** a camera pan, a hard cut from dark to bright, or a large dark shape crossing a bright area reads 1 with no flash (a slow pan past columns at 60 fps: 2 at 3 %); dark rain alone reads 1 and 1, and dark streaks over 15 % of the frame on top of a flicker read one too many at some rates (a 4 Hz flicker: 5 at 10 % at 24 fps and 5 at 3 % at 60 fps; a 5 Hz flicker: 6 at 10 % at 24 and 30 fps, 6 at 3 % at 60 fps); a folder that starts in the middle of a rise may count one more in its first second (a 4 Hz swell read 5). The busiest-scene folder has a fixed camera (§1), so the pan and cut cases belong to cinematics and edits. This is a count, not a photosensitivity analyser: saturated red flashes and the exact area rule stay "not verified" unless an analyser measures them, and the spec holds no saturated red flash (S).
- **Pulse rate (IG-08):** read each pulse and blink rate from the spec first (sequence or tween period, server timer). Confirm it on the effect's own folder (the dark test place), or on a stretch of the busiest folder where nothing else changes inside the box, with a tight box around the pulsing layer: the script prints the box's luminance range, the rises and the rate from the first to the last rise (tested on a ground ring at 1.5 Hz with 0-160 and 0-60 level swings: 1.5 Hz both; at 4 Hz: 4.02). A screening aid: a rate over the cap, or within about 10 % of it, is checked on the frames of two cycles before a fix note. Known false readings: a box that also holds another moving thing mixes the rates (a 2 Hz zone read 2.0 Hz alone; one large flash through the box turned it into n/a or 2.29 Hz); a loose box hides a weak swing (the 0-60 ring in a whole-frame box read "no pulse"); rates above about 10 Hz alias at 30 fps and read slower, so flicker rates come from the spec only. Fewer than 2 rises gives n/a: record at least 2 cycles.
