# VFX design: what the effect must say, feel and use

## 1. Role and the two skills
This skill answers **what and why**: what the effect tells the player, how loud it may be, what is read first, which context and technique, and the target picture (a written **frame script**, never a 2D mock-up). **`vfx-craft` answers how**: layers, values, curves, textures, meshes, the spec file and the build passes.

**An effect is game information first.** It has three jobs, in this order: (1) the player reads the gameplay core at a glance: where it is, what it hits, whose it is, what just happened; (2) it feels right: it answers the input, peaks on the real event and sells the weight; (3) it looks like this game. Three things carry that read in any engine: **shape** says help or harm, **colour** says what it is (its role), **timing** says cause and effect. When those are right, the cheapest tool that shows them wins.

**Order for a new effect:** §2 steps 1-6 (read card, technique, research, options, frame script, envelope) → `vfx-craft` passes (blocking → check-in → value and silhouette gates → colour and polish → spec → in-game) → the self-review lines of `references/review.md` §I → the owner's final look.
**Who reads what:** the VFX agent reads both skills. Art director, sound designer and UI builders read this skill only (shared feel rules). UI effects are built with the `ui-basics` skill (`ui-basics/references/ui-effects.md`).
**After a context compaction**, re-read §2, §4, §6 and §9 of this file before you hand on a plan: only the start of a skill survives compaction.

## 2. Planning steps
1. **Read the brief and the feature sheets; fill the read card** (`references/read-card.md`). From the game-designer's feature spec take the feedback stack (named stages, attempt cue and outcome response, outcome tiers, frequency), the threat or hazard sheet (reaction window, danger area as tuning keys) and the scene's read rank; from the animator's timing list take the event ids and the mechanic verb. Write: what it is, what it does, how big; the three read lines (shape, colour role, timing); the primary, secondary and tertiary reads with the focus point; importance 1-10 per viewer (self, other, threatened) and the loudness tier with its caps; each truth value with its data field (never set or invent one; nothing attached → "visual only", no radius ring); the information areas it must not cover; rate and burst; the per-viewer and reduced variants; the budget line; the stop criteria and pass budget. A missing gameplay number is UNKNOWN with its owner. *Check: `review.md` §I1 PL-01 to PL-07.*
2. **Context and technique** (the chooser, §3): the context, then the cheapest tool per part. Name the crafts that join (animator: markers, pops, rigs, the hit-stop freeze frame; sound designer always; UI side for world-to-HUD hand-overs) and plan them on one event timeline with the same event ids and stage names.
3. **Research before options.** (a) The real thing: observe the phenomenon (frames, footage descriptions, how fire, water, lightning or an impact really behaves) and write the one or two properties you take: a shape, a timing phase. When the job's target-experience sentence points to a field outside games that already does what it asks (stage lighting, film editing, animal warning displays), take one method from it the same way, written as an action. A method gives the idea only: tier caps, flash rules and the truth edge win. (b) 1-3 pro frame scripts of the same type (`knowledge/reference/vfx/training/frame-scripts/`, S and A first): peak coverage, peak time, tail, layer list. (c) Reuse first: the game's shipped effects and the shared texture set; what can be recoloured and retimed instead of made new. (d) The caster's main colours, the game's shape code and colour roles (art bible). Take ideas, never copy an effect. *Check: PL-08.*
4. **Options (the thumbnail stage).** Write 2-3 short frame-script options at equal finish (build, peak, result only), each with its hook from a different source (another shape idea, another reading of the verb), one deliberately atypical. The first idea is a warm-up, not the answer. An option outside the game's tone and theme words is dropped, however cool. Pick with a goal question ("which tells the threatened player where it hurts, and when, at phone size?") and record why each other option lost; parts of two options may merge when the record says which. Image budget: options are text; key-frame images come through `reference-images` (`vfx` family: build, peak, result panels), one sheet for each of at most two options the direction stage shows. No build starts before an approved image exists. *Check: PL-09.*
5. **Our frame script** at the game camera (format and screen-size math: `vfx-craft/references/plan-and-spec.md` §A-B): 6-10 moments in ms with screen %, shapes, colours, brightness and motion, on the timeline's stage names. Tell or build → a short beat → one peak frame on the contact event → the result after its cause → a tail sized to the tier. Compare peak, timing and tail with the references and fix the plan, not the reference. Add the per-viewer and reduced variants. *Check: PL-10 to PL-13.*
6. **Markers and envelope before building.** Take the animator's draft timing list (blocking markers) and co-design each event's envelope (wind-up, peak, tail) with the sound designer on the event ids, in exchange notes (at most 2 rounds); merge it into the event timeline. Then switch to `vfx-craft`.
7. **Judge your own work pass by pass** (`references/review.md` §I) and hand back with the image counts, the could-not-check list and one honest weakness.

**Short route for T1-T2 effects and fixes (a retime, a recolour):** options as text only (no key-frame sheet unless the brief asks), one bracket round, one capture round; the read card, truth, the gates and the spec lines still run.

## 3. Technique chooser
**Context sets budget and freedom:**
| Context | Examples | Allows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gameplay moment | weapon hit, pickup, object break, sell | short, repeats, phone budget, many at once; merge windows and caps |
| World event | tsunami over the city, meteor shower, stage boss | rare, big, seconds long, everyone sees it; bigger budget, level of detail by distance, shake allowed |
| Object change | wall completes and transforms, building upgrade, freeze, charge | the object itself changes; a flash hides the swap; the animator helps with the pop |
| Cutscene | opening film, stage intro | camera is ours, plays once; heavy assets allowed (even mesh flipbooks, loaded and unloaded); frame per shot; shots, cue ids and times come from the film director's shot list (`film-craft`); effects go onto a frame that already passes composition and value, and the subject stays first |
| UI | button, HUD pop, counter, reward screen | screen layer: build with `ui-basics`; feel rules from here |

**The shape the eye must read picks the tool (one effect can use several):**
| The player must see | Tool (`vfx-craft/references/techniques.md`) |
| --- | --- |
| many small, soft or chaotic bits (fire, smoke, sparks, magic dust) | particles (T1) |
| one big **solid** shape that keeps its form while it moves or grows (wave, dome, tornado, pillar) | mesh + particles on top (T2, T5) |
| a line between two points, a ribbon behind something (laser, arc, sword trail) | beam or trail (T3) |
| an existing object changing (completes, upgrades, charges) | change the object + flash to hide the swap (T4) |
| the ground or a wall looking damaged (crater, crumble, rubble, flying chunks) | fake destruction with the effect's own parts (T8) |
| a feeling over the whole screen (rare heavy moment) | camera and post effects (T7, §4) |
Know the toolbox before writing code, and keep meshes for the few: small rings, slashes and streaks are particles; many spawned meshes cost real parts (`vfx-craft/references/techniques.md` intro, T1).

## 4. Feel and safety rules
- **VFX is visuals only.** An effect never adds or changes gameplay: no damage, no knockback, no hit areas, no new gameplay numbers, and it never touches real game parts (map parts, players, gameplay objects). **Fake destruction is part of VFX and encouraged:** the effect may spawn its own cosmetic parts (rock rings, crater rubble, floor crumble, flying chunks, cracks) that look like damage, then clean them up (`vfx-craft` T8). You never need to be told this per task.
- **Truth first, only when gameplay exists.** A drawn danger shape (ring, flash edge, dust stop line) sits on the gameplay area from warning to the last damage tick, in every quality tier, skin and viewer variant (edge ±5 % PROPOSAL, never inside the hit area); the values come from the data, never set by eye; the danger signal ends on the last tick; countdowns follow the server timer, never a random fuse; scorch and crack marks never overstate the affected area; an offset indicator is an owner decision. No gameplay attached (tests, showcases, cosmetics): no truth area, no radius ring for a value you took or invented; size by the frame script. Gameplay accuracy > readability > theme.
- **The gameplay core is the primary read.** Primary (where it is, what it hits) is the brightest, highest-contrast layer at the named focus point; the secondary carries the theme, clearly weaker; the tertiary is a few small bits, never brighter or bigger than the secondary. Effects never cover other players, targets, the HUD, the shop or placement areas, and they stay low: no tall columns over characters (`references/scene-and-clutter.md` SC-33, SC-69).
- **Importance drives everything.** Rate each effect 1-10 per viewer and give it a loudness tier with caps on screen area, peak brightness, duration and camera channels (`references/read-card.md` §2); size, brightness, saturation, sharpness and lifetime rise with the rating. The brightest pixel belongs to the top priority: threat to me > my own feedback > pickups and objectives > other players' effects > ambient. If everything is loud, nothing is.
- **Per viewer.** Other players' non-threat effects are toned down on my screen and never shake, flash or tint it; threats and pickups stay full for whoever must act; team colour follows my relation to the source and always pairs with a shape or outline.
- **Value.** Effects and UI own the value extremes, but nothing blows out: clipped white only at the single focus on the peak frames (a 1-2 frame flash), a large pure-white core only at the top tier (`references/read-card.md` §2); additive layers stacked at the core sum toward white, so count them. A weak read is fixed with contrast (calm the surroundings first, then raise the focus), never with more layers or more brightness.
- **Shape code.** One game-wide code: heal = plus or rising round shapes, shield = circles, damage = spikes and angles; sharp and downward = threat, round = safe; outward = burst, aimed = streak, inward = gathering. One focus, one primary shape; the effect type reads in grayscale and black-fill.
- **Colour.** The colour comes from the role table, never from taste: one main hue with its lighter and darker versions, at most one accent with a role; next to the caster's hue on the wheel; reserved gameplay colours never decorate; one hue never means both help and harm; a reused layer is recoloured to the target mood.
- **Timing tells cause and effect.** Tell → a build that rises (smaller to bigger, dim to bright, sparse to dense) → a short beat → one peak on the real event (the brightest layer for 1-3 frames) → the result after its cause (holds tuned in 0.1-0.2 s steps) → the eye moves to the result while the old spot calms. Bursts reach most of their size fast and ease out slowly; gaps between beats differ; exits are staggered. Telegraphs last the game-designer's reaction window (PROPOSAL 600-1000 ms) and end on the damage frame; a timed or breakable state (a shield, a stun, a charge) gets a tell that builds before it ends, on the animator's end-of-state event. Frequent effects are shorter and quieter.
- **Weight is not intensity.** Fix a flat effect with outcome link, truth, readability and timing before making it bigger (`references/juice-repetition-sync.md`).
- **Feedback follows the real outcome.** The attempt cue plays on the input frame, small, never covering the impact point or the next contact; the big layer plays only on the confirmed outcome, graded by a cause the player can learn; a request the server rejects never shows success; slow confirmation shows a pending state, never an early success.
- **Camera shake is occasional:** rotation only, local, smoothed noise; none on frequent effects or from other players; only rare heavy moments of the local player (rating ≥8 or the owner asks); off under reduced motion (`references/camera-and-screen.md`). **Hit-stop** is local (attacker, target and the hit's own particles), short, rising with hit strength and well under half a second; it freezes on the animator's contact pose from the timing list; never on explosions, charges, projectile throwers, rapid fire or crowds.
- **Flash safety** on the composite screen: design ≤2 large flashes per rolling second, never >3; no saturated red flashes; Bloom and colour pulses count. Pulses carry meaning: slow = safe, fast = urgent, never above 3 Hz at full contrast. A flicker layer stays low and behind the focus; the focus never strobes.
- **Colour is never the only channel.** Reduced-motion and reduced-flash variants keep all information: footprint, contact, outcome, threats and pickups. A warning label is not a fix.
- **Repetition:** frequent effects get a merge window, a live-copy cap and variants; randomise decoration only, lock what carries meaning (contact frame, footprint, team and danger colour, the start frame of a telegraph or flash).
- **Paid random items:** one identical windup for every result up to the result frame, no near-miss, no rarity hint before the result, no celebration of a result worth less than its cost, luck effects never suggest better odds than the numbers, no effect covers or animates the odds. The disclosure rules belong to `platform-policy` (load it); they win over this line.
- **Sound:** one trigger schedules the picture punch and the sound transient; the sound onset lands 0 to about 40 ms after the first lit peak frame, never more than one frame (16.7 ms at 60 fps) early; measured on the target device, else "not verified". The sound brief says what is on screen and when; the sound designer chooses the sounds.
- **Claims:** never say you saw, played or measured anything without a capture, log or tool output in this task; never invent asset ids, limits or results. Label checks gate-verified / MCP-verified / owner-confirmed / not verified.

## 8. Game specifics
- **Game and style:** read `knowledge/design/game-profile.md` (pillars, art style, camera). Where it is silent, house style is a PROPOSAL: ask the owner before building.
- **Where to build and test:** a separate, dark, plain test place, never the game place (the game profile names it). Judge in the dark first, then check in the game's own lighting.
- **Decisions:** VFX decisions for this game live in `knowledge/design/decisions.md`; the owner's final look closes every effect.
- **Files per effect:** `content/vfx/<effect-id>/` (brief.md with the read card, frame-script.md, spec.json).

## 9. Core principles for this craft
The studio's universal principles, stated for effects: the standard, a short example, a check you can run, and where the depth sits. Line ids (PL, BL, VS, CP, IG, SP) are the self-review lines of `references/review.md` §I and `vfx-craft` §5.

| # | Principle, for my work | Example | Check | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The gameplay read comes first:** the gameplay core (where, what it hits, whose) is the primary read; beauty never costs it; effects never cover players, targets or the HUD. | A boss slam's danger ring reads before its dust; the dust stops short of the ring edge. | Busiest-moment capture at phone size: threat, own action and pickup named (IG-01); the primary holds the top luminance (VS-01). | §4, read-card §1 |
| 2 | **Judge it where players meet it:** the game camera, phone size, the real place and lighting, moving, at the worst-case count, with HUD and other players. | A muzzle flash fine in the dark test place hides the target on the bright stage. | Approval rests on frame-folder numbers and captures at the game camera, dark place then game lighting, worst case (IG-02, IG-13); never an editor close-up. | `vfx-craft` §3 |
| 3 | **Spend contrast only where it is read first:** rank effects per viewer with tier caps; inside one effect primary > secondary > tertiary; nothing blows out: clipped white only at the single focus on the peak frames (1-2 frames), a large white core only at the top tier. | A basic-attack spark stays smaller and dimmer than the enemy telegraph next to it; only the boss finisher gets a white core. | Tier caps against the clip numbers (BL-09); rank order in the spec (VS-03); in the frame folder's `metrics.json`, `bright_pct` above 0 only on the 1-2 flash or white-core rows the tier allows, none in T1 (BL-03), and near-white only at the focus in the zoomed still (VS-02). Screening aid; known false readings: a saturated light core (yellow, luma ≥ 230) counts as near-white, and a white core less than 40 levels from a bright sky or floor counts nothing, so check those rows on the stills (`vfx-craft/references/measure.md` §2). | §4 Value, read-card §2 |
| 4 | **Big before small:** shape, colour role and timing first, as a blocking pass with placeholder sprites; sparks, textures and polish last. | A fireball blocked as a grey dot and a plain trail on the real timing before any flame flipbook. | The blocking capture and its check-in are dated before the first texture pick (BL-13). | `vfx-craft` §3 steps 2-3 |
| 5 | **Shape carries meaning:** one game-wide shape code, one primary shape, read filled solid and small. | Heal rises as soft plus shapes; damage bursts as spikes. | Black-fill of the peak still at phone size names the effect type and the direction (VS-05). | §4 shape code |
| 6 | **Less is more:** the fewest layers, hues and particles that give the read; a few tertiary bits; ambient tiny and slow. | A pickup with one pop, one ring and three specs instead of nine emitters. | Remove a layer in a bracket: the read numbers hold → it stays removed (BL-10); hue families counted (CP-01). | `vfx-craft` C-01 |
| 7 | **One code for meaning:** colour roles, the shape code, pulse classes and tells mean one thing everywhere. | Poison is purple because green is heal. | Palette against the role swatches (CP-02); every pulse class measured (IG-08); no hue with two meanings in the effect list. | §4 colour |
| 8 | **Timing tells cause and effect:** the build rises, a beat, one peak on the contact marker, the result after its cause, the eye moves on; fast in, slow out; one event timeline. | Clouds swirl in and brighten, a two-frame flash on `contact_candidate`, debris after 0.1 s, the old spot darkens. | Clip rows: build rising (BL-02), one peak on the marker (BL-03), fast in (BL-04), result after cause (BL-05), eye moved (BL-06); sync on a device (IG-12). | §4 timing, elements §F |
| 9 | **Push what must read past real, on a believable base:** brighter, bigger and faster than physics where it must read; believable order, arcs and gravity everywhere else. | A blast core peaks in 2 frames at twice the real size; its chunks fly on arcs and brake hard. | Fast-in numbers (BL-04); debris and chunks fly on arcs, accelerate and brake in the spec and the stills (BL-12). | `vfx-craft` C-09, C-21 |
| 10 | **The subject's character drives the choices:** the verb and the caster set shape, colour and timing. | A "slow" frost bolt spreads and decelerates; a stun snaps in. | The read card names the verb; a fresh read of the black-fill names it (VS-05). | read-card §1 |
| 11 | **One world, one style; identity survives every variant:** art-bible shape vocabulary and colour roles; a skin keeps footprint, timing, tell and shape code and re-themes the ability, not only its colour. | A candy skin of the slam keeps the ring and the 0.9 s contact; candy chunks replace the rocks. | Footprint from the same data field, area and box from the same camera within tolerance; shape by `silhouette --compare`; same peak frame (PL-13). | read-card §1 |
| 12 | **Controlled variation, never exact repetition:** randomise decoration only, lock meaning; masses blend without popcorning; repeated objects vary. | Pebbles vary in rotation and size; the telegraph never starts on a random frame. | Spec lines CP-07, CP-09; range extremes compared side by side. | `vfx-craft` C-13, C-24 |
| 13 | **Problem, constraints and target before any layer:** the read card first. | A dash puff: rate 3 per second, tier T2, footprint one character width, no camera channel, written before the first emitter. | PL-01 to PL-07 dated before the first spec. | §2 step 1 |
| 14 | **Research, options, a reasoned pick:** an observed property, 2-3 options with different hooks, one atypical. | "Cracking ring", "rising spikes" and an atypical "inward suck"; the ring wins, the suck becomes the build. | PL-08, PL-09 in the job record. | §2 steps 3-4 |
| 15 | **Cheap stages first; gates freeze; not precious:** options, blocking, check-in; after it footprint and timing move only by a recorded decision; a layer that keeps failing is deleted and rebuilt. | A smoke layer that keeps popping is rebuilt as two overlapping layers, not patched a fifth time. | BL-13 and the decisions in the job record. | `vfx-craft` §3 |

| 17 | **Build for the next craft and the engine:** the spec is data under one root, on the event ids, within the measured budget, with reuse named. | The slam reads its radius from config and scales from one root factor. | Spec lines SP-01 to SP-13 (`vfx-craft` §5). | `vfx-craft` §5 |
| 18 | **Know when it is done:** stop criteria and a pass budget in the read card; stop when the read, timing and budget pass at the game camera. | After the second capture round the notes repeat: stop and log the rest. | The hand-back states the budget used and each stop criterion with evidence. | read-card §1 |
| 19 | **Feedback follows the real outcome:** attempt cue at once; the big layer on the confirmed outcome; frequent effects merged and capped; paid reveals honest. | A swing trail on input; the impact burst on `contact_confirmed`. | Rejected-request capture (IG-05); 100-trigger repeat (IG-09); identical reveal windups (IG-14). | §4 feedback |
