# Level review artifact

## 1. Route graph
An annotated route graph, not only a beauty image. Show sightlines and scale from the real gameplay camera; mark where players may lose orientation and how they recover. Where routes cross, they must differ in something meaningful: timing, information, exposure, resource use, skill demand, exploration or expression. Draw it as a top-down diagram (§6.7) and keep its data as route JSON (§6.2) next to `route-graph.md`.

Per node, 10 fields:
| # | Field | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Entry view | what the camera shows on arrival, and the read rank of what must be seen there (1-3) |
| 2 | Visible and hidden goals | what the player can see to aim for; what is there to find |
| 3 | Action choices | what the player can do here, and the tool or ability each needs |
| 4 | Information | what the player learns or must know: the critical facts (where to go, where am I, ally or enemy, threat, objective) and the cue that carries each |
| 5 | Commitment | what is hard to undo once chosen |
| 6 | Risk | what can go wrong |
| 7 | Reward | what the player gains |
| 8 | Recovery | how the player gets back after failure, in seconds |
| 9 | Exit | where the player can go next, and how they know |
| 10 | Optional content | side content and how it is signalled |

Per node, its geometry (the blockout brief and the measures read these): Studio position of the floor (x, y, z) · stop or pass (players stop here, or run past: detail and story go to stop points) · sight lines that must stay clear of motion, bright effects and reserved colours (from → to) · key positions (where players stand to fight, wait or decide) · floor types and the smallest gameplay object on them · events that must be heard from here, with their source.

Per edge, the route table row: from → to · distance and seconds at walk speed (§6.2) · the limit (from the brief) · what the player sees or does on the way · timed seconds from the walk test once it exists. When travel is part of an earning cycle (sell trips, restocks, respawn to goal), the route table also goes to the progression designer as an input for income per minute.

## 2. Walk-throughs and traces
Walk the route as 5 players: uninformed · returning · expert · grouped · one who missed an optional clue. Trace 4 events: a wrong turn · a death · a disconnect · an empty resource. Checkpoints keep what the player learned, not only transit. Test boundaries with the real movement tools; a visible obstacle explains its rule and stays consistent when abilities change.
A walk on paper is labelled "analytic walkthrough"; a walk the studio-operator played in Studio is labelled MCP-verified with its timed seconds and its captures (approach, decision and after-commit view at each key node; filed as evidence and looked at only where the timed walk runs over, a fall or stuck spot happens, or a check names that node). Only the played walk can pass a time or a reach.

## 3. Encounters
- State the behaviour each enemy or hazard asks of the player, and its movement pattern (a hop, a dash-and-stop, a shamble); two enemies that differ only in health need a reason to both exist.
- Each encounter names the counter: the tool, ability or position that beats it; a counter nobody has yet is taught first (teaching sheet).
- Check 6 combinations: overlapping attacks · escape routes · camera occlusion · running out of safe space · resource recovery · simultaneous demands. An enemy fair alone can be unfair in a group; a ring of small threats around the player reads as danger by its shape and count.
- Telegraphs work at the moment of decision, at real speed, on a phone; the same attack gives the same tell (two uses compared on the partner's captures); the drawn danger area is the real hit area from warning to the last tick (the server's hit shape as a debug overlay: `game-design/references/review.md` §7).
- The worst-case crowd (every player's effects at once in the busiest node) keeps the threat, the route and the objective readable.
- Hazards have written rules before art (`spaces.md` §5 and the hazard sheet), a visible source and containment.

## 5. Output
Ranked findings table (check id, what is wrong, where: node, coordinate or zone, why it matters to the player, fix proposal, cost small / medium / large, the input that showed it); a marked-up top-down image when an image of the layout exists (route arrows, numbered problem circles matching the table: §6.7); the rating table with one column per round (C1-C19, Pass / Fail / Unproven / n/a and the capture or number behind each); "keep: what works"; "could not check" (step, missing input, the capture or test that would close it); an honest recap (what works, what is too tame, too big or empty, the next iteration). Proposals labelled PROPOSAL; a live map's open findings go to the revisit backlog with their evidence.

### 6.2 Route table and ledges (C3, C8, C10, C14)
Route JSON (`knowledge/jobs/<job>/route.json`): `walk_speed`, `jump` (measured, §6.6), `margin` (starting `PROPOSAL` 1), `nodes` {id: [x, y, z] floor position in studs}, `edges` [{from, to, via: [[x, y, z], ...] corner points of the real path, limit_s, sees}], `ledges` [{name, height, meant: climb or block}]. Save as `ld_route_table.py` in the job folder and run `python ld_route_table.py route.json`:
```python
import json, math, sys
g = json.load(open(sys.argv[1])); v = g['walk_speed']; N = g['nodes']; j, m = g.get('jump'), g.get('margin', 1)
for e in g['edges']:
    p = [N[e['from']]] + e.get('via', []) + [N[e['to']]]
    d = sum(math.dist((a[0], a[2]), (b[0], b[2])) for a, b in zip(p, p[1:])); s = d / v
    print(f"{e['from']}->{e['to']}\t{d:.0f} studs\t{s:.1f} s\t{'OVER ' + str(e['limit_s']) if s > e.get('limit_s', 1e9) else 'ok'}\t{e.get('sees', '-')}")
for l in g.get('ledges', []):
    bad = l['height'] > j - m if l['meant'] == 'climb' else l['height'] < j + m
    print(f"ledge {l['name']}\t{l['height']} vs jump {j}\t{'CHECK' if bad else 'ok'} ({l['meant']})")
```
- Silent behaviour: distance is horizontal (x, z; Roblox y is up) along straight segments between the points you give; an edge with no `limit_s` never reports OVER; ledge heights are your input (from §6.5).
- Gives a wrong number with no error when: the path bends but the edge has no `via` points (straight line: too short); the route climbs stairs or ramps, or the player must jump or wait (time not counted); the game changes walk speed (sprint, mounts, boosts) and `walk_speed` is the default; a node position is a part's centre, not the floor the player stands on.
- The played walk is the truth: a timed walk more than about 20 % over the computed time means a detour, a stall or a slope the graph does not show; find it before judging the limit.

### 6.3 First-view focus and phone size (C2, C7, C19)
Two readings per view, on boxes drawn on each subject's outline (environment `measure-captures.md` M1 box rules; M5 for small objects):
1. **Rank.** The shared run (M1 form): `python tools/art/look_check.py value <cap> --box <goal> --name goal --box <c1> --name c1 --box <c2> --name c2 --compare-subjects --out-dir <dir> --out <dir>/<view>-m1.json`. Read `compare_subjects.rank_by_median` only; its `ratio_to_ring` is one ring around all boxes together and is not read (false readings below).
2. **Own contrast.** One run per subject (the goal and each of its 2-3 competitors), the other boxes and the HUD taken out of its ring: `python tools/art/look_check.py value <cap> --box <subject> --exclude-box <other box> ... --out-dir <dir> --out <dir>/<view>-own-<name>.json`; then `python -c "import sys,json,glob;[print(f,round(m['subject_ring_ratio'],2),'lighter' if m['subject']['p50']>m['ring']['p50'] else 'darker') for f in sorted(glob.glob(sys.argv[1]+'-own-*.json')) for m in [json.load(open(f))['measurements']]]" <dir>/<view>` (it globs itself, so it runs the same in PowerShell, cmd and Bash) prints each subject's own ratio against its 8 px ring and its direction.
- **The screen passes** when the goal's own ratio reaches the marker target (`art-review/references/art-bible.md` §7 separation targets; starting `PROPOSAL` 3:1, a check may change it), the goal is first among the goal and its competitors by median or by own ratio, and the grey image (`<cap>.luminance.png`) agrees. The p5-p95 spread is support only.
- **Phone size** (M10): `python tools/art/look_check.py thumb <cap> --size <phone WxH> --out-dir <dir> --out <dir>/<view>-thumb.json` writes the thumbnail and its 4× sheet (the sheet is what I look at); both runs again on the thumbnail with every box scaled by `scale` plus the `content_box_px` offsets (a phone of another aspect is letterboxed).
- Silent behaviour: with no `--box` an in-scene capture gives `invalid-mask` (`value`, `silhouette`, `thumb`; `thumb` still writes the thumbnail and its sheet): boxes are the method. The ratio is symmetric and never below 1; 1.0 means no value separation.
- Gives a wrong number with no error when: the shared ring is read: when competitors stand against the sky its median is the sky, so the darkest box gets the highest `ratio_to_ring` whatever its own surroundings (a goal invisible on its wall: own 1.09, shared 3.62); a goal the same value as its wall or sky still ranks first by median when its competitors are darker: the marker target on the own ratio catches both; a dark goal against a light sky ranks last by median: it passes on its own ratio; the goal's box takes in sky, so its median and spread read the sky: box below the horizon or tight to its body; a box drawn inside the object puts the ring on the object itself (ratio 1.0): box on the outline; a hollow or thin subject (arch, ring, gate frame): the box median reads what shows through the gap: box its solid part or judge it on the still; the HUD or a character sits inside a box (exclude it, or box around it). The ring is a median: a neighbour covering a small part of it does not move it, but a goal surrounded on most sides by one surface reads against that surface. The own ratio measures value only: a goal that stands out by hue alone, or that is a few pixels big, is judged on the still (LD17's contrast list).
- **With coordinates only** (no capture yet; analytic, C2 stays Unproven until the capture): take the bearing from the spawn facing to the goal and to each competitor and compare it with half the horizontal field of view (`Camera.FieldOfView` is the vertical angle, default 70: about 51° at 16:9, about 57° on a wide phone such as 844x390; an inference from 2·atan(tan 35° · aspect)). A subject outside it is not in the first frame; one near the edge is borderline, since the default camera sits behind the character.

### 6.4 Landmark silhouettes (C4)
Flat-key captures (§6.1: shadows off, each landmark alone, a key colour far from every landmark). Per pair: `python tools/art/look_check.py silhouette <landmark-A.png> --compare <landmark-B.png> --out <dir>/sil-A-B.json`; read `measurements.compare.iou`. IoU at or above 0.8 (starting `PROPOSAL`) means the two read as one silhouette: one of them changes its outline, not only its colour. J018 run: the same tower recoloured 0.94; tower against dome 0.23. Then one look, which covers passing pairs too: run `python tools/art/look_check.py silhouette <landmark.png> --out <dir>/sil-<landmark>.json` once per landmark (it writes the black fill `<landmark>.silhouette.png` next to the input; a `--compare` run writes only A's), put them on one sheet with `python -c "import sys;from PIL import Image;ims=[Image.open(p).convert('RGB') for p in sys.argv[2:]];[i.thumbnail((256,256)) for i in ims];s=Image.new('RGB',(256*len(ims),256),'white');[s.paste(i,(256*k,0)) for k,i in enumerate(ims)];s.save(sys.argv[1])" <dir>/landmarks-sheet.png <A>.silhouette.png <B>.silhouette.png ...`, and check that each outline is the landmark alone.
- Silent behaviour: the comparison scales both outlines to one height, so it judges shape, not size (size comes from §6.5). Loud: an in-scene capture, or a floor key shaded apart from the backdrop key by more than the key tolerance (J018 run), gives `invalid-mask`: match the floor and backdrop shades and recapture.
- Gives a wrong number with no error when: a cast shadow falls on the key and joins the outline, which lowers the IoU, so two alike landmarks pass as different (even a small shadow read 0.78): shadows off, and the sheet; part of a landmark is close to the key colour and is keyed out (a pink roof on a magenta key vanished): pick a key colour far from every landmark; the landmark touches the frame edge.

### 6.5 Part tops, heights and ledges (C4, C10, C11)
Use environment `measure-captures.md` M8 (the scene probe loaded with `scene_audit.load_probe`; prints each part's world top and largest size). A ledge's height = its top minus the top of the floor at its foot; a landmark is the tallest when its top is above every neighbour in its zone; touch points (doors, stairs, rails, counters) compare with the character's height.
- Gives a wrong number with no error when: the ledge is a wedge, mesh or union (the bounding-box top is not the walkable lip); the ledge or floor is terrain (not in the probe: a played jump test decides); parts are named differently in greybox and art (match by position).

### 6.6 The jump (L-07)
**Probe first** in the game's place, once per game, written to the game profile: in play mode the studio-operator walks the default character at a test ladder of blocks 5.0, 5.5, 6.0, 6.5, 7.0 and 7.5 studs high and records the highest it lands on. Until then the starting value is 6.4 studs (JumpPower 50 at Workspace.Gravity 196.2: 50² ÷ (2 × 196.2) ≈ 6.4, an inference); a game that sets `CharacterUseJumpPower` false uses its JumpHeight; Roblox's greybox tutorial says 5 studs. Read the game's own values (`StarterPlayer.CharacterUseJumpPower`, `CharacterJumpPower` or `CharacterJumpHeight`, `Workspace.Gravity`) with `inspect_instance` before the test.

### 6.7 Marked-up top-down (C3, §5)
Marks JSON: `{"arrows": [[[x, y], ...], ...], "circles": [[x, y], ...]}` in image pixels (circle n = finding n). `python -c "import sys,json;from PIL import Image,ImageDraw;im=Image.open(sys.argv[1]).convert('RGB');d=ImageDraw.Draw(im);M=json.load(open(sys.argv[2]));[d.line([tuple(p) for p in a],fill=(0,200,255),width=6) for a in M['arrows']];[(d.ellipse([x-18,y-18,x+18,y+18],outline=(255,40,40),width=5),d.text((x-4,y-7),str(i+1),fill=(255,255,255))) for i,(x,y) in enumerate(M['circles'])];im.save(sys.argv[3])" <top-down.png> <marks.json> <marked.png>`. The default font is small on large captures: the findings table carries the numbers too.

### 6.8 What these numbers cannot tell you
Whether a route feels long or a fight feels fair at normal speed, whether players notice a cue, how it sounds, and anything outside the boxes and coordinates you gave. Feel at speed comes from the studio-operator's play captures and the owner's play-test of a playable build at the gate; sound from the sound designer.

## 7. Self-review checklist (run before every hand-back)
Each line names its input. A line you cannot run is listed `not verified` with the input that would close it. A review-only job runs LD15-LD18; a plan runs LD1-LD8 and LD18; a blockout review runs all.
**Plan**
- LD1 Job card complete; every field has a source or `PROPOSAL`; movement metrics measured or marked "probe first". Input: the file.
- LD2 Layout options: three distinct hooks (route shape, landmark or first view), one atypical, the warm-up labelled, the pick with its reason and a line per rejected option; diagrams only. Input: the option sheet.
- LD3 Route graph: every node has its 10 fields (or "unknown" with who closes it) and its geometry; every edge has a time. Input: the file + §6.2 output.
- LD4 Route table: no edge over its limit without something to see or do; return trips short or worth taking. Input: §6.2 output.
- LD5 Teaching sheet: four beats in order; the first exposure cheap to fail; each beat states the inference and the action that shows it. Input: the sheet.
- LD6 Pacing map: a rest after each peak; gaps between peaks not all equal; the climax in the last third; reveals behind a turn or rise; rewards and new views on achievement nodes; each planned surprise or ambush sits outside the bright focus of its view or behind a turn (environment `review.md` RD12 measures it). Input: the map.
- LD7 Blockout brief: per node the position, stop or pass (detail and story go to stop points), critical facts and read rank, cue, sight lines, key positions, floor types and the smallest object, events that must be heard from it; the environment designer's intake lists no open question. Input: the brief + their hand-back.
- LD8 Encounters: each enemy's asked behaviour, movement pattern and counter; the same attack gives the same tell (two uses compared on the partner's captures); the 6 combinations; a group of threats reads by shape and count; drawn area = hit area; the worst-case crowd named; each hazard has its rules sheet, a visible source and containment.
