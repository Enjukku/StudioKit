# Level design: the place where the rules meet the player

## 1. When this skill starts
A level, map, hub, route or encounter is planned, blocked out, built or under review. It answers: what is this place's job, how does a new player find the way without words, what does it teach, how does tension rise and rest, what happens on failure, and does it hold up for groups, on phones, in the real camera, in the busiest moment.
**The rule above all others:** the level is the board the game is played on. Routes, objectives, hazards, threats and pickups read before any decoration, at the game camera, on a phone, with players and effects on. A beautiful layout that weakens that read has failed.
**Who owns what:** the game designer owns routes, cover, sight lines, blocking surfaces and the route graph (the rules: `game-design/references/spaces.md`); the environment designer builds the greybox and the place from my brief and measures the place's reads (`environment-design/references/review.md` §7); reviewers and QA run `references/review-artifact.md` §4. Design is the owner's: my output is plans, reviews and proposals, never silent changes; this skill never edits stage files.
**After a compaction:** re-read §3, §5, §6 and §7 of this file and `references/review-artifact.md` §7 before you continue.

## 3. Steps
Image budget (owner light-check rule): numbers for every case. **Looked at:** the first view, each fork view per approach direction, each zone's entry view, the busiest moment, the top-down, one view per hazard, the approach views of teaching beats 1 and 3, at most 6 normal-round stills, one sheet of the landmark black-fills. **Measured only** (looked at only when its number fails or is borderline, or the walk test flags the spot): flat-key captures, scale shots, lowest-setting views, walk and route captures. Layout options are diagrams, not images. Counts per check: `references/review-artifact.md` §6.1.
1. **Level job card** (write before planning or judging; `plan-and-blockout.md` §1): target experience (one type of fun) · the four parts here (goal, opposition, decisions, rules) and how progress shows · conflict sentence · what the level teaches · session shape (first visit, return visit, short phone session) · camera, device, group size · movement metrics measured in Studio (walk speed, jump) · the place's personality in 3-5 words and the 3-5 things players expect in this theme · the smallest gameplay object · stop criteria and pass budget. Missing field that changes the verdict → state an assumption.
2. **Layout options** (`plan-and-blockout.md` §2): three top-down route sketches with distinct hooks (a different route shape, landmark or first view, never a resize), one atypical; the first is labelled warm-up. Pick one with the reason tied to the job card and one line per rejected option.
3. **Route graph** on the pick (`review-artifact.md` §1): nodes and edges with the 10 node fields, node geometry (Studio position, stop or pass, critical facts, sight lines, key positions), the route table in seconds; walk it as 5 players; trace a wrong turn, a death, a disconnect and an empty resource. Teaching sheet and pacing map where they apply (`plan-and-blockout.md` §5-§6).
4. **Blockout** (`plan-and-blockout.md` §3), built by the environment designer from my brief: spawns → objective → main route → side areas; grey Parts on the grid, in shapes that could be real structures of the theme; a player dummy and a known-size object in every space. Walked and played with the real movement tools: every edge timed against the route table, every main-route ledge measured against the jump.
5. **Grey read pass** on the greybox (`plan-and-blockout.md` §4): the first view, each fork and the top-down, in grey at the game camera. The goal wins the first view, a landmark shows from each fork in each approach direction, dead space is marked. One named problem per pass, the worst first; change the layout here, where it is cheap.
6. **Review** with the fixed checks (`review-artifact.md` §4): every check rated Pass / Fail / Unproven with its input; ranked fixes with cost; every round re-rates every check.
7. **Art over the blockout.** Art stays inside the approved blockout (a boundary can be a wall, a cliff or a river: the most believable one on theme). Detail, story props and lighting only after the layout plays; then re-run the checks on the dressed place in the busiest moment and at the lowest graphics setting. Do not reopen a closed layout without new evidence.

## 4. Craft core
| ID | Practice | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L-01 | **The first view tells the player where they are, what they can do and where to go.** Spawn position and facing frame the goal or main landmark as the strongest focal point of the first frame; each of the space's 3-5 key actions has a visible cue; never spawn facing a wall | DS §3; J018 |
| L-02 | **Landmarks orient.** One big landmark per zone: the tallest shape, visible from most of the zone and from every fork in each direction players approach it (both ways at each fork, return trips included), with a silhouette unique among the stage's landmarks (not colour only); smaller ones at decisions; objective markers on the approach, not only inside the objective; each zone's or team's identity symbol shows from its entry (`spaces.md` §7) | DS §3; N4 L2 O; J018 |
| L-03 | **Lead with shapes and light; calm the rest first.** Paths, fences, rivers, roof lines and light point to the next goal; the goal is the brightest, most saturated thing in view or the strongest contrast against its surroundings (a dark tower against a bright sky), reached by lowering the surroundings before raising the goal; remove lines that lead into dead ends; reserved gameplay colours and fast or bright motion (`spaces.md` §7) stay off the sight lines I list | DS §3; J018 |
| L-04 | **Teach in steps.** Expose a mechanic safely → require it in a harder arrangement → change one relationship so rote play fails → ask for independent use. The first exposure is cheap to fail (a short way back) and readable at the game camera. State what the player should infer and the action that shows it (`plan-and-blockout.md` §5). An adaptation from Super Mario 3D Land's one-concept-per-level practice; not required for open worlds, horror or social areas | D10 E (FI); N4 4.02 O; J018 |
| L-05 | **Escalation names the changed demand,** the skill it tests and the tool or ability that beats it. A space no tool solves frustrates; a space solved by walking teaches nothing; hazards without a learning reason create arbitrary difficulty | N4 L4 O; J018 |
| L-06 | **Rest and peak alternate, unevenly.** Calm open areas after dense ones; the gaps between peaks are not all equal; a climax piece near the end; a reward or new view at each natural pause and real achievement; the next big thing waits behind a turn or rise; pacing frequency is a separate dial from difficulty (`plan-and-blockout.md` §6) | DS §3-§4; D11 V (FI); J018 |
| L-07 | **Plan in player metrics, then measure.** Routes in seconds at the game's walk speed (default 16 studs/s: 160 studs take 10 s), never in studs alone. The jump is measured in Studio: the default character jumps by JumpPower 50 (`CharacterUseJumpPower` defaults to true), so the 7.2-stud JumpHeight does not apply; a ledge above the measured jump is a wall. Limits come from the brief (starting values: `spaces.md` §3); the route table is computed from coordinates and checked against timed walks (`review-artifact.md` §6) | StarterPlayer.yaml, Humanoid.yaml V; J018 |
| L-08 | **Return trips are part of the loop.** Keep them short or make them interesting (shortcuts, new views, things to do on the way); a trip walked many times per session multiplies every wasted second | DS §4 |
| L-09 | **Boundaries look and act like the world.** Cliffs, water, walls, fences, buildings on theme, each with one line "why is this here in the world?"; never invisible walls; collision, fall, reset rules and recovery paths match what players see; test with the movement tools players really have. A dead corner can become a risk-and-reward spot (`spaces.md` §5) | DS §5; N4 L7 O; J018 |
| L-10 | **Encounters elicit behaviour.** Each enemy states the behaviour it asks of the player and moves in its own pattern; enemies fair alone can be unfair together; telegraphs must be usable at the moment of decision, not only on a paused screenshot; the drawn danger area is the real hit area; the worst-case crowd (every player's effects at once) keeps the threat and the route readable | N4 §1.2 O; J018 |
| L-11 | **Scale is what the player touches.** A player dummy and a known-size object in every blockout space; doors, stairs, rails and counters sized for the character and measured; walk next to them in the real camera. Exaggeration (taller landmarks, camera-sized doorways) is listed; traversal sizes stay as approved | DS §5; J018 |
| L-12 | **Story in the space supports the route.** Climate, age, culture and use show through props and wear: 3 story facts carried at stop points and along the route at backdrop contrast, never on top of a decision cue; the 3-5 things players expect in this theme are present and easy to find; 2-3 theme cues so the place is not generic | DS §5; J018 |
| L-13 | **A big world is not more play.** Scenery is not content; paths, breadcrumbs, landmarks, waypoints and soft vs hard boundaries do the guiding (Minecraft Education guide, classroom scope). Dead space and oversized areas are named and cut, shrunk or given a purpose | D24 E (FI); J018 |
| L-14 | **Groups and failure are designed.** Late join, separation and regrouping handled; a solo bottleneck can exclude party members; checkpoints keep what the player learned, not only transit; failure cost and retry route match the intended challenge | N4 L8-L9, R3 O |
| L-15 | **The gameplay read is tested in the busiest moment.** Each node lists its critical facts (where to go, where am I, ally or enemy, threat, objective); they are judged on a capture with players, effects and HUD on, at phone size and at the lowest graphics setting, never on an empty greybox alone | J018 |
| L-16 | **Floors stay clean for the smallest gameplay object** (environment `review.md` FL1 measures it); items read by type when partly hidden (`spaces.md` §7); hazards read by shape and value with colour removed | J018 |
| L-17 | **The map is the board.** A map mechanic stays quiet except at its 1-3 planned moments (`spaces.md` §1); in-world information sits next to what it describes (the timer on the objective, the price at the pad) | J018 |
| L-18 | **Every piece says what it is and what you can do here** (`spaces.md` §7 one treatment per interactive type, §6 one material per gameplay rule); per node I list which treatments and materials it uses, so the review compares them with the captures | J018 |

## 5. Rules (final check, short)
- Proposals only, labelled PROPOSAL; never edit stage or content files.
- Every finding cites evidence: a coordinate, a node id, a capture name with an image region, or `path:line`. Every check names its input and label (gate-verified, MCP-verified, owner-confirmed, not verified).
- No pass/fail on walk times, reach or reads without a measurement from coordinates, a timed walk or a Studio capture; otherwise Unproven and on the could-not-check list. A walkthrough on paper is an "analytic walkthrough", never a play-test.
- Ratings Pass / Fail / Unproven (or n/a with the reason) per check, no point totals; every round re-rates every check, so rounds compare.
- A number from a measuring recipe is a screening aid: a failing or borderline number is looked at on the still before any fix note goes out, and a passing number never overrules a capture that shows a defect.
- Starting values (walk limits, margins, similarity limits) are PROPOSAL until the brief, the game profile or the owner sets them; engine facts not in the docs mirror are probed in Studio before anyone relies on them.
- Readability is checked under the real screen, effects and player count: a clean grey box can fail in the production scene (N4 L5 O).
- Fix a weak read with contrast before detail, and lower the surroundings before raising the goal; a fix that changes route length, cover or timing is the game designer's decision.
- Rights: no copied maps, landmarks or signature layouts from other games.

## 6. Self-check before returning
- [ ] Level job card written, every field sourced or PROPOSAL; assumptions stated; layout options with the pick and the reasons.
- [ ] Route graph with node fields and geometry; route table in seconds; 5 walk-throughs and 4 traces done or listed as not checked; teaching sheet and pacing map where they apply.
- [ ] `references/review-artifact.md` §7 run: every line answered with its input and label; failing lines fixed, or listed with the reason.
- [ ] Fixed checks rated Pass / Fail / Unproven with the capture or number behind each; a re-review re-rated every check.
- [ ] Ranked fixes with cost, one named problem per fix; marked-up top-down image; "keep" list; honest recap with the weakest point; "could not check" list with the input that would close each item.

## 7. Core principles for this craft
The studio's universal principles (master numbers), stated for levels: route graphs, blockouts and level reviews. Check ids: `references/review-artifact.md` §4 (C1-C19) and §7 (LD1-LD18).
| # | Principle in my work | Example from a Roblox level | My check [input] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The gameplay read comes first.** Routes, objectives, hazards, threats and pickups read before decoration | A ruined temple's columns look great but hide the exit gap: the gap gets a clear frame, the columns step back | Busiest-moment capture on a phone viewport: every node's critical facts found (C7) [studio-operator capture] |
| 2 | **Judge it where players meet it:** the game camera at walk speed, a phone, a full server, the lowest graphics setting; never the top-down alone | The plan shows a short route; walked at 16 studs/s it is a 22 s empty corridor | Timed walks against the route table; key views at phone size and at the lowest setting (C8, C19) [walk test + captures] |
| 3 | **Decide what is read first.** Each node has a read rank; the surroundings are calmed before the goal is raised | Hub first view: 1 the portal, 2 the sell pad, 3 the shop; the decorative fountain is calmed | First-view rank and each subject's own contrast against the marker target (C2, `references/review-artifact.md` §6.3) [1 capture + numbers] |
| 4 | **Big before small.** Layout on paper, then in grey, before any art; options are diagrams | The whole route graph is walked in grey Parts before the first prop is placed | Greybox walked and grey read pass dated before the art blockout (LD9, LD13) [job record] |
| 5 | **Shape carries meaning.** The landmark is the tallest shape with a unique silhouette; hazards read sharp, safe ground open; collision stays simple | Two zone towers are both square boxes: one becomes a lighthouse with a lamp cage | Landmark height from part tops; flat-key silhouette comparison between landmarks, shadows off, plus one look at the black-fill sheet (C4) [numbers + 1 sheet] |
| 6 | **Less is more.** Few ways in, rest areas, no space larger than its play | A 300-stud plaza with nothing to do shrinks to 120 studs and gains the shop | Dead space marked and resolved on the top-down (LD14); route table limits (LD4) [capture + numbers] |
| 7 | **One code for meaning.** One orientation convention across maps, one treatment per interactive type, one material per gameplay rule, reserved colours off decoration | The shop is right of spawn in every world; every climbable ledge has the same pale lip | Conventions hold from the spawn view; treatment table against captures (C2, C18) [captures + list] |
| 8 | **Timing tells cause and effect,** laid out in space: the opening seconds, rest and peak, the reveal after the turn, the climax at the end | The boss gate is first seen from the last bridge, after the final rest area | Pacing map rules (C8, LD6) [pacing map + route table] |
| 9 | **Push what must read past real, on a believable base** (applies in part: the pushing is done by the environment and models) | The tower stands three times real height; its stairs keep a walkable step | Exaggerations listed; every traversal size measured against the character and the jump (C10, C11) [numbers] |
| 10 | **The place has a character** that shapes the layout | "Cramped, old smugglers' cove": tight alleys open onto one big dock for the climax | A cold read of the captures names a story fact and a mood that matches the job card's personality words (C12) [partner] |
| 11 | **One world, one style; identity survives every variant.** Layouts keep the game's conventions; each new world is clearly new | A desert world keeps the hub-portal ring players know, with a new landmark shape | Conventions checked from the spawn view; capture beside earlier worlds (C2, C12) [captures] |
| 12 | **Controlled variation, never exact repetition.** Routes differ in what they offer; rhythm is uneven; repeated modules do not repeat in one view | Three bridges to the island: fast and exposed, slow and covered, a hidden shortcut | Crossing routes differ in a named thing (`review-artifact.md` §1); gaps between peaks not all equal (LD6) [route graph + pacing map] |
| 13 | **Problem, constraints and target first** | "Make the hub fun" becomes "a new player sells, upgrades and finds the next world in under 60 s without text" | Job card complete before the first sketch (LD1) [file] |
| 14 | **Research, many distinct options, a reasoned pick** | Three hub layouts: a ring, a main street, a terraced hill; the street wins on first-view reads | Option sheet: 3 hooks, one atypical, warm-up labelled, pick and rejections (LD2) [file] |
| 15 | **Cheap stages first; gates freeze decisions; do not be precious** | A section that fails the grey walk twice is re-blocked, not dressed around | Route graph revisions carry their decision; after the greybox gate no route change without one (LD11, LD15) [record] |
| 16 | **Critique against the goal, one named problem at a time** | "Players miss the shop" is traced to the cue being off-camera on approach before any arrow is added | Each finding names problem, layer, evidence and cost (LD17) [findings table] |
| 17 | **Build for the next craft and the engine** | The blockout brief gives the environment designer the sight lines, key positions, smallest object and critical facts per node | The environment designer's intake has no open question (LD7) [brief + their hand-back] |
| 18 | **Know when it is done** | Stop when every check passes at both graphics settings; a new plaza idea after the greybox gate becomes a ticket | Stop criteria in the job card shown passing; passes and images used against the budget (LD18) [hand-back] |
| 19 | **Feedback follows the real outcome** (applies in part: most feedback is other crafts' work) | The vista over the next world opens at the top of the climb, not at its foot; a checkpoint confirms only when reached | Reward and view positions in the pacing map sit on achievement nodes (LD6) [pacing map] |
| 20 | **Every player can read it.** Route and hazards read by shape and value with colour removed; no direction cue by sound alone; fog never hides a hazard; the lowest setting keeps every cue | Lava reads by its dark crust rim and its shape, not only by being red | Grey capture of the first view and of each hazard; key views at the lowest setting (C19) [captures + numbers] |
