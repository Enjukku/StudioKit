## 1. The route: big to small, each stage freezes the one before
Work like box art: the icon and the Home thumbnails are the highest-effort images in the job, because a browsing player decides from a small tile in a glance. Every stage below solves one level of the picture and is judged only at that level; detail never rescues a wrong big read. The self-review lines (MR-xx) are in `image-review.md`; the standards they test are `marketing-design` §4.

**A brief that caps images below these budgets** (for example 8 images for an icon and three thumbnail concepts): spend them on the earliest open stage and stop at its gate with the rest listed as not done; never thin a round below 3 roughs to reach a later stage. A new game's icon roughs and the thumbnail roughs are two rounds of stage 3, each at least 3 roughs; under a cap one warm-up serves both (8 images: warm-up 1, thumbnail roughs 3, icon roughs 3, one spare for a regeneration). Masks, overlays, sheets and crops are measurement files, not generated images: they count against the looking budget of §1 of `image-review.md`, not against the generation cap.

**Final assets (owner J012-D7).** The uploaded icon or thumbnail can come from three routes: (a) the ChatGPT image as generated, (b) a ChatGPT image touched up by hand (paint-over, fixes, title text), or (c) a staged render in Blender with the game's real models and set, made by the environment designer or modeler on a ticket (the ChatGPT image then serves as the composition target). Pick the route per image and say why: (c) is closest to the real game when the models exist; (a) and (b) when they do not yet. Every route goes through the same `thumb_check.py` run, checklist, art director review and the owner's approval of that one image. The asset record names the route and keeps the source files (prompt record, edit file, .blend and camera); it says **key art**, never "gameplay capture". For every other craft, reference images stay inputs (`reference-images`).

**Regeneration vs a new option.** Fixing a failed image (a generation fault from MR-33: wrong hands, a wrong count, a drifted face, stray text) is a regeneration with the same identity: at most 2, then report. A different idea is a new manifest row in the round, never a regeneration.

## 2. `thumb_check.py`: what to run and how to read it
```text
python tools/art/thumb_check.py icon <icon.png> --title "<title>" --mask <hero mask> [--out-dir <dir per variant>] --out <json>
python tools/art/thumb_check.py thumbnail <thumb.png> --size WxH --size-source "<capture id, device>" --bottom-band N --title "<title>" --mask <hero mask> [--out-dir <dir per variant>] --out <json>
```
| You need | How |
| --- | --- |
| **The small size** | Icon: default 150×150 (the size the Roblox icon docs name, FD-20). Thumbnail: no Roblox doc gives the phone tile size (searched 2026-10-21, T04). Measure it: a phone screenshot of Roblox Home with a game tile (owner, or studio-operator on a device), the tile's width × height in screenshot pixels, the device named. Store it as `knowledge/design/marketing/surfaces.md` (one row per device and surface, with the capture file). Re-measure when Roblox changes the Home layout. No row yet: run with the master's aspect at a stated stand-in size and mark every small-size line `not verified: tile size not measured` |
| **The subject (hero mask)** | Without a subject the one-moment and contrast fields say "not measured". **Full scenes (every ChatGPT thumbnail and most icons): a traced hero mask.** Trace the outline from the image as one polygon per part (head, body, each limb, the signature item, the headgear, each member of a group), following each edge to about one tile pixel; a thin part that shows at tile size (a handle, a plume) gets its own thin polygon. Turn the parts into one mask (§4), then make its overlay (§4) and look at it once before any number: no part of the hero left untinted, no background tinted wider than a tile pixel; fix and re-check. A coarse polygon round the hero is my drawing, not the image: its black-fill panel shows the shape I drew and its `component_count` is the number of polygons, so it can never pass or fail MR-07 or MR-08. Every line that reads the mask names its source (traced and overlay-checked, magenta pass, key, alpha). **Staged renders: the hero-only pass on magenta**, `--key FF00FF` or a mask made from it. Cut-out icons on a flat colour: `--key auto` or the alpha channel. A head-and-shoulders icon that touches the frame edge has no flat border: `--key auto` finds no subject, use a mask. `--box` gives a rectangle that includes background, so its contrast is diluted and its `component_count` is just the number of boxes you gave: use it only for a tight region such as the title area |
| **Bottom band** | `--bottom-band N` with N measured from the same capture (how much of the tile the metadata covers), never a guessed percent |
| **Tolerances** | none by default. A tolerance (`--min-ratio`, `--max-components`, `--max-bottom-share`) needs `--tolerance-source` = an owner or art-director decision id. Without one the verdict stays "measured": you read the numbers |
| **One sheet per variant** | The sheet's name comes from the input file and the size only: a second run on the same image (another mask, another band) overwrites it. Give each variant its own `--out-dir` and `--out` |

**Reading the numbers (diagnostics, not grades):**
| Field | Read as |
| --- | --- |
| `master.*.meets` | the docs' own format rules (square, ≥ 512, 16:9, size, 3 MB). A `false` is a format FAIL: fix it before anyone looks. `all_met` true does not mean sharp: an image upscaled from a small one passes every check |
| `one_clear_moment.small.subject_share_of_frame` | read first: it must match the size of the hero you drew. An inverted mask (white subject on black) gives the background's share (a thumbnail hero near 0.04 reads as 0.96) and every other subject number is wrong, with no error. A thumbnail hero is rarely above 0.5; a head-and-shoulders icon often is, so there the mask panel decides |
| `one_clear_moment.small.component_count` and `largest_share_of_subject` | with a hero mask: how many separate pieces of the hero group survive at tile size (pieces under 0.5 % of the frame are not counted: they are texture, not information) and whether one dominates. Several equal pieces = the moment splits; look at the mask panel |
| `contrast.small.subject_ring_ratio` | hero vs a thin ring around it, by luminance, at tile size; it is lighter-over-darker either way, so read `subject.p50` against `ring.p50` for the direction. Low = the hero merges with its surround in grey |
| `contrast.small.whole_p95_p5_ratio` | the image's overall value range; low = flat, everything mid-grey; compare with the tone the brief set |
| `bottom_band.subject_share_in_band` | how much of the hero sits where metadata can cover it |

Then look at the sheet yourself: can you say the action from the small preview with the title under it, in one sentence, without the full-size image? If not, the image fails I1 whatever the numbers say. The tool never says "good"; it shows where to look.

**Other measures** (`tools/art/look_check.py`, read its `--help`): `silhouette <img> --mask <a> --compare-mask <b>` compares two outlines after aligning them (shape similarity of two options; it ignores position); `palette <img> [--mask M] --roles '{"brand":"#hex",...}'` gives colour clusters with their shares and each role's nearest cluster (k-means splits a gradient into several clusters: sum the shares per hue family yourself; restrict to the hero with `--mask` before reading a hero role; it shows that a colour is present, never that a reserved colour is absent or where it sits); `value <img> --box <title area>` for the calm room behind the title.

**Reserved-colour share** (MR-15; a screening aid): the share of pixels within a colour distance of one reserved swatch, the art director's one-liner in `art-review/references/look-check.md` §5 line 4, run on the PNG master with the bible's distance. Its mask argument counts white and skips dark. For the image outside the things that carry the meaning, pass a black-fill of those things (the enemy shown as the enemy, the object the colour belongs to: the hero-mask command in §4 traces it); for the hero alone, pass the hero mask inverted (§4). Run it once per swatch: the bible's reserved hex and the colour as painted in this image (the fg `hex` of `contrast --sample` with a box inside the thing that carries it). Read `px` and look at the marked image it writes. Wrong without an error: light from a source in the reserved colour (fire, lanterns, a glow) pools on snow, skin or rock and counts: the marked image shows it and the bible decides whether that light is allowed; a carrier's mask that misses its glow counts the halo; the hero mask passed without inverting counts everything except the hero; a JPEG or a resized tile moves the hues. Tested on a generated key art (1536 × 1024) whose bible keeps the egg's gold for the egg: outside the egg mask, 1.77 % (25,943 px) for the gold as painted (`#FDBF69`), in torch flames and lantern-lit snow; `palette` with the egg excluded gave one gold cluster (4.6 %, ΔE 9.6) and no place.

## 3. Variants and personalization
- 2-5 active thumbnails, each a different, honest concept (FD-26). Each concept states the one player expectation it changes (checklist I12). The live set comes from the round's distinct options (stage 3), not from colourways of one image.
- Uploads: under 3 MB and up to 1920×1080 per image (FD-27, read as per image).
- Before activating: tell the analyst the hypothesis, the metric label as the dashboard shows it (qPTR today, FD-43), the guardrails (first-play bounce, playtime of the arriving cohort) and the earliest read date. Uploading and activating is an **owner action** in Creator Hub.
- Do not restart within 7 days unless something is broken (FD-39). Change the set with substantial content updates (FD-40); each refresh keeps the brand pattern and changes one property, so the family still reads as the same game.
- Keep every image's asset id, version, the date it went live and the image it replaced (checklist I13).

## 4. Short commands (run on copies; never overwrite a master)
| Need | Command |
| --- | --- |
| Hero mask from traced parts (JSON: a list of polygons `[[[x,y],...], ...]` in master pixels, one per part; a single polygon `[[x,y],...]` also works) | `python -c "import sys,json;from PIL import Image,ImageDraw;i,p=sys.argv[1:3];W,H=Image.open(i).size;P=json.load(open(p));P=[P] if isinstance(P[0][0],(int,float)) else P;m=Image.new('L',(W,H),255);d=ImageDraw.Draw(m);[d.polygon([tuple(q) for q in s],fill=0) for s in P];m.save(p[:-5]+'.mask.png')" <image> <hero.json>` |

| Mirror and grey copies | `python -c "import sys;from PIL import Image,ImageOps;p=sys.argv[1];i=Image.open(p);ImageOps.mirror(i).save(p[:-4]+'.mirror.png');ImageOps.grayscale(i).save(p[:-4]+'.grey.png')" <image>` |

| Frame IoU of two masks (same frame, no alignment) | `python tools/art/look_check.py iou <mask a> <mask b>` |

| Grid among competing tiles (2 columns, tile size) | `python -c "import sys;from PIL import Image;w,h=map(int,sys.argv[1].split('x'));t=sys.argv[3:];g=Image.new('RGB',(2*w+30,((len(t)+1)//2)*(h+10)+10),(24,24,28));[g.paste(Image.open(p).convert('RGB').resize((w,h),Image.LANCZOS),(10+(i%2)*(w+10),10+(i//2)*(h+10))) for i,p in enumerate(t)];g.save(sys.argv[2])" WxH <out.png> <ours.png> <tile2.png> ...` |
| Real-layout mock (the tile pasted over a tile's box in the phone capture) | `python -c "import sys;from PIL import Image;c,t,b,o=sys.argv[1:5];x0,y0,x1,y1=map(int,b.split(','));C=Image.open(c).convert('RGB');C.paste(Image.open(t).convert('RGB').resize((x1-x0,y1-y0),Image.LANCZOS),(x0,y0));C.save(o)" <capture.png> <tile.png> X0,Y0,X1,Y1 <out.png>` |
| Colour profile of an export | `python -c "import sys,io;from PIL import Image,ImageCms;c=Image.open(sys.argv[1]).info.get('icc_profile');print(ImageCms.getProfileDescription(ImageCms.ImageCmsProfile(io.BytesIO(c))) if c else 'none: treated as sRGB')" <image>` |

Every command was run once on a synthetic sample of its real input (J018 coverage table d); the 3-value study, frame IoU and planned crop are tested `look_check` modes since J019 G3-P8. A crop box comes from the crop plan; the icon crop of a 1920×1080 master is at most 1080 px square, enough for the 512 px icon master.

Reading the 3-value study: use the default cuts unless the art bible names 3-value cut points in grey levels; its value bands are per-layer ranges in percent (environment, characters, effects), not cuts, and given as cuts they make a quiet background read as the light group with no error. The shares are the value plan in numbers (a quiet background is one group; a calm image sits mostly in one or two neighbouring groups, a dramatic one has real shares at both ends). A "near a cut" share above about 0.1 (a starting value) means a large area sits on a cut line and may split into speckle: re-run once with both cuts moved about 15 levels away from it; groups that keep their shares are real.

## 5. Worked examples
**A. Update thumbnail: a new world in a pet-digging simulator.** Launched thing: the Crystal Caves world. Sell list: (1) a new glowing world, (2) the dig action breaks into it, (3) it is ours (the game's digger and its blocky style). Premise: "a digger breaks through the rock into a giant glowing cave". Message: "a new world opens"; symbol: the crack of light. Fans want (trend scout, dated): bigger worlds and the moment of discovery. Tone: dramatic, wide value range. Claim: the cave is playable at world 5 in the update build.
- Warm-up: the digger standing in front of the cave with pets, the typical tile of the genre; kept as the baseline every rough must beat.
- Roughs: **A** scale: a tiny digger at the mouth of a giant geode, back to us; **B** the break-in: a pickaxe strike cracks a crystal wall and light bursts out; **C** social: three friends on a ledge above the cave; **D** atypical: the view from inside a crystal looking out at the digger. Shape IoU A-B 0.2, C-D 0.3: four directions.
- Masks: traced per part (digger, pickaxe, each friend) and checked on their overlays; B's first trace left the pickaxe handle untinted, so it was re-traced as its own thin polygon. B's black-fill at the tile then shows the handle as a 2 px sliver: noted for the green light (thicken it or turn the strike toward the camera).
- Sheets at the tile: C's three friends survive as three equal pieces (`component_count` 3, largest share 0.36): the moment splits. D's grey panel shows no hero. B names "a digger breaks into a glowing cave" in one sentence; A reads "big cave", but the digger is 6 px tall. Recommendation: B with A's scale (the geode wall behind the strike), reasons tied to sell items 1 and 2; C's friends are closed as a direction for this tile, kept as a candidate for the social thumbnail in the live set.
- Green light: the pickaxe head is enlarged and the strike turned so the handle is 4 tile px wide in the black-fill. The 3-value study (default cuts) gives dark 0.24, mid 0.70, light 0.06: a dark rock frame on the left (foreground), the crystals behind in one mid group, and the light group only on the digger and the crack. Light set-ups: top light vs light from the crack; the crack light wins (motivated, and it makes the focal point the brightest area). Crop squares: the icon square around the crack and the digger's head and pickaxe; the Home tile; a 16:9 ad tile. Real-layout mock: the tile stands apart in the grid of the genre's cave tiles because it is the only one with a single bright point.
- Colour: cave purple dominant, rock brown support, the warm crack light as the complementary accent, the brand yellow only on the logo; `palette --mask` puts the hero's saturated pops on the pickaxe and the crack.
- Self-review found: `bottom_band.subject_share_in_band` 0.12 (the pickaxe handle) → raised the digger; the mirror showed the horizon tilting right → levelled; MR-28 frame IoU of the render against the green-lit mask 0.86; the symbol (the crack), traced as a black shape and cropped square, is one piece at 150×150 and still reads as a jagged split of light.
