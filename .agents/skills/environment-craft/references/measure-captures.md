# Measuring captures and the place

## 0. Before any measure
- **Captures are in-scene.** A Studio game-camera capture has sky and ground at its border, so `look_check.py` cannot key a background: every measure names its regions with `--box x0,y0,x1,y1` (pixels, x1/y1 exclusive), `--name` per region where several are compared. With no box, `value` and `silhouette` give `invalid-mask`; `palette` gives no error and measures the whole frame (sky, HUD and effects included). With `--box` it clusters the boxes only: used for palette roles on the material zoo (M12), never for reserved hues or saturated shares, because k-means merges a small accent into a neighbour cluster (those are pixel shares from the M3 script).
- **Boxes are the method.** Write them in the packet beside the capture name. Environment boxes cover ground, walls and props only: never the sky, a character, an effect or the HUD. The landmark is a named region of its own.
- **Units.** `look_check.py` reports WCAG relative luminance (0-1), not grey value. Grey value → luminance (conversions only; the band values are the art bible's, `art-review/references/art-bible.md` §7): 5 % ≈ 0.004, 8 % ≈ 0.007, 15 % ≈ 0.020, 85 % ≈ 0.69, 92 % ≈ 0.83.
- **The separation ratio.** `subject_ring_ratio` = (lighter median + 0.05) / (darker median + 0.05) of subject and ring: symmetric, never below 1, and 1.0 means no value separation, so a test of "above 1" passes almost every capture. Compare it with the separation targets in `environment-design/references/light-and-air.md` VB-a, and read `subject.p50` against `ring.p50` for the direction. Once the art bible gives a target, pass `--min-ratio <target> --tolerance-source "<bible revision>"` so the JSON carries pass or fail.
- **Same conditions.** Before and after pairs share camera, graphics setting, time of day, post effects and seed; every number names its capture, quality level and viewport size. Captures are PNG (JPEG noise raises the M3 spreads).
- No number becomes PASS without a tolerance from the art bible, the game designer or the owner; until then it is MEASURED against a starting value marked `PROPOSAL`.

## 2. What these numbers cannot tell you
Taste, feel at normal speed, whether a player notices a cue, sound, and anything outside the boxes. Those come from the key-view images you look at, the studio operator's play captures, and the game designer's, art director's or owner's check-ins (`environment-design/references/review.md` §2).

## 3. The colour script (M3, M4, M11)
Save it once per stage as `content/stages/<stage-id>/review/measure/env_lab.py`; run it from the repository root. Spec keys: `boxes` {group name: list of boxes}; `exclude` (gameplay objects, characters and effects inside the boxes); `reserved` {name: hex}, authored and as rendered; `max_de` (required: the CIEDE2000 distance that counts as the reserved hue, from the reserved-colours row of `art-review/references/art-bible.md` §7 or the game's bible); `sat_chroma` (CIELAB chroma counted as saturated: starting `PROPOSAL` 40); `grey` (under a coloured look: the hex of a mid-grey reference block as rendered under the same light, sampled with `contrast --sample` like the reserved swatches from the zoo's grey block under that look or a grey block placed out of the play area in the view); `flat` {surface name: one box on one lit side of one surface, no cast shadow, no exclusions; on a capture with Atmosphere and fog off}.
```python
import sys, json, numpy as n
sys.path.insert(0, 'tools/art'); import look_check as c
from PIL import Image
img = n.asarray(Image.open(sys.argv[1]).convert('RGB')).astype(float); sp = json.load(open(sys.argv[2])); H, W = img.shape[:2]
lab, lin = c.srgb_to_lab(img), c.srgb_to_linear(img / 255)
g = c.srgb_to_linear(n.array(c.hex_to_rgb(sp['grey']), float) / 255) if 'grey' in sp else n.ones(3)
labw = c.srgb_to_lab(c.linear_to_srgb(lin * g.mean() / g) * 255)
ex = n.zeros((H, W), bool)
for x0, y0, x1, y1 in sp.get('exclude', []): ex[y0:y1, x0:x1] = True
out = {'capture': sys.argv[1], 'spec': sp, 'boxes': {}, 'flat': {}}
for name, boxes in sp.get('boxes', {}).items():
    m = n.zeros((H, W), bool)
    for x0, y0, x1, y1 in boxes: m[y0:y1, x0:x1] = True
    L, Lw = lab[m & ~ex], labw[m & ~ex]; ch = n.hypot(Lw[:, 1], Lw[:, 2])
    r = {'n_px': len(L), 'mean_chroma': ch.mean(), 'share_chroma_above': (ch > sp.get('sat_chroma', 40)).mean(), 'mean_a': L[:, 1].mean(), 'mean_b': L[:, 2].mean()}
    for k, h in sp.get('reserved', {}).items():
        r['reserved_share_' + k] = (c.ciede2000(L, c.srgb_to_lab(n.array(c.hex_to_rgb(h), float))) <= sp['max_de']).mean()
    out['boxes'][name] = {k: round(float(v), 5) for k, v in r.items()}
for name, (x0, y0, x1, y1) in sp.get('flat', {}).items():
    b = lin[y0:y1, x0:x1]; h4, w4 = b.shape[0] // 4, b.shape[1] // 4
    b = b[:h4 * 4, :w4 * 4].reshape(h4, 4, w4, 4, 3).mean((1, 3)); s = b.sum(-1); ok = s > 0.03
    rg = b[..., :2][ok] / s[ok][:, None]; ly = n.log2(b @ [.2126, .7152, .0722])[ok]
    yy, xx = n.nonzero(ok); A = n.c_[xx, yy, n.ones(len(xx))]; res = ly - A @ n.linalg.lstsq(A, ly, rcond=None)[0]
    out['flat'][name] = {'hue_spread': round(float(1000 * n.sqrt(rg.var(0).sum())), 3), 'value_spread': round(float(res.std()), 4)}
print(json.dumps(out, indent=1))
```
- **Reserved share** = the share of box pixels (exclusions removed) within `max_de` of the swatch, pixel by pixel: a small accent is not merged into a neighbour cluster, as k-means palettes do.
- **`grey`** white-balances every pixel to the grey block's rendered colour before `mean_chroma` and `share_chroma_above` (each linear channel scaled so the grey block reads neutral); `mean_a` and `mean_b` stay raw, because they measure the light's temperature (M11), and the reserved share uses its as-rendered swatches. Without `grey` the output is unchanged. Repair round 3 (beige floor, muted terracotta upper wall, one loud orange stray sign, one grey block; player chroma / upper-wall chroma / saturated share of the upper wall, raw → with `grey`): neutral sun 7.6 / 32.0 / 0.039, unchanged; warm sun 13.5 / 37.5 → 7.7 / 31.9; warm dusk 17.4 / 39.8 → 7.2 / 28.4; deep sunset 23.2 / 44.9 / 1.000 → 7.5 / 28.0 / 0.039 (raw, the neutral floor fails the neutral limit and the whole muted wall counts as stray saturation); dim interior 10.3 / 28.1 → 6.2 / 24.1. The orange sign's 0.039 stays in every run. Set `grey` when the grey block's own chroma as rendered is above 3 (warm sun 5.6, dusk 10.3, sunset 15.8, dim interior 3.9; neutral sun 0.0).
- **Flat fill: use `python tools/art/look_check.py hue-spread <capture> --box <one material's lit face>`** (the studio's one flat-fill measure since J019: the CIELAB hue-angle spread, starting flat limit 3°, PROPOSAL; it reads one-hue materials correctly on captures and on renders with a studio highlight, where this script's linear-RGB `hue_spread` false-passed them at 19.7 and 36.3). The script's `flat` field below is kept for history only; do not judge VB12 on it. Old note: `hue_spread` = spread of linear-RGB chromaticity (×1000) over 4 × 4-pixel blocks, so a pure light gradient adds nothing; `value_spread` = spread of log2 luminance after a fitted plane takes out a smooth gradient. Flat limits (starting `PROPOSAL`): `hue_spread` 8, `value_spread` 0.04. A surface passes when `hue_spread` is above its limit; `value_spread` above its limit with `hue_spread` under it is `monochrome` (value texture in one hue: fails); both under is `flat` (fails). J018 runs: flat brown, red and grey walls under a 0.8-1.1 light gradient 1.0-2.7 / 0.008-0.009 as PNG (up to 5.9 / 0.021 as a noisy JPEG); hue patches 27.3 / 0.070; value-only stone patches 2.6 / 0.114; faint tint patches 11.0 / 0.026. Repair round 2: one-hue bricks (±15 % value, darker mortar) 2.8 / 0.207 and a built-in-material value texture under one Color 2.7 / 0.197 (both `monochrome`); the same bricks with a per-brick hue shift of ±2° 5.6, ±4° 10.1, ±12° 29.2 (a shift of a few degrees passes); silt and damp patches in other hues 34.7 / 0.181; darker damp patches in the wall's own hue 2.8 / 0.486 (`monochrome`). Repair round 3: a flat wall receding into haze reads 8.0, 14.6, 25.7 with 5, 10, 20 % haze at its far end (false passes from 10 %), its near third 3.6, 6.1, 11.1; with no haze 1.8 (`flat`): the `flat` boxes come from a capture with Atmosphere and fog off.
