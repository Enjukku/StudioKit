# Game concept: a fantasy players want to try

## 1. When this skill starts
The owner wants new game ideas, wants an idea grown bigger, or wants a concept judged before pre-production. It answers: what does the player become and do, why would they click, which proven loop carries it, what is the twist, what is the smallest build that proves it, and which of 3 pitches the designer recommends. After the owner picks one, `preproduction` takes over. The core principles in `game-design` §7 apply.

## 3. Steps
1. **Intake.** Read `knowledge/design/game-profile.md` (if a game exists), the taste log (`knowledge/design/taste-log.md`), `tried-and-failed.md`, the closed directions, and any opportunity records from the trend scout. No trend data → ask for it (ticket) or mark every "why now" as `not verified`. Name the audience you design for and one line on what players of the loops you consider want (from player sources: comments, Discord, the trend scout's report; or `not verified`). *Check:* you can name the audience, its "want" line and the device mix you design for.
2. **Set the search budget** before generating: how many raw concepts, why that number. Record the real counts at every step (no invented quotas, N2 §5.1 O).
3. **Warm up, then diverge.** The first handful of ideas sit in the range of expectation: write them down as the warm-up round, label them, and go further. Generate one-sentence concepts across 6 axes (action, role, setting, threat, social relationship, consequence) and from proven loops (C-03). Each candidate takes its hook from a different source (a real-world system, another medium, an odd reading of the theme); at least one comes from outside games, chosen from what the concept must make the player do (a field where people already get that done), with the method taken written as an action, labelled O unless cited; never a method that works by pressure or chance (retail scarcity, streaks, casino tricks: `platform-policy`, G-11); and at least one is a big swing that breaks an expectation of its loop. No criticism yet (C-05).
4. **Expand and dedupe.** Vary the mechanism, not the words (C-06): change one named element per variant. Group by playable action and consequence; keep distinct fantasies even on the same loop. *Check:* the batch diagnosis in `idea-method.md` §4 finds more than one mechanism family, and no two survivors share a hook source.
5. **Critic pass, separate.** Rate each survivor with the rubric (FAIL / UNKNOWN / PLAUSIBLE / OBSERVED). No weighted total. Name the cheapest test for each open risk. A survivor whose loop has no real decision (goal, opposition, decisions, rules) or whose theme breaks the owner's tone, or overlaps an existing theme with no niche of its own, is flagged.
6. **Pick 3, recommend 1.** Write a pitch card + full record for each (`pitch-template.md`). If fewer than 3 are defensible, show the shortfall and ask for more exploration; never pad (S15 O). First images of the 3 are made the same way (same camera, same finish), so none wins by polish. *Check:* each pitch passes the title test (C-02), names its proven loop and its twist, names its target experience, and its first image reads at tile size (C-08).
7. **Present** in one message to the owner (`ste-writing`): the 3 cards, the recommendation with its tradeoff and strongest objection, and the decision asked as a goal question ("which would players most want to try from the title alone?"), never "which do you like?". Tell each pitch as the moment the player has, with conviction. Files go to `knowledge/design/concepts/<concept-id>.md`, or the path the brief names (the brief wins).
8. **Record the owner's choice** with the owner's words in the taste log and `decisions.md`, including what was rejected and why; rejected directions go to the closed-doors list for this job. Then start `preproduction`.

## 4. Craft core (what separates a great pitch from a list of reskins)
| ID | Practice | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C-01 | **Fantasy first, as a role with a consequential verb.** "As [role], I [verb] so that [change], while [pressure]." Features come after. A genre label is not a fantasy | S07, T7 field 3 O (N1, N2) |
| C-02 | **The title is the fantasy.** A short verb phrase with an outsized object or a stake that makes the reader picture the action and ask "what happens if…": *Destroy the Sun*, *Build a Dam to Save the City*, *Fly or Die*. Test: a stranger who reads only the title can say what they will do | Owner; Roblox hits use verb-object titles (*Grow a Garden*, *Steal a Brainrot*), names V, pattern O |
| C-03 | **Proven loop + new theme + one twist.** Borrow the loop's functional structure; the theme changes the role, the emotional meaning, the social context or the first-image appeal; the twist changes one decision or consequence, written as a rule: "when X, the player must choose Y, and Z follows" (not a feature noun). The twist need not be new tech | Owner; T8 O; B4 O |
| C-04 | **Search decisions and consequences, not reskins.** Two ideas with the same action, reward and escalation are one idea with two skins. Keep both only if the fantasy, role or social meaning gives the audience a different reason to care | S03 O; N1 §3 |
| C-05 | **Diverge, then select, in separate passes.** Splitting generation from selection raised goal-path discovery in one small 2026 study (GPT-5.4 27→38 of 56 paths), but originality fell for another model. Criticism before alternatives exist narrows the batch | A08 V (small, model-specific) |
| C-06 | **Expand by mechanism.** Per seed vary: what the player can do, what can go wrong, who changes the outcome, what persists. Use inversion, distant analogy, a constraint, a counterfactual, an anti-cliché pass | N2 §5.1 step 3 O |
| C-07 | **Quality next to novelty.** Turning up randomness raised novelty and cut quality in small-model tests; an AI novelty score swung 52.6 points between near-identical prompts. Never present an AI score as a fact; judge each pitch on the rubric | A02 V, A01 V (see FI) |
| C-08 | **First image.** Exactly one camera view (alternatives go on a separate variant line), from the angle players really get (the game camera, not a trailer angle): readable player action, scale relationship (the outsized object clearly bigger than the tiny player), immediate stakes, one message and one symbol. If you cannot describe the thumbnail, the concept is not ready. When the brief allows images, render it (`reference-images`, `concept`), label it concept art, and check it at tile size (`hook-promise.md` §3) | T7 field 5 O; HC01 O; E |
| C-09 | **Signature moment through ordinary play.** Setup → player decision → readable consequence → payoff, reachable with normal inputs, not only in a trailer. The opening teaches the verb the title promises | H6, HC03, HC04 O |
| C-10 | **Smallest version keeps the decisive action.** Cut breadth (content, world size, modes) before the action, relationship or consequence that makes the fantasy different. Split feasibility: the defining interaction vs the production burden around it | S11 O; N1 N2-N3 O |
| C-11 | **Name the failure.** The strongest reason it may fail and the cheapest test that could show it. Keep a bold, uncertain concept alive when a cheap test can settle its risk | S05 O; T7 field 11 |
| C-12 | **Learn taste without narrowing.** Log what the owner picked, rejected and why; tell apart "wrong fantasy" from "wrong scope", "weak pitch" or "too risky". Keep close-fit, adjacent and wildcard ideas in each batch; past taste is never a permanent veto | S06, T3-T7 O |
| C-13 | **Separate measures stay separate.** Novelty, owner taste, market evidence and policy compliance are different ratings. Do not add them into one score | rubric rules O; D2 O |
| C-14 | **Evidence or nothing.** Every number in a pitch (players, growth, sales) has a source and a date; peak concurrent users are not daily users; a reach figure (visits, concurrency) never supports a design claim; a trend can be temporary. No source → write `not verified`. "Number" means a measured quantity about players, money, time or performance; names, ids and values given in the brief are not | R2 O; FR |
| C-15 | **Name the experience, not "fun".** Each pitch names one or two target experiences (power fantasy, thrill, fellowship, mastery, discovery, expression, collection, competition, comfort), and its loop sentence shows a goal, an opposition and a real decision | E |
| C-16 | **Warm up, then swing.** The first ideas are the expected ones: label them warm-up and go further; every batch holds at least one atypical candidate and one big swing; the pick is never the first idea by default, and most candidates are meant to die | E |
| C-17 | **A different hook source per candidate.** Each candidate takes its hook from its own source (a real system, another medium, an odd reading of the theme), at least one from outside games, naming the method taken (an action, not a theme noun); options that differ only in detail are one option | E |
| C-18 | **For a named audience.** Pitch for a specific audience and what they want to see, not for everyone; pick themes from player data, say which proven themes a pitch combines and its maturity (young audience: lower maturity by default); a theme that breaks the world's tone or has no niche of its own is cut, however cool | E |
| C-19 | **Pitch the moment with conviction.** Describe what the player sees, hears and feels in the signature moment in one or two concrete sentences; a list of features is not a pitch | E |
| C-20 | **Equal finish, goal questions, closed doors.** The 3 pitches are shown at the same finish (same camera, same image treatment); the owner gets a goal question with one recommendation; rejected directions are logged and not re-pitched without new owner words | E |

## 5. Rules (final check, short)
- The owner decides. You recommend exactly one concept and say its tradeoff; never claim the owner or another lead approved anything without a decision-log line.
- 3 pitches when 3 are defensible. Never pad with known weak ideas; never invent player counts, trends, retention or revenue.
- Borrow loops, never expression: no other game's characters, art, names, music, text, branding or confusingly similar look; no franchise without a licence. Roblox's IP and repetitive-content rules apply (`platform-policy`).
- Anything players buy, roll or trade in a pitch is checked against `platform-policy` before the pitch goes out. A pitch never depends on paid random items or ads to deliver its fantasy.
- Concepts for young audiences: no fantasy that needs harassment, exploitation or mature content to work. A theme that could read badly (captivity, cruelty) moves onto a motif or prop instead of an act, or is dropped.
- Numbers, names and content are PROPOSAL until the owner confirms (`knowledge/design/open-questions.md`).
- A direction the owner closed is not pitched again without new owner words or new evidence, stated as such.

## 6. Self-check before returning
- [ ] Every pitch has: title, one-line fantasy, target experience, first image, proven loop named + twist, signature moment, smallest version, why now (sourced or `not verified`), failure hypothesis + cheapest test.
- [ ] The title test passes for each title (a reader can say the action from the title alone).
- [ ] The 3 pitches are different mechanism families or different fantasies, not 3 skins of one idea; their hooks come from different sources, and the batch held at least one outside-games source and one big swing.
- [ ] The warm-up round is labelled and the counts show more ideas after it; the record shows why each rejected survivor lost.
- [ ] Each loop sentence shows a goal, an opposition and a real decision; each pitch's art and mechanics promise the same thing (the first image shows an action the loop really has).
- [ ] First images: one camera from the game's view, same finish for all 3, the action, scale and stake readable; checked at tile size (`thumb_check.py thumbnail` with the `--size` measured in `knowledge/design/marketing/surfaces.md` and `--box` on the hero and its action, or `icon` at 150x150; no measured size = `not measured`) with one clear subject; "measured" results reported, never a click score.
- [ ] The audience and its "want" line named; each theme fits the tone, has its niche, states the themes it combines and its maturity.
- [ ] One recommendation, its tradeoff, its strongest objection, what evidence would change it; the owner question is a goal question.
- [ ] Actual counts recorded and recounted from the tables (raw, expanded, deduped, critic survivors); no weighted total; every UNKNOWN or FAIL has its cheapest test.
- [ ] Survivors not pitched are listed with the rubric reason; the reserve is named; closed directions not re-pitched.
- [ ] Policy, rights, sensitivity and audience checks done or listed as blocking.
- [ ] Owner message in `ste-writing`, telling each pitch as the moment the player has; files in `knowledge/design/concepts/`.
