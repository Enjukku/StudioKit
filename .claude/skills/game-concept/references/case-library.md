# Case library: 15 breakout concepts read as fantasy, image, loop and twist

## 1. How to read it (O)
- The roster is chosen to show possibilities, not hit rates or causes. Timing, distribution, community, execution and updates explain success as much as the concept does.
- Sales, active users, awards and views are different outcomes; never add them up or compare them.
- A "smallest version" names functional scope only: no staffing, budget or content count.
- Use a case for its **lesson** (last column): what to protect when you build your own concept. Never borrow its expression.
- When a game's appeal rests on a licensed name (an anime, a film, a brand), part of its clicks came from the licence. The studio cannot borrow that; judge the loop and twist without it, and flag the rights question (J003 hidden-test lesson). Licence status: write "check the live listing" unless you read it this task; what is blocked is borrowing the IP, not studying the loop.

| Case | Fantasy | First image | Loop + twist | Smallest version | Lesson to transfer |

| Splatoon | a stylish shape-shifting squid whose paint is weapon, territory and escape route | a humanoid splashes a wall, transforms, swims up it | team territory shooter; shots also build movement routes and visible team progress | compact arena, two paint colours, a paint weapon, swim, a clear territory finish | one material or verb doing several emotionally coherent jobs beats unrelated features |
| Portal | escape a hostile lab by making two distant places adjacent | a player sees themselves through a doorway they made, falls in one portal, flies out another | spatial puzzles; player-placed topology replaces fixed routes | compact rooms, portal traversal, enough taunting voice to suggest an enemy | cut content breadth, never the impossible verb |
| Minecraft | carve a home and a personal history from a world you can rearrange | a raw hillside becomes a lit refuge as night falls | gather, craft, build, survive; the terrain is both resource and canvas | bounded land, small block set, a real night threat (or a separate creative test) | protect player authorship; watch for uses you did not plan |
| Stardew Valley | leave alienating work and make a place flourish by daily care | the farmhouse framed by weeds, then by the player's thriving garden | farm routines fused with relationships, exploration and restoration | a plot and crop cycle, a home, one responsive neighbour, a visible repair goal | stakes need not be danger; a clear before/after makes ordinary verbs aspirational |
| Among Us | keep the ship alive while finding the saboteur, or lie well enough to win | cheerful crewmates doing jobs while one slips from a vent beside a body | social deduction where movement, whereabouts, chores and sabotage create evidence | compact map, hidden traitor, tasks, report, discuss, vote | design the situation that gives players something consequential to say; a chat box alone does not |

| Slay the Spire | improvise a unique combat engine that climbs a lethal tower | a hand of cards facing a threatening enemy, a branching route ahead | fight and pick rewards; the deck changes the next decision space, not only numbers | one character, small synergistic card pool, contrasting enemies, a route choice, a boss | an acquisition must change how the player thinks next fight; more collectibles alone do not |
| Hades | a rebellious immortal fights out of the family business of death | an escape run ends; the hero arrives home to an invested antagonist | action-roguelite runs become episodes of a persistent story | one weapon, a short route, a boss, a home character whose replies change | attach a second, compatible reward to retrying |
| Untitled Goose Game | be a petty, unstoppable nuisance in a tidy world | a goose proudly carries a stolen key while its owner chases it | non-lethal stealth-puzzle mischief | a goose, one expressive human, a small garden, chase and recovery | a character fantasy sets verbs, feedback, tone and failure rules together; a mascot on generic tasks does not |

| Outer Wilds | an astronaut-archaeologist finding out why a dangerous world repeats | a small handmade ship over a planet whose surface visibly changes | explore, connect clues, revisit; understanding (not items) unlocks actions | linked places, an environmental timer, one revelation that re-reads an earlier obstacle; test on paper first | a question must point to an actionable investigation |
| Grow a Garden (Roblox) | cultivate a garden that becomes visibly yours | a player beside a conspicuous harvest in a personal plot | plant, grow, harvest, improve; change continues between visits | a readable plant-to-harvest cycle, meaningful variation, a place to display it | keep the action-to-result promise clear; waiting alone does not create value |
| Steal a Brainrot (Roblox) | acquire a conspicuous collection while risking it in social conflict | a player carrying a contested object while its owner chases | collector progression plus a player-driven theft decision | acquire, show ownership, steal, defend, recover in a compact social space | make the contested object readable; prototype newcomer protection and comebacks with the theft rule, not after |
| 99 Nights in the Forest (Roblox) | keep a fragile camp alive with friends while something watches from the forest | a lit camp inside an unreadable dark treeline | prepare, survive, recover, extend the group toward a named horizon | camp preparation, a readable shared threat, recovery | keep the preparation/threat contrast; test cooperation without assuming open chat |

## 4. Patterns across the cases (O, this kit's reading)
- **One verb or object, many jobs** (Splatoon ink, Minecraft blocks, Portal portals): the strongest concepts make one action or object do movement, conflict and expression. Ask of every case and pitch: which one object does several jobs here?
- **The first image is a before/after or a threat/safety contrast** (Stardew, Minecraft, 99 Nights, Vampire Survivors).
- **The twist is a rule about consequence**, not a new theme noun (Steal a Brainrot's theft, Hades' story on retry, Slay the Spire's deck changing the next decision).
- **Social consequence makes moments shareable** (Among Us, Steal a Brainrot, 99 Nights): something another player did to you, for you or with you.
