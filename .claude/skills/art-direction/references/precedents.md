Sources: `knowledge/reference/art-direction/01` (precedents), `02` §1, §4, `03` §2, `04` §2-3; checked in `fact-check-studios.md` (FC-S, 2026-10-21; corrections applied). ## 0. How to cite a precedent
| Provenance | Means | You may write |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **S** studio-published | the studio's own page, patch note, guide or announcement | "Valve's update says ..." |
| **D** developer or artist account | an interview, dev blog or post about their own process | "a Riot skins lead describes ..." |
| **P** the paper's reading | the research paper's interpretation or transfer | "a proposed check: ..." — never "Valve's rule" or "Riot requires" |
Copy relationships, never looks: no franchise characters, silhouettes, UI or logos (`platform-policy`). A number from a precedent stays in its game, engine and year.

## 1. Deadlock (Valve) — a world grammar, not a rendering recipe
**What Valve published (S, checked FC-S):**
- *City Never Sleeps* (2026-09-29): an occult New York with named lanes and four districts (Theater, Chinatown, Haunted Lot, Plaza); neutral creatures redesigned as "Haunts"; landmarks such as an occult university and Wall Street warlocks. Presentation changed with it: new health bars and status display, the parry cooldown shown near the reticle, and an "Enemy UI Color" accessibility setting. In the Sunken Plaza, outside sounds "fade away" as stamina drains (place, sound and gameplay tied together).
- *Old Gods, New Blood* (2026-01-22): the two Patrons are described through how each relates to the same city — one through skyscraper shadow, boarded housing and the city's cracks, the other through glass, steel, stone, order and power.
- Hero introductions (2026-10): a pigeon-lady hero whose bench becomes mobile in combat (signature object shaping silhouette, movement and sound); a chess-themed hero whose abilities map to piece roles (bishop advances and plants damage, knight protects or retreats, rook charges and stalls, queen locks down); a painter hero whose abilities are named for art terms.
- In the same post Valve says polish such as updating older hero art comes after core work, and some systems simply have not been reached yet (D, Valve's own account of priorities).
- None found: a public Deadlock art bible, palette count, value bands, silhouette size, review quota or phone budget (search as of 2026-10-21, not proof of absence).

**Lessons (P, proposed checks):**
| Check | From |
| --- | --- |
| Write one causal world rule (familiar institutions changed by one premise) and require every craft to show what it changes | the district and creature redesign |
| Give each district or faction a navigational identity and a gameplay reason: landmark silhouette, material family, spatial function, plus VFX and sound state cues; test the landmark at the phone camera with the HUD | the districts, Sunken Plaza |
| Share construction rules across factions; separate them by how they relate to the world (upkeep, massing, surface, sound, ceremony) — these sub-rules are extrapolations, not Valve's | the Patrons |
| Give a hero one signature object whose identity survives with surface detail stripped; check names, icons, animation accents and roles tell one story | the bench, the chess mapping |
| Keep a family resemblance across an ability set but distinct state and danger cues; review overlaps at gameplay scale on a phone | the painter's abilities |
| Track asset status (current target / approved legacy / placeholder / scheduled migration); never turn a placeholder into an anchor; mixed fidelity is not missing direction | Valve's priority note |
**Do not copy:** an unverified internal rule, a shader recipe, a fan concept, or desktop-MOBA density unchanged on phones.

## 2. League of Legends (Riot) — clarity as a system of priorities
**What Riot said (D unless marked; checked FC-S):**
- *Clarity in League* (2021-03-12): clarity means communicating gameplay, keeping the importance hierarchy and limiting noise. A champion keeps an identity silhouette feature, shows facing and suggests its power source. Attention an ability grabs should match its importance, ranked by damage, crowd control, dodgeability and how it changes the opponent's play. Riot's example of a telegraph that promises more dodging than is real is the Arcane Comet rune. Animation can carry much of a silhouette (Lee Sin), which then constrains skins. Skin clarity is one "bucket" that animation, VFX and SFX fill; Riot will "never turn everything up to 11" and aims for clarity parity with the base skin.
- *Ask Riot: Let's Talk Clarity* (2021-04-09): one robot skin kept hands, head and shoulder shapes, so it could change the walk; another had already changed hands and shoulders, leaving less room for motion change. Riot notes that earlier decision predated the clearer framework.
- *Defining the Rift's Visual Style* (2014, read via a third-party reprint): a painterly but graphic forest; layers ranked by contrast and visibility — background, characters, effects, UI; the map should clarify game space, not clutter it. A 2026 patch added a differently themed map variant, so a 2014 style description does not cover every current map (S, patch notes).
- *A Story of Fog and War* (2016): the map update could not raise the minimum hardware spec, and fog of war that was fine on the old map looked poor against the new art (engineering account).
- Splash and chroma practice (2018-2019 accounts): thumbnails resolve composition, light, anatomy and value before rendering; a chroma set is "generally" 8-10 designs; one splash took 4-6 weeks with daily team review. Historical, human, one kind of work — not an AI time estimate or quota.
- VALORANT (2020): environment, level, lighting and QA work together; critical geometry is kept; materials avoid excess contrast and darkness where characters must read; decoration mostly above player height; quality-level parity for competitive integrity. Its 144 FPS / 6.9 ms figures are that game's budget.
- TFT *K.O. Coliseum* retrospective (2025): the team's first full style pass; some players read the look as a different genre than intended (S, team retrospective).
- Wild Rift (2022): a champion kept its fantasy while a hard-to-use mobile interaction was redesigned.
- Teemo update (2024): inconsistent costume parts reconciled while chosen older charm was kept; shared base geometry; updated base effect colors to separate abilities.
- Arcane (Fortiche): Piltover draws on Art Deco, Zaun on Art Nouveau, Mucha as the main reference (S, studio blog).

**Lessons (P):**
| Check | From |
| --- | --- |
| Before approving any effect, write a priority contract: what the player must recognize, which event matters now, what survives cosmetic change, which craft owns each cue | clarity priorities |
| For every character or tool: the identity feature, the facing cue, the power-source cue | champion identity |
| Rank each effect by consequence and make its screen attention match; fix a telegraph that over-promises a dodge in the picture, not the rule | attention = importance |
| One clarity budget per variant across model, motion, VFX, sound; name the cues spent and the cues that must stay; compare base and variant at gameplay scale | the skin bucket, the two robot skins |
| Define a common stage for the whole cast: quiet fighting surfaces, deliberate landmarks, room for effects, one material and edge grammar; derive the layer order from the current interaction, not as a law | the Rift |
| When the art changes, re-check neighbor systems (fog, UI, effects) and state the hardware floor first | fog of war |
| Ask reviewers what genre, mood and role an image shows without telling them the intended label | TFT |
| Keep fantasy and recognition locks across platforms; redesign controls, framing and density for the real interaction | Wild Rift |
**Do not copy:** every craft at maximum spectacle; the 2014 layer order as an absolute; VALORANT or League performance numbers; Arcane's surfaces as a game look.

## 3. Team Fortress 2 and Dota 2 (Valve)
- TF2 rendering paper (2007, S + D): the nine classes were checked as silhouettes with no internal shading; proportions, gear and clothing tell them apart; high-frequency detail is cut; shadows are cool, not black; rim light stresses the silhouette; the two teams' buildings contrast warm/natural/angular with cool/industrial/orthogonal. The Half-Lambert numbers are renderer settings, never Roblox values. TF2's alpine maps widened the palette without making the characters feel out of place (D, 2008).
- Dota 2 Workshop character art guide (S, guide for community item makers): the gameplay view outranks the loadout view; silhouette carries identity and orientation; changed silhouettes are fine if identity stays legible; darker lower body to lighter upper body is a "generally" default, not a rule; rest areas balance detail; judge in game context.
- Lessons (P): an untextured silhouette contact sheet from gameplay views at one camera and scale, labeling confusions; exploration that answers a product question and logs what was rejected and why; a new biome reviewed with existing characters, effects and UI; next to each style rule, its camera, background samples, an approved exception, a failed example and a pass criterion.
- Do not copy: a three-color cap, a universal light-top rule, approval from close-ups only.

## 4. Other studios (D unless marked; lesson = P)
| Case | What they said | Lesson | Do not copy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Overwatch concept art (Blizzard) | a "Shield Guy" concept piled up gear without a clear role; its beam "didn't feel right in playtests"; skins keep recognition and must fit animation | solve the gameplay role and recognition before decoration; identity contract before skins | their costumes; a hero pipeline for every avatar system |
| Hades (Supergiant, 2021) | design-led team; tone moved from painterly to pen-and-ink; an early sketch and a color script anchored the journey | a compact color script for the whole journey; keep "why this look" | dense linework as premium; their asset counts as targets |
| Splatoon 3 (Nintendo, 2022) | props and weapons derived from the residents' culture; "look like a Splatoon weapon, without making it look like a firearm" | a short culture generator: what residents value, make, repair, collect | their ink, type or weapon forms |
| Super Mario Bros. Wonder (Nintendo, 2023) | the model was shaped for what the player sees; sound timed to the visible action; direction converged late after wide exploration | design for the real camera; sync sound and effects to the visible action | camera cheats on a free camera; late convergence as an excuse |
| Fortnite Chapter 4 (Epic, 2023) | Lumen made interiors too dark; artists raised indirect light on the sun for warmth and used Skylight Leaking and Local Exposure (tools Epic's engineers added) | target renders must show playable space; record deliberate departures from realism | Unreal settings or budgets in Roblox |
| Sea of Thieves Ravenwood (Rare, 2023) | a shared iridescence exposed material differences; artists compared assets under in-game light | approve outsourced or AI items beside related assets in a real scene; give one accepted material and silhouette, not adjectives | cosmetic-set complexity for a whole game |
| Brawl Stars logo (Supercell, 2025) | an evolution, not a rebrand; kept skull, colors and lettering; no test numbers published | review icon, thumbnail, title, loading and UI as one family at real sizes; name what must survive | a conversion promise |
| Luca press kit (Pixar, 2021) | characters get the highest contrast; buildings lean and sag for age and history | deformation expresses construction or age, never a random filter; check the lit color | film framing in a player-controlled camera |
| James Gurney (artist blog) | gamut masking limits the colors in a picture | a dominant gamut per area; reserve out-of-gamut colors for emphasis | "nine swatches" as a palette limit |
