# Worked examples

## 1. A new hub: the mining town of an ore simulator (third-person, phones, 1-12 players)
- **Intake.** The game designer's sheet: job "sell ore, upgrade the pick, find the next mine"; route table spawn → sell stall 6 s, stall → mine gate 9 s; read rank: mine gate, sell stall, upgrade shop. The art bible has no environment band yet: I use the starting band (`PROPOSAL`) and ask the art director. Reserved: red (danger), gold (rare ore).
- **Read plan.** Personality "busy, dusty, proud, frontier". Story facts: miners live above the shops; a cave-in closed the old shaft; dry wind from the north. Smallest gameplay object: a dropped ore chunk on cobble, dirt and planks.
- **Options** (3 images, one round): A a timber gate in a collapsed mine face, B a carved temple door, C a rail yard with the gate at its end. B is the unexpected one: beautiful, but it says "ancient ruin", not "frontier". I recommend A: the boarded old shaft beside it shows the cave-in fact, and its frame is the tallest shape from spawn. C lost because its rails pull the eye to the yard, not the gate; I keep its rail tracks as a theme cue.
- **Grey pass.** On the greybox I compare the sun at 10:00 and at 15:00. At 15:00 the gate face catches the sun and ranks first by median luminance among gate, stall and shops (RD2, VB3); at 10:00 the stall wins. 15:00 goes into the look sheet. The top-down shows a dead corner behind the smithy: it becomes the sell trip's shortcut, agreed with the game designer.
- **Floor check** (FL1). The ore chunk is the same value as the mid-grey cobble: on a synthetic capture of that case the tight-box value ratio at phone size is 1.0, so it reads by hue only. Darkening the cobble a step (still inside the band) gives 2.2. That is below the 3:1 starting target, so I ask the game designer for the chunk's white rim; with the rim confirmed the line passes, and both numbers go in the packet.
- **Hand-off.** The modeler gets the gate brief with the personality words, the two story facts it carries, the band and reserved hues, and a request for the in-set capture back; the sound designer gets the footstep table (cobble, dirt, plank) and "dry wind, far pickaxes, one signature: the mine bell".

## 2. A lava obby stage (third-person, phones; red is the game's danger colour)
- **Hazard sheet** from the game designer: lava kills on touch; the drawn edge is the kill volume.
- **Hazard explains itself** (RD8, VB13): lava wells up from a cracked basalt vent at the far end and is held by a dark crust rim; the stone next to it stays plain. Its glow is tamed toward orange and its emission lowered until a dummy standing in front of it reaches the character separation target (M6, light and dark dummy each).
- **Player band neutral, saturation on the focus** (VB5, VB6): the walls run from neutral grey-brown at the floor to a warm terracotta above head height. On a synthetic capture with this split, the colour script (M3) reports a mean chroma of about 35 above head height and about 13 in the player band, and with the vent glow boxed apart no saturated pixels in the environment boxes; the same upper walls in a loud orange (chroma about 75) put 35 % of the environment boxes over the saturated limit and strong colour behind every jumping player.
- **Reserved red** (VB4): banners and lanterns are ochre and teal, never red; in the environment boxes, lava excluded, the danger-red share is 0, both for the authored red and for the red as the lava renders it (sampled from the capture as a second swatch).
- **Motion** (AM1, AM3): heat shimmer is slow, low and kept away from the jump sight lines; the eruption flash has an off path and stays under 3 flashes a second.
- **Lowest setting** (QV1): with shadows gone the jump pads still read by their lighter value and square shape.

## 3. A fix: "the haunted village feels dead"
- **Cause first.** Story: no facts were written, so nothing tells who lived here. Flat fills: the lit side of each long wall measures under both flat limits (`hue_spread` about 3, `value_spread` about 0.01: VB12). Repetition: the same window frame at the same angle four times in the first view (FL3).
- **Read plan added.** Facts: a fishing village, left in a hurry (nets still on racks, a table set for dinner), a flood twenty years ago (water line on the walls, silt in the doorways). Personality "damp, abandoned, quiet, uneasy".
- **Fix, one problem per pass.** Pass 1: the water line and silt across every building (a story fact that also breaks the flat walls: a darker band below the line, green-brown silt stains in patches inside it, cooler grey damp patches above it; hue shifts, because patches that are only darker leave the wall monochrome). Pass 2: the nets, racks and the dinner table as story clusters at the two stop points, nowhere on the path. Pass 3: window frames rotated, swapped for the second variant or grouped differently.
- **Retest what it touches.** The silt raised the floor value near doorways: the floor check (FL1) is re-run there, and the doorway decision views (RD4) are re-taken from the same cameras; VB12 is re-measured on each wall's lit side, one box above and one below the water line (a box across the line would read the line itself as variation), and each now has its `hue_spread` above the flat limit (on a synthetic wall with these patches about 35; darker patches in the wall's own hue read about 3 and fail as monochrome). The art director names "flooded fishing village" at the check-in (FL5, FL8).
