# Light and air: what they must do

Light and atmosphere do two jobs at once: they keep the place **readable** for play and they set the **mood** of the art target. When the two fight, readability wins and the conflict goes to the art director.

## 1. Light
| ID | Practice | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LA-01 | **Light is composition.** It draws the eye to the focal point and the next route choice. | RU06 O; G04 O |
| LA-02 | **Light supports a route; it never creates one.** A mandatory route, hazard or exit stays readable when optional light, shadows or post effects are gone (low quality, weak phone). Light makes it nicer; shapes make it possible. | L07 O (from P03 V) |
| LA-03 | **Leading light must end somewhere useful.** Lamps that lead to the exit help; an equally strong row that leads to a dead wall competes. A bright cave mouth pulls the eye and can hide what is behind it; whether that is right is the game designer's pacing call. | G04 O |
| LA-04 | **Every light has a visible reason.** Outdoors: sky light from above plus the sun; a torch, screen or lava pool motivates its own light. Match the light's shape to its fixture (a lamp radiates, a spot aims, a window face spills). Shadows across characters and props agree. Record each light's position, range and shadow state in the scene manifest. | L09 O; L08 V; J018 |
| LA-05 | **Interiors must not go too dark.** Dark interiors and big exposure jumps at doors are common failures when lighting gets more physical. Prefer cheap global controls (ambient, environment light, exposure) before many shadowed local lights. | K01-b E; K07-a E (Epic) |
| LA-06 | **Accept lighting across every state, not one sunny angle.** List the outdoor-to-indoor thresholds, doors, time of day, weather and events that change readability, and capture each one. | K07-c O |
| LA-07 | **Review at the lowest and highest graphics setting.** Quality level changes draw distance, shadows, textures, particles and post effects, and the exact behavior depends on the device. Compare key views at the lowest and highest setting, and on a real phone; one editor beauty shot proves nothing. The high setting must not be noisier in the player band. (QV1) | L06 V; RU06 O; PF11 V; DN09 O; J018 |
| LA-08 | **Pick the lighting style from the art target, then test it.** Neither the soft style nor the realistic style is automatically "the phone setting". Approve the value structure and silhouettes first; spend on costly shadow detail after. | L10 O |
| LA-09 | **No light-count rule exists.** No source gives a universal number of lights or shadow casters for phones. Approve the look and the degraded readability; budget lights from a phone measurement. | L13 O; BT06 U; LF31 |
| LA-15 | **One key light direction per place, chosen early, never moved late.** On the greybox, in grey, compare 2-3 sun positions (time of day and latitude) and keep the one whose numbers win: the lightest area on the objective, readable cast shadows, no key position in deep shadow. Record it in the look sheet; every review render and capture uses it; moving it later means re-judging every view. (VB8) | J018 |
| LA-16 | **The lightest area is the focus.** Put the lighter values and the strongest local light on the goal, door, pickup or path; let the ground darken slightly toward the edges and lower the ambient elsewhere. Even, flat light leads nowhere. (VB3) | J018 |
| LA-17 | **Light and shadow get opposite temperatures.** Warm light with cool shadows, or cool light with warm shadows: one direction per scene, set in the art bible. Tint the shadow side through the ambients instead of leaving them neutral grey, so the shadows keep colour instead of going muddy. (VB8) | J018 |
| LA-18 | **Shadows are tuned for readability.** No key position (where a player stands to fight, aim or choose) falls into deep shadow where a character could hide by accident; re-check after every light change. The read never depends on shadows: they switch off at low quality. (VB9) | J018 |
| LA-19 | **Contrast and light direction follow intensity.** Boss rooms, reveals and climaxes use the widest value range the band allows; hubs and calm areas use a narrower one. A threatening place may be lit from below by a local source it has (lava, braziers); a safe or sacred one from above. (VB7) | J018 |

## 2. Atmosphere, fog, sky, post effects
| ID | Practice | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LA-10 | **Depth haze is not information fog.** Haze may suggest distance and mood; it must never hide something the player needs (a route, an enemy, an edge, a hazard). A true gameplay boundary stays crisp even when the air is soft. Check: with the final Atmosphere on, each route cue, edge and hazard still separates in its decision view (VB7). | RU07 O; K02-b E; K02-d O |
| LA-11 | **Sky, atmosphere and clouds are one palette and one horizon.** Compose them together. Cloud cover does not mean rain, wind audio or a weather state unless the shared environment state says so. | A05 O |
| LA-12 | **Post effects are seasoning.** They can change or vanish at low quality, and they affect everyone unless they are per camera. The art direction must survive without them: a key view re-taken with bloom and colour correction off still reads. (VB10) | A08 V; AS3 V+O; J018 |
| LA-13 | **Tutorial values are experiments, not presets.** Official samples (a cool morning, a warm evening, a hazy cabin) worked in one scene with its own geometry and sky. Start from them if useful, re-derive each value against the art bible, and check on a phone. Keep `source_recipe` apart from `project_approved_settings` in the manifest. | A09 V/O; A11 O; W06 O |
| LA-14 | **No biome presets exist.** There are no validated fog, light or post values for "mountain", "swamp", "cave" or "storm". They come from this stage's geometry, navigation needs and art target. | A12 U; RC5 U |
| LA-20 | **Distance lowers contrast and detail.** Air and simpler far models drop the contrast of the background so the near and middle ground, where play happens, stays sharp; the landmark is the exception that must still win. Far life (a distant crowd, birds, traffic on a far road) stays small and low in contrast: it adds activity without competing. Check: the far band's luminance spread is narrower than the near band's, with the landmark measured apart (VB7). | J018 |
| LA-21 | **Three depth planes.** A darker foreground element may frame a view (never between the player and gameplay), the middle ground carries the play and most of the care, the background takes global changes for unity. | J018 |

## 3. Diagnosing a lighting problem (classify before fixing)
**Who decides what:** the art director sets the look target, the value bands and the reserved colours; the environment designer owns the lighting craft that reaches them. When the art director proposes a technique ("add more lights"), ask for the target it serves (what should read first, what mood) and answer with captures and the cause; pick the technique yourself. A real conflict goes to the owner.

When a stage drifts from the target, decide which layer is wrong before changing anything: wrong large shape, right shape badly framed, wrong value hierarchy, inconsistent material response, or a different camera, exposure or graphics tier. Fixing the wrong layer breeds compensating errors (a darker texture hides an over-strong fill light and ruins the material elsewhere) (CO07 O).

## 4. The environment's value and colour budget
The screen has layers: UI and gameplay effects, then characters, then the environment. The layer with the widest value range wins attention, so the environment keeps the narrowest one. The bands, reserved colours and palette roles are the art director's (art bible); until they exist, use the starting values below, marked `PROPOSAL`, and ask.
| ID | Practice | Check (review line) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VB-a | **Value band per layer.** Starting values: the value-band row of `art-review/references/art-bible.md` §7, the kit's one copy (`PROPOSAL` until the game's bible sets them; a check may change them); `look_check.py` reports WCAG luminance, so read the band in luminance (`environment-craft/references/measure-captures.md` §0). No pure black anywhere in the environment; no large white surface. Tune materials and lighting, not the capture. **Separation targets** (subject/ring median ratio, `environment-craft/references/measure-captures.md` §0; 1.0 = no separation): the starting values are the separation-targets row of `art-review/references/art-bible.md` §7 (`PROPOSAL` until the game's bible sets them): the **character target**, a character or dummy against its environment ring; the **marker target**, a critical marker or the smallest gameplay object against its floor. | environment boxes p5/p95 inside the band; near-black share ≈ 0 (VB1); characters reach the character target (VB2, VB9) |
| VB-b | **Focus by lowering everything else first.** The space between focal zones gets fewer details and lower contrast; background detail stays in values close to the background's own. Only then, if still needed, raise the focus. Bright marks (lamps, emissive strips, light decals) sit in deliberate groups near the focus, never a dense shimmering row or a lone outlier far from it. | focal region has the widest spread and the top median among named regions (VB3) |
| VB-c | **Reserved colours stay out of decoration.** The art bible's gameplay hues (danger, enemy, team, route, interactable, rare), the same reserved-colour table that UI and gameplay effects use, are not used on set dressing; every reserved colour also has a shape or value cue for colour-blind players. A hazard that must be red or bright is tamed: shift red toward orange, lower its emission, until a character in front of it still reaches the character target. | reserved-hue pixel share in environment boxes, gameplay objects excluded, ≈ 0 (VB4); character in front of the hazard reaches the target (VB13) |
| VB-d | **The player band stays neutral.** From the floor to head height surfaces stay low in saturation; stronger colour sits higher up (a wall running from neutral beige at the floor to a warm terracotta above head height: warm, not loud). Large areas (ground, sky, far crowds) a little desaturated; small saturated pops only on focal spots, pickups and a few accents along the eye path, never outshining the focus. | player-band chroma below the band above it, or under the neutral limit (VB5); saturated pixels outside the focal elements and eye-path accents under the stray limit (VB6) |
| VB-e | **Colour with life, inside the family.** The scene has a dominant theme hue on its large areas, support colours on the middle ones and a small accent; a new material takes its colour from the palette roles already in the place, and a piece or material reused from another stage is recoloured to this stage's palette and mood. No flat single-colour fills on large surfaces: small hue and value shifts in patches, and a value texture alone is still one hue (a built-in material under one part Color reads monochrome): vary the Color of neighbouring parts a few degrees of hue, use a MaterialVariant or SurfaceAppearance ColorMap, and on terrain paint patches of two or three materials whose MaterialColors differ in hue; shadow areas shift hue (not only darken) through tinted ambients; gold and warm metals stay saturated in shadow. | each large surface's `hue_spread` above its flat limit on its lit side, value texture alone failing as monochrome; metal keeps its chroma in shadow (VB12); every zoo material near a palette role (VB11) |
| VB-f | **Materials and light are chosen together.** Every key material is tested under the stage's final light, not default light, in a material zoo (one sample block per material, in the test place, beside reference blocks of the palette roles, reserved hues and a mid grey, so the light's own tint is not read as the material's colour), and checked against the band before it is used. | zoo capture: every material inside the band and saturation range (VB11) |
| VB-g | **Rebalance after every colour, light or effect change.** A balanced grey pass does not stay balanced after colour: re-run the focus check on the full capture after each change; check the result on a phone-size view before it ships. | focus and band lines re-run after the change (VB3, QV2) |
