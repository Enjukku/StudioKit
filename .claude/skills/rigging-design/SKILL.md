# Rigging design: what to rig, why, and where the cuts go

## 1. When this skill starts
At rig-pipeline S2, with the animator's §B needs and the model or reference set in hand. It answers: what the rig must make possible, which route per region, where joints and pivots go, which controls the read needs, which parts must be separate and why, what each cut must hide through, and what is tested before release. `rigging-craft` answers how to build and test it in Blender and Roblox.

**Governing principle [O] (RP:12):** keep the approved silhouette and contacts across the approved motion envelope with the simplest exportable articulation that passes the in-engine tests. A generated mesh, auto segmentation, auto weights or a clean import is a candidate, never an acceptance.

**A rig is good when three things hold [O]:** (1) every pose the animator needs is reachable with no seam, gap, stretch, collision or body skew; (2) in those poses the character still reads at the game camera: limbs, hands, head and feet separate, identity anchors and hook shapes visible from the side the camera sees; (3) it is the simplest rig that passes in Roblox. A rig that bends cleanly but cannot show the wind-up, the head held through a spin or the hand shape the action needs has failed the animation before it starts.

**Design for motion comes before final topology:** most rig failures are designed in (mass on a joint, a drape over the arm, a cape nobody costed, a mouth modelled shut); catch them at S2, while a change costs a sentence, not a re-cut (§3 step 4).

**After a context compaction**, re-read §8 and §9 of this file before you hand on a plan: only the start of a skill survives compaction.

## 3. Steps (S2: from needs to a rig plan)
1. **Classify the asset and its read** [O] (RP:87): platform avatar body (R15 contract, Roblox template and validator are the authority) / in-experience custom player / NPC / pet / weapon / vehicle / prop. Renaming bones does not make a custom creature an R15 avatar. From the brief, write the role it must read as (tank, assassin, friendly pet, boss), its movement pattern (hop, dash-and-stop, shamble, stomp, glide) and the game camera with its typical distance: these decide where articulation is spent. Check: §A purpose filled; role, pattern and camera written.
2. **Read the needs.** Every §B record: driver (active / passive / rigid / mechanical), ROM with unit and the pose that needs it, contacts, own-motion parts, stress poses, sockets. Then the standard needs of rig-pipeline K1 (shoulders and hips, the head through a spin, hands, hook shapes, costume near joints in the stress poses), each answered by the animator as needed with its pose or "not needed". Missing or unsourced → K1 fails, ask the animator. Check: K1 recorded, standard needs answered.
3. **Inspect the geometry and the concept before deciding** [O] (RP:168): objects, mesh data, connected parts, open edges, intersecting shells, UV islands, materials, floating bits, triangles. One object is not one connected surface; one visibly separate sleeve can be welded to the torso (rule 06). Note where painted texture fakes structure (a drawn mouth has no mouth cavity, RP:327). Read the concept's intent, not its strokes: what each region really is (a gold plate is rigid, shiny cloth bends, fur bends), what holds each costume piece on, what is under a coat or behind the back. Decide each ambiguity that changes the rig, or ask the art director in one note. Check: defect map with views (back included); a material-and-attachment line per moving region.
4. **Screen the design for motion** (`references/design-for-motion.md` §1) on the reference set or blockout against the §B stress poses. Write the findings as a `design_for_motion` list in your rig sheet keys (§C): each with its owner, the fix asked and the pose that shows it. Each goes to the modeler in the S3 brief, or to the art director (a look change) through the lead. Check: the screen is written; every finding has an owner.
5. **Pick a route per region, not per asset** (§4 and `references/region-routes.md`): R-cut, R-skin, R-mix or R-reject, with the reason that names the §B need and the geometry fact. What looks rigid moves rigidly; what looks soft bends. When two routes are close, pose both as proxies at the worst §B pose from the game camera (one black-fill per option); if both pass, pick the cheaper; if the choice changes the look, the owner picks. Check: every moving region has a pivot or a deformation plan.
6. **Place joints and plan the controls** [O] (RP:38, 115): joints from landmarks and the needed rotations, never at a bounding-box centre; head pivot at the neck base, not the skull centre (RP:524). Record pivot coordinates and space, axes, ROM limits from §B. Plan the read controls for every standard need marked "needed" in step 2 (`references/design-for-motion.md` §2), left and right independent, with the rotation mode of each spin-heavy control. An invented creature's joints are placed from a named real skeleton per part (`references/creatures-mechanisms-faces.md` §1). Check: rotation preview of each joint looks right from side and front; the control list names each read need it serves.
7. **Write the cut list** for R-cut parts: where, why (which need), what hides it (collar, sleeve, cuff, glove, tuft, crease, socket, overlap, or an art-directed gap), and the extremes it must stay hidden through. Write **bend zones** for R-skin parts: which loops are needed where, which regions are protected (must not move) up to where the planned transition starts. Mark both on renders for the modeler, back and game-camera views included. Check: every cut and zone ties to a §B row.
8. **Route every push past real** (`references/design-for-motion.md` §3): for each squash, stretch, smear or oversized hold the art bible or §B asks for, name the Roblox route (a keyframe pose stores only a CFrame: no scale in clips). Check: no push relies on clip scale.
9. **Set budgets with sources** (rig-pipeline §6): platform limits fixed; everything else a game budget or PROPOSAL until measured on the target phone. Count costly chains against the budget. Check: §E has no number without a source.
10. **Pose the plan at the game camera** (`references/design-for-motion.md` §4): the hardest 2-3 §B stress poses and the signature pose on a proxy (one bone per part on the blockout; at the character proof this is the proxy pose the recipe asks for): numbers first (non-adjacent overlaps, cap exposure, black-fill components), then the black-fills for the read. Image budget: §8 line P-11. When no mesh exists yet at S2 (the character recipe plans from the reference set), run this at the proof on the modeler's blockout, after K2, and send any finding as a plan revision (bump the sheet revision; the animator re-signs only the changed rows). Inside a saved job workflow the proof stage owns only `proof/`: write the finding in the proof hand-back for the lead, who opens the revision. Check: P-11 recorded, or marked "at the proof".
11. **Answer every §B record** (supported / with limit / not supported + alternatives) and send the plan to the animator for K2. A limit that changes the look goes to the owner with both options rendered. Then brief the modeler (`rig-pipeline/references/modeler-handoff.md`) with the cut list, bend zones and the design-for-motion findings.

## 4. Craft core: what separates great rigs from weak ones
| ID | Practice | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D-01 | Judge a rig in motion, not in the bind pose: shoulders keep mass, elbows bend without imploding, hips allow locomotion and crouch, a neck turn does not drag the chest, rigid gear keeps its shape; and the pose still reads as black-fill at the game camera | [O] RP:36 |
| D-02 | Controls are judged by reset, local axes, IK/FK continuity, space switching and contact holding, not by control count; complexity is only worth it if it makes the needed poses easier. Offer the read controls (D-17 to D-19) by default for characters; everything else only when the clip list needs it | [O] RP:36, 12 |
| D-03 | Animation intent changes rig features: prototype a requested motion on a minimal chain, add only the controls it needs, bake secondary motion onto export joints when it must play identically | [V] (RP:42); [O] transfer RP:44 |
| D-04 | Route per joint, both methods in one character: rigid separate pieces where a cover hides the cut or the part is hard; skinning where there is no cover and the surface must bend continuously | [O] rule 05, RP:196; owner J005-D9 |
| D-05 | Rigid piece = 100% weight on one bone (or its own Motor6D part); a skinned region gets localized weights; a rigid element can be its own MeshPart (physics, removal, damage) or geometry weighted fully to one bone (visual only) | [O] RP:79, 269 |
| D-06 | Fix topology and joint placement before weights. Weights cannot repair a fused bridge, a wrong pivot or a wrong parent | [O] rule 08, RP:186, 275; [E] FG01 |
| D-07 | Hide a rigid cut with overlap, socket, collar, sleeve or an approved gap, sized from the motion envelope (not a fixed fraction of limb size); then sweep every extreme from the review cameras | [O] rule 07, RP:178; [E] FG11 |
| D-08 | A flat cut across exposed organic skin is a design change, not an invisible repair: either a cover exists, or the region is skinned, or the owner approves a visible break | [O] RP:172 |
| D-09 | Protected regions: name the vertices that must stay still in a head-only or hand-only test, up to where the planned transition starts; measure their largest move. A smooth gradient can still be wrong; a tiny remote weight moves the mesh, and a protected group that stops short of it measures zero | [O] RP:273; studio test 2026-10-22 (Blender 5.1.2, `rig_audit.py`) |
| D-10 | Combined stress poses, not single-axis sweeps: arm raised + forearm twist, head turn + torso bend, hip flex + leg spread, tail curve + base rotation; and costume near joints (collar, pauldron, hair at the nape) in the same poses | [O] RP:285 |
| D-11 | The Roblox result is authoritative. Blender-only deformers (Preserve Volume, drivers, corrective shape keys, IK, spline constraints) do not run in Roblox; bake to supported bones and weights, or treat as film-only | [V] FBX does not carry constraints (RB fact-check); [O] RP:281; [E] FG12 |
| D-12 | Separate visual articulation from physics: bone motion moves the picture, not the collision shape; give physics its own proxies; never drive one degree of freedom from physics, Animator and a script at once without an owner | [V] RR12; [O] RP:310, do-not 18 |
| D-13 | Freeze the motion envelope and asset role before final skinning; later topology, joint, name or rest-pose changes are a versioned migration with retests | [O] RP:119 |
| D-14 | Spend articulation where it reads at the game camera and clump it: head, hands, the hook shape and the source of power get the joints; large calm regions stay rigid. A small pet may need only a bobbing body and a turning head; choose by camera test and measured crowd cost, not a universal bone count | [O] RP:298, 203 |
| D-15 | A missing number is a result, not a gap to fill: no invented bone cap, joint angle, loop count, tolerance or phone budget | [O] do-not 4; [V] RR31 (no documented custom-rig cap found 2026-10-21) |
| D-16 | Design for motion before final topology: the screen of `references/design-for-motion.md` §1, every finding with an owner | [O] |
| D-17 | Shoulders and hips carry a wind-up's energy: clavicle (or upper-torso) and hip controls with the range the action needs (`references/design-for-motion.md` §2) | [O]; [V] among R15 bodies only advanced R15 has clavicles; a custom rig may add them (`characters/index.md`) |
| D-18 | The head can hold its world orientation through a spin: a world-orient switch that moves nothing at rest and survives the bake (`references/design-for-motion.md` §2) | [O]; studio test 2026-10-22 (a visual-key bake keeps it through FBX re-import, a default bake loses it) |
| D-19 | Every hook-shape part gets its own control; a chain is driven as one mass, then offset (`references/design-for-motion.md` §2) | [O] |
| D-20 | Exaggeration only through Roblox-safe routes: a keyframe pose stores only a CFrame, so squash, stretch and smears are a mesh swap, a code-scaled separate part, a VFX layer or a smear mesh; bone scale only after a Studio probe; extremes are spent on joints that can take them | [V] `_api-reference/classes/Pose.yaml` (Pose.CFrame); `Bone.yaml` (Transform is a CFrame) |
| D-21 | One convention across the game's rigs: the same names, axes, side convention, control names and socket names for the same jobs, so shared clips, tools and the animator's habits transfer | [O] |
| D-22 | Left and right stay independent: no control forces symmetric poses or shared timing; mirroring is an authoring tool, never a constraint | [O] |

## 5. What the animation needs from the rig (summary; detail `references/motion-needs.md`)
| Need from §B | Rig consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Wind-ups and landings, spins, hands that must read, hook shapes on held moments | The read controls: `references/design-for-motion.md` §2 (D-17 to D-19) |
| Active appendage (balancing or steering tail, gripping tail, pointing ears) | Controls that drive it as a primary part while the body responds; no "always lags" setup [V] gecko tails B05 (AP:228) |
| Passive appendage (strap, floppy ear, tail tip) | Chain with enough segments and geometry to bend both ways from a neutral rest; key, procedural or hybrid decided per part; driven as one mass, then offset [O] RP:300, AP:508 |
| Wings | Name the type first (feathered, membranous, insect: different systems); wing, body and tail controllable together; left and right retract independently; fold and glide reachable without intersecting the body [V] B06-B10 (AP:234-240) |
| Extra legs | Explicit leg order, side convention, ground-contact map, no cross-limb IK target mix-ups; stance feet must hold in world space [O] RP:304; AP:250 |
| Held tool | Tool root, grip anchors (primary, secondary), impact surface, stable grip / muzzle frames; hand switching and reload detachment are hierarchy changes, not skin gradients; grip positions identical across variants [O] RP:306; AP:316-330 |
| Contacts (feet, hands, perch, hinge) | Pivots that allow planted, rolling and sliding states; IK feet that stay planted while the body moves; contact-hold test after export [O] RP:441; AP:209 |
| Face | Texture swap, skeletal face or mix chosen from shot size and needed transitions; dynamic heads follow the FACS mapping [V] RR15-RR17, < 50 face joints where possible RR34 |
| Squash and stretch, smears | Only what the rig and asset contract allow, by a Roblox route (D-20); check after export [O] AP:383 |

## 6. Roblox runtime choices
| Choice | Use when | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Skinned mesh with Bones | continuous bending surfaces; many visual joints without a part per bone | [V] RR11-RR12; [O] AP:829 |
| Separate MeshParts + Motor6D | rigid mechanical pieces, detachable or physical parts, doors, turrets, weapons | [V] Motor6D joins two parts animatably (RP:73); [O] RP:75 |
| Humanoid + Animator | player characters and NPCs that need Humanoid behaviour | [V] RR13, RR21 |
| AnimationController + Animator | creatures, pets, props without Humanoid needs; cheaper for static NPCs | [V] RR13 (use `Animator:LoadAnimation`, the controller's own method is deprecated); [V] Roblox Improve Performance (RP:772) |
| Avatar R15 / advanced R15 | only for platform avatar bodies: 15 named meshes, +Z forward, +Y up, I/A/T pose, own triangle budgets, up to 37 optional joints (Spine and Chest, LeftClavicle and RightClavicle, HeadBase, up to 15 finger joints per hand, toe bases) | [V] RR02, RR06, RR07; `characters/index.md` (advanced R15 rigs) |
| Standard R15 (15 joints) when the game uses the default body | no collarbones and no fingers: wind-up energy goes into UpperTorso and upper-arm range; hand reads come from bigger hand poses or a hand-mesh swap | [V] `characters/index.md` |
| Choose by measured A/B on the target phone, not folklore that bones or Motor6Ds are always cheaper | | [O] AP:829; [E] forum reports only |

## 7. Rules (final check)
- Max 4 influences per vertex; no weights on Root; ≤ 20,000 triangles per mesh; watertight (RR01-RR05).
- Never rely on a Blender-only deformer for anything that plays in Roblox (D-11).
- Never smooth weights globally to hide a wrong joint, wrong parent or fused topology (do-not 5).
- Never assume Separate by Loose Parts can split connected geometry (do-not 6).
- Never treat a semantic segment label or an auto-rig success as a finished joint (do-not 8, rule 12).
- Never move a pivot to hide a bad cut boundary (RP:524).
- Never invent a cap, angle, loop count, tolerance or budget; mark UNKNOWN and measure (D-15).
- Never approve a plan from the bind pose or a close render alone; the stress poses are judged at the game camera (D-01, P-11).
- Never key scale for a Roblox clip or promise a push that needs it (D-20).
- Never accept a costly chain (cape, long hair, loose cloth, tassel) without a written reason, a fallback and a count in the budget (D-16).
- A look change forced by a technical limit goes to the owner with both options rendered (rig-pipeline §1).

## 9. Core principles for this craft
The studio's universal principles, stated for rigging: the standard, an example, a check you can run, and where the depth sits.

| # | Principle, for my work | Example | Check | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The gameplay read comes first:** the rig must let every action read: the tell pose reachable, the source of power held clear, own-character moves readable at the key pose. A clean bend that blocks the read fails. | A boss slam needs the arms high over the head; the pauldron cut is planned so the raise reaches 160° without the pauldron hiding the head. | P-11: the signature and tell poses as black-fill at the game camera name the action; R-10 in Studio. | §1, §3 step 10 |
| 2 | **Judge it where players meet it:** stress poses are judged at the game camera, at phone size, from the side the camera sees (often the back), then in Studio in the real place. | A pet's tail curl judged from 12 studs behind and above, not from a front orthographic render. | P-11 images at the game camera; R-10 Studio captures; no approval from a Blender close-up alone. | §3 step 10; craft tests §6 |
| 3 | **Big before small:** routes and pivots on a proxy, posed at the game camera, before topology, weights, correctives and polish; fix the earliest broken layer first. | The proxy shows the collar blocking the head turn before the modeler builds the collar. | P-11 dated before the S3 brief; diagnosis debug order followed in every fix note. | §3 steps 5-10; `references/diagnosis.md` §1 |
| 4 | **Shape and silhouette carry identity:** in every stress pose limbs, hands, head and feet stay separable; anchors and hook shapes stay in the outline; spikes and plates stay rigid. | A ponytail gets its own control so its swoop shows in the jump's apex pose. | Black-fill component count and box (P-11, G-05); anchors listed and seen in the key images. | D-19; `references/design-for-motion.md` §4 |
| 5 | **Less is more:** the fewest joints and controls that give the needed poses; articulation clumped where it reads, calm regions rigid. | A low-detail pet: body bob, head turn, two ears, one tail control; no spine chain. | Every joint and control in the plan names the §B need it serves (P-07); none without one. | D-02, D-14 |
| 6 | **One code for meaning:** one naming, axis, side and socket convention across the game's rigs; the same control means the same thing on every character. | `Grip_R` is the primary grip socket on every humanoid. | R-04: names, axes, sides and sockets diffed against the game's earlier rig manifests: no conflicting use. | D-21 |
| 7 | **Push what must read, on a believable base:** extremes go where the anatomy can take them and through a Roblox route; contacts and weight stay true around them. | A cartoon landing squash is a swap mesh for two frames, not scaled bones. | P-09: every push named with its route; R-09 finds no scale key other than 1 in any export clip. | D-20; `references/design-for-motion.md` §3 |
| 8 | **The subject's character drives every choice:** the role, the movement pattern and the feel words decide where joints and range go. | A heavy brute gets deep hip and knee range for a low stance and a wide clavicle range for shrugs; a darting assassin gets a long spine twist. | P-01 and P-02: each control's range traces to a feel word, pattern or pose. | §3 steps 1-2 |
| 9 | **Identity survives every variant:** a variant keeps the base joints, names, rest pose, sockets, contact points and secondary chains (a premium variant never loses the base's moving parts), so every shared clip still plays. | A sword skin keeps the grip and guard sockets of the base sword. | R-11: socket and contact positions equal the base within tolerance; chain count ≥ the base; the base clips play on the variant. | D-13; `references/design-for-motion.md` §1 |
| 10 | **Controlled variation, never exact repetition:** the rig never forces twinning; chains can be offset; left and right move on their own. | Both feet can land a frame apart; a ponytail chain lags bone by bone. | P-07 lists no symmetric-only constraint; the chain is driven as one mass plus offsets. | D-22, D-19 |
| 11 | **Problem, constraints and target before building:** the read, the needs, the camera, the budgets and the platform limits are written before a bone is placed. | Pivots for a turret wait for its yaw and pitch ranges and the camera distance. | P-01, P-02, P-10 dated before the plan renders. | §3 steps 1-2, 9 |
| 12 | **Options, then a reasoned pick:** when two routes or two pivot placements are close, both are posed at the worst pose and the pick is written with its reason. | Shoulder as a socketed piece vs a skinned bend: both posed at the 160° raise; the skinned one wins because the cap shows. | P-05: one black-fill per option and the recorded pick. | §3 step 5 |
| 13 | **Cheap stages first; gates freeze decisions:** plan, posed proxy, geometry acceptance, build, release; after release any joint, name or rest change is a versioned migration. | The cape is cut to a three-bone chain at the plan, not after weights. | P-11 before S3; the revision log names the retests of every post-release change. | D-13; rig-pipeline §6 |
| 14 | **Critique against the goal, one named problem at a time:** each fix note names the layer (pivot, parent, bridge, weights) and one pass condition; a note's suggested fix is checked against the problem behind it. | "Add a neck bone" from the animator turns out to be a pivot inside the skull: the pivot moves, no bone is added. | Every fix and rig-note answer names the problem, the layer and the re-check. | `references/diagnosis.md` §1 |
| 15 | **Build for the next craft and the engine:** the animator gets a control manifest, the read controls and stated limits; VFX gets sockets; the modeler gets cuts with reasons; everything exports and plays in Roblox; repeated steps become scripts. | A head-orient switch that survives the bake and the FBX re-import. | Zero open questions in the K2 and S6 answers; R-06 after re-import. | D-18; rigging-craft §3 |
| 16 | **Know when it is done:** the rig is done when the release lines pass with Blender and Roblox evidence for one revision; no extra controls, no polish beyond the clip list. | After the release record passes, a requested "nice to have" finger chain is logged as a ticket. | The release record complete (tests §5, §6); extras logged as tickets. | rigging-craft §6 |

What is read first by contrast, timing and cause-and-effect, feedback sized by the real outcome, and reading without colour or sound are decided by the crafts that work on top of the rig (art, animation, VFX, sound, UI). The rig's part of them is principle 1: every tell, contact and outcome pose is reachable and reads as a pose, without effects.
