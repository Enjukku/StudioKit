# Cases: what durable games teach, and what not to copy

Only two of these are Roblox games (Adopt Me, BedWars). Intervals from other platforms are not Roblox guidance. No source here gives a causal retention or revenue lift (OQ-01).

| Game | What the source says (checked) | Lesson to consider (O) | Do not copy | FG |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Adopt Me** (Roblox) | After 56 weekly updates (2020-04-30) Uplift moved from a fixed Friday release to a flexible schedule with announced times, naming the wellbeing of the team, the developers and the players: late Friday finishing, players in other time zones staying up late, creators waiting for hours. It did not say capacity was exceeded | A fixed release-day appointment has costs for the team and the players; announce times instead of making everyone wait | a permanent weekly obligation; reading the post as "they ran out of capacity" (the paper's reading) | FG-01 |
| Adopt Me | Cherry Blossom Festival: 4 weeks (2025-04-04 to 05-02) with weekly additions. A 2023 year-end post's "56 weekly updates" is a different statement ("only 52 weeks in a year!") | one event can carry several beats without new systems | a "4 weeks is right" rule | FG-02, FG-03 |
| Adopt Me (Roblox case study, 2026-08-24) | trading-hub tests on server size and matchmaking; a friend-based currency led to spam friend requests and private-server avoidance; summer decorative fish caused performance problems (crash and frame rates) | test what a reward makes players do; a small visual addition can sink weak devices | a "social" reward without watching the behaviour | FR-52 |
| **BedWars** (Roblox) | the listing promises "Updates are every FRIDAY"; one publish-date check (2026-10-20, a Tuesday) shows a listing line cannot prove the promise is kept | a clear, predictable appointment, if the team can keep it | inferring team size, cost or effect from a public cadence | FG-04 |
| **Fortnite** | over 44.7 million players and 102 million hours on 2023-11-04 (via press copy of Epic's post); OG ran as phases through November 2023; a synchronized live finale was set for 2024-11-30, 2 PM ET | familiar content with better access can be a return reason; a synchronized moment needs explicit, localized time | treating scale counts as a return rate; celebrity, licence or infrastructure scale | FG-05..FG-07 (*unverified*) |
| **League of Legends** | 2026 patches about every 14 days with exceptions (Oct 7, Oct 21); Arena "aims" to balance on odd-numbered patches so players have time to discover builds | stable discovery time is a design resource; separate oppressive from unfamiliar | balancing everything every patch; a large team's load | FG-08, FG-09 |
| **Teamfight Tactics** | 3 sets per year from 2023-09; adding "a couple" of champions every couple of weeks moved sentiment and playtime almost not at all, while the bundled mid-set brought a "brutal" workload; a shorter test period tied to an outside date cost balance and let bugs through | meaningful novelty and production cost both matter; protect test time | a set as a cheap reskin; a partner date as permission to skip QA | FG-10..FG-12 |
| **Brawl Stars** | seasons became about a month, from the first Thursday (announced 2023-12-06, effective January 2024). A collaboration needed work equal to 3 Brawlers; its animations alone drew 40M+ views; revenue and MAU fell ~20 % (Dec 2024 → Feb 2025), which Supercell tied to an event it called underwhelming, with named faults (an overpowered, everywhere limited unit; a free release it called a mistake; off-theme store skins; an event-currency bug) and to being unable to "pivot fast enough". Ranked raised mid-trophy playtime 27 % in its first month, then normalized, while high-trophy participation fell 50 %. A 2026 test split players 80/20 on monthly vs weekly quests, no result stated | price a temporary feature against what the same effort could fix permanently; execution faults can sink a well-promoted event; an early lift in one group can fade while another group falls; experiments are a normal tool | "attention is not retention" as Supercell's own finding (it is the paper's reading); an overpowered promotional unit | FG-13..FG-16 |
| **Genshin Impact** | version 2.7 was expected to last 6 weeks (2022, second-hand); 2026 versions launched Aug 12 and Sep 23 | durable world + bounded reasons to revisit | a character-release treadmill without its budget; a release interval as development time | FG-17, FG-18 (*unverified*) |
| **Overwatch** | 2022: 9-week seasons; 2026: Seasons 1-6 joined into an annual story, with a section for returning players | a coherent arc makes separate patches readable; returners need orientation first | a historical interval as a 2026 contract | FG-19, FG-20 |
| **Dota 2** | irregular gaps between patches (8 to 62 days); a 2026 event update paid Scrap tickets win or lose and improved reward information, bundled with fixes | repair event friction; reward participation that helps the match | a fixed monthly package regardless of readiness | FG-21, FG-22 |
| **Minecraft** | smaller free drops instead of one big summer update (press copy; date and the community-testing example unconfirmed) | smaller coherent releases can coexist with longer work | adding disconnected mechanics to fill a calendar | FG-23 (*unverified*) |
| **Hearthstone** | 2026 expansions in March, July, October; Mini-Sets "taking a break" for "Four Class Sets" per wave (first wave Patch 35.4, May 5) | revise packaging when it no longer serves players | bolting a second pass onto the first | FG-24, FG-25 |
| **Clash Royale** | a 2026 progression change: King Tower level "can only stay the same, or go up" | state what happens to old progress before asking for new investment | a migration that silently lowers progress | FG-26 |
| **Destiny 2** | a May 2025 roadmap: 2 paid expansions, 2 free major updates (press copy) | separate paid value from health work everyone needs | putting a fix behind a paid product | FG-27 (*unverified*) |
| **Riot (feedback practice)** | uses focus groups and surveys and watches social media, Discord channels and forums (skin research) | triangulate methods | treating channel monitoring as conversations or weights | FG-30 |

**Cross-case reading (O).** No two studios agree on a rhythm; each fits its production system and its player promise. The transferable things are mechanisms: announce times instead of fixed waits, carry several beats in one event, protect test time, test reward behaviour, orient returners, state what happens to old progress, and judge by later behaviour rather than the first week (FG §4).

**Dropped:** G18 (no register entry), the Minecraft community-testing example, "4 classes per wave" (it is four Class Sets) (FG §5-6).
